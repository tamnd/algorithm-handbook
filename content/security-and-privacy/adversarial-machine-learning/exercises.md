The exercises use tiny models with stated numbers, so that every answer can be checked by hand. Inputs lie in the box $[0,1]^d$ unless an exercise says otherwise, $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function, and the margin of a point with label $y$ is $m(x) = z_y(x) - \max_{j \ne y} z_j(x)$. Several exercises use the network $N$ with two inputs, two hidden ReLU units and two logits:

$$h = \mathrm{relu}(x_1 + x_2,\ x_1 - x_2), \qquad z_0 = 2h_1 - h_2 - \tfrac12, \qquad z_1 = h_2,$$

at the point $x^0 = (0.5, 0.25)$, where $h = (0.75, 0.25)$, $z = (0.75, 0.25)$ and the label is $0$. Its margin is $m(x) = 2h_1 - 2h_2 - \frac12$. A ball of radius $\epsilon$ means the $\ell_\infty$ ball $B_\infty(x^0, \epsilon)$ intersected with the box.

1. ◦ A linear classifier predicts class 1 when $f(x) = x_1 - 2x_2 + 2x_3 - 1 > 0$. At $x = (0.8, 0.2, 0.4)$, compute $f(x)$, the smallest $\ell_2$ change $\rho_2$ and the smallest $\ell_\infty$ change $\rho_\infty$ that reach the boundary $f = 0$, and the closest boundary point in each norm (see [adversarial example](/adversarial-example/)). Check that the box $[0,1]^3$ does not change either answer.

2. ◦ A [logistic regression](/logistic-regression/) model gives $p(y = 1 \mid x) = \sigma(2x_1 - x_2 + x_3)$. Take $x = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$ with label $y = 1$. Compute one [FGSM](/fast-gradient-sign-method/) step of size $\epsilon = 0.1$, the new logit and the new probability of class 1. What is the smallest $\epsilon$ at which one FGSM step brings the logit to $0$?

3. ◦ A smoothed classifier with Gaussian noise of $\sigma = 0.25$ has, at a point, top class probability $p_A = 0.8$. Compute the certified $\ell_2$ radius of [randomized smoothing](/randomized-smoothing/) when the runner-up has $p_B = 0.2$, and when $p_B = 0.1$. What radius does $\sigma = 0.5$ give with $p_A = 0.8$, $p_B = 0.2$?

4. ◦ A threshold [membership inference attack](/membership-inference-attack/) says "member" when the model's confidence on the true label is at least $\tau$. On four members the confidences are $0.95, 0.9, 0.7, 0.6$, and on four nonmembers $0.8, 0.55, 0.5, 0.3$. Give the TPR, the FPR and the advantage $\mathrm{TPR} - \mathrm{FPR}$ for $\tau = 0.65$ and for $\tau = 0.85$. Which $\tau$ maximizes the advantage on this table, and why is that maximum a biased estimate of the attack's advantage?

5. ◦ A [green list watermark](/language-model-watermarking/) detector uses $\gamma = 0.25$. A text has $T = 64$ scored tokens. Compute the z-score when 30 of them are green and when 20 are green, and say which text is flagged at the threshold $z^* = 4$. For $z^* = 4$, which of the two numbers $3.2 \times 10^{-5}$ and $e^{-6} \approx 0.0025$ is a guaranteed bound on the false positive rate, and under what model?

6. ◦ Five clients send the updates $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(2,1)$ and $(6,6)$, and at most $f = 1$ of them is Byzantine. Check the condition of [Krum](/krum-aggregation/), compute the five scores and give the update that Krum returns. Compare it with the mean.

7. ◦ Least squares through the origin fits $w = \sum x_i y_i / \sum x_i^2$. The data are $(1, 2)$, $(2, 3)$, $(3, 7)$, $(2, 5)$. Give the stored state $(\sum x_i y_i, \sum x_i^2)$ and $w$. A deletion request names the point $(3, 7)$: give the new state and $w$, and say why this is exact [machine unlearning](/machine-unlearning/). Someone proposes instead to run a few gradient steps on the remaining data from the old $w$. Why is that not exact unlearning in general?

8. ◦ A model is trained by [SISA](/sisa-training/) with $n = 1000$ records, $S = 5$ shards and no slicing ($R = 1$), each shard trained for $E$ full-batch steps. Count the cost of one deletion in per-record gradients, and compare it with retraining one model on all remaining records for $E$ steps. How many of the five votes can a deletion change?

9. ◦ Find the error. A report says: "At $\epsilon = 2/255$, [interval bound propagation](/interval-bound-propagation/) gives a negative margin bound on 38 of 100 test points, so 38 points have adversarial examples and the robust accuracy is 62%." Assume the other 62 points are correctly classified with a positive bound. State what the numbers do show, and how robust accuracy should be reported.

10. ◦ Find the error. A report says: "With $\sigma = 0.5$, 900 of 1000 noisy samples voted for class A, so our network $f$ is certified robust at this point within $\ell_2$ radius $0.5\,\Phi^{-1}(0.9) = 0.64$." Name three things that are wrong or missing, using the CERTIFY procedure of randomized smoothing with $\alpha = 0.001$.

11. • The classifier $f(x) = 3x_1 - x_2 + 2x_3 - 2$ is positive at $x = (0.9, 0.9, 0.6)$. Compute $\rho_\infty = f(x)/\lVert w \rVert_1$, show that the box $[0,1]^3$ stops the point from reaching the boundary at that distance, and find the true smallest $\ell_\infty$ change inside the box. Describe the general method for a linear model with a box.

12. • Run interval bound propagation through the network $N$ at $x^0$, folding the margin into the last layer. Give the bounds of the two pre-activations and of the margin for $\epsilon = 0.05$ and $\epsilon = 0.1$, and say whether the point is certified. Show that the IBP bound is $\frac12 - 8\epsilon$ for $\epsilon \le 1/8$ and give the radius IBP certifies.

13. • For the network $N$, find the exact minimum of the margin over the ball of radius $\epsilon \le 1/4$ and the exact robust $\ell_\infty$ radius of $x^0$. Then compute one FGSM step on the margin with $\epsilon = 0.1$ and with $\epsilon = 0.15$, and say what each result shows about the radius. Why does IBP certify only half of the exact radius here?

14. • An affine three-class model has $z_0 = x_1 + 2x_2$, $z_1 = 2x_1 + \frac12$ and $z_2 = -x_1 + x_2 + 1$, with $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (no box). At $x = (1, 1)$, run one step of [DeepFool](/deepfool/) with overshoot $\gamma = 0.02$: give the distances to the two linearized boundaries, the step, the new point and its label. Show that $0.2236$ is the exact smallest $\ell_2$ change of the label.

15. • A two-class linear model has logit difference $\mu(x) = w \cdot x + b$, and [defensive distillation](/defensive-distillation/) at temperature $T = 20$ turns it into a model whose logit difference is $T\mu(x)$, as in the linear case of the entry. At a point with $\mu = 0.5$, compute the probability of the top class and its derivative with respect to $\mu$ for the original model and for the distilled one. Does the smallest $\ell_2$ change of the label differ between the two models? What does this say about an evaluation that measures only gradient-based attack success?

16. • CERTIFY with $\alpha = 0.001$ and $\sigma = 0.5$ draws $n = 100$ noisy samples, and all of them vote for class A. Show that the one-sided Clopper-Pearson lower bound is $\underline{p_A} = \alpha^{1/n}$, and compute it and the certified radius. What is the smallest $n$ with all votes for A that certifies radius at least $1$?

17. • Four honest clients send $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(2,1)$. (a) Which fifth update moves the mean of the five to $(10, 10)$, and what does Krum with $f = 1$ return then? (b) The Byzantine client sends $(0.5, 0.5)$ instead. Show that Krum returns it, compute $\rho^2 = \min_{i \in H} \max_{j \in H} \lVert V_i - V_j \rVert^2$ and the bound $\frac{n-f-2}{n-2f-1}\rho^2$ of the Krum entry, and check the bound.

18. • In the [likelihood ratio attack](/likelihood-ratio-membership-attack/), record C has IN values $\{2, 4, 6\}$ and OUT values $\{0, 2, 4\}$ of the logit-scaled confidence, and the target gives $\phi^* = 3.5$. Record D has IN $\{6, 7, 8\}$, OUT $\{5, 6, 7\}$ and $\phi^* = 5$. Using the fitted means and the population variances (equal within each record), compute $\Lambda$ for both records and the decisions at the cut $t = 0$. Show that no single global threshold on $\phi^*$ gives the same two decisions. Compute the offline score $\Pr[Z \le \phi^*]$ of record C.

19. • A watermarked text has $T = 100$ scored tokens, 50 of them green, with $\gamma = 0.25$ and threshold $z^* = 4$. An editor substitutes tokens; each substitution lowers the green count by at most $2$, and assume the number of scored tokens stays $100$. What is the least number of substitutions that could bring the text to $z \le 4$? Answer the same question for $T = 400$ scored tokens with 200 green.

20. • SISA with $n = 1200$ records, $S = 4$ shards and $R = 3$ slices of equal size trains each shard on growing prefixes, $E$ steps per slice. Compute the cost of training in per-record gradients, the cost of deleting a record of the first slice, and the mean cost of deleting a uniformly chosen record. Compare the mean with the approximation $(2R + 1)/(3SR)$ of the entry. If four requests arrive and are batched, how many shards retrain in expectation?

21. • The learned features of ten training points of one label are $(-1, 2)$, $(-1, -2)$, $(-1, 1)$, $(-1, -1)$, $(-2, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, $(0, 2)$, $(0, -2)$, $(3, 0)$, $(3, 0)$, where the last two are poisoned. Run the [spectral signatures](/spectral-signatures-defense/) filter: give the mean, the matrix $C$, the top direction $v$ and the scores. Which points are removed for $\epsilon = 0.2$ and for $\epsilon = 0.1$? Which direction would $v$ have for the eight clean points alone?

22. • Find the error. A defense puts a random resize-and-pad step and a pixel quantization step in front of a classifier. Its authors run [PGD](/pgd-attack/) for 100 steps, with the gradient of one random draw per step and a zero gradient through the quantizer, report 2% attack success, and call the model robust. Name the two flaws, the two tools from the entries that address them, and how the success rate of the corrected attack must be measured.

23. ★ Run linear bound propagation (CROWN, see [linear bound propagation](/linear-bound-propagation/)) on the network $N$ for $\epsilon = 0.1$ and $\epsilon = 0.15$, using the adaptive lower slope. Give the relaxation of each unit, the final linear bound in $x$ and its minimum over the ball. Compare with IBP and with the exact minimum, and find the radius that CROWN certifies.

24. ★ Prove that for an affine layer $z = Wx + b$ and the box with center $\mu$ and radius vector $r$, the interval with center $W\mu + b$ and radius $|W|r$ is exactly the range of each coordinate of $z$ over the box, and that a ReLU maps an exact interval to an exact interval. Then show that IBP is not exact after two layers: for $N$ at $\epsilon = 0.1$, compare the IBP interval of $h_1 - h_2$ with its exact range.

25. ★ A mechanism $M$ is $\epsilon$-differentially private, and $D$, $D'$ differ in one record. A test sees $M(D)$ or $M(D')$ and says "member" with probability $\mathrm{FPR}$ on $D$ and $\mathrm{TPR}$ on $D'$. Show that $\mathrm{TPR} \le e^\epsilon\,\mathrm{FPR}$ and $1 - \mathrm{FPR} \le e^\epsilon (1 - \mathrm{TPR})$, and deduce $\mathrm{TPR} - \mathrm{FPR} \le (e^\epsilon - 1)/(e^\epsilon + 1)$. Compare this with $e^\epsilon - 1$ for $\epsilon = 0.5$, $1$ and $2$.

26. ★ A partition defense assigns each training point to one of $m$ parts by a hash of the point, trains one model per part and predicts by plurality vote, as in [data poisoning](/data-poisoning/). Prove that a prediction whose vote gap to the runner-up exceeds $2r$ cannot be changed by inserting or deleting $r$ training points. With $m = 40$ and votes $21$, $12$, $7$, find the largest certified $r$, and show that one more point could change the prediction. Why must the part of a point depend on the point alone?

27. ★ Show that $\lVert \delta \rVert_2 \le \sqrt d\, \lVert \delta \rVert_\infty$ with equality for $\delta \in \{-\epsilon, \epsilon\}^d$, so that an $\ell_2$ certificate of radius $R$ covers the $\ell_\infty$ ball of radius $R/\sqrt d$ and, by this argument, no larger one. For $d = 3072$ and $\epsilon_\infty = 8/255$, find the $\ell_2$ radius needed. With $\alpha = 0.001$ and all votes for the top class, find the least number of samples that CERTIFY needs for $\sigma = 0.5$ and for $\sigma = 1$, and say why the larger $\sigma$ is not a free gain.
