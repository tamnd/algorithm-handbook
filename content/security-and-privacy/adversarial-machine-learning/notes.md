## History

Attacks on learned models were studied by security researchers before deep learning. Barreno, Nelson, Sears, Joseph and Tygar (2006) gave a taxonomy that separates causative attacks on the training data from exploratory attacks at test time, and Barreno, Nelson, Joseph and Tygar extended it in 2010. Biggio, Nelson and Laskov (2012) gave the first gradient-based poisoning attack, on a support vector machine. In 2013 Biggio and colleagues stated evasion at test time as an optimization problem with an explicit adversary model, and Szegedy and colleagues found that small, gradient-guided changes fool deep networks (posted in 2013, ICLR 2014).

The attacks came quickly after that. Goodfellow, Shlens and Szegedy (ICLR 2015) explained adversarial examples by the near-linearity of networks and gave FGSM. Papernot and colleagues gave JSMA and defensive distillation (2016), Moosavi-Dezfooli, Fawzi and Frossard gave DeepFool (CVPR 2016), and Carlini and Wagner (IEEE S&P 2017) gave optimization attacks that broke distillation. Madry, Makelov, Schmidt, Tsipras and Vladu (ICLR 2018) made PGD the standard first-order attack and the inner step of adversarial training. Athalye, Carlini and Wagner (ICML 2018) showed that 7 of 9 defenses of ICLR 2018 relied on obfuscated gradients, and gave BPDA and the use of EOT against them; Tramer, Carlini, Brendel and Madry (NeurIPS 2020) later broke 13 more defenses with adaptive attacks.

Certificates were the answer to that cycle. Katz, Barrett, Dill, Julian and Kochenderfer (CAV 2017) gave Reluplex and proved that verifying ReLU networks is NP-complete. In 2018 Wong and Kolter, Weng and colleagues, and Zhang and colleagues found fast sound bounds from linear relaxations, and Gowal and colleagues trained networks with interval bounds. Lecuyer and colleagues and then Cohen, Rosenfeld and Kolter (ICML 2019) certified noisy classifiers, the latter with the tight $\ell_2$ radius of Gaussian smoothing.

Training-time and privacy attacks grew in parallel. Gu, Dolan-Gavitt and Garg and Chen and colleagues published backdoors in 2017, Shafahi and colleagues gave clean-label poisons in 2018, and Tran, Li and Madry gave spectral signatures the same year. Blanchard, El Mhamdi, Guerraoui and Stainer (NeurIPS 2017) proposed Krum. Fredrikson and colleagues showed model inversion (2014, 2015), Tramer and colleagues model extraction (2016), Shokri, Stronati, Song and Shmatikov membership inference with shadow models (2017), Zhu, Liu and Han gradient inversion (2019), and Carlini and colleagues training data extraction from GPT-2 (2021) and the likelihood ratio attack (2022). Cao and Yang (2015) posed machine unlearning, and Bourtoule and colleagues gave SISA (IEEE S&P 2021).

Language models brought the newest problems. Perez and Ribeiro (2022) studied text that overrides an application's instructions, and Greshake and colleagues (2023) the indirect form through retrieved data. Zou and colleagues (2023) showed automated attacks on safety training, and Kirchenbauer and colleagues (ICML 2023) gave the green list watermark.

## Further reading

- Battista Biggio and Fabio Roli, *Wild patterns: Ten years after the rise of adversarial machine learning*, Pattern Recognition 84, 2018. The history of the field and the three-part threat model that the entries use.
- Marco Barreno, Blaine Nelson, Anthony D. Joseph and J. D. Tygar, *The security of machine learning*, Machine Learning 81, 2010. The taxonomy of causative and exploratory attacks and of their goals.
- Nicholas Carlini, Anish Athalye, Nicolas Papernot, Wieland Brendel, Jonas Rauber, Dimitris Tsipras, Ian Goodfellow, Aleksander Madry and Alexey Kurakin, *On evaluating adversarial robustness*, arXiv 2019. A checklist for evaluating a defense, written by people who broke many.
- Anish Athalye, Nicholas Carlini and David Wagner, *Obfuscated gradients give a false sense of security: Circumventing defenses to adversarial examples*, ICML 2018. The case study of how defenses fail and how to test them.
- Jeremy Cohen, Elan Rosenfeld and J. Zico Kolter, *Certified adversarial robustness via randomized smoothing*, ICML 2019. A short, clear paper with the tight radius and the procedures for prediction and certification.
- Nicholas Carlini, Steve Chien, Milad Nasr, Shuang Song, Andreas Terzis and Florian Tramer, *Membership inference attacks from first principles*, IEEE S&P 2022. How to measure privacy leakage, and why the TPR at a low FPR matters.
