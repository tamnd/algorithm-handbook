In these exercises left-to-right square and multiply starts from the value 1, squares once for every bit of the exponent from the top bit down, and multiplies by the base after the squaring when the bit is 1, as in the [timing attack](/timing-attack/) entry; S is a squaring and M a multiplication. $S$ is the 4-bit S-box of PRESENT, $S = (\mathrm{C}, 5, 6, \mathrm{B}, 9, 0, \mathrm{A}, \mathrm{D}, 3, \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{F}, 8, 4, 7, 1, 2)$ in hexadecimal for the inputs $0, \dots, \mathrm{F}$, and $\mathrm{HW}$ is the Hamming weight. "The example" is the one of [differential power analysis](/differential-power-analysis/): key $k = \mathrm{B}$, the 16 plaintexts $p = 0, \dots, \mathrm{F}$ once each, and the noise-free leakage $\mathrm{HW}(S(p \oplus k))$.

1. ◦ Write the list of per-bit costs, in multiplications, of square and multiply for the exponent $d = 89$. What are the total, the number of squarings and the number of multiplications by the base?

2. ◦ A stopwatch shows that square and multiply with a 20-bit secret exponent (top bit 1) did 31 multiplications in all. (a) What is the Hamming weight of the exponent? (b) How many 20-bit exponents are consistent with this reading, and how many bits of the key has the reading given away, compared with the $2^{19}$ exponents possible before?

3. ◦ The Montgomery ladder does one squaring and one multiplication for every bit. For an 8-bit exponent, what is its list of per-bit costs? Square and multiply on a random 8-bit exponent, with the top bit 1 and the other seven bits fair coin flips, does how many multiplications on average? How much slower is the ladder, as a ratio?

4. ◦ An ECDSA implementation runs its scalar multiplication loop once for each bit of the nonce, and the nonces are uniform in $[0, 2^{256})$. An attacker keeps the signatures whose nonce is below $2^{252}$. What fraction of the signatures is that, and about how many signatures must the attacker collect to keep 100?

5. ◦ One power trace of square and multiply shows the operations S M S S S M S M S M S. Decode the exponent, in binary and in decimal. How long is the sequence for a $k$-bit exponent of Hamming weight $h$?

6. ◦ The first round of DES uses a 48-bit round key, split into eight 6-bit parts, one for each S-box, and these 48 bits are 48 of the 56 key bits. A power analysis can test the guesses of each part separately. How many guesses does it test to recover the round key, and how many more trials with a known plaintext and ciphertext finish the key? Compare with $2^{56}$.

7. ◦ On $w = 8$ bits, the constant-time equality test of the entry on [constant-time programming](/constant-time-programming/) is $\mathrm{EQ}(x, y) = 1 - ((d \mid (0 - d)) \gg 7)$ with $d = x \oplus y$ and arithmetic modulo $2^8$. Compute it for $x = 12_{16}$, $y = 16_{16}$ and for $x = y = 5\mathrm{A}_{16}$. Explain why $d \mid (0 - d)$ has its top bit set exactly when $d \ne 0$.

8. ◦ A TVLA test uses the threshold 4.5, and with no leak each $|t|$ exceeds it with probability $6.8 \times 10^{-6}$. A trace has 20000 samples. How many false alarms do you expect in one set, and what is the probability of at least one? What is the expected number with the rule that a sample must exceed 4.5 in two independent sets?

9. ◦ A website fingerprinting classifier has a true positive rate of 85 percent and a false positive rate of 0.6 percent. (a) What is its precision when 1 in 100 visits is to a monitored page? (b) When 1 in 1000 visits is, what false positive rate would give a precision of 0.9?

10. • A server checks a 16-byte MAC tag with a byte comparison that returns at the first byte that differs, and the attacker can tell from the time how many bytes matched. Describe how to forge the tag for a chosen message byte by byte, and give the largest number of tags the attacker must try. Compare with guessing the whole tag.

11. • Take [Montgomery multiplication](/montgomery-multiplication/) with $n = 13$ and $R = 16$, so that $\mathrm{Mont}(a, b) = a b R^{-1} \bmod 13$, computed as $t = ab$, $m = (t \bmod R)\, n' \bmod R$ with $n' = -n^{-1} \bmod R$, $u = (t + mn)/R$, and an extra reduction $u \leftarrow u - n$ when $u \ge n$. (a) Find $n'$. (b) Compute $\mathrm{Mont}(7, 11)$ and $\mathrm{Mont}(3, 4)$, and say for each whether the extra reduction happens. (c) Write a short program to count the pairs $0 \le a, b \le 12$ for which it happens.

12. • A fixed-window exponentiation reads the exponent in windows of 2 bits from the top. For each window $w$ it squares twice and then, if $w \ne 0$, multiplies by the table entry $c^w$. (a) Give the per-window costs and the table indices read for $d = 157 = 10011101_2$ and for $d = 135 = 10000111_2$. (b) What does an attacker learn from the costs alone, and what from the addresses of the table reads?

13. • Each timing measurement has noise with standard deviation 400 cycles, and the attacker wants to see a difference of 20 cycles between the mean times of two sets of inputs. With $k$ independent measurements in each set, the standard error of the difference of the two means is $400 \sqrt{2/k}$. How large must $k$ be for the standard error to be at most a third of the difference?

14. • In the example, run differential power analysis on bit 2 and on bit 3 of $S(p \oplus g)$ instead of bit 1: for each guess $g$, the score is the mean leakage of the traces whose predicted bit is 1 minus the mean of the others. Write a short program for the 16 scores of each bit. Which bit separates the key from every wrong guess, and does it still do so if the attacker ranks the guesses by the absolute value of the score?

15. • In the example, [mutual information analysis](/mutual-information-analysis/) gives the right guess 2.031 bits. (a) Show that this is $H(\mathrm{HW}(U))$ for a uniform 4-bit $U$, computing it from the counts $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$. (b) Show that if the model is the predicted value $S(p \oplus g)$ itself instead of its weight, every guess scores 2.031 bits.

16. • The entry on [correlation power analysis](/correlation-power-analysis/) gives the rule $m \approx 3 + 8 z^2 / \ln^2 \frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}$ with $z = 3.719$, and $\rho = 1/\sqrt{1 + \sigma^2/\varepsilon^2}$ for Hamming weight leakage. Take $\varepsilon = 1$. Compute $\rho$ and $m$ for $\sigma = 2$ and $\sigma = 4$, compare with the approximation $28/\rho^2$, and compare the first value with the entry's measured success rates at $\sigma = 2$.

17. • In a fixed-versus-random test at one sample, the fixed set of 500 traces has mean 10.3 and variance 4, and the random set of 500 traces has mean 10.0 and variance 5. Compute the [Welch t](/welch-t-test/) statistic. If the means and variances stay the same, how many traces in each set make $|t|$ reach 4.5?

18. • A software AES reads a table of 256 entries of 4 bytes, aligned to the start of a 64-byte cache line, at the index $x = p \oplus k$ in its first round, for each key byte $k$ and known plaintext byte $p$. A [Prime+Probe](/prime-and-probe/) attacker learns which line of the table each first-round lookup used. How many lines does the table fill, which bits of $x$ does a line give, and how many of the 128 key bits does the first round reveal?

19. • The [ISW](/isw-private-circuits/) AND gadget with $n$ shares has $2n$ input shares, $n^2$ products, $n(n-1)/2$ random bits and $2n(n-1)$ XOR gates. Check that counting each of these as one wire gives the entry's 13 wires for $n = 2$ and 30 for $n = 3$, and its 78 pairs and 4060 triples. How many wires and how many sets of 3 probes does an exhaustive check of the gadget with $n = 4$ against 3 probes examine?

20. • A web page reflects a string chosen by the attacker into the same compressed body as a secret token of 32 hexadecimal characters, which follows the fixed text `token=`. Assume that a guess of `token=` followed by the right next character always compresses one byte shorter than a wrong one. (a) How many requests at most does the attacker need to read the token one character at a time? (b) For each of these defenses, say which of the conditions COMBINE, MATCH, LENGTH and REPEAT of the [compression side channel](/compression-side-channel/) entry it removes, fully or partly: a separate compression context for the token; a fresh random mask on the token in every response; a random number of padding bytes in every response; a limit of 100 requests for each client.

21. ★ [RSA blinding](/rsa-blinding/) computes $(c\, r^e)^d \bmod N$ for a random $r$ and then multiplies by $r^{-1}$. Assume $r$ is uniform over the units modulo $N$ and $\gcd(e, \varphi(N)) = 1$. Prove that for every unit $c$ the blinded input $c\, r^e \bmod N$ is uniform over the units, and conclude that the distribution of the number of extra reductions is the same for every chosen $c$.

22. ★ In Kocher's attack the bits of $d$ are recovered from the top, and each test uses the bits found so far to simulate the device. Suppose one bit is guessed wrong and the attack goes on. What do the tests of the following bits show, and how can the attacker use this to find and correct the error?

23. ★ A [template attack](/template-attack/) models the trace of class $v$ as Gaussian with mean $\mu_v$ and covariance $\Sigma_v$. Show that when all classes share one covariance $\Sigma$, the difference of the log-likelihoods of two classes is an affine function of the trace, as in [linear discriminant analysis](/linear-discriminant-analysis/), and that with one covariance for each class it is a quadratic function, as in [quadratic discriminant analysis](/quadratic-discriminant-analysis/). In the pooled case, what does the sum of the differences over $m$ independent traces of one class, for example with a fixed plaintext, depend on?

24. ★ The victim code of the [Spectre attack](/spectre-attack/) is `if (x < n) y = B[64 * A[x]]`, with $n = 2^j$. A patch replaces `A[x]` by `A[x & (n - 1)]`. (a) Show that the index is in $[0, n)$ for every $x$, so that the load from `A` stays inside the array on every path, including a wrong one. (b) Which of the conditions PREDICT, STEER, ACCESS, ENCODE and CHANNEL does the patch remove, and for which loads? (c) Why does it not help against an attack that steers an indirect jump to code chosen by the attacker, and what does one do when $n$ is not a power of 2?

25. ★ A bit $x$ is masked with three shares $s_1 = m_1$, $s_2 = m_2$, $s_3 = x \oplus m_1 \oplus m_2$, with $m_1$ and $m_2$ uniform and independent, and each share leaks $\ell_i = s_i + N_i$ with independent noise of mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$. (a) Show that $\mathrm{E}[\ell_i]$ and $\mathrm{E}[(\ell_i - \frac12)(\ell_j - \frac12)]$ for $i \ne j$ do not depend on $x$. (b) Show that $\mathrm{E}[\prod_{i=1}^{3} (\ell_i - \frac12)] = -\frac18 (-1)^x$. (c) Show that the variance of this product is $(\frac14 + \sigma^2)^3 - \frac1{64}$, and conclude how the number of traces needed to see the difference grows with $\sigma$ for large noise.

26. ★ In a model of [differential fault analysis](/differential-fault-analysis/), the last round computes $y = S(u) \oplus k$ with the PRESENT S-box, the attacker sees $y$, and a fault flips one known bit $e$ of the unknown $u$ and gives $y^* = S(u \oplus e) \oplus k$. A guess $g$ is consistent when $S^{-1}(y \oplus g) \oplus S^{-1}(y^* \oplus g) = e$. (a) For $e = 1$ the attacker gets the pairs $(y, y^*) = (7, \mathrm{E})$ and $(1, 6)$. Find the consistent guesses of each pair and of both. (b) Show that the guess $k \oplus 5$ is consistent with every pair for $e = 1$, whatever $u$ is, by checking that $f(u) = S^{-1}(S(u) \oplus 5)$ satisfies $f(u \oplus 1) = f(u) \oplus 1$. (c) A fault on bit 3 ($e = 8$) gives the pair $(\mathrm{D}, 4)$. Which key is left?
