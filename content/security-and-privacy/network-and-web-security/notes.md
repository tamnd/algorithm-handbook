## History

The web's boundary grew one feature at a time. Netscape browsers applied an origin check to JavaScript from the mid 1990s, and each browser grew its own version until Adam Barth wrote the common core down as RFC 6454 in 2011. Norm Hardy had described the confused deputy in 1988, the pattern behind request forgery; Barth, Collin Jackson and John C. Mitchell studied the defenses against it in 2008 and proposed the `Origin` header. CERT advisory CA-2000-02 of February 2000 described cross-site scripting, and Amit Klein described its DOM-based kind in 2005. Public descriptions of SQL injection date from the late 1990s. Buehrer, Weide and Sivilotti checked queries by their parse trees in 2005, and Su and Wassermann (POPL 2006) defined injection as input that is not syntactically confined. Stamm, Sterne and Markham proposed the content security policy at Mozilla in 2010, Samuel, Saxena and Song (CCS 2011) gave context-sensitive auto-escaping for templates, and Weichselbaum, Spagnuolo, Lekies and Janc (CCS 2016) showed that most host allowlists can be bypassed.

The transport and network attacks came from protocols designed without forgery in mind. William Cheswick and Steven Bellovin found the SYN flooding weakness in 1994, and it became public in 1996, when a flood stopped the mail service of a New York internet service provider. Daniel J. Bernstein proposed SYN cookies that September and worked out the details with Eric Schenk; RFC 4987 (2007) surveyed the defenses. Ferguson and Senie wrote ingress filtering down as RFC 2267 in 1998 and RFC 2827 (BCP 38) in 2000, and Baker and Savola gave the reverse path checks for multihomed networks in RFC 3704 (2004). Savage, Wetherall, Karlin and Anderson proposed probabilistic packet marking at SIGCOMM 2000, Song and Perrig refined it in 2001, and Snoeren and colleagues at BBN traced single packets with the Source Path Isolation Engine at SIGCOMM 2001.

Routing and naming came next. RFC 4272 (2006) analyzed the lack of origin and path checks in BGP, and on 24 February 2008 a more specific announcement made a video site unreachable from much of the internet for about two hours. The RPKI (RFC 6480, 2012), route origin validation (RFC 6811, 2013) and BGPsec (RFC 8205, 2017) followed, and RFC 9319 (2022) advised minimal ROAs. RFC 5452 (Hubert and van Mook, 2009) made random query IDs and ports a requirement for resolvers, Dagon et al. (CCS 2008) added random case in query names, and DNSSEC (RFC 4033 to 4035, 2005) signs the records. Al-Shaer and Hamed classified the anomalies of firewall rule lists in 2004.

Passwords and people were the last strand. Von Ahn, Blum, Hopper and Langford named the CAPTCHA and defined it at EUROCRYPT 2003, and reCAPTCHA (2008) used the solving effort to digitize books. Narayanan and Shmatikov (2005) modeled passwords with character Markov chains, Weir, Aggarwal, de Medeiros and Glodek (2009) with a grammar of base structures, and Ma, Yang, Luo and Li (2014) compared the two. Juels and Rivest proposed honeywords in 2013, and Wang, Cheng, Wang, Yan and Huang (NDSS 2018) measured that the first generators are far from flat. John Douceur named the Sybil attack and proved his lemmas at the first IPTPS in 2002. Yu, Kaminsky, Gibbons and Flaxman turned to social networks with SybilGuard (SIGCOMM 2006) and SybilLimit (2008), and Cao, Sirivianos, Yang and Pregueiro ranked fake accounts at Tuenti with SybilRank (NSDI 2012).

## Further reading

- Michal Zalewski, *The Tangled Web: A Guide to Securing Modern Web Applications*, No Starch Press, 2011. How browsers grew their security model, feature by feature, with the origin rules and their exceptions.
- William R. Cheswick, Steven M. Bellovin and Aviel D. Rubin, *Firewalls and Internet Security: Repelling the Wily Hacker*, Addison-Wesley, 2003. Network attacks of the TCP/IP era and firewalls as the defense against them.
- Stefan Savage, David Wetherall, Anna Karlin and Tom Anderson, "Practical network support for IP traceback", ACM SIGCOMM 2000. The paper of packet marking, with its analysis and the encoding of edges in the IP header.
- Sharon Goldberg, "Why is it taking so long to secure internet routing?", Communications of the ACM, 2014. The deployment problem of RPKI, origin validation and BGPsec, and what each one stops.
- Joseph Bonneau, "The science of guessing: analyzing an anonymized corpus of 70 million passwords", IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2012. How to measure the guessability of a password distribution.
