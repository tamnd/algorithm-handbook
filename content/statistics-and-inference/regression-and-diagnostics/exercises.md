1. ◦ A line is fitted by least squares to the four points $(1, 2)$, $(2, 1)$, $(3, 4)$, $(4, 5)$. Compute the fitted line, the residual sum of squares, the [coefficient of determination](/coefficient-of-determination/) $R^2$ and the adjusted $R^2$. Then compute the overall F statistic and its p-value, using $P(F_{1,2} > f) = 1 - \sqrt{f/(f + 2)}$. Is the slope significant at level 0.05?

2. ◦ A model with an intercept and two predictors $x_1, x_2$, in that column order, is fitted to $n = 10$ observations through a thin [QR decomposition](/qr-decomposition/) $X = Q_1R$. The vector $Q_1^\top y$ is $(c_1, 6, 3)$ and the residual sum of squares is 14. Without refitting, compute the partial [F statistic](/regression-f-test/) for dropping $x_2$, the overall F statistic for dropping both slopes, and $R^2$. Which hypotheses are rejected at level 0.05, given $F_{1,7,0.95} = 5.59$ and $F_{2,7,0.95} = 4.74$?

3. ◦ In a regression on several predictors, the regression of $x_1$ on the other predictors has $R^2 = 0.36$. Compute the [variance inflation factor](/variance-inflation-factor/) of $x_1$ and the factor by which its standard error is inflated. In a regression on exactly two predictors whose sample correlation is 0.95, compute the same two numbers for each predictor.

4. ◦ The Poisson counts $0, 1, 5$ are fitted by the model with only an intercept and the log link, so every fitted mean is $\bar y = 2$. Compute the [deviance](/deviance-statistic/) $D = 2\sum_i [y_i\log(y_i/\hat\mu_i) - (y_i - \hat\mu_i)]$, with $0 \log 0 = 0$, and the Pearson statistic $X^2$. Compute the upper tail probabilities of both on $\chi^2_2$, whose survival function is $e^{-x/2}$, and say why these numbers are not a reliable test of fit here.

5. ◦ Two normal linear models are fitted to $n = 20$ observations; the larger has three columns, one more than the smaller, and it lowers the residual sum of squares from 50 to 40. With $\mathrm{AIC} = n\log(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + 2k$ up to a constant, compute the change in [AIC](/akaike-information-criterion/) and in [BIC](/bayesian-information-criterion/) from the smaller model to the larger. Compute also the partial F statistic, to be compared with $F_{1,17,0.95} = 4.45$, and the likelihood ratio statistic $n\log(\mathrm{RSS}_0/\mathrm{RSS}_1)$, to be compared with $\chi^2_{1,0.95} = 3.84$. Which of the four rules choose the larger model?

6. ◦ A simple linear regression with an intercept has the predictor values $x = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 14)$. Compute every [leverage](/leverage-statistics/) $h_{ii}$ as an exact fraction, check that they sum to $p$, and say which points exceed the cutoff $2p/n$.

7. ◦ A regression with $n = 10$ and $p = 2$ has $s^2 = 2$. Observation $i$ has residual $e_i = 2$ and leverage $h_{ii} = 0.5$. Compute its internally [studentized residual](/studentized-residual/), the variance estimate $s_{(i)}^2$ without it, its externally studentized residual, its leave-one-out prediction error and its [Cook's distance](/cooks-distance/).

8. ◦ The sample is $0.2, 0.5, 0.9, 1.6, 3.8$. Using the plotting positions $p_i = (i - 1/2)/5$, compute the five normal quantiles of a normal [Q-Q plot](/q-q-plot/). Compute the correlation between the sorted data and these quantiles, and the same correlation for the logarithms of the data. On which scale does the sample look closer to normal, and which way does the raw plot bend?

9. ◦ The residuals of a regression on time-ordered data are $1, 2, 1, -1, -2, -1$. Compute the [Durbin-Watson](/durbin-watson-test/) statistic $d$ and the lag-one autocorrelation $r_1 = \sum_{t \ge 2}e_te_{t-1}/\sum_te_t^2$, and check the identity $d = 2(1 - r_1) - (e_1^2 + e_n^2)/\sum_te_t^2$. What kind of correlation does $d$ suggest?

10. ◦ Three laboratories measure the same quantity and report the means 10, 12 and 15, with variances 1, 4 and 4. Compute the [weighted least squares](/weighted-least-squares/) estimate with weights equal to the inverse variances and its variance. Compare both with the unweighted mean and its variance.

11. • For two normal linear models that differ by one column, with $n$ observations and $p$ columns in the larger one, show that AIC prefers the larger model exactly when the partial F statistic exceeds $(n - p)(e^{2/n} - 1)$, and BIC exactly when it exceeds $(n - p)(n^{1/n} - 1)$. For $n = 20$ and $p = 3$, compute both thresholds and the level of the F-test that uses each one as its cutoff.

12. • A [best subset](/leaps-and-bounds/) search over $m = 4$ candidate columns, each model with an intercept, gives the smallest residual sums of squares $120, 70, 52, 48, 47$ for the sizes $s = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$, with $n = 30$. Count $k = s + 2$ parameters (the intercept and the variance included) and compute $\mathrm{AIC} = n\log(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + 2k$ and $\mathrm{BIC} = n\log(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + k\log n$ for each size. Which size does each criterion choose?

13. • Fit a line to $x = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$ and $y = (1, 2, 6, 4, 5)$. Show that deleting the third point, which sits at $\bar x$, leaves the slope unchanged, and compute its Cook's distance both from the formula $D_i = \frac{r_i^2}{p}\frac{h_{ii}}{1 - h_{ii}}$ and from its definition as the sum of squared changes of the fitted values over $ps^2$. Then show that in any simple regression a point at $\bar x$ has $D_i = r_i^2/(2(n - 1))$.

14. • A $2 \times 2$ factorial design with levels $\pm 1$ has each of its four runs replicated twice, and the model has an intercept, both main effects and their interaction, so $n = 8$ and $p = 4$. Show that every leverage equals $1/2$. Deduce that each leave-one-out residual is twice the ordinary residual and that the PRESS statistic is four times the residual sum of squares.

15. • The model is $y_i = a + bx_i + \varepsilon_i$ at $x = 1, 2, \dots, 6$, with uncorrelated errors of variance $\sigma^2$. The grouping estimator of the slope is $\tilde b = (\bar y_{4,5,6} - \bar y_{1,2,3})/3$, a difference of the mean responses of the upper and the lower half. Show that it is linear and unbiased, compute its variance and compare it with the variance of the least squares slope. Which theorem predicts the direction of the comparison?

16. • In the model $y_i = a + bx_i + \varepsilon_i$ with $x = (1, 2, 3, 4)$, the errors are uncorrelated with $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = x_i$. Compute the true variance of the least squares slope, the variance of the weighted least squares slope with weights $1/x_i$, and the expected value of the usual estimate $s^2/S_{xx}$, where $S_{xx} = \sum(x_i - \bar x)^2$. What is the efficiency of least squares here, and is the usual estimate biased for this design?

17. • A line is fitted to $x = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$ and its residuals are $e = (0, 1, 0, -3, 2)$. Check that these are possible least squares residuals. Regress $e_i^2$ on $x_i$ with an intercept and compute Koenker's form $nR^2$ of the [Breusch-Pagan test](/breusch-pagan-test/) and the original form $\mathrm{ESS}/(2\hat\sigma^4)$, with $\hat\sigma^2 = \sum e_i^2/n$ and ESS the explained sum of squares of that regression. Give both asymptotic $\chi^2_1$ p-values, and show that the ratio of the two statistics is $\hat v/(2\hat\sigma^4)$, where $\hat v$ is the mean of $(e_i^2 - \hat\sigma^2)^2$.

18. • The responses $1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 8$ are fitted by the model with only an intercept, and they form three clusters of two in that order. Compute the usual standard error of the mean, and the [cluster-robust](/cluster-robust-standard-errors/) CR0 and CR1 standard errors, with the CR1 factor $\frac{G}{G - 1}\cdot\frac{n - 1}{n - p}$. Then show that for the intercept-only model with $G$ clusters of equal size, the CR1 t statistic is the one-sample t statistic of the $G$ cluster means. If those means are iid normal, what is the true size of the test that rejects when $|t| > 1.96$, for $G = 2$, 5 and 10?

19. • In a balanced one-way [linear mixed model](/linear-mixed-model/) $y_{gj} = \mu + u_g + e_{gj}$, four groups of two have the responses $(3, 5)$, $(6, 8)$, $(9, 9)$, $(2, 4)$. Compute the mean squares MSA and MSE, the ANOVA estimates of $\sigma_u^2$ and $\sigma_e^2$, which here are also the REML estimates, the intraclass correlation, the estimate of $\mu$ and the predictions of the four random effects $u_g$, taking the estimated variances as known.

20. • For Poisson counts with mean $e^b$, a model with only an intercept $b$ and the log link, show that one pass of [iteratively reweighted least squares](/iteratively-reweighted-least-squares/) is $b \leftarrow b + \bar ye^{-b} - 1$. Run it from $b = 0$ with $\bar y = 3$ until six decimals agree, and say how the error behaves from one pass to the next and why.

21. • In an [errors-in-variables](/errors-in-variables-regression/) problem the centred sample moments are $s_{xx} = 4$, $s_{yy} = 9$ and $s_{xy} = 3$. Compute the least squares slope of $y$ on $x$, the slope implied by the reverse regression of $x$ on $y$, the orthogonal regression slope, which is $\bigl(s_{yy} - s_{xx} + \sqrt{(s_{yy} - s_{xx})^2 + 4s_{xy}^2}\bigr)/(2s_{xy})$, and the least squares slope corrected for a known reliability ratio of 0.8. Which of these are ordered as theory says they must be for every data set?

22. • An effect $y \sim N(\mu, 1)$ is reported only because it passed the screen $y > 1.2$, and the observed value is $y = 1.8$. Compute the naive one-sided p-value for $\mu = 0$ and the selective one, $P_0(Y \ge 1.8 \mid Y > 1.2)$. Then compute the equal-tailed 95% selective interval for $\mu$, the set of $\mu$ for which $P_\mu(Y \le 1.8 \mid Y > 1.2)$ lies between 0.025 and 0.975, and compare it with the naive interval $1.8 \pm 1.96$. This is the [polyhedral lemma](/polyhedral-lemma/) in one dimension.

23. ★ For $n = 3$ observations and the model with only an intercept, show that the Durbin-Watson statistic always lies in $[1, 3]$, with the ends reached at residuals proportional to $(1, 0, -1)$ and $(1, -2, 1)$. Show that under iid normal errors $P(d \le c) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\sqrt{(c - 1)/(3 - c)}$ for $1 \le c \le 3$. Deduce $P(d \le 1.5)$ and the critical value of the one-sided 5% test against positive correlation.

24. ★ Two distinct positive responses $y_1 < y_2$ are fitted by the model with only an intercept after a [Box-Cox transformation](/box-cox-transformation/), with the profile log-likelihood $\ell(\lambda) = -\frac n2\log(\mathrm{RSS}(\lambda)/n) + (\lambda - 1)\sum_i\log y_i$. Show that $\ell(\lambda) = -2\log(\sinh(\lambda a)/\lambda) - \log(y_1y_2)$ with $a = \frac12\log(y_2/y_1)$, so that $\ell$ is even in $\lambda$ and $\hat\lambda = 0$ for every such pair. For $y = (1, 4)$ compute $\ell(0)$, $\ell(1)$ and the likelihood ratio statistic for $\lambda = 1$.

25. ★ The errors of the model $y_t = \mu + \varepsilon_t$, $t = 1, \dots, n$, are a stationary AR(1) process with $\rho = 1/2$ and innovation variance 1, so $\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_s, \varepsilon_t) = \rho^{|s-t|}/(1 - \rho^2)$. Show that the [generalized least squares](/generalized-least-squares/) estimate of $\mu$ gives the two end observations weight proportional to 1 and the inner ones weight proportional to $1 - \rho$, and that its variance is $1/\bigl((1 - \rho)(2 + (n - 2)(1 - \rho))\bigr)$. For $n = 3$ and $n = 5$, compute this variance, the variance of the sample mean and the efficiency of the sample mean.

26. ★ In a balanced one-way random effects layout with $a = 2$ groups of $m = 2$, let $\gamma = \sigma_u^2/\sigma_e^2$. Show that the ANOVA estimate of $\sigma_u^2$ is negative with probability exactly $1/\sqrt{3 + 4\gamma}$, using that $P(F_{1,2} \le c) = \sqrt{c/(c + 2)}$. Evaluate it at $\gamma = 0$, $1/2$ and 1. What does [restricted maximum likelihood](/restricted-maximum-likelihood/) return for $\sigma_u^2$ in these cases?

27. ★ A regression has two orthogonal centred predictor columns, normal errors and known $\sigma$. List the pairs $(j, M)$ of a coefficient and a submodel that [post-selection inference](/post-selection-inference/) by PoSI must cover, and show that the PoSI constant at $\alpha = 0.05$ is the constant $z$ for which two independent $|Z|$ values are both at most $z$ with probability 0.95. Compute it and compare it with the Bonferroni constant $z_{1 - 0.05/4}$ for the distinct statistics and the Scheffé bound $\sqrt{\chi^2_{2,0.95}}$, where $\chi^2_{2,0.95} = 2\log 20$. What is the simultaneous coverage of the naive intervals $\hat b_j \pm 1.96\sigma_j$?
