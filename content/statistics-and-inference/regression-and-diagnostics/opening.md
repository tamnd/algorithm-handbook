Six points have the predictor $x = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10)$ and the response $y = (2, 3, 5, 4, 6, 4)$. The least squares line is $3.2623 + 0.1770x$, and the residual at $x = 10$ is $-1.03$, the third smallest of the six in size. Yet that point decides the fit. Its [leverage](/leverage-statistics/) is $0.836$, so its fitted value puts weight $0.836$ on its own response. Without it the line is $1.3 + 0.9x$, which predicts $10.3$ at $x = 10$, a miss of $6.3$. Its externally [studentized residual](/studentized-residual/) is $-3.21$, and its [Cook's distance](/cooks-distance/) is $7.89$, while no other point reaches $0.45$. A small residual at a far point says little about the fit there. The tools that show this come from the one fit, without refitting.

The second example is a standard error. For $x = (1, 2, 3, 4, 10)$ and $y = (1, 3, 2, 5, 14)$, the usual formula $s^2(X^\top X)^{-1}$ gives the slope a standard error of $0.151$. The [heteroscedasticity-consistent](/heteroscedasticity-consistent-standard-errors/) estimates give $0.060$ for HC0 and $0.368$ for HC3; HC3 divides each squared residual by $(1 - h_{ii})^2$ and HC0 does not, and the point at $x = 10$ has leverage $0.92$. All three are estimates of one number, and with five points they disagree by a factor of six. Regression output prints a standard error for every coefficient. Whether it means anything depends on assumptions about the errors that the output does not check.

The chapter keeps three kinds of statement apart. Some results are exact in finite samples: under normal errors of equal variance, the [F-test](/regression-f-test/) has size exactly $\alpha$, and the external studentized residual has the $t_{n-p-1}$ law. Some hold only in the limit: the sandwich standard errors, the chi-squared law of the [Breusch-Pagan test](/breusch-pagan-test/) and the cluster-robust intervals, which need many clusters. Some numbers are descriptive and have no null law at all: $R^2$, the [variance inflation factor](/variance-inflation-factor/) and Cook's distance. On top of all three, a model chosen by looking at the data no longer has its usual tests, whichever kind they are.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Fit and the F-test.** The [coefficient of determination](/coefficient-of-determination/), the F-test for nested linear models, the variance inflation factor and the [deviance](/deviance-statistic/) of a generalized linear model.
2. **Choosing among models.** The [Akaike](/akaike-information-criterion/) and [Bayesian](/bayesian-information-criterion/) information criteria, [stepwise regression](/stepwise-regression/), which is greedy, and [leaps and bounds](/leaps-and-bounds/), which finds the best subset of each size exactly.
3. **Points that pull the fit.** The [hat matrix](/hat-matrix/), leverage, studentized residuals and Cook's distance.
4. **Checking the error assumptions.** The [Q-Q plot](/q-q-plot/), the [Gauss-Markov theorem](/gauss-markov-theorem/) and the assumptions it needs, the [Box-Cox transformation](/box-cox-transformation/) and the [Durbin-Watson test](/durbin-watson-test/) for correlated residuals.
5. **Unequal variances.** [Heteroscedasticity](/heteroscedasticity/), the Breusch-Pagan test, sandwich standard errors and [weighted least squares](/weighted-least-squares/).
6. **Correlated errors and random effects.** [Generalized least squares](/generalized-least-squares/), the [linear mixed model](/linear-mixed-model/), [cluster-robust standard errors](/cluster-robust-standard-errors/), [Henderson's mixed model equations](/henderson-mixed-model-equations/) and [restricted maximum likelihood](/restricted-maximum-likelihood/).
7. **Beyond least squares.** [Iteratively reweighted least squares](/iteratively-reweighted-least-squares/), [quasi-likelihood](/quasi-likelihood/), [generalized estimating equations](/generalized-estimating-equations/) and the [errors-in-variables model](/errors-in-variables-regression/).
8. **Inference after selection.** [Post-selection inference](/post-selection-inference/) and the [polyhedral lemma](/polyhedral-lemma/).

## A first reading

Read the coefficient of determination and the F-test first, then leverage, studentized residuals and Cook's distance. Read the Q-Q plot and the Gauss-Markov theorem to see which assumptions the exact tests need. Then read heteroscedasticity, the heteroscedasticity-consistent standard errors and weighted least squares, and after them generalized least squares and the linear mixed model. Finish with the Akaike information criterion, stepwise regression and post-selection inference, in that order, since the last one explains what is wrong with the tests that stepwise regression prints. That is fifteen entries.

On a first pass you can skip the hat matrix beyond its definition, leaps and bounds, Henderson's equations, REML, quasi-likelihood, generalized estimating equations, the errors-in-variables model and the polyhedral lemma. Come back to the deviance and iteratively reweighted least squares after the entry on generalized linear models.
