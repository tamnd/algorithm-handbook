## History

The linear models came first. Yule fitted a second-order autoregression to Wolfer's sunspot numbers in 1927, and Slutsky showed in the same year that moving sums of random shocks produce irregular cycles. Walker wrote the general equations between coefficients and autocorrelations in 1931. Wold proved his decomposition in his 1938 thesis, and Kolmogorov gave the formula for the prediction error in terms of the spectrum in 1941. Whittle studied the mixed ARMA model in his 1951 thesis and gave his frequency-domain likelihood in 1953. Levinson's recursion of 1947 and Durbin's version of 1960 made the fits fast. Box and Jenkins's book of 1970 turned all this into a method of identification, fitting and checking, with the portmanteau test of Box and Pierce (1970), improved by Ljung and Box in 1978. Holt's smoothing of 1957 and Winters's seasonal version of 1960 came from forecasting practice, and STL from Cleveland and coauthors in 1990.

Econometrics added the questions about trends and several series. Granger defined his notion of causality in 1969, and Sims proposed small unrestricted VARs in 1980. Dickey and Fuller derived the law of the unit-root statistic in 1979, and Said and Dickey added the lagged differences in 1984. Granger and Newbold showed in 1974 that regressions between independent random walks look significant. Granger introduced cointegration in 1981, Engle and Granger gave the residual test and the error correction form in 1987, and Johansen gave the likelihood analysis of cointegrated VARs in 1988 and 1991. Kwiatkowski, Phillips, Schmidt and Shin reversed the null in 1992. Engle introduced ARCH in 1982 and Bollerslev generalized it to GARCH in 1986.

Sequential analysis began with Wald, who developed the SPRT in 1943 and published it in 1945; Wald and Wolfowitz proved its optimality in 1948. Page proposed the CUSUM in 1954, Lorden proved its asymptotic optimality in 1971 and Moustakides its exact optimality in 1986. In clinical trials, Armitage, McPherson and Rowe computed the error rates of repeated significance tests in 1969. Pocock gave constant group sequential boundaries in 1977, O'Brien and Fleming gave boundaries that are strict early in 1979, and Lan and DeMets introduced alpha spending functions in 1983.

Offline change points grew from Scott and Knott's recursive splitting of 1974 and Vostrikova's binary segmentation of 1981. Auger and Lawrence and then Jackson and coauthors gave exact dynamic programs, and Killick, Fearnhead and Eckley added pruning in PELT in 2012. Adams and MacKay published the online run-length recursion in 2007, and Fearnhead and Liu gave an equivalent filter, with resampling to bound its cost, in the same year.

Ville proved his inequality in his 1939 thesis. Darling and Robbins defined confidence sequences in 1967, and Robbins built tests of power one by mixing likelihood ratios in 1970. The modern theory of test martingales and anytime-valid inference was set out by Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk in 2011, by Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon in 2020 and 2021, and by Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk and Shafer in 2023, while Johari, Koomen, Pekelis and Walsh brought the mixture SPRT to online experiments in 2017.

## Further reading

- Peter J. Brockwell and Richard A. Davis, Time Series: Theory and Methods, second edition, Springer, 1991. The rigorous treatment of stationary processes, ARMA models, prediction and estimation.
- George E. P. Box, Gwilym M. Jenkins, Gregory C. Reinsel and Greta M. Ljung, Time Series Analysis: Forecasting and Control, fifth edition, Wiley, 2015. The model-building method, with seasonal models and many worked series.
- James D. Hamilton, Time Series Analysis, Princeton University Press, 1994. Unit roots, VARs, cointegration and GARCH from the econometric side.
- Christopher Jennison and Bruce W. Turnbull, Group Sequential Methods with Applications to Clinical Trials, Chapman and Hall/CRC, 2000. Boundaries, spending functions and their computation.
- Alexander Tartakovsky, Igor Nikiforov and Michèle Basseville, Sequential Analysis, Hypothesis Testing and Changepoint Detection, CRC Press, 2014. The SPRT, CUSUM and their optimality theory.
- Aaditya Ramdas, Peter Grünwald, Vladimir Vovk and Glenn Shafer, Game-theoretic statistics and safe anytime-valid inference, Statistical Science 38(4), 2023. A survey of test martingales, e-values and confidence sequences.
