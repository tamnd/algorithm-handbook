Seven mice given a new drug lived 94, 197, 16, 38, 99, 141 and 23 days, and nine controls lived 52, 104, 146, 10, 51, 30, 40, 27 and 46 days. The treated mean is larger by 30.63 days. A t test would need normal data, and these times are skewed. The [permutation test](/permutation-test/) needs no such model: if the drug does nothing, each of the $\binom{16}{7} = 11440$ ways to split the times into groups of 7 and 9 is equally likely, and 1613 of them give a difference at least as large. So the exact one-sided p-value is $1613/11440 = 0.1410$. The [bootstrap](/bootstrap-method/) answers a second question, how precise the treated mean is, by recomputing it on samples drawn with replacement from the seven times: its ideal bootstrap standard error is 23.36 days. The same recipe gives 37.83 days for the [median](/median/), a statistic with no simple formula. And the median does not move at all if the 197 is mistyped as 1970.

These three ideas carry the chapter. The first is to use the sample in place of the unknown distribution. The [empirical distribution function](/empirical-distribution-function/) puts mass $1/n$ on each point, and for independent data two theorems say how close it is to the truth: uniformly in the limit, and within $\varepsilon$ everywhere with probability at least $1 - 2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}$ for every $n$. The bootstrap, the goodness-of-fit tests and the kernel smoothers are all built on it. The bootstrap's guarantees are asymptotic and need conditions. It fails for the maximum, for heavy tails and at the boundary of a parameter space, and the chapter shows the repairs.

The second idea is to count instead of assume. Under the null hypothesis some rearrangements of the data are equally likely, so the null law of a statistic is a finite list of values. The [sign test](/sign-test/), the rank tests and the permutation test have exact level at every sample size when the data are exchangeable under the null. Their chi-square and normal approximations are only asymptotic, and the entries give both.

The third idea is to survive bad data. The mean is carried away by one wild value; the median needs half the sample. The [breakdown point](/breakdown-point/) and the [influence function](/influence-function/) make this precise, and the chapter gives estimators of location, scale, regression lines and multivariate centres that tolerate a stated fraction of outliers, with what they pay in efficiency and in computing time. Many of the fast algorithms are selection problems in disguise: a median of $\binom n2$ slopes, or of $n(n+1)/2$ pairwise averages, found without listing them.

The chapter ends with smoothing and with [conformal prediction](/conformal-prediction/), which turns any predictor into prediction sets whose coverage holds in finite samples under exchangeability, marginally and not for each point separately.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **The empirical distribution.** The empirical distribution function, the [Glivenko-Cantelli theorem](/glivenko-cantelli-theorem/), the [DKW inequality](/dkw-inequality/), and the [Kolmogorov-Smirnov](/kolmogorov-smirnov-test/) and [Anderson-Darling](/anderson-darling-test/) tests.
2. **Resampling.** The bootstrap, the [jackknife](/jackknife-resampling/), the [parametric bootstrap](/parametric-bootstrap/), the [percentile](/bootstrap-percentile-interval/), [bootstrap-t](/bootstrap-t-interval/) and [BCa](/bca-bootstrap-interval/) intervals, the [block bootstrap](/block-bootstrap/) for time series and [subsampling](/subsampling-inference/).
3. **Rank and permutation tests.** The sign test, the permutation test, the [Wilcoxon signed-rank](/wilcoxon-signed-rank-test/), [Mann-Whitney](/mann-whitney-u-test/), [Kruskal-Wallis](/kruskal-wallis-test/) and [Friedman](/friedman-test/) tests, [Spearman's rank correlation](/spearman-rank-correlation/) and [U-statistics](/u-statistic/).
4. **Measuring robustness.** The [Huber contamination model](/huber-contamination-model/), the breakdown point and the influence function.
5. **Robust location and scale.** The [median absolute deviation](/median-absolute-deviation/), the [trimmed mean](/trimmed-mean/), the [Huber M-estimator](/huber-m-estimator/), [Tukey's biweight](/tukey-biweight/) and the [Hodges-Lehmann estimator](/hodges-lehmann-estimator/).
6. **Robust regression.** The [Theil-Sen estimator](/theil-sen-estimator/), [repeated median regression](/repeated-median-regression/), [least median of squares](/least-median-of-squares/), [least trimmed squares](/least-trimmed-squares/) and the [MM-estimator](/mm-estimator/).
7. **Robust multivariate estimation.** The [minimum covariance determinant](/minimum-covariance-determinant/), [halfspace depth](/halfspace-depth/), the [Tukey median](/tukey-median/) and the [filter](/robust-mean-estimation-filter/) for a mean in high dimension.
8. **Smoothing.** [Kernel density estimation](/kernel-density-estimation/), [bandwidth selection](/kde-bandwidth-selection/), the [Nadaraya-Watson estimator](/nadaraya-watson-estimator/) and [local polynomial regression](/local-polynomial-regression/).
9. **Conformal prediction.** The full method and [split conformal prediction](/split-conformal-prediction/).

## A first reading

Take these fourteen entries in order: the empirical distribution function, the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem, the bootstrap, the percentile interval, the sign test, the permutation test, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, the Mann-Whitney test, the breakdown point, the median absolute deviation, the Huber M-estimator, the Theil-Sen estimator, kernel density estimation and split conformal prediction. They give the basic object, the resampling recipe and its simplest interval, the exact tests, the measure of robustness with three robust estimators, one smoother and one prediction method with a finite-sample guarantee.

Leave for later the second-order intervals, the block bootstrap and subsampling, U-statistics, the high-breakdown regressions after Theil-Sen, and the multivariate group. The Tukey median and the filter are research entries and assume the rest.
