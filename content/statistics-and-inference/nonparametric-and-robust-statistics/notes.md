## History

Rank methods are the oldest part. Spearman defined his rank correlation in 1904. Fisher described the permutation test in his book on the design of experiments in 1935, and Pitman worked out its theory in 1937. Friedman gave his test for blocks in 1937, and Wilcoxon the signed-rank and rank-sum tests in 1945. Mann and Whitney allowed unequal sample sizes with the pair count $U$ in 1947, Hoeffding introduced U-statistics in 1948, and Kruskal and Wallis extended the rank-sum test to several groups in 1952. Hodges and Lehmann showed in 1956 that the signed-rank test loses at most 13.6 percent efficiency against the t test for symmetric laws, and in 1963 they turned rank tests into estimators.

The empirical distribution function came into theory in 1933, when Glivenko proved uniform convergence for continuous laws, Cantelli for all laws, and Kolmogorov found the limit law of the largest distance. Smirnov published tables in 1948, and Anderson and Darling gave their weighted statistic in 1952 and 1954. Dvoretzky, Kiefer and Wolfowitz proved the exponential bound in 1956 with an unspecified constant, and Massart found the constant 2 in 1990. Lilliefors showed in 1967 how to test normality when the parameters are estimated.

Resampling began with Quenouille's jackknife for bias, in 1949 and 1956, and Tukey's use of it for variance in 1958. Efron introduced the bootstrap in 1979, as another look at the jackknife, and the BCa interval in 1987. Hall used Edgeworth expansions in 1988 to show which intervals are second-order accurate. For dependent data, Carlstein proposed nonoverlapping blocks in 1986, and Künsch, and Liu and Singh, gave the moving block bootstrap in 1989 and 1992; Politis and Romano gave the stationary bootstrap and subsampling in 1994. Athreya showed in 1987 that the bootstrap of the mean fails for infinite variance.

Robust statistics as a theory starts with Huber's paper of 1964, which defined the contamination neighbourhood and found the minimax M-estimator. Hampel defined the breakdown point in 1971 and the influence function in 1974, and Tukey proposed halfspace depth in the same years. Theil's slope estimator dates from 1950 and Sen's analysis from 1968. Siegel's repeated median of 1982 was the first regression estimator with breakdown point one half, and Rousseeuw's least median of squares of 1984 the first affine equivariant one; the minimum covariance determinant comes from the same work. Yohai's MM-estimator of 1987 joined high breakdown with high efficiency. Fast algorithms followed: FAST-MCD in 1999, FAST-LTS in 2006, and in 2016 the filter of Diakonikolas, Kamath, Kane, Li, Moitra and Stewart, with the related method of Lai, Rao and Vempala, which made robust estimation of a high-dimensional mean computable.

Rosenblatt defined the kernel density estimate in 1956 and Parzen studied it in 1962. Nadaraya and Watson gave the kernel regression estimate independently in 1964, and Cleveland's LOWESS of 1979 made local polynomial fits with robust reweighting common. Conformal prediction comes from the work of Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer, collected in their book of 2005; the split version appeared in 2002, and Lei and coauthors analysed it for regression in 2018.

## Further reading

- Bradley Efron and Robert J. Tibshirani, An Introduction to the Bootstrap, Chapman and Hall, 1993. The bootstrap, the jackknife and the intervals, with the examples used in this chapter.
- E. L. Lehmann, Nonparametrics, Statistical Methods Based on Ranks, revised edition, Springer, 2006. The rank tests, their exact laws and their efficiency.
- Peter J. Huber and Elvezio M. Ronchetti, Robust Statistics, second edition, Wiley, 2009. The contamination model, M-estimators and the minimax theory.
- Peter J. Rousseeuw and Annick M. Leroy, Robust Regression and Outlier Detection, Wiley, 1987. Least median of squares, least trimmed squares and high-breakdown regression in practice.
- B. W. Silverman, Density Estimation for Statistics and Data Analysis, Chapman and Hall, 1986. Kernel density estimates and the choice of bandwidth.
- Vladimir Vovk, Alex Gammerman and Glenn Shafer, Algorithmic Learning in a Random World, Springer, 2005. Conformal prediction and its guarantees under exchangeability.
