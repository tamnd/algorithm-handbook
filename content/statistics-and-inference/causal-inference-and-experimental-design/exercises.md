1. ◦ In the science table of [potential outcomes](/potential-outcomes-framework/), units 1 to 6 have $Y(0) = (3, 2, 5, 4, 1, 6)$ and $Y(1) = (5, 6, 5, 7, 4, 6)$. Suppose units 1, 2 and 5 are treated and the others are not. Compute the naive difference of the observed means, the ATT and the selection bias $\mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid T = 1] - \mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid T = 0]$ over the six units, and check that the first is the sum of the other two. Then compute the ATC and check the identity $\tau = p\,\tau_{\text{ATT}} + (1 - p)\,\tau_{\text{ATC}}$ of the [average treatment effect](/average-treatment-effect/).

2. ◦ A [randomized experiment](/randomized-experiment/) treats exactly half of $n$ units, all halves being equally likely. A Fisher randomization test of the sharp null of no effect uses the difference in means. What is the smallest one-sided p-value that any data can give? What is the smallest even $n$ for which a two-sided test at level 0.05 can reject at all?

3. ◦ Two treatments are compared on 200 patients. Treatment A: 18 of 20 mild cases recover and 24 of 80 severe cases. Treatment B: 64 of 80 mild cases and 4 of 20 severe cases. Show that A has the higher recovery rate in each severity class and the lower rate overall. Then compute each treatment's rate standardized to the pooled mix of severity (half mild, half severe). Severity affects both the choice of treatment and recovery, and treatment does not change severity. Which comparison should guide the choice for a new patient? (This is [Simpson's paradox](/simpsons-paradox/).)

4. ◦ Run [one-way ANOVA](/one-way-anova/) on the groups $(1, 2, 3)$, $(3, 4, 5)$ and $(5, 6, 7)$. Give the two sums of squares, the F statistic and its degrees of freedom. For the $F_{2, \nu}$ law the upper tail is $\Pr[F > f] = (1 + 2f/\nu)^{-\nu/2}$; use it to find the exact p-value.

5. ◦ How many degrees of freedom does the error term have in a [randomized complete block design](/randomized-block-design/) with $t$ treatments and $b$ blocks, and in a single $t \times t$ [Latin square](/latin-square-design/)? Why is one $3 \times 3$ Latin square a weak experiment? Show that no two Latin squares of order 2 are orthogonal.

6. ◦ A $2^3$ [factorial design](/factorial-design/) gives the responses $(1) = 10$, $a = 14$, $b = 12$, $ab = 18$, $c = 11$, $ac = 15$, $bc = 13$, $abc = 21$, in the usual notation where a letter means that factor is at its high level. Estimate the three main effects and the four interactions.

7. ◦ A $2^{5-2}$ [fractional factorial design](/fractional-factorial-design/) uses the generators $D = AB$ and $E = AC$. Write the defining relation, give the resolution, and list the aliases of $A$ and of $BC$.

8. ◦ Four units have treatment $T$, known propensity $e$ and outcome $Y$: $(1, \tfrac12, 4)$, $(0, \tfrac12, 2)$, $(1, \tfrac14, 8)$, $(0, \tfrac34, 3)$. Compute the naive difference in means, and the Horvitz-Thompson and Hájek forms of the [inverse probability weighting](/inverse-probability-weighting/) estimate of the ATE. What is the sum of the weights in each arm, and why does the Hájek form divide by it?

9. ◦ In an online experiment the outcome has correlation 0.7 with the same metric measured before the experiment. By what factor does [CUPED](/cuped/) with the best $\theta$ cut the variance of the difference in means, if the same $\theta$ is used in both arms? About how many fewer users does the experiment then need for the same standard error?

10. ◦ Show that for $k = 2$ groups the multiplier $\sqrt{(k - 1)F_{k-1, \nu}(\alpha)}$ of [Scheffé's method](/scheffe-method/) equals the two-sided $t$ critical value $t_\nu(\alpha/2)$, so that Scheffé's interval for the one contrast is the ordinary [t-test](/student-t-test/) interval.

11. • Four units have $Y(0) = (1, 2, 3, 6)$ and $Y(1) = (2, 4, 3, 9)$, and two of them are treated at random. List the difference in means for each of the six assignments and confirm that their average is the ATE. Compute the exact variance of the estimator, and compare it with $S_1^2/n_1 + S_0^2/n_0$, the expectation of Neyman's variance estimate. Explain the gap by the term $S_\tau^2/n$.

12. • A covariate $X$ takes the value 0 with probability $3/5$ and 1 with probability $2/5$. The propensities are $e(0) = 1/5$ and $e(1) = 7/10$. The mean potential outcomes are $\mathbb{E}[Y(1) \mid X = 0] = 1/2$, $\mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid X = 0] = 3/10$, $\mathbb{E}[Y(1) \mid X = 1] = 9/10$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid X = 1] = 3/5$. Assume [ignorability](/ignorability/) given $X$. Compute the raw difference $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid T = 1] - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid T = 0]$, the ATE, the ATT and the ATC. Check that the population value of the weighted estimator $\mathbb{E}[TY/e(X)] - \mathbb{E}[(1 - T)Y/(1 - e(X))]$ is the ATE.

13. • $X$ is uniform on $\{1, 2, 3\}$ with $e(1) = e(2) = 3/10$ and $e(3) = 3/5$. Show that $b(X) = [X = 3]$ is a balancing score, in the sense of the [propensity score](/propensity-score/) entry, and that $b'(X) = [X = 1]$ is not, by computing $\Pr[X = 2 \mid b' = 0, T = t]$ for $t = 0, 1$. State the general rule that separates the two.

14. • Two treated units have propensity scores 0.40 and 0.50, and two controls have 0.45 and 0.90. Match each treated unit to a different control. Find the matching that greedy [matching](/propensity-score-matching/) gives when it takes the treated units in decreasing order of score and gives each the nearest free control, and find the optimal matching. Then prove that when the cost is $\lvert e_t - e_c \rvert$, some optimal matching never crosses: if $t_1 < t_2$, then $t_1$ gets the smaller control.

15. • In a [difference in differences](/difference-in-differences/) study the treated group's mean goes from 10 to 15 and the control group's from 8 to 11. Compute the estimate if parallel trends holds for the outcome in levels. Then suppose it holds for the logarithm of the outcome. What is the counterfactual mean of the treated group in the second period, and what is the effect in levels? Can parallel trends hold on both scales here?

16. • A trial has one-sided noncompliance: nobody assigned to control can get the treatment. Among those assigned to treatment, 60% take it, with mean outcome 14, and the rest have mean outcome 9. Those assigned to control have mean outcome 10. Compute the Wald ratio of [instrumental variables](/instrumental-variables-estimation/). Then use the exclusion restriction to find the mean of $Y(0)$ and of $Y(1)$ among compliers, and check that their difference is the [local average treatment effect](/local-average-treatment-effect/). Why does monotonicity need no argument here?

17. • A Latin square design should be chosen at random from all Latin squares of the right order. A common shortcut takes one fixed square and permutes its rows, its columns and its symbols at random. Call a $2 \times 2$ subsquare an *intercalate* if it is itself a Latin square on two symbols. Count the intercalates of the cyclic square of order 4 (entry $i + j \bmod 4$) and of the addition table of $\{0, 1\}^2$ (entry $i \oplus j$). Show that the number of intercalates does not change under the three kinds of permutation, and conclude that the shortcut started from the cyclic square never produces the second square. There are 576 Latin squares of order 4; how many of them can the shortcut reach, and how many can it reach for order 3, where there are 12?

18. • Compare two $2^{6-2}$ designs: $d_1$ with $E = ABC$ and $F = BCD$, and $d_2$ with $E = AB$ and $F = ACD$. For each one write the three words of the defining relation other than $I$, the resolution and the counts of words of length 3, 4 and 5. Which main effects of $d_2$ are aliased with two-factor interactions? Which design would you run, and why?

19. • The 12-run [Plackett-Burman design](/plackett-burman-design/) has first row $+ + - + + + - - - + -$, ten more rows that are its cyclic shifts, and a last row of 11 minus signs. Check that its columns are orthogonal and balanced. Compute $\frac{1}{12}\sum_r x_{r1}x_{r2}x_{r3}$ for the first three columns, and say what it means for the estimate of the main effect of factor 1 when factors 2 and 3 interact.

20. • A [synthetic control](/synthetic-control-method/) is built for one treated unit from three donors, using two pre-period outcomes. The treated unit has $(3, 5)$. The donors have $A = (2, 4)$, $B = (4, 6)$ and $C = (6, 2)$. Find the weights, nonnegative and summing to 1, that match the treated unit exactly. In the post-period the donors have 5, 9 and 1 and the treated unit has 10; estimate the effect. Then show that no such weights match a treated unit at $(7, 7)$, and say what an unconstrained regression on the donors would do there.

21. • In a population $X$ is 0 or 1 with probability $1/2$ each, $e(0) = 1/4$, $e(1) = 3/4$, $\mathbb{E}[Y(1) \mid X] = 2$ and $6$, and $\mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid X] = 1$ and $2$ for $X = 0$ and $1$. Compute the population value of the [AIPW](/augmented-inverse-probability-weighting/) estimator in four cases: both models right; the propensity model wrong ($\hat e = 1/2$ everywhere) and the outcome models right; the propensity right and both outcome models wrong ($\hat m_0 = \hat m_1 = 0$); and both wrong. Compare with the ATE.

22. • Let the true DAG be $A \to C \leftarrow B$, $C \to D$. List the conditional independences among $A, B, C, D$ that it implies. Run the [PC algorithm](/pc-algorithm/) with a perfect independence oracle, and show that its output has every edge directed. Then do the same for the chain $A \to B \to C$, and list the DAGs in its Markov equivalence class.

23. • The bias of the cross-fitted score of [double machine learning](/double-machine-learning/) is bounded by a constant times $\lVert e - \hat e \rVert\,(\lVert \mu_1 - \hat m_1 \rVert + \lVert \mu_0 - \hat m_0 \rVert)$, under overlap. Suppose the propensity error is of order $n^{-a}$ and the outcome errors of order $n^{-b}$. The standard error is of order $n^{-1/2}$. For which pairs $(a, b)$ is the bias negligible next to it? Decide the cases $a = b = 1/4$, $a = b = 0.3$, $a = 1/2$ with $b = 0.01$, and $a = 0.2$ with $b = 0.25$. What is gained by a condition on the product compared with a condition on each error separately?

24. ★ Prove that $S_\tau^2 \ge (S_1 - S_0)^2$, where $S_1$, $S_0$ and $S_\tau$ are the finite-population standard deviations of $Y(1)$, $Y(0)$ and the unit effects. Deduce an upper bound on the variance of the difference in means that is never larger than Neyman's. Evaluate it for the table of Exercise 11, and for the six-unit table of the randomized experiment entry, where $S_1^2 = 11/10$, $S_0^2 = 7/2$, three of six units are treated and the true variance is $16/15$. When is the bound exact?

25. ★ In a sharp [regression discontinuity design](/regression-discontinuity-design/) with cutoff 0, suppose the running variable is spread uniformly near the cutoff and the regression functions are $\mu_0(x) = a_0 + b_0x + \kappa_0x^2$ on the left and $\mu_1(x) = a_1 + b_1x + \kappa_1x^2$ on the right. Show that a straight line fitted by least squares, with equal weights, to $x^2$ on $[0, h]$ has intercept $-h^2/6$, and deduce the bias of the local linear estimate of the jump with a uniform kernel. Evaluate it for $a_1 - a_0 = 2$, $\kappa_0 = 1$, $\kappa_1 = 3$ and $h = 1$. Why does the bias vanish when the two curvatures are equal? Compare your term with the entry's leading bias $-(h^2/20)(\mu_+'' - \mu_-'')$ for the triangular kernel.

26. ★ The model is $\mathbb{E}[y] = \beta_1x + \beta_2x^2$ for $x \in [0, 1]$, with no intercept. Among designs with equal weight on two points $a < b$, find the one that maximizes $\det M$. Then use the [Kiefer-Wolfowitz theorem](/kiefer-wolfowitz-equivalence-theorem/) to prove that this design is D-optimal among all approximate designs: compute $d(x) = f(x)^\top M^{-1}f(x)$ with $f(x) = (x, x^2)$ and show that $d(x) \le 2$ on $[0, 1]$, with equality at the support points.

27. ★ An unmeasured $U$ is 0 or 1 with probability $1/2$ each. Treatment $X$ is 1 with probability $1/5$ if $U = 0$ and $4/5$ if $U = 1$. A mediator $M$ is 1 with probability $1/10$ if $X = 0$ and $7/10$ if $X = 1$, whatever $U$. The outcome $Y$ is 1 with probability $1/10$, $1/2$, $3/10$, $9/10$ for $(M, U) = (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)$. Compute the causal effect $\Pr[Y = 1 \mid \mathrm{do}(X = 1)] - \Pr[Y = 1 \mid \mathrm{do}(X = 0)]$ from the model and the naive difference $\Pr[Y = 1 \mid X = 1] - \Pr[Y = 1 \mid X = 0]$. Then compute the [front-door](/front-door-criterion/) formula from the joint law of $(X, M, Y)$ alone and check that it gives the causal effect.

28. ★ Let $X$ and $E$ be independent with mean 0, variance 1 and excess kurtoses $\kappa_X = \mathbb{E}[X^4] - 3$ and $\kappa_E$, and let $Y = bX + E$ with $b \ne 0$. Regress $X$ on $Y$ and let $r$ be the residual. Show that $\operatorname{Cov}(r, Y) = 0$ and that $\operatorname{Cov}(r^2, Y^2) = b^2(\kappa_X + \kappa_E)/(1 + b^2)^2$. What does this give for normal $X$ and $E$, and for $X$ and $E$ uniform on $\{-1, 1\}$ with $b = 1$? Give a pair of non-normal laws for which this covariance is 0, and say why [LiNGAM](/lingam/) uses a test of full independence and not this one moment.

29. ★ For a [balanced incomplete block design](/balanced-incomplete-block-design/) with parameters $(v, k, \lambda)$, use $bk = vr$ and $\lambda(v - 1) = r(k - 1)$ to find $r$ and $b$ for $(10, 4, 1)$, $(16, 6, 1)$, $(6, 3, 2)$, $(21, 5, 1)$ and $(22, 7, 2)$. Decide for each whether a design exists, using integrality, Fisher's inequality $b \ge v$, an explicit construction, or the Bruck-Ryser-Chowla theorem: a symmetric design ($b = v$) with $v$ even needs $k - \lambda$ to be a perfect square. For $(21, 5, 1)$, try the translates of a 5-element set modulo 21 whose differences cover each nonzero residue once.
