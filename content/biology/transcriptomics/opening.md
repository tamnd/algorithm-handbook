A genome is nearly the same in every cell of a body. What differs between a liver cell and a neuron, or between a tumor and the tissue around it, is which genes are being read, and how much. Transcriptomics measures that. The cell copies a gene into RNA, cuts the introns out, and the mature RNA carries the message. [RNA sequencing](/rna-sequencing/) breaks the RNA of a sample into fragments, reads a short stretch of each, and maps the reads back to the genome or to a list of known transcripts. The count of reads on a gene is the raw measurement of its expression.

Turning counts into biology takes a chain of algorithms, and each link has its own kind of guarantee. Some steps are exact computations on a fixed input: the number of paths through a splice graph, a quantile normalization, a maximum matching. Some are maximum likelihood under a model, such as the [EM algorithm](/em-isoform-quantification/) that shares reads between isoforms. Its log-likelihood is concave, so every fixed point with all abundances positive is a global maximum, though the maximum need not be unique. Some are statistical tests, such as the negative binomial tests of DESeq2 and edgeR and the moderated t-test of limma, whose p-values are right only as far as their distributional assumptions hold. Some are heuristics with no promise of the best answer, such as greedy transcript assembly. The examples are concrete: which genes change after a drug, which isoform a tissue prefers, which cell types a sample of blood holds, and in what order cells pass through a stage of development.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Counts and units.** [RPKM and FPKM](/rpkm-and-fpkm/) scale by length and depth; [TPM](/transcripts-per-million/) scales so that each sample sums to one million. Between samples, [library size normalization](/library-size-normalization/) by [median of ratios](/median-of-ratios-normalization/), [TMM](/tmm-normalization/) or [quantile normalization](/quantile-normalization/).
2. **Alignment and quantification.** [Spliced alignment](/spliced-read-alignment/) and STAR's [maximal mappable prefixes](/maximal-mappable-prefix-alignment/). [Transcript abundance estimation](/transcript-abundance-estimation/) by EM, fed by [pseudoalignment](/pseudoalignment/) or [selective alignment](/selective-alignment/).
3. **Assembling transcripts.** The [splice graph](/splice-graph/), [transcript assembly](/transcript-assembly/) as a [minimum path cover](/minimum-path-cover-transcript-assembly/) or a [flow decomposition](/flow-based-transcript-assembly/), and [de novo assembly](/de-novo-transcriptome-assembly/) without a genome.
4. **Count models and differential expression.** The [negative binomial model](/negative-binomial-count-model/), [dispersion shrinkage](/dispersion-shrinkage/), [differential expression analysis](/differential-expression-analysis/) and the [moderated t-statistic](/moderated-t-statistic/).
5. **Gene sets and coexpression.** [Over-representation analysis](/over-representation-analysis/), [GSEA](/gene-set-enrichment-analysis/) and [WGCNA](/weighted-gene-coexpression-network-analysis/).
6. **Single cells.** [Single-cell RNA sequencing](/single-cell-rna-sequencing/), [UMI deduplication](/umi-deduplication/), [doublet detection](/doublet-detection/), [pooled size factors](/pooling-size-factors/), [highly variable genes](/highly-variable-gene-selection/) and [shared nearest neighbor clustering](/shared-nearest-neighbor-clustering/).
7. **Batch effects.** [Batch effect correction](/batch-effect-correction/) by [ComBat](/combat/) and by [mutual nearest neighbors](/mnn-batch-correction/).
8. **Trajectories.** [Trajectory inference](/trajectory-inference/) by [MST pseudotime](/mst-pseudotime/) or [diffusion pseudotime](/diffusion-pseudotime/), and [RNA velocity](/rna-velocity/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: RNA sequencing, TPM, median of ratios, spliced read alignment, EM isoform quantification, pseudoalignment, splice graph, the negative binomial count model, differential expression analysis, over-representation analysis, single-cell RNA sequencing and shared nearest neighbor clustering. They give the bulk pipeline from reads to a list of changed genes and the main single-cell pipeline. Skip at first the assembly entries after the splice graph, the alignment internals, and the batch and trajectory groups. Read UMI deduplication next if you work with droplet data.

## Before you start

The alignment entries rest on [read mapping](/read-mapping/) from the chapter on sequence alignment, on [k-mers](/kmer/) from the chapter on genome assembly, and on the [suffix array](/suffix-array/) from Volume 8. The splice graph is a [directed acyclic graph](/directed-acyclic-graph/), and the assembly entries use [bipartite matching](/bipartite-matching/), [maximum flow](/max-flow-problem/) and [flow decomposition](/flow-decomposition/) from Volume 7. The statistics come from Volume 33: [Fisher's exact test](/fisher-exact-test/), the [Benjamini-Hochberg procedure](/benjamini-hochberg-procedure/), [empirical Bayes](/empirical-bayes/) and the [permutation test](/permutation-test/). From Volume 25 you need [maximum likelihood estimation](/maximum-likelihood-estimation/), [expectation maximization](/expectation-maximization/), [generalized linear models](/generalized-linear-model/) and [principal component analysis](/principal-component-analysis/). The single-cell entries cluster with the [Louvain method](/louvain-method/) from Volume 7. The [Poisson distribution](/poisson-distribution/) is in Volume 1. No biology is assumed beyond the idea that a gene is a stretch of DNA copied into RNA, and that one gene can give several transcripts by joining different exons.
