## History

Expression was first measured at scale with microarrays, and several methods of the chapter were made for them. Bolstad, Irizarry, Astrand and Speed compared normalizations for oligonucleotide arrays in 2003 and recommended quantile normalization. Mootha and colleagues introduced gene set enrichment analysis in 2003 with an unweighted Kolmogorov-Smirnov walk, and Subramanian and colleagues gave the weighted score and the software in 2005. Smyth defined the moderated t-statistic of limma in 2004, Zhang and Horvath described weighted coexpression networks in 2005, and Johnson, Li and Rabinovic proposed ComBat for array batches in 2007.

RNA sequencing replaced arrays from 2008, when Mortazavi, Williams, McCue, Schaeffer and Wold mapped mouse transcriptomes and reported expression in RPKM. TopHat (Trapnell, Pachter and Salzberg 2009) aligned spliced reads, and Cufflinks (Trapnell and colleagues 2010) assembled the fragments of each locus into the fewest transcripts by a constructive proof of Dilworth's theorem. Li, Ruotti, Stewart, Thomson and Dewey gave the EM for reads that map to several isoforms in 2010, and RSEM (Li and Dewey 2011) added TPM. STAR (Dobin and colleagues 2013) found junctions by maximal mappable prefixes in a suffix array, and StringTie (Pertea and colleagues 2015) assembled by heaviest paths and maximum flows.

The count models came from the same years. Robinson and Smyth modelled tag counts as negative binomial with a moderated dispersion in 2007. In 2010 Anders and Huber defined the median of ratios size factors of DESeq, and Robinson and Oshlack described the composition effect and TMM. DESeq2 (Love, Huber and Anders 2014) added shrinkage of fold changes. Alignment-free quantification followed: kallisto (Bray, Pimentel, Melsted and Pachter 2016) replaced alignment by pseudoalignment, and Salmon later moved to selective alignment with decoys (Srivastava and colleagues 2020).

Single-cell sequencing began with one cell (Tang and colleagues 2009) and became routine with droplets (Macosko and colleagues 2015). Unique molecular identifiers were introduced by Kivioja and colleagues in 2012 and Islam and colleagues in 2014, and UMI-tools (Smith, Heger and Sudbery 2017) added the directional network. Monocle ordered cells on a minimum spanning tree in 2014, PhenoGraph clustered Jaccard-weighted neighbor graphs in 2015, and diffusion pseudotime appeared in 2016. Haghverdi, Lun, Morgan and Marioni matched mutual nearest neighbors across batches in 2018, the year La Manno and colleagues introduced RNA velocity.

## Further reading

- Ana Conesa, Pedro Madrigal, Sonia Tarazona and others, *A survey of best practices for RNA-seq data analysis*, Genome Biology 17 (2016). A walk through the whole bulk pipeline and its choices.
- Cole Trapnell, Brian A. Williams, Geo Pertea and others, *Transcript assembly and quantification by RNA-Seq reveals unannotated transcripts and isoform switching during cell differentiation*, Nature Biotechnology 28(5) (2010). The path cover assembly and the FPKM unit.
- Michael I. Love, Wolfgang Huber and Simon Anders, *Moderated estimation of fold change and dispersion for RNA-seq data with DESeq2*, Genome Biology 15(12) (2014). The negative binomial GLM with shrinkage that most bulk studies use.
- Nicolas L. Bray, Harold Pimentel, Páll Melsted and Lior Pachter, *Near-optimal probabilistic RNA-seq quantification*, Nature Biotechnology 34(5) (2016). Pseudoalignment and the EM on equivalence classes.
- Malte D. Luecken and Fabian J. Theis, *Current best practices in single-cell RNA-seq analysis: a tutorial*, Molecular Systems Biology 15(6) (2019). The single-cell workflow step by step.
- Veli Mäkinen, Djamal Belazzougui, Fabio Cunial and Alexandru I. Tomescu, *Genome-Scale Algorithm Design*, Cambridge University Press (2015). The graph algorithms behind transcript assembly, with proofs.
