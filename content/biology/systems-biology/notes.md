## History

The rate laws are old. Guldberg and Waage stated the law of mass action for chemical equilibrium in 1864. Hill fitted the oxygen binding curve of haemoglobin with his sigmoid in 1910. Michaelis and Menten derived their enzyme rate law in 1913 from a fast binding equilibrium, and Briggs and Haldane derived it in 1925 from a steady state of the complex. Delbrück wrote a master equation for an autocatalytic reaction in 1940, and McQuarrie reviewed the stochastic approach to chemical kinetics in 1967.

The theory of whole networks came in 1972. Horn and Jackson set up the network of complexes and the general mass action system, Feinberg related complex balancing to the structure of the network, and Kurtz showed that the ODE model is the large-volume limit of the Markov chain model. Feinberg later stated the deficiency zero theorem in its standard form. Kauffman had already proposed random Boolean networks as a model of genetic control in 1969, reading their attractors as cell types.

Gillespie gave the direct method and the first reaction method in 1976 and applied them to coupled reactions in 1977; in 1992 he derived the master equation from the physics of a well-stirred gas. Gibson and Bruck made the next reaction method in 2000. Gillespie derived the chemical Langevin equation in 2000 and proposed tau-leaping in 2001, and Cao, Gillespie and Petzold improved its step rule in 2005 and 2006. Munsky and Khammash gave the finite state projection in 2006. In 2000 two synthetic circuits were built in *Escherichia coli*: the toggle switch of Gardner, Cantor and Collins and the repressilator of Elowitz and Leibler. Shen-Orr, Milo, Mangan and Alon found the feed-forward loop over-represented in the transcription network of *E. coli* in 2002.

On the metabolic side, Schuster and Hilgetag defined elementary flux modes in 1994, and in the same year Varma and Palsson showed that one linear program predicts the growth and by-product secretion of *E. coli*. Segrè, Vitkup and Church proposed MOMA in 2002, Mahadevan, Edwards and Doyle dynamic FBA in 2002, Burgard, Pharkya and Maranas OptKnock in 2003, and Mahadevan and Schilling flux variability analysis in 2003. For inference, Margolin and colleagues published ARACNE in 2006 and Huynh-Thu, Irrthum, Wehenkel and Geurts GENIE3 in 2010; Singh, Xu and Berger presented IsoRank in 2007.

## Further reading

- Uri Alon, *An Introduction to Systems Biology, Design Principles of Biological Circuits*, 2nd edition, CRC Press (2019). Gene circuits, motifs and their dynamics.
- Darren J. Wilkinson, *Stochastic Modelling for Systems Biology*, 3rd edition, CRC Press (2018). The master equation, exact simulation and inference for stochastic kinetic models.
- Daniel T. Gillespie, *Exact stochastic simulation of coupled chemical reactions*, Journal of Physical Chemistry 81(25) (1977). The direct method, with its derivation.
- Martin Feinberg, *Foundations of Chemical Reaction Network Theory*, Springer (2019). Deficiency theory in full.
- Bernhard O. Palsson, *Systems Biology, Constraint-based Reconstruction and Analysis*, Cambridge University Press (2015). Metabolic reconstructions, FBA and its many variants.
- Jeffrey D. Orth, Ines Thiele and Bernhard O. Palsson, *What is flux balance analysis?*, Nature Biotechnology 28(3) (2010). A short first introduction to FBA.
