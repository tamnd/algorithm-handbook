Systems biology studies how the parts of a cell work together: enzymes and metabolites, genes and the proteins that switch them on and off. The parts are joined into networks, and a network is turned into a model that can be computed. Three kinds of model run through the chapter. A reaction network read with [mass action](/mass-action-kinetics/) gives an ODE for the concentrations. The same network read as a continuous-time Markov chain gives the [chemical master equation](/chemical-master-equation/) for the counts of molecules, which matters when a gene has one copy and its mRNA a handful. A metabolic network with unknown rates gives a polytope of steady fluxes, searched by linear programming.

The examples are concrete. A culture of *Escherichia coli* on glucose grows, secretes acetate, and turns to the acetate once the glucose is gone; [dynamic flux balance analysis](/dynamic-flux-balance-analysis/) predicts that time course. Two genes that repress each other make a memory, the [toggle switch](/genetic-toggle-switch/); three in a ring make a clock, the [repressilator](/repressilator/). Cells with identical genes differ because reactions among few molecules are random, and the [Gillespie algorithm](/gillespie-algorithm/) samples that randomness exactly.

Each result holds under stated assumptions. The ODE and master equation models assume a well-mixed volume. The Gillespie algorithm and its variants sample the master equation exactly; [tau-leaping](/tau-leaping/) and the [chemical Langevin equation](/chemical-langevin-equation/) are approximations that improve with larger counts. [Flux balance analysis](/flux-balance-analysis/) is an LP with a unique optimal value but often many optimal flux vectors, and its prediction of growth rests on the assumption that the cell maximizes growth. Network inference from expression data has no general guarantee and is judged on benchmarks.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Reaction networks and their matrices.** The [stoichiometric matrix](/stoichiometric-matrix/), the [chemical reaction network](/chemical-reaction-network/), mass action, and the [deficiency zero theorem](/deficiency-zero-theorem/).
2. **Rate laws and model reduction.** The [Hill equation](/hill-equation/), the [quasi-steady-state approximation](/quasi-steady-state-approximation/) and [Michaelis-Menten kinetics](/michaelis-menten-kinetics/).
3. **Gene circuits.** The [gene regulatory network](/gene-regulatory-network/), the [feed-forward loop](/feed-forward-loop/), the toggle switch, the repressilator, [random Boolean networks](/random-boolean-network/) and [attractor detection](/boolean-network-attractor-detection/).
4. **Stochastic kinetics.** The master equation, the Gillespie algorithm, the [first reaction](/first-reaction-method/) and [next reaction](/next-reaction-method/) methods, tau-leaping, the chemical Langevin equation, the [linear noise approximation](/linear-noise-approximation/), [moment closure](/moment-closure/) and the [finite state projection](/finite-state-projection/).
5. **Constraint-based metabolism.** [Constraint-based modeling](/constraint-based-metabolic-modeling/), flux balance analysis, [flux variability analysis](/flux-variability-analysis/), dynamic FBA, [MOMA](/minimization-of-metabolic-adjustment/), [OptKnock](/optknock/), [gap filling](/metabolic-network-gap-filling/), [elementary flux modes](/elementary-flux-mode/) and [their enumeration](/elementary-flux-mode-enumeration/).
6. **Inferring and comparing networks.** [Network inference](/gene-regulatory-network-inference/), [ARACNE](/aracne/), [GENIE3](/genie3/), [network alignment](/biological-network-alignment/) and [IsoRank](/isorank/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these eleven entries in order: the stoichiometric matrix, mass action kinetics, the Hill equation, Michaelis-Menten kinetics, the gene regulatory network, the toggle switch, the chemical master equation, the Gillespie algorithm, tau-leaping, flux balance analysis and flux variability analysis. They give the three kinds of model and one exact method for each. Skip at first the deficiency theory, the approximations of the master equation after tau-leaping, the mixed-integer designs, and the inference group.

## Before you start

The linear algebra is from Volume 11: [rank](/rank/), the [rank-nullity theorem](/rank-nullity-theorem/) and the [null space](/nullspace/). The ODE models need [initial value problems](/ode-initial-value-problem/) and [stiff problems](/stiff-ode-problem/) from Volume 12, and their stability is read from the [Jacobian](/jacobian-linearization/) and [limit cycles](/limit-cycle/) of Volume 36. The stochastic group needs [Markov chains](/markov-chain/) and [inverse transform sampling](/inverse-transform-sampling/) from Volume 17, the [Poisson distribution](/poisson-distribution/) from Volume 1, and [stochastic differential equations](/stochastic-differential-equation/) from Volume 12. The metabolic group rests on [linear programming](/linear-programming-problem/), [quadratic programming](/quadratic-programming/) and [mixed-integer programming](/mixed-integer-programming/) from Volume 15. The circuits use [Boolean functions](/boolean-function/) and [SAT](/sat/) from Volume 16, and the inference group uses [random forests](/random-forest/) from Volume 25 and [PageRank](/pagerank/) from Volume 7. No chemistry is assumed beyond the idea that a reaction turns some molecules into others at a rate.
