A sequence of nucleotides or amino acids folds into a shape, and the shape decides what the molecule does. A transfer RNA works because its 76 or so bases pair into four helices; an enzyme works because a few residues far apart in the chain meet in its active site. Structure prediction computes that shape from the sequence. For RNA the usual target is the secondary structure, the list of base pairs, and there the main methods are exact dynamic programs over a stated energy model. For proteins the target is the three-dimensional position of every atom, the problem is hard even in toy lattice models, and the methods that work are heuristics and trained models whose accuracy is measured on benchmarks such as CASP.

The chapter keeps these kinds of result apart. Each method is stated with its model: base pairs for the [Nussinov algorithm](/nussinov-algorithm/), nearest-neighbor loop energies for the [Zuker algorithm](/zuker-algorithm/) and the [McCaskill algorithm](/mccaskill-algorithm/), a lattice for the [HP model](/hp-model/), a force field for [molecular dynamics](/molecular-dynamics/), and a scoring function such as [RMSD](/rmsd/), [TM-score](/tm-score/) or lDDT for evaluation.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **RNA secondary structure.** [Secondary structures](/rna-secondary-structure/) and [pseudoknots](/pseudoknot/), the Nussinov algorithm, the [nearest-neighbor model](/nearest-neighbor-model/), the [prediction problem](/rna-secondary-structure-prediction/), and the Zuker and McCaskill algorithms for the minimum free energy and the partition function.
2. **RNA families, design, pseudoknots and kinetics.** [Covariance models](/covariance-model/) and [RNA homology search](/rna-homology-search/), [simultaneous alignment and folding](/simultaneous-alignment-and-folding/) and the [Sankoff algorithm](/sankoff-rna-algorithm/), [inverse folding](/rna-inverse-folding/), [pseudoknot prediction](/rna-pseudoknot-prediction/) and its [NP-hardness](/pseudoknot-np-hardness/), and [folding kinetics](/rna-folding-kinetics/).
3. **Protein structure.** The [thermodynamic hypothesis](/thermodynamic-hypothesis/), the [hydrophobic effect](/hydrophobic-effect/), the [Levinthal paradox](/levinthal-paradox/), [dihedral angles](/dihedral-angle/), [secondary structure](/protein-secondary-structure/), the [Ramachandran plot](/ramachandran-plot/), [rotamers](/rotamer/) and the [contact map](/contact-map/).
4. **Comparing structures.** RMSD, [optimal superposition](/optimal-superposition/) by the [Kabsch algorithm](/kabsch-algorithm/) or by [quaternions](/quaternion-superposition/), [GDT-TS](/gdt-ts/) and TM-score, and [structure alignment](/structure-alignment/) with [CE](/combinatorial-extension/) and [DALI](/dali/).
5. **Protein secondary structure prediction.** The [problem](/protein-secondary-structure-prediction/) and three generations of classifiers: [Chou-Fasman](/chou-fasman-method/), [GOR](/gor-method/) and [PSIPRED](/psipred/).
6. **Energies and the folding problem.** [Protein structure prediction](/protein-structure-prediction/), [energy functions](/energy-function/) and [the energy surfaces they define](/energy-landscape/), the HP model and the [NP-completeness of HP folding](/hp-folding-np-completeness/), [ab initio folding](/ab-initio-protein-folding/), [fragment assembly](/fragment-assembly/), molecular dynamics and [Rosetta](/rosetta/).
7. **Templates.** [Comparative modeling](/comparative-modeling/), [MODELLER](/modeller/), [loop modeling](/loop-modeling/), [fold recognition](/protein-fold-recognition/), [threading](/protein-threading/) and [I-TASSER](/i-tasser/).
8. **Side chains and design.** [Side-chain packing](/side-chain-packing/), [dead-end elimination](/dead-end-elimination/) and [protein design](/protein-design/).
9. **Contacts and learned prediction.** [Contact map prediction](/contact-map-prediction/), [direct coupling analysis](/direct-coupling-analysis/) and [AlphaFold](/alphafold/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fourteen entries in order: RNA secondary structure, pseudoknot, the Nussinov algorithm, the nearest-neighbor model, the Zuker algorithm, the McCaskill algorithm, dihedral angle, RMSD, optimal superposition, the Kabsch algorithm, TM-score, the HP model, protein structure prediction and AlphaFold. They give the exact RNA methods and the models they assume, the geometry of a protein chain, the way two structures are compared, the simplest model of protein folding and why it is hard, and a trained model judged by its benchmark scores.

Skip at first the second RNA group except for pseudoknot NP-hardness, the secondary structure predictors, the structure alignment programs CE and DALI, and the template methods of group 7 other than comparative modeling. Come back to them in that order. Side-chain packing and dead-end elimination are a self-contained story about an NP-hard problem solved by pruning, and can be read at any point after rotamers.

## Before you start

The RNA algorithms are [interval dynamic programs](/interval-dp/) with a [traceback](/traceback/), from Volume 2; the Nussinov recurrence has the same shape as [matrix chain multiplication](/matrix-chain-multiplication/). Covariance models are [probabilistic context-free grammars](/probabilistic-context-free-grammar/) parsed by the [CYK algorithm](/cyk-algorithm/) and the [inside-outside algorithm](/inside-outside-algorithm/) of Volume 9. The McCaskill algorithm computes a [partition function](/partition-function/), and direct coupling analysis fits a [Potts model](/potts-model/) by the [mean-field approximation](/mean-field-approximation/); PSIPRED and AlphaFold are neural networks trained by [backpropagation](/backpropagation/). These come from Volume 25.

The protein geometry uses the [Euclidean distance](/euclidean-distance/), the [dot product](/dot-product/), the [cross product](/cross-product/) and [vector rotation](/vector-rotation/) from Volume 13, and superposition uses the [singular value decomposition](/singular-value-decomposition/) and the [symmetric eigenproblem](/symmetric-eigenproblem/) from Volume 12, where molecular dynamics also finds [Verlet integration](/verlet-integration/). The search methods use [simulated annealing](/simulated-annealing/) and the [Metropolis criterion](/metropolis-criterion/) from Volume 15 and [parallel tempering](/parallel-tempering/) and [Markov chains](/markov-chain/) from Volume 17. The hardness proofs are [polynomial-time reductions](/polynomial-time-reduction/), from [3-SAT](/3-sat/) for pseudoknots. From the first chapter of this volume you need [global alignment](/global-alignment/), the [multiple sequence alignment](/multiple-sequence-alignment/), the [position-specific scoring matrix](/position-specific-scoring-matrix/) and the [profile HMM](/profile-hmm/). No chemistry is assumed beyond the idea that A pairs with U and G with C, and that proteins are chains of twenty kinds of amino acid.
