This chapter models populations: species that grow, eat and compete, and infections that spread through hosts. The same few tools serve both. A population that grows toward a limit follows the [logistic equation](/logistic-growth-model/), and the infected fraction in the [SIS model](/sis-epidemic-model/) follows the same equation. A predator and its prey, or a disease and its hosts, give a pair of coupled ODEs whose equilibria and their stability decide the long-run outcome. When counts are small, chance matters, and a [branching process](/galton-watson-process/) gives the probability that a new infection dies out before it takes hold.

The central number for an epidemic is the [basic reproduction number](/basic-reproduction-number/) $R_0$, the mean number of new cases that one case causes in a population with no immunity. An epidemic can grow only if $R_0 > 1$. From $R_0$ follow the share of the population that must be immune to stop spread, $1 - 1/R_0$, and the share that is infected by the end, which solves the [final size relation](/final-size-relation/) $1 - z = e^{-R_0 z}$. For ecology the matching numbers are a growth rate and a carrying capacity, and for a set of patches a colonization rate and an extinction rate.

Each result holds under stated assumptions. The ODE models assume a large, well-mixed population, so a threshold such as $1 - 1/R_0$ is a property of the model, not a guarantee for a real city. Diversity indices and the [Chao1 estimator](/chao1-estimator/) assume that the sample is random, and Chao1 is a lower bound on the number of species, not an unbiased estimate. The estimates of the reproduction number over time depend on the generation interval that you put in. Most of the chapter is small systems of equations that you can solve by hand, and the rest are simulations and estimators that you run on data.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Diversity and sampling.** The [Shannon](/shannon-diversity-index/) and [Simpson](/simpson-diversity-index/) indices, [rarefaction](/rarefaction/), Chao1 and the [Lincoln-Petersen estimator](/lincoln-petersen-estimator/).
2. **Growth of a single population.** The logistic model, the [logistic map](/logistic-map/), the [Ricker model](/ricker-model/), the [Leslie matrix](/leslie-matrix/) and the [Levins metapopulation model](/levins-metapopulation-model/).
3. **Interacting species and space.** The [Lotka-Volterra predator-prey model](/lotka-volterra-predator-prey/), the [Holling functional response](/holling-functional-response/), [Lotka-Volterra competition](/lotka-volterra-competition/), the [competitive exclusion principle](/competitive-exclusion-principle/), the [Fisher-KPP equation](/fisher-kpp-equation/) and the [Turing instability](/turing-instability/).
4. **Compartmental epidemics.** The [compartmental model](/compartmental-epidemic-model/), $R_0$, the [herd immunity threshold](/herd-immunity-threshold/), the [Kermack-McKendrick SIR model](/kermack-mckendrick-sir-model/), the final size relation, the SIS model and the [SEIR model](/seir-epidemic-model/).
5. **Structured transmission.** The [next-generation matrix](/next-generation-matrix/), the [Ross-Macdonald model](/ross-macdonald-model/), the [metapopulation epidemic model](/metapopulation-epidemic-model/) and the [heterogeneous mean-field model](/heterogeneous-mean-field-epidemic/).
6. **Chance in epidemics.** The Galton-Watson process, the [extinction theorem](/branching-process-extinction-theorem/), the [Reed-Frost model](/reed-frost-model/), the [stochastic SIR model](/stochastic-sir-model/) and [agent-based simulation](/agent-based-epidemic-simulation/).
7. **Inference from case data.** The [generation interval](/generation-interval/), the [renewal equation](/epidemic-renewal-equation/), [Cori estimation](/cori-rt-estimation/) and [data augmentation MCMC](/epidemic-data-augmentation-mcmc/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: the Shannon index, the logistic model, the logistic map, the Lotka-Volterra predator-prey model, Lotka-Volterra competition, the compartmental model, $R_0$, the herd immunity threshold, the Kermack-McKendrick model, the final size relation, the Galton-Watson process and the generation interval. They give the main models and the main threshold, deterministic and random. Skip at first the spatial models, the structured transmission group and the inference methods after the generation interval.

## Before you start

The models are ODEs, so you need [initial value problems](/ode-initial-value-problem/) and the [Euler method](/euler-method/) from Volume 12, and the stability of an equilibrium is read from the [Jacobian](/jacobian-linearization/) of Volume 36. The Fisher-KPP and Turing entries add the [heat equation](/heat-equation/) of Volume 12. The Leslie matrix and the next-generation matrix need [eigenvalues](/eigenvalue/), the [spectral radius](/spectral-radius/) and the [Perron-Frobenius theorem](/perron-frobenius-theorem/) from Volume 12. The random models use [Markov chains](/markov-chain/) from Volume 17 and the [binomial](/binomial-distribution/) and [Poisson](/poisson-distribution/) distributions from Volume 1, and the stochastic SIR model uses the [Gillespie algorithm](/gillespie-algorithm/) of chapter 27.9. The network models start from the [SIR model on a network](/sir-epidemic-model/) and the [degree distribution](/degree-distribution/) of Volume 7. The inference group uses [conjugate priors](/conjugate-prior/) from Volume 25 and the [Metropolis-Hastings](/metropolis-hastings/) and [Gibbs](/gibbs-sampling/) samplers from Volume 17. No biology is assumed beyond the idea that individuals are born, die, meet and infect each other.
