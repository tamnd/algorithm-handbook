1. The 3-mers are ACG, CGT, GTT, TTG, TGC, GCA, CAA and AAC: eight, all distinct. They pair up as reverse complements, ACG with CGT, GTT with AAC, TTG with CAA and TGC with GCA, so four canonical 3-mers remain: ACG, AAC, CAA and GCA. For odd $k$ no k-mer is its own reverse complement, so there are $4^3/2 = 32$ canonical 3-mers. For $k = 4$ the 16 reverse palindromes count once, which gives $(256 + 16)/2 = 136$.

3. The total is $T = 200$. The running sums of the sorted lengths are 50, 95, 125, so N50 = 30 and L50 = 3. The sums reach 180 at the contig of 15, so N90 = 15. For NG50 the target is 150, reached at 25, so NG50 = 25 and LG50 = 4. After the misjoin the lengths are 75, 50, 25, 20, 15, 10, 5; the sums 75, 125 pass 100 at 50, so N50 = 50 and L50 = 2, while NG50 stays 25. The error raised N50.

4. The 3-mers are ATG, TGG, GGC, GCG, CGT, GTG, TGC and GCA. The vertex TG has in-arcs from AT and GT and out-arcs to GG and GC, and GC has in-arcs from GG and TG and out-arcs to CG and CA. All other vertices are simple or are ends. The five unitigs are ATG, TGGC, TGC, GCGTG and GCA.

9. $N = cG/L = 16\,000$. Islands: $N e^{-6} \approx 39.7$. True gaps: $N e^{-8} \approx 5.37$, so most of the islands end because the overlap is too short to detect, not because a base is unread. Reads per island: $e^{6} \approx 403$. Island length: $L[(e^{6} - 1)/8 + 1 - 0.75] \approx 50\,550$ bases.

10. X and Y interleave, so $\ell_{\text{interleaved}} = 300$, the shorter of the two, and $\ell_{\text{triple}} = 200$. Ukkonen's condition fails for $L \le 301$, so $L \ge 302$ is required. It is also enough when every 302-mer is known: no interleaved or triple repeat has length $\ge 301$, so Theorem 3 of Bresler, Bresler and Tse, quoted in [repeat resolution](/repeat-resolution/), gives a unique Eulerian cycle. On a circle, a repeat with two copies splits the genome into two loops at one vertex, and the two orders of the loops are rotations of the same circular string.

12. At $\ell = 3$ the edges are the five with offset 2 (overlap 6), the four with offset 4 (overlap 4) and one chance edge from CTTAGCAT to CATGCTTA, which share CAT. The offset-4 edges are transitive through the read in between. The chance edge is not, so 6 of the 10 edges remain. At $\ell = 4$ the chance edge is gone, and 5 of the 9 edges remain, a single chain.

14. A substitution at $i$ is in $\min(i + 1, k, L - i, L - k + 1)$ k-mers. For $i \le k - 2 = 29$ the false path begins at a source: a tip of $i + 1$ edges. For $i \ge L - k + 1 = 70$ it ends at a sink: a tip of $L - i$ edges. For $30 \le i \le 69$ the false path rejoins the true one at both ends: a bubble of 31 edges. Every tip has at most 30 edges, below the bound 62. The mean over $i$ is $(465 + 40 \cdot 31 + 465)/100 = 21.7$.

16. The shortest walks are $sbct$ (11), $sact$ and $sabct$ (12), $sbt$ (13) and $sabt$ (14). Each turn round the cycle $b \to c \to b$ adds 4, so every length from 11 to 20 occurs. The walks of length 14 to 16 are $sabt$ (14), $sbcbct$ (15), $sabcbct$ (16) and $sacbct$ (16). Only $sbcbct$ has length exactly 15, so a gap required to be exactly 15 has a unique fill.

17. Write each link as a sign. A cycle can be fully satisfied only if it has an even number of opposite links. Each of the four triangles ABC, ABD, ACD and BCD has an odd number, and the three 4-cycles have an even number. The links AC and BD, of total weight 3, meet every triangle, so dropping them is optimal: A+, B−, C−, D+ satisfies the rest, a weight of 18 out of 21.

22. Sort the lengths $l_1 \ge l_2 \ge \cdots$ and let $j$ be the first index with $l_1 + \cdots + l_j \ge T/2$. Let $F(x)$ be the total length of the contigs of length $\ge x$. Then $F(l_j) \ge T/2$, and for $x > l_j$ only contigs among $l_1, \ldots, l_{j-1}$ count, so $F(x) < T/2$. A join of lengths $a$ and $b$ keeps $T$ and replaces $a[a \ge x] + b[b \ge x]$ by $(a + b)[a + b \ge x]$, which is at least as large, so $F$ does not drop anywhere. A new contig of length $s$ below N50 raises $T/2$ and leaves $F(x)$ unchanged for $x > s$, so no $x$ above the old N50 can pass the new threshold.

23. Put $x = c\sigma$. Since $N = cG/L$, the count is $\frac{G}{L\sigma}\, x(e^{-x} - e^{-2x})$. Setting the derivative to 0 gives $e^{x}(1 - x) = 1 - 2x$, with root $x \approx 1.446$, so $c \approx 1.446/\sigma$ and the maximum is about $0.260\, G/(L\sigma)$. The islands $\frac{G}{L\sigma}\, x e^{-x}$ peak earlier, at $x = 1$, that is $c = 1/\sigma$.

24. Search all orders of the nine 3-mers that chain by 2-letter overlaps. There are four strings: AAACAAGGACC, AAAGGACAACC, AACAAAGGACC and AAGGACAAACC. Add the arc CC $\to$ AA. Out-degrees are 3 at AA, 2 at AC and 1 elsewhere. In a spanning tree directed into AA, only AC has a choice, CA or CC, so $t_w = 2$. The BEST theorem gives $2 \cdot 2!\,1! = 4$ circuits, one for each string.

25. Let $v$ start before $x$. A suffix of $v$ equal to a prefix of $x$, of length $\ge \ell$, is a repeat unless the two copies are the same place in the genome. So every edge is the positional overlap, and goes forward. An edge $v \to x$ that skips a read $w$ is the sum of $v \to w$ and $w \to x$, which exist because both overlaps are longer than that of $v$ and $x$. So it is transitive. The edges between neighbours remain, and no read lies between them.

26. The overlap of $c(ab)^k$ with $(ab)^k c$ is $2k$, and every other overlap is at most $2k - 1$. Greedy joins that pair into $c(ab)^k c$, and no overlap with $(ba)^k$ remains, so the output has length $(2k + 2) + 2k$. The order $c(ab)^k$, $(ba)^k$, $(ab)^k c$ has overlaps $2k - 1$ and $2k - 1$, which gives $2k + 4$. The ratio $(4k + 2)/(2k + 4)$ tends to 2, so no bound below 2 can hold. Tarhio and Ukkonen conjectured that 2 is the truth; the proved bound is 3.5, and about 3.425 with a newer analysis. A 2026 preprint, not yet peer reviewed, claims instances whose ratio approaches 2.4, which would refute the conjecture.
