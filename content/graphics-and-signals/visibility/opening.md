A camera sees only the nearest surface along each ray from the eye. Everything behind it is hidden, and a renderer that draws hidden surfaces wastes time or shows the wrong picture. Take a cube with its 12 triangles and an eye at $(3,3,3)$, outside a corner. Six triangles face the eye and six face away, and the six that face away cannot be seen at all, so [back-face culling](/back-face-culling/) drops them with one dot product each. In a building with a thousand rooms, the eye in one room sees perhaps ten others, and [portal culling](/portal-culling/) finds them by walking through the doors. In a gallery shaped as a simple polygon, a guard at one point sees a star-shaped region, and the [Lee algorithm](/lee-visibility-polygon-algorithm/) finds it in $\Theta(n)$ time. These are three faces of one question: which parts of a scene can be seen from a point, or from a region.

Each method in the chapter has a model, and each claim names it. The first part of the model is the precision. An object-space method works on the exact geometry and returns the visible pieces themselves; an image-space method decides visibility only at a finite set of samples, usually the pixel centers, and is exact there and nowhere else ([object space and image space](/object-space-vs-image-space/)). The second part is the scene: a list of polygons or triangles, closed oriented solids, a polygon in the plane, a height field, or a building split into cells and portals. The third part is the cost. Image-space costs count pixels and fragments, as in the $\Theta(WH + n + P)$ of the [z-buffer](/z-buffer/), with $W \times H$ pixels, $n$ triangles and $P$ covered samples. Object-space costs count polygons, or the size $k$ of the output, which is the output-sensitive model. In the worst case $n$ triangles can have $\Theta(n^2)$ visible pieces, so [hidden-surface removal](/hidden-surface-removal/) in $O(n^2)$ time is optimal in the worst case.

A culling test has its own contract, set out in [visibility culling](/visibility-culling/). If $V$ is the set of visible objects and $K$ is the set kept, a test is exact when $K = V$, conservative when $K \supseteq V$, aggressive when $K \subseteq V$, and approximate otherwise. Back-face, [frustum](/frustum-culling/), [occlusion](/occlusion-culling/) and portal culling are conservative, and the exact image-space pass behind them removes the hidden objects they keep. A [potentially visible set](/potentially-visible-set/) built by sampling is aggressive, and it can drop an object that is visible.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **The problem and its two precisions.** Hidden-surface removal, object space and image space, and [visibility coherence](/visibility-coherence/).
2. **Culling by facing and by frustum.** Visibility culling, back-face culling and frustum culling.
3. **List priority.** The [painter's algorithm](/painters-algorithm/), the [Newell-Newell-Sancha algorithm](/newell-newell-sancha-algorithm/) and [BSP tree visibility](/bsp-tree-visibility/).

6. **Hidden lines.** [Hidden-line removal](/hidden-line-removal/), the [silhouette edge](/silhouette-edge/), the [Roberts algorithm](/roberts-hidden-line-algorithm/) and the [Appel algorithm](/appel-hidden-line-algorithm/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: hidden-surface removal, object space and image space, visibility culling, back-face culling, frustum culling, the painter's algorithm, BSP tree visibility, the z-buffer, z-fighting, occlusion culling, shadow mapping and the visibility polygon. They give the problem, the two precisions, the culling contract, one method of each kind, the precision limit of the depth buffer, culling against occluders, one shadow method and the planar view of the same question.

Skip at first the historical methods that the z-buffer replaced in practice: Newell-Newell-Sancha, Warnock and Watkins. Skip also the A-buffer, depth peeling and the early depth test, which matter once transparency and shading cost enter. The hidden-line group and the guard group can be read on their own, after the visibility polygon.
