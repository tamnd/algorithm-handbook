## History

Hidden-line removal is one of the oldest problems of computer graphics. Lawrence Roberts drew solids with their hidden lines removed in his 1963 MIT thesis on machine perception of three-dimensional solids, by clipping each edge against each convex body in object space. Arthur Appel at IBM in 1967 counted how many faces hide a point of an edge, its quantitative invisibility, and updated the count only where the edge passes behind a contour. Ivan Sutherland, Robert Sproull and Robert Schumacker compared ten hidden-surface methods in 1974, separated object precision from image precision, and described each method as a sort in $x$, $y$ and $z$ in some order.

Most image-space methods come from Utah and from the flight simulators of the late 1960s. Schumacker and others at General Electric in 1969 drew faces by precomputed priorities, the idea behind the [BSP tree](/bsp-tree/) of Fuchs, Kedem and Naylor in 1980. John Warnock subdivided the screen in 1969, Gary Watkins resolved depth along scanlines in his 1970 thesis, and Newell, Newell and Sancha sorted and split polygons for the painter's order in 1972. Edwin Catmull described the depth buffer in his 1974 thesis as a side result of his work on curved surfaces. Loren Carpenter's A-buffer of 1984 kept fragment lists with coverage masks for antialiasing, and depth peeling, from Mammen in 1989 and Everitt in 2001, peeled the layers one pass at a time.

Culling grew with the size of scenes. James Clark in 1976 proposed hierarchical models, so that one box test removes a group of objects. Airey, Rohlf and Brooks in 1990 split buildings into cells with precomputed visible sets, Teller and Séquin in 1991 computed those sets exactly at the level of cells through portal sequences, and Luebke and Georges in 1995 culled through portals at run time with screen rectangles. Greene, Kass and Miller in 1993 combined an octree, a depth pyramid and the visible set of the last frame. Hardware occlusion queries made run-time tests common, and coherent hierarchical culling by Bittner and others in 2004 hid their latency.

Shadows are visibility from the light. Franklin Crow described shadow volumes in 1977, and Lance Williams shadow maps in 1978. Reeves, Salesin and Cook in 1987 filtered the comparisons, which became percentage-closer filtering. Tim Heidmann in 1991 counted the volume crossings in the stencil buffer, the z-pass method; Bilodeau and Songy in 1999, and Carmack in 2000, counted behind the surface instead, and Everitt and Kilgard in 2002 gave the robust form with z-fail and an infinite far plane.

In computational geometry the question was asked for polygons. Victor Klee asked in 1973 how many guards a gallery with $n$ walls needs, and Václav Chvátal answered $\lfloor n/3 \rfloor$ in 1975; Steve Fisk gave the proof by 3-coloring in 1978. Kahn, Klawe and Kleitman proved $\lfloor n/4 \rfloor$ for orthogonal galleries in 1983. Lee in 1983 computed the visibility polygon of a point in linear time, and Joe and Simpson corrected the algorithm for polygons that wind around the point in 1987. Lee and Lin showed in 1986 that the least number of vertex guards is NP-hard to find, and Abrahamsen, Adamaszek and Miltzow showed in 2018 that point guards are complete for the existential theory of the reals.

## Further reading

- Joseph O'Rourke, *Art Gallery Theorems and Algorithms*, Oxford University Press, 1987. The guard theorems, their proofs and the hardness reduction, at the level of a graduate course.
- Subir Kumar Ghosh, *Visibility Algorithms in the Plane*, Cambridge University Press, 2007. Visibility polygons, weak visibility and visibility graphs, with full algorithms.
- Ivan E. Sutherland, Robert F. Sproull and Robert A. Schumacker, A characterization of ten hidden-surface algorithms, *ACM Computing Surveys* 6(1), 1974. The survey that set the terms object space, image space and coherence.
- Daniel Cohen-Or, Yiorgos Chrysanthou, Cláudio T. Silva and Frédo Durand, A survey of visibility for walkthrough applications, *IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics* 9(3), 2003. The classes of culling methods, from point and from region.
- Tomas Akenine-Möller, Eric Haines, Naty Hoffman and others, *Real-Time Rendering*, fourth edition, CRC Press, 2018. Culling, depth buffers, transparency and shadow maps as they are used in current hardware.
