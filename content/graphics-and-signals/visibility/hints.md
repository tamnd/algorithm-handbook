13. Measure cones by slopes from the eye. The first door gives $[-0.2, 0.2]$, and the door on $x = 2$, with slopes $\pm\frac13$, does not narrow it. The door on $y = 1$ subtends the slopes $[\frac15, \frac13]$, and its intersection with $[-0.2, 0.2]$ is the single ray to the corner $(3,1)$, so cell $(2,1)$ is not reached. With the wider door the cone after two doors is $[-\frac13, \frac13]$, and its intersection with $[\frac15, \frac13]$ has an interior, so all four cells are reached.

14. The rectangle is convex and seen whole. A point $(x, y)$ of the alcove sees some point of $e$ when the segment from $a$ to it passes the opening $y = 2$, $0 \le x \le 1$, which gives $y \le 4 - 2x$. So $V(e)$ is $(0,0)$, $(6,0)$, $(6,2)$, $(1,2)$, $(0,4)$, with area 13. From $b$ the condition is $y \le (12 - 2x)/5$, which is the smaller one, so $V(a) \cap V(b)$ ends at the window from $(1,2)$ to $(0, \frac{12}{5})$ and has area $\frac{61}{5}$. The polygon has area 16, so the umbra is 3, the penumbra $\frac45$ and the fully lit part $\frac{61}{5}$.

15. The segment from $(p, 0)$ to $(l, 3)$ crosses $y = 1$ at $x = p + (l - p)/3$, so it is blocked when $-3 - 2p \le l \le 3 - 2p$. The umbra is $|p| \le \frac12$, the penumbra $\frac12 < |p| < \frac52$, and on $[\frac12, \frac52]$ the visible fraction is $(2p - 1)/4$. A point light at $(0,3)$ gives the hard shadow $|p| \le \frac32$.

17. The step is $\Delta z \approx 2^{-24} / d'(z)$ with $d'(z) = fn/((f-n) z^2)$, which is about 0.149 for $n = 0.1$ and 0.0149 for $n = 1$. Neither resolves 0.01 reliably: for $n = 0.1$, 500 and 500.01 store the same integer. For reversed depth, $d_r \approx 10^{-4}$ at $z = 500$, the float spacing there is at most $10^{-4} \cdot 2^{-23}$, and $|d_r'| \approx 4 \cdot 10^{-7}$, so $\Delta z$ is at most about $3 \cdot 10^{-5}$. Setting $d(z) = \frac12$ gives $z = 2nf/(f + n) \approx 0.2$ for $n = 0.1$.

23. The tangent at $t = x_i$ lies above the concave parabola and touches it only at $x_i$, so the lower envelope of the lines follows the parabola and uses line $i$ exactly around $t = x_i$. Clip all lines to one interval that contains every $x_i$. The pieces of the envelope from left to right then give the $x_i$ in sorted order, after $O(n)$ extra work, and sorting needs $\Omega(n \log n)$ in this model.

24. A line $y = \alpha x + \beta$ is linear, so $y(4) = (3 y(3) - y(1))/2$. With $y(1) \le 1$ and $y(3) \ge 2$ this is at least $\frac52$, and the bound is reached only by the line through $(1,1)$ and $(3,2)$, which passes $x = 2$ at $1.5$. So the rooms see each other through the door interiors exactly when $b > \frac52$; at $b = \frac52$ only one grazing line remains.

25. A face is front when $n_f \cdot u > 0$ for the view direction $u$, and for a uniform $u$ this has probability $\frac12$, whatever the mesh. For the eye $E = Ru$ the condition is $n_f \cdot u > h_f / R$, and by Archimedes' hat-box theorem $n_f \cdot u$ is uniform on $[-1, 1]$. For the cube every $h_f = 1$, so each triangle is front with probability $\frac14$: 3 front triangles expected, against 6 for the orthographic view.

26. If $p$ sees $a$ and $b$, the triangle $pab$ has its boundary in $P$, and since a simple polygon has no holes, its interior is in $P$ as well, so $p$ sees each point of $ab$. With the edge from $(0,0)$ to $(4,0)$ in the rectangle $[0,4] \times [0,5]$, the point $p = (2,4)$ and the hole $[1.9, 2.1]^2$, $p$ sees both ends but not $(2,0)$.
