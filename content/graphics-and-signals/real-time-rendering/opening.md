A game or a flight simulator must draw a full frame in about 16 milliseconds, at sixty frames a second, with millions of pixels, many lights, shadows and surfaces that look like metal, cloth or stone. Real-time rendering is the set of methods that make this possible on a graphics processor. Most of them are approximations chosen for speed: a light integral replaced by a table lookup, a shadow by a depth map, a distant mountain by a coarser mesh, the space around a pixel by what the depth buffer shows. The entries say what each shortcut costs, and they separate the few bounds that are proved from the errors that are only measured on test scenes.

Three ideas recur. The first is precomputation: integrals that do not change from frame to frame, such as the filtered light of an environment or the transfer of light over a static mesh, go into textures before the program runs. The second is the screen as a data structure: once the visible surface of each pixel is stored, lighting, ambient occlusion and reflections run per pixel and not per triangle. The third is a budget of detail: the renderer spends triangles, texels and shadow resolution where the viewer can see them.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Shading models.** The [Blinn-Phong model](/blinn-phong-reflection-model/), the rules of [physically based shading](/physically-based-shading/) and the three parts of its specular term: the [GGX distribution](/ggx-distribution/), the [Smith masking function](/smith-masking-shadowing-function/) and the [Schlick approximation](/schlick-fresnel-approximation/) of Fresnel reflection. Then the [Oren-Nayar model](/oren-nayar-reflectance-model/) for rough diffuse surfaces, the [Disney principled BRDF](/disney-principled-brdf/) and [linearly transformed cosines](/linearly-transformed-cosines/) for polygonal area lights.
2. **Stylized shading and tone mapping.** [Toon shading](/toon-shading/) and [Gooch shading](/gooch-shading/), then [tone mapping](/tone-mapping/) and [Reinhard's operator](/reinhard-tone-mapping/), which map scene luminance to the range of a display.
3. **Light from the environment.** The [cube map](/cube-map/), [environment mapping](/environment-mapping/), [image-based lighting](/image-based-lighting/) and the [split sum](/split-sum-approximation/); [spherical harmonic lighting](/spherical-harmonic-lighting/) with the [irradiance environment map](/irradiance-environment-map/), [precomputed radiance transfer](/precomputed-radiance-transfer/) and the [irradiance volume](/irradiance-volume/); [light maps](/light-mapping/); and the skies of [Preetham](/preetham-sky-model/) and of [precomputed atmospheric scattering](/precomputed-atmospheric-scattering/).
4. **Procedural and compressed textures.** [Perlin](/perlin-noise/), [simplex](/simplex-noise/), [Worley](/worley-noise/) and [fractal](/fractal-noise/) noise; the block formats [S3TC](/s3-texture-compression/) and [ASTC](/adaptive-scalable-texture-compression/); the [texture clipmap](/texture-clipmap/) and [virtual texturing](/virtual-texturing/) for textures larger than memory.
5. **Surface detail.** [Bump mapping](/bump-mapping/), [normal mapping](/normal-mapping/), [parallax mapping](/parallax-mapping/), [parallax occlusion mapping](/parallax-occlusion-mapping/) and [displacement mapping](/displacement-mapping/), from a tilted normal to moved vertices.
6. **Level of detail.** The [level of detail](/level-of-detail/) template, [billboards](/billboard/), [impostors](/impostor/), [geomorphing](/geomorphing/), [view-dependent refinement](/view-dependent-refinement/), [ROAM](/roam-terrain/) and [geometry clipmaps](/geometry-clipmaps/).
7. **Shadows.** [Cascaded shadow maps](/cascaded-shadow-maps/), [percentage-closer filtering](/percentage-closer-filtering/), [percentage-closer soft shadows](/percentage-closer-soft-shadows/), [variance shadow maps](/variance-shadow-maps/) and [moment shadow mapping](/moment-shadow-mapping/).
8. **The deferred frame, screen-space effects and transparency.** The [G-buffer](/g-buffer/), [deferred](/deferred-shading/), [tiled](/tiled-shading/) and [clustered](/clustered-shading/) shading, and the [visibility buffer](/visibility-buffer/); [SSAO](/screen-space-ambient-occlusion/), [HBAO](/horizon-based-ambient-occlusion/), [GTAO](/ground-truth-ambient-occlusion/) and [screen-space reflections](/screen-space-reflections/); [order-independent transparency](/order-independent-transparency/) with [per-pixel linked lists](/per-pixel-linked-lists/) or [weighted blended OIT](/weighted-blended-oit/).

## A first reading

Start with Blinn-Phong and physically based shading, then GGX and Schlick: together they are the shading of most current engines. Read the cube map, environment mapping and image-based lighting next, and stop at the split sum, which ties the microfacet terms to a prefiltered environment. Then read normal mapping and cascaded shadow maps, which nearly every renderer uses. From the last group, read the G-buffer, deferred shading and SSAO, and finish with level of detail. That is fourteen entries.

On a first pass, skip the research entries: linearly transformed cosines, precomputed radiance transfer, the visibility buffer, GTAO and moment shadow mapping. Each one refines an earlier entry and is easier once that entry is known. The terrain methods, ROAM, view-dependent refinement and geometry clipmaps, and the two sky models can also wait.

## Before you start

The chapter assumes the GPU [graphics pipeline](/graphics-pipeline/), [texture mapping](/texture-mapping/) with [bilinear filtering](/bilinear-filtering/) and the [mipmap](/mipmap/), and [alpha blending](/alpha-blending/) with [Porter-Duff compositing](/porter-duff-compositing/), all from the rasterization chapter. From the visibility chapter it needs the [z-buffer](/z-buffer/), [shadow mapping](/shadow-mapping/) and [frustum culling](/frustum-culling/). The shading entries rest on the [BRDF](/brdf/), [Lambertian reflectance](/lambertian-reflectance/), the [Fresnel equations](/fresnel-equations/), the [microfacet BRDF](/microfacet-brdf/), the [rendering equation](/rendering-equation/), [ambient occlusion](/ambient-occlusion/) and [radiosity](/radiosity/) from the ray tracing chapter, which states the integrals that this chapter approximates. [Gamma correction](/gamma-correction/) and [color spaces](/color-spaces/) matter for tone mapping and for textures.

From other volumes: [Monte Carlo integration](/monte-carlo-integration/) in Volume 12 for the prefiltering of environment maps, the [Chebyshev inequality](/chebyshev-inequality/) in Volume 1 for variance shadow maps, the [Cholesky decomposition](/cholesky-decomposition/) in Volume 12 for moment shadow maps, [prefix sums](/prefix-sums/) in Volume 6 for summed-area filtering and light lists, and the [progressive mesh](/progressive-mesh/) in Volume 31 for view-dependent refinement.
