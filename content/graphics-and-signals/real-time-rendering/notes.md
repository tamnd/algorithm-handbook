## History

The shading models start in the 1970s. Phong published his model in 1975, and Blinn replaced its reflection vector by the half vector in "Models of light reflection for computer synthesized pictures" in 1977. Blinn and Newell introduced environment mapping in 1976, and Blinn's "Simulation of wrinkled surfaces" of 1978 introduced bump mapping. Trowbridge and Reitz described the distribution now called GGX in 1975; Walter, Marschner, Li and Torrance gave it that name in 2007. Smith's masking function dates from 1967, and Schlick published his approximation in 1994, the same year as the rough diffuse model of Oren and Nayar. Burley's Disney course notes of 2012 and Karis's "Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4" of 2013 moved film-style physically based shading into production and into games, with the split sum for image-based lighting.

Light from the environment was stored in tables early. Greene described cube maps in 1986, and graphics hardware supported them in the late 1990s. Debevec lit scenes from photographs of real light in 1998. Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan showed in 2001 that nine spherical harmonic coefficients give the irradiance with a small error, and Sloan, Kautz and Snyder extended the idea to shadowed transfer in 2002. Greger, Shirley, Hubbard and Greenberg introduced the irradiance volume in 1998, and Quake (1996) shipped light maps. Preetham, Shirley and Smits published their analytic sky in 1999, and Bruneton and Neyret precomputed atmospheric scattering in 2008.

Texture detail and level of detail have a longer line. Clark proposed hierarchies of models for visible surface algorithms in 1976. Perlin's "An image synthesizer" of 1985 introduced gradient noise; his "Improving noise" of 2002 added the quintic fade, and Worley published cellular noise in 1996. Cook's shade trees (1984) and the Reyes architecture of Cook, Carpenter and Catmull (1987) made displacement routine in film. Cohen, Olano and Manocha stored normals in textures in 1998, Kaneko and coauthors proposed parallax mapping in 2001, and Brawley and Tatarchuk described parallax occlusion mapping in 2004. Hoppe's progressive meshes (1996) and their view-dependent refinement (1997) appeared next to ROAM of Duchaineau and coauthors (1997), Tanner, Migdal and Jones's clipmap (1998) and the geometry clipmaps of Losasso and Hoppe (2004).

Shadow filtering began with percentage-closer filtering by Reeves, Salesin and Cook in 1987. Fernando added the blocker search of PCSS in 2005, Donnelly and Lauritzen introduced variance shadow maps in 2006, and Peters and Klein moment shadow mapping in 2015. Engel described cascaded shadow maps in 2006, the same year as the parallel-split maps of Zhang, Sun, Xu and Lun.

The deferred frame goes back to the pipeline of Deering and coauthors (1988) and the G-buffer of Saito and Takahashi (1990). Tiled shading (Olsson and Assarsson, 2011), clustered shading (Olsson, Billeter and Assarsson, 2012) and the visibility buffer (Burns and Hunt, 2013) followed as light counts and resolutions grew. Mittring's CryEngine 2 talk of 2007 brought screen-space ambient occlusion to games, followed by HBAO (Bavoil, Sainz and Dimitrov, 2008) and GTAO (Jimenez, Wu, Pesce and Jarabo, 2016). For transparency, Yang, Hensley, Grün and Thibieroz built per-pixel linked lists in 2010, and McGuire and Bavoil proposed weighted blended OIT in 2013.

## Further reading

- Tomas Akenine-Möller, Eric Haines, Naty Hoffman, Angelo Pesce, Michał Iwanicki and Sébastien Hillaire, *Real-Time Rendering*, fourth edition (CRC Press, 2018). The standard reference for nearly every entry of the chapter.
- Matt Pharr, Wenzel Jakob and Greg Humphreys, *Physically Based Rendering: From Theory to Implementation*, third edition (Morgan Kaufmann, 2016). The offline integrals that the real-time methods approximate.
- Eric Heitz, *Understanding the Masking-Shadowing Function in Microfacet-Based BRDFs*, Journal of Computer Graphics Techniques 3(2), 2014. A careful derivation of the Smith function and its height-correlated form.
- Brian Karis, *Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4*, SIGGRAPH 2013 course Physically Based Shading in Theory and Practice. GGX, Schlick and the split sum as one engine uses them.
- David S. Ebert, F. Kenton Musgrave, Darwyn Peachey, Ken Perlin and Steven Worley, *Texturing and Modeling: A Procedural Approach*, third edition (Morgan Kaufmann, 2003). Noise, fractal sums and procedural textures from the people who made them.
