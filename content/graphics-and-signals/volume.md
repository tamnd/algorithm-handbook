A screen shows a few million pixels, and a game redraws all of them sixty times a second. Each frame starts as a list of triangles in three dimensions and ends as a grid of colors. On the way, each triangle is clipped, projected and turned into the pixels it covers, the hidden pixels are dropped, and the rest are shaded and smoothed. A film renderer makes the same picture another way, by following random paths of light through the scene. A designer drags the control points of a curve until a reflection runs smoothly across a car door, an image editor finds the edges in a photograph, and a phone codes each 20 ms of speech in a few dozen numbers. All of these are algorithms on samples of a continuous world.

This volume is about those algorithms. Its six chapters hold 401 entries: rasterization, curves and surfaces, visibility, ray tracing, image processing and signal processing.

## Why graphics and signals get a volume of their own

A graphics or signal algorithm turns a continuous object into samples, or samples back into a continuous object, and it is judged at the samples. A few ideas come back in every chapter:

- **A sample is a point.** A [pixel](/pixel/) is a point sample at its center, not a little square, and an audio sample is the value of a wave at one instant. The rules that decide which samples a shape covers, such as the [top-left fill rule](/top-left-fill-rule/), are stated for points, and so are the bounds on error.
- **Aliasing is the price of sampling.** Detail finer than the sample spacing comes back as false patterns: jagged edges, crawling textures, wrong tones. The [sampling theorem](/sampling-theorem/) says when it can be avoided, and [antialiasing](/antialiasing/), the [mipmap](/mipmap/) and the [reconstruction filter](/reconstruction-filter/) fight it when it cannot.
- **Exact answers need exact arithmetic.** With integer coordinates, [Bresenham's algorithm](/bresenham-line-algorithm/) and the [edge function](/edge-function/) give exactly the pixels their definitions name, and the [watertight ray-triangle test](/watertight-ray-triangle-intersection/) lets no ray slip through a shared edge. With careless floating point these guarantees fail, and the entries show how.
- **The cost is per pixel, per ray or per sample.** Bounds count covered pixels, rays, triangles or samples. The best are output-sensitive, and many are stated per sample, like the $O(\log M)$ operations per output of fast convolution.
- **Not every guarantee is a theorem.** Some results are exact, some hold in expectation, like the unbiased estimate of [path tracing](/path-tracing/), and some are heuristics judged by measurement or by eye, like [temporal antialiasing](/temporal-antialiasing/) or the [structural similarity index](/structural-similarity-index/). The entries say which kind each result is.

## The chapters

**Chapter 28.1, Rasterization**, turns lines, polygons and triangles into pixels. It starts with the pixel, the [graphics pipeline](/graphics-pipeline/) and the [viewport transform](/viewport-transform/), clips with [Cohen-Sutherland](/cohen-sutherland-line-clipping/) and [Liang-Barsky](/liang-barsky-line-clipping/), and draws lines and circles with Bresenham's algorithm and the [midpoint circle algorithm](/midpoint-circle-algorithm/). It fills polygons by the [even-odd](/even-odd-fill-rule/) and [nonzero winding](/nonzero-winding-rule/) rules with the [scanline fill](/scanline-polygon-fill/), and triangles by edge functions and [Pineda's loop](/pineda-rasterization/). It ends with [perspective-correct interpolation](/perspective-correct-interpolation/), shading, [textures](/texture-mapping/), compositing, the [stencil buffer](/stencil-buffer/) and antialiasing from [supersampling](/supersampling-antialiasing/) to [multisampling](/multisample-antialiasing/).

**Chapter 28.2, Curves and surfaces**, turns a few control points into smooth shapes. A [Bézier curve](/bezier-curve/) is a weighted average of its points with [Bernstein polynomials](/bernstein-polynomials/) as weights, which gives the [convex hull property](/convex-hull-property/) and evaluation by the [de Casteljau algorithm](/de-casteljau-algorithm/). The chapter goes on to the [Hermite](/hermite-spline/), [Catmull-Rom](/catmull-rom-spline/) and [natural cubic](/natural-cubic-spline/) splines, to [B-splines](/b-spline/) with the [Cox-de Boor recursion](/cox-de-boor-recursion/), the [de Boor algorithm](/de-boor-algorithm/), [knot insertion](/boehm-knot-insertion/) and [NURBS](/nurbs/), and to intersection by [Bézier clipping](/bezier-clipping/). On surfaces it treats subdivision by [Catmull-Clark](/catmull-clark-subdivision/), [Loop](/loop-subdivision/) and [Doo-Sabin](/doo-sabin-subdivision/), meshes from data by [marching cubes](/marching-cubes/) and [Poisson reconstruction](/poisson-surface-reconstruction/), and simplification by [quadric error metrics](/quadric-error-metrics/) and [Ramer-Douglas-Peucker](/ramer-douglas-peucker/).

**Chapter 28.3, Visibility**, decides what the viewer sees. It defines [hidden surface removal](/hidden-surface-removal/) and solves it in image space with the [z-buffer](/z-buffer/) and the [hierarchical z-buffer](/hierarchical-z-buffer/), and in object order with the [painter's algorithm](/painters-algorithm/), [BSP tree visibility](/bsp-tree-visibility/) and [Warnock's algorithm](/warnock-algorithm/). It covers culling of [back faces](/back-face-culling/), the [view frustum](/frustum-culling/), [occluders](/occlusion-culling/) and [portals](/portal-culling/), shadows by [shadow mapping](/shadow-mapping/) and [shadow volumes](/shadow-volume-algorithm/), and the geometry of seeing: the [visibility polygon](/visibility-polygon/), [Chvátal's theorem](/chvatal-art-gallery-theorem/) that $\lfloor n/3 \rfloor$ guards suffice, and the [NP-hardness](/minimum-guard-np-hardness/) of the fewest guards.

**Chapter 28.4, Ray tracing**, follows rays from the [pinhole camera](/pinhole-camera-model/) into the scene. It intersects rays with [spheres](/ray-sphere-intersection/), triangles by [Möller-Trumbore](/moller-trumbore/) and the watertight test, and boxes by the [slab method](/slab-method/), and implicit surfaces by [sphere tracing](/sphere-tracing/). It speeds the search with the [bounding volume hierarchy](/bounding-volume-hierarchy/), built by the [surface area heuristic](/surface-area-heuristic/), and with grid traversal by [Amanatides-Woo](/amanatides-woo-traversal/). For light it gives the [BRDF](/brdf/) from [Lambert](/lambertian-reflectance/) to [microfacets](/microfacet-brdf/), [Whitted ray tracing](/whitted-ray-tracing/), the [rendering equation](/rendering-equation/), path tracing with [Russian roulette](/russian-roulette/), [next event estimation](/next-event-estimation/) and [multiple importance sampling](/multiple-importance-sampling/), then [bidirectional path tracing](/bidirectional-path-tracing/), [Metropolis light transport](/metropolis-light-transport/), [participating media](/participating-media/), [photon mapping](/photon-mapping/), [radiosity](/radiosity/) and [ReSTIR](/restir/).

**Chapter 28.5, Image processing**, works on the [digital image](/digital-image/) itself. It starts with [color spaces](/color-spaces/), [gamma](/gamma-correction/), [histograms](/image-histogram/) and [equalization](/histogram-equalization/), [Otsu's threshold](/otsu-thresholding/) and [Floyd-Steinberg dithering](/floyd-steinberg-dithering/). It filters with [linear filters](/linear-filtering/), the [Gaussian](/gaussian-filter/), [separable filters](/separable-filter/) and the [integral image](/integral-image/), and finds edges with [Sobel](/sobel-operator/), [Canny](/canny-edge-detector/) and the [Hough transform](/hough-transform/). It covers [dilation](/morphological-dilation/) and [erosion](/morphological-erosion/), the [distance transform](/distance-transform/), [labeling](/connected-component-labeling/) and [watershed segmentation](/watershed-segmentation/), [pyramids](/laplacian-pyramid/), [Harris corners](/harris-corner-detector/) and [SIFT](/sift/), optical flow by [Lucas-Kanade](/lucas-kanade-method/), and restoration and editing with the [bilateral filter](/bilateral-filter/), [non-local means](/non-local-means/), [Richardson-Lucy](/richardson-lucy-deconvolution/) and [seam carving](/seam-carving/), measured by [PSNR](/psnr/) and SSIM.

**Chapter 28.6, Signal processing**, treats one-dimensional sampled signals. It starts with [convolution](/discrete-convolution/), the [z-transform](/z-transform/), [aliasing](/aliasing/) and the sampling theorem, and [linear time-invariant systems](/linear-time-invariant-system/). It builds [FIR](/fir-filter/) and [IIR](/iir-filter/) filters, runs long ones by [overlap-add](/overlap-add-method/), and designs them by the [bilinear transform](/bilinear-transform/) and [Parks-McClellan](/parks-mcclellan-algorithm/). It changes rates by [polyphase resampling](/polyphase-resampling/), splits signals by the [discrete wavelet transform](/discrete-wavelet-transform/) and the [lifting scheme](/lifting-scheme/), and estimates spectra from the [periodogram](/periodogram/) and [Welch's method](/welch-method/) to [MUSIC](/music-algorithm/). It ends with [linear prediction](/linear-prediction/), the [Wiener](/wiener-filter/), [LMS](/lms-algorithm/), [RLS](/rls-algorithm/) and [Kalman](/kalman-filter/) filters, and sparse recovery by [basis pursuit](/basis-pursuit/) and [compressed sensing](/compressed-sensing/).

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: the pixel, the graphics pipeline, the edge function, [triangle rasterization](/triangle-rasterization/), the top-left fill rule, the z-buffer, the Bézier curve, the de Casteljau algorithm, the B-spline, ray-sphere intersection, the bounding volume hierarchy, path tracing, linear filtering, the Canny edge detector, aliasing, the sampling theorem, the FIR filter, the overlap-add method and the periodogram. Do the exercises marked ◦ at the end of each chapter you touch.

If you already know the basic algorithms, start with the opening page of each chapter. It names the groups in the chapter and the order to read them. Chapters 28.1 and 28.3 belong together, as do 28.5 and 28.6; otherwise the chapters can be read in any order.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.

## What you need first

Some linear algebra is needed throughout: vectors, dot and cross products, and $4 \times 4$ matrices acting on homogeneous coordinates. From Volume 1 you need the [word RAM](/word-ram/) and the [real RAM](/real-ram/), the two cost models of the entries, and [loop invariants](/loop-invariant/). Volume 3 has the spatial trees behind visibility and ray tracing, the [octree](/octree/), the [kd-tree](/kd-tree/) and the [BSP tree](/bsp-tree/), and Volume 7 has [flood fill](/flood-fill/). Volume 11 has the [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/), the [fast Fourier transform](/fast-fourier-transform/), [convolution](/convolution/) and the [fast wavelet transform](/fast-wavelet-transform/), which this volume uses and does not repeat. Volume 12 has the [floating-point model](/floating-point-model/), [IEEE 754](/ieee-754/), [bilinear interpolation](/bilinear-interpolation/) and [Monte Carlo integration](/monte-carlo-integration/); Volume 13 has the [orientation test](/orientation-test/), [point in polygon](/point-in-polygon/) and [polygon clipping](/polygon-clipping/). Ray tracing rests on [importance sampling](/importance-sampling/) and [Metropolis-Hastings](/metropolis-hastings/) of Volume 17, and the Kalman filter on the [state-space model](/state-space-model/) and [filtering](/filtering/) of Volume 25.

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely; you should be able to do them in a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use an idea in a new place; each takes some thought, and some need a short program. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some are results from research papers. Hints for many exercises are in the appendix *Hints to the exercises* at the end of the volume.
