## History

The curves of this chapter come from the car and aircraft industries. Paul de Casteljau worked out his algorithm at Citroën in 1959, in internal reports that became widely known only in the 1970s. Pierre Bézier developed the same curves at Renault around 1960 and published them, so they carry his name. Sergei Bernstein had introduced the polynomials in 1912 for a constructive proof of the Weierstrass theorem, and Elizaveta Voronovskaya gave the asymptotic rate of the approximation in 1932. At Boeing, James Ferguson described parametric cubic curves and surfaces in 1964, and Steven Coons at MIT described the patches bounded by four curves in a 1967 report.

Splines are older than the computer-aided design that uses them. Isaac Schoenberg named them in 1946. Haskell Curry and Schoenberg proved in 1966 that B-splines are a basis of the spline space, and Schoenberg and Anne Whitney had characterized the solvable interpolation problems in 1953. In 1972 Maurice Cox and Carl de Boor independently published the stable recurrence for B-spline values, and de Boor's book *A Practical Guide to Splines* followed in 1978. Kenneth Versprille's 1975 thesis at Syracuse introduced rational B-splines. Wolfgang Boehm published knot insertion in 1980, and in the same year Elaine Cohen, Tom Lyche and Richard Riesenfeld published the Oslo algorithm. Lyle Ramshaw's blossoms of 1989 gave one language for all of these identities.

Subdivision began with George Chaikin's corner cutting of 1974, which Riesenfeld showed in 1975 to converge to a quadratic B-spline. Jeffrey Lane and Riesenfeld gave the algorithm for any degree in 1980. Edwin Catmull and Jim Clark, and Daniel Doo and Malcolm Sabin, published their surface schemes in the same 1978 issue of *Computer-Aided Design*. Charles Loop's triangle scheme appeared in his 1987 master's thesis at Utah, the four-point scheme of Nira Dyn, David Levin and John Gregory in 1987, their butterfly scheme in 1990, and Leif Kobbelt's $\sqrt 3$ subdivision in 2000. Jos Stam's exact evaluation of 1998 and the analysis of Ulrich Reif and Jörg Peters made the smoothness at extraordinary vertices precise.

The methods for meshes from data came from medicine, scanning and cartography. William Lorensen and Harvey Cline published marching cubes in 1987. Martin Dürst pointed out its ambiguous cases in 1988, and Gregory Nielson and Bernd Hamann resolved the face ambiguity with the asymptotic decider in 1991. Akio Doi and Akio Koide used tetrahedra in 1991, and Tao Ju, Frank Losasso, Scott Schaefer and Joe Warren published dual contouring in 2002. Fausto Bernardini and his colleagues at IBM described ball pivoting in 1999, and Michael Kazhdan, Matthew Bolitho and Hugues Hoppe described Poisson reconstruction in 2006. For simplification, Urs Ramer published his algorithm in 1972 and David Douglas and Thomas Peucker theirs in 1973, Michael Garland and Paul Heckbert introduced quadric error metrics in 1997, and Maheswari Visvalingam and James Whyatt published their area method in 1993.

## Further reading

- Gerald Farin, *Curves and Surfaces for CAGD: A Practical Guide*, 5th ed. (Morgan Kaufmann, 2002). The standard text on Bézier, B-spline and rational curves and patches, with the de Casteljau and blossoming view throughout.
- Les Piegl and Wayne Tiller, *The NURBS Book*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 1997). Algorithms for B-splines and NURBS in implementable detail, including knot insertion, refinement and fitting.
- Hartmut Prautzsch, Wolfgang Boehm and Marco Paluszny, *Bézier and B-Spline Techniques* (Springer, 2002). A compact and rigorous treatment that includes subdivision and box splines.
- Carl de Boor, *A Practical Guide to Splines*, revised ed. (Springer, 2001). The numerical side of B-splines: the recurrence, interpolation, least-squares fitting and their conditioning.
- Joe Warren and Henrik Weimer, *Subdivision Methods for Geometric Design* (Morgan Kaufmann, 2002). The refinement relation, convergence and smoothness of curve and surface schemes.
- Mario Botsch, Leif Kobbelt, Mark Pauly, Pierre Alliez and Bruno Lévy, *Polygon Mesh Processing* (A K Peters, 2010). Mesh data structures, reconstruction, simplification and remeshing as they are done in practice.
