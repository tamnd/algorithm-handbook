A ray tracer makes an image by following lines of sight. For each pixel it sends a ray from the camera into the scene, finds the first surface the ray meets, and asks how much light leaves that point towards the camera. Shadows, mirrors and glass come from tracing more rays from the hit point. Light that bounces between surfaces, which the rasterizer of Chapter 28.1 can only approximate, comes from tracing random paths. Most film rendering now works this way, and graphics hardware has interfaces for ray queries against a two-level tree of meshes and instances.

The subject has two halves, and each has its own kind of guarantee.

The first half is geometry. A ray meets a sphere where a quadratic in the ray parameter has a root, and meets a triangle where a $3 \times 3$ linear system has a solution with nonnegative barycentric coordinates. These tests take $\Theta(1)$ time and are exact in exact arithmetic; in floating point the [Möller-Trumbore test](/moller-trumbore/) can miss both triangles at a shared edge, and the [watertight test](/watertight-ray-triangle-intersection/) is built so that it cannot. A scene has millions of triangles, so a ray must not test them all. A [bounding volume hierarchy](/bounding-volume-hierarchy/) skips every subtree whose box the ray misses. Its worst case is still $O(n)$ per ray, for $n$ primitives, and the near-logarithmic cost seen on real scenes is a measurement, not a theorem. The [surface area heuristic](/surface-area-heuristic/) that builders minimize is an expected cost under one model, uniform random lines, and real rays do not follow it.

The second half is an integral. The [rendering equation](/rendering-equation/) writes the light leaving a point as its emission plus an integral, over incoming directions, of light that is itself unknown. Its Neumann series sums the light by the number of bounces, and in a closed room with albedo $0.9$ the sum needs 43 bounces to get within 1 percent. [Path tracing](/path-tracing/) estimates the series by Monte Carlo with one random path per sample. Its estimate is unbiased: its expected value is the exact pixel. Its error is a variance, not a bound. With $N$ paths the standard error is $\sigma/\sqrt N$, so ten times less noise costs a hundred times more paths, and no finite $N$ guarantees any error at all. Each Monte Carlo method in the chapter is unbiased, biased but consistent, or biased and not consistent, and the entries say which: [photon mapping](/photon-mapping/) with a fixed radius blurs the light and stays blurred however many photons it traces, while [progressive photon mapping](/progressive-photon-mapping/) shrinks the radius and converges.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Cameras, rays and ray casting.** The [pinhole](/pinhole-camera-model/) and [thin-lens](/thin-lens-camera/) cameras, [primary ray generation](/primary-ray-generation/), the [closest-hit](/closest-hit-query/) and [any-hit](/any-hit-query/) queries, and [ray casting](/ray-casting/).
2. **Intersecting one primitive.** Planes, [spheres](/ray-sphere-intersection/), boxes by the [slab method](/slab-method/), and triangles by Möller-Trumbore, the [Plücker test](/plucker-ray-triangle-test/) and the watertight test.
3. **Surfaces given by a function.** [Signed distance functions](/signed-distance-function/), [ray marching](/ray-marching/) and [sphere tracing](/sphere-tracing/).
4. **Acceleration structures.** The BVH, its [traversal](/bvh-traversal/), the surface area heuristic, [SAH construction](/bvh-construction/), [linear construction](/lbvh-construction/) and [refitting](/bvh-refitting/); [kd-tree traversal](/kd-tree-traversal/) and [grid traversal](/amanatides-woo-traversal/); [instancing](/object-instancing/).
5. **Coherence and rays with width.** [Ray packets](/ray-packet-tracing/), [beams](/beam-tracing/), [cones](/cone-tracing/) and [ray differentials](/ray-differentials/).
6. **Light at a surface.** The [BRDF](/brdf/), [albedo](/albedo/), the [Lambertian](/lambertian-reflectance/) and [microfacet](/microfacet-brdf/) models, [Snell's law](/snells-law/), the [Fresnel equations](/fresnel-equations/) and [Helmholtz reciprocity](/helmholtz-reciprocity/).
7. **Recursive and distributed ray tracing.** [Whitted ray tracing](/whitted-ray-tracing/), [distributed ray tracing](/distributed-ray-tracing/) and [ambient occlusion](/ambient-occlusion/).
8. **The rendering equation and path tracing.** The equation, the [path integral](/path-integral-formulation/), [Russian roulette](/russian-roulette/), path tracing, [next event estimation](/next-event-estimation/), [multiple importance sampling](/multiple-importance-sampling/), [bidirectional path tracing](/bidirectional-path-tracing/) and [Metropolis light transport](/metropolis-light-transport/).
9. **Participating media.** [Delta tracking](/delta-tracking/) and [volumetric path tracing](/volumetric-path-tracing/).
10. **Caching, density estimation and reuse.** [Radiosity](/radiosity/), [irradiance caching](/irradiance-caching/), photon mapping and its progressive form, [instant radiosity](/instant-radiosity/), [vertex connection and merging](/vertex-connection-and-merging/) and [ReSTIR](/restir/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fifteen entries in order: the pinhole camera model, primary ray generation, the closest-hit query, ray casting, ray-sphere intersection, the slab method, Möller-Trumbore, the bounding volume hierarchy, BVH traversal, the surface area heuristic, the BRDF, Whitted ray tracing, the rendering equation, Russian roulette and path tracing. They give one complete renderer, from the camera ray to an unbiased estimate of the light, with the one structure that makes it fast.

Skip at first the second and third triangle tests, the kd-tree, grid and interval hierarchies, and the group on coherence. In the light transport groups, read next event estimation and multiple importance sampling before anything else; the bidirectional, Metropolis, volume and caching methods can wait until path tracing is clear.
