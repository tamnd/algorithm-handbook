## History

Image processing began with the first scanned pictures and the first programs that looked at them. Lawrence Roberts used two diagonal differences to find the edges of blocks in photographs in his 1963 MIT thesis. Azriel Rosenfeld and John Pfaltz described, in the Journal of the ACM in 1966, the two-pass labeling of connected components with equivalences between provisional labels, and the city block and chessboard distances computed by two raster passes. Irwin Sobel and Gary Feldman presented their $3 \times 3$ operator in a talk in 1968, and Judith Prewitt gave her masks in 1970. Paul Hough patented line detection by voting in 1962, and Duda and Hart gave the normal form $\rho = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$ in 1972. At the École des Mines de Paris, Georges Matheron and Jean Serra built mathematical morphology on erosion and dilation in the 1960s; Serra's book of 1982 is the standard account.

The 1970s added the point operations and the reduction to few levels. Bryce Bayer described his threshold matrices for two-level displays in 1973, Robert Floyd and Louis Steinberg published their error diffusion weights in 1976, Robert Hummel described histogram equalization in 1977, and Nobuyuki Otsu chose the threshold by discriminant analysis of the histogram in 1979. Paul Heckbert published median cut in 1982. Richardson in 1972 and Lucy in 1974 derived their deconvolution from Bayes' rule, and Shepp and Vardi showed in 1982 that it is the EM algorithm for Poisson data.

The 1980s brought scale and motion. Marr and Hildreth proposed in 1980 to find edges as zero crossings of the Laplacian of a Gaussian, and Witkin introduced scale-space filtering in 1983. Lucas and Kanade, and Horn and Schunck, published their optical flow methods in 1981. Burt and Adelson defined the Gaussian and Laplacian pyramids in 1983. Franklin Crow introduced the summed-area table, later called the integral image, in 1984. Zhang and Suen published their thinning algorithm in 1984, and Gunilla Borgefors studied chamfer weights in 1986. John Canny derived his edge detector from three criteria in 1986. Pizer and coauthors described contrast-limited adaptive equalization in 1987. Kass, Witkin and Terzopoulos introduced snakes in 1988, the year of the Harris and Stephens corner detector and of octree quantization by Gervautz and Purgathofer. Perona and Malik proposed anisotropic diffusion in 1990, Vincent and Soille gave the fast immersion algorithm for watersheds in 1991, and Rudin, Osher and Fatemi proposed total variation denoising in 1992.

From the mid-1990s the field moved toward features, learned detectors and edge-preserving filters. Lewis showed in 1995 how running sum tables make normalized cross-correlation fast, and Tomasi and Manduchi named the bilateral filter in 1998. David Lowe presented SIFT in 1999 and its full form in 2004; Hanno Scharr derived his rotation-accurate derivative filters in 2000; Viola and Jones presented their cascade detector in 2001. Pérez, Gangnet and Blake introduced Poisson image editing in 2003. The year 2004 saw graph-based segmentation and the lower-envelope distance transform of Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher, GrabCut by Rother, Kolmogorov and Blake, Criminisi, Pérez and Toyama's exemplar inpainting, Chambolle's dual algorithm for total variation, and SSIM by Wang, Bovik, Sheikh and Simoncelli. Non-local means followed in 2005, seam carving by Avidan and Shamir in 2007, and the guided filter and SLIC superpixels in 2010.

## Further reading

- Rafael C. Gonzalez and Richard E. Woods, *Digital Image Processing*, 4th ed. (Pearson, 2018). The standard textbook for point operations, filtering, morphology, thresholding and segmentation.
- Richard Szeliski, *Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2022). Features, pyramids, alignment, optical flow and segmentation from the side of vision.
- Pierre Soille, *Morphological Image Analysis: Principles and Applications*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2003). Morphology in full, with watersheds, reconstruction and fast algorithms.
- Jean Serra, *Image Analysis and Mathematical Morphology* (Academic Press, 1982). The founding book of the theory, with the hit-or-miss transform, openings and closings.
- Robert Ulichney, *Digital Halftoning* (MIT Press, 1987). Ordered dithering and error diffusion, and the frequency analysis of their patterns.
- Mehmet Sezgin and Bülent Sankur, "Survey over image thresholding techniques and quantitative performance evaluation", Journal of Electronic Imaging (2004). Many thresholding methods, Otsu's among them, compared by measured error on test images.
