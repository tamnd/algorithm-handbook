A signal in this chapter is a sequence of samples taken at equal steps of time: the pressure at a microphone 44100 times a second, the voltage at an antenna, the reading of a seismometer or an accelerometer. Signal processing filters such sequences, measures their frequencies, changes their rate and recovers what noise hides. A phone call runs most of the chapter in a few milliseconds. The codec models each frame of 20 to 30 ms of speech by [linear prediction](/linear-prediction/), an echo canceller adapts a long FIR filter by the [LMS rule](/lms-algorithm/), and the receiver finds its training pulse with a [matched filter](/matched-filter/). A tracking radar points its beam by [delay-and-sum beamforming](/delay-and-sum-beamforming/) and follows the target with a [Kalman filter](/kalman-filter/).

The chapter rests on a few facts, each with a precise model. A signal with no frequencies at or above $B$ is fixed by its samples at any rate $f_s > 2B$; this is the [sampling theorem](/sampling-theorem/), stated for continuous-time signals of finite energy, and below that rate different signals share the same samples. A [linear time-invariant system](/linear-time-invariant-system/) is fixed by its impulse response, and its output is a convolution. The DFT turns circular convolution into a product, so a filter of $M$ taps costs $O(\log M)$ operations per output sample by the [overlap-add](/overlap-add-method/) or [overlap-save](/overlap-save-method/) method instead of $M$ by the direct sum. An FIR filter is always stable, and a causal IIR filter, with its transfer function in lowest terms, is stable exactly when its poles lie inside the unit circle.

Many results here are statements about random signals, and they hold in expectation or in the limit of long records, not on each record. The [periodogram](/periodogram/) is asymptotically unbiased, but its variance does not shrink as the record grows; [Welch's method](/welch-method/) lowers it by averaging. The [Wiener filter](/wiener-filter/) has the least mean-square error among linear filters given the second-order statistics, and the Kalman filter is exact for linear Gaussian models. Other methods are judged mainly by measurement. [YIN](/yin-algorithm/) was built step by step against its error rate on speech databases, the musical noise of [spectral subtraction](/spectral-subtraction/) is heard, not bounded, and [empirical mode decomposition](/empirical-mode-decomposition/) has no convergence theory at all. The entries say which kind each result is, and the pages that follow keep that distinction.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Convolution, correlation and the frequency domain.** [Discrete](/discrete-convolution/) and [circular convolution](/circular-convolution/), [autocorrelation](/autocorrelation/), the [discrete-time Fourier transform](/discrete-time-fourier-transform/), the [z-transform](/z-transform/), [Parseval's theorem](/parsevals-theorem/) and the [power spectral density](/power-spectral-density/).
2. **Sampling and reconstruction.** [Aliasing](/aliasing/), the sampling theorem, the [Nyquist rate](/nyquist-rate/) and [sinc interpolation](/sinc-interpolation/).
3. **Linear time-invariant systems.** The [impulse response](/impulse-response/), the [frequency response](/frequency-response/), the [transfer function](/transfer-function/) with its poles and zeros, [BIBO stability](/bibo-stability/) and [minimum phase](/minimum-phase/).
4. **Digital filters and their design.** The [FIR](/fir-filter/) and [IIR](/iir-filter/) filters, fast block convolution, the [Butterworth filter](/butterworth-filter/) with the [bilinear transform](/bilinear-transform/), the [Parks-McClellan algorithm](/parks-mcclellan-algorithm/), and two smoothers, [Savitzky-Golay](/savitzky-golay-filter/) and the [median filter](/median-filter/).
5. **Multirate processing and wavelets.** [Sample-rate conversion](/sample-rate-conversion/), [polyphase resampling](/polyphase-resampling/), the [discrete wavelet transform](/discrete-wavelet-transform/) and the [lifting scheme](/lifting-scheme/).
6. **Spectra and sensor arrays.** [Spectral leakage](/spectral-leakage/) and [windows](/window-functions/), the periodogram, Welch's method, [beamforming](/beamforming/), and the subspace methods [MUSIC](/music-algorithm/) and [ESPRIT](/esprit/).
7. **Time-frequency analysis.** The [short-time Fourier transform](/short-time-fourier-transform/), the [uncertainty principle](/time-frequency-uncertainty/), the [continuous wavelet transform](/continuous-wavelet-transform/) and the [analytic signal](/analytic-signal/).
8. **Linear prediction, models and pitch.** [Linear predictive coding](/linear-predictive-coding/), [Burg's method](/burg-method/), [Prony's method](/prony-method/), the [cepstrum](/cepstrum/) and [pitch detection](/pitch-detection/).
9. **Detection, estimation and adaptive filters.** [Signal detection](/signal-detection/), the matched filter, the Wiener filter, [adaptive filtering](/adaptive-filtering/) by LMS and [RLS](/rls-algorithm/), and [state estimation](/state-estimation/) with the Kalman filter.
10. **Sparse recovery and phase retrieval.** [Sparse signal recovery](/sparse-signal-recovery/), [basis pursuit](/basis-pursuit/), [orthogonal matching pursuit](/orthogonal-matching-pursuit/), [compressed sensing](/compressed-sensing/), [phase retrieval](/phase-retrieval/) and the [Griffin-Lim algorithm](/griffin-lim-algorithm/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fourteen entries in order: discrete convolution, circular convolution, autocorrelation, the discrete-time Fourier transform, aliasing, the sampling theorem, the linear time-invariant system, the frequency response, the FIR filter, the overlap-add method, the IIR filter, window functions, the periodogram and the LMS algorithm. They give the operations, the frequency view, the limits of sampling, the two kinds of filter, fast convolution, the first spectrum estimate and the first adaptive filter.

Skip at first the subspace methods, the time-frequency group after the short-time Fourier transform, and the last group, which needs some convex optimization. The Kalman filter can wait until you have read the filtering entries of Volume 25.

## Before you start

The transforms come from Chapter 11.3: the [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/), the [fast Fourier transform](/fast-fourier-transform/), [convolution](/convolution/), the [convolution theorem](/convolution-theorem/), [convolution via FFT](/convolution-via-fft/), [Parseval's theorem](/parseval-theorem/) for the DFT, and the [Haar](/haar-wavelet-transform/) and [fast wavelet transforms](/fast-wavelet-transform/); these pages do not repeat them. Chapter 24.3 has [filter banks](/filter-bank/) and [perfect reconstruction](/perfect-reconstruction/). Linear prediction solves a Toeplitz system by the [Levinson-Durbin recursion](/levinson-durbin-recursion/), and Parks-McClellan runs the [Remez algorithm](/remez-algorithm/) of Volume 12 on cosine polynomials. The Kalman filter is the linear Gaussian case of [filtering](/filtering/) on a [state-space model](/state-space-model/) in Chapter 25.5, where the [extended Kalman filter](/extended-kalman-filter/), the [unscented Kalman filter](/unscented-kalman-filter/), the [particle filter](/particle-filter/) and the [Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother](/rauch-tung-striebel-smoother/) live. Iterative shrinkage-thresholding is the [proximal gradient method](/proximal-gradient-method/), accelerated in [FISTA](/fista/). You need complex numbers, sums of geometric series, matrices with their eigenvalues, and the expectation and variance of a random variable.
