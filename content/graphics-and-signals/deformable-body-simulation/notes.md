## History

Deformable bodies entered computer graphics in the 1980s. Reeves (1983) animated the fire of the Genesis Demo sequence of *Star Trek II* with a particle system. Terzopoulos, Platt, Barr and Fleischer (1987) brought elasticity theory itself into animation, with an energy built from the metric and curvature tensors of a surface, finite differences and a semi-implicit step, and Terzopoulos and Fleischer (1988) let such models break. Pentland and Williams (1989) cut the cost by simulating only the lowest vibration modes of a body.

Cloth drove the next decade. Provot (1995) built cloth from structural, shear and flexion springs, measured how soft springs stretch like rubber, and corrected them after each step; in 1997 he added impact zones for many collisions at once. Baraff and Witkin (1998) solved each step by one linearized backward Euler step with a filtered conjugate gradient solve, which allowed large steps. Bridson, Fedkiw and Anderson (2002) combined repulsions, continuous collision impulses and a corrected rigid motion for the zones into a collision method that ends each step free of intersection. Discrete geometry gave better bending: discrete shells by Grinspun, Hirani, Desbrun and Schröder (2003), the quadratic bending model by Bergou, Wardetzky, Harmon, Zorin and Grinspun (2006), and discrete elastic rods for hair by Bergou, Wardetzky, Robinson, Audoly and Grinspun (2008).

For solids, O'Brien and Hodgins (1999) cracked finite element meshes with a separation tensor. Müller, Dorsey, McMillan, Jagnow and Cutler (2002) removed the false forces of linear elements under rotation by stiffness warping, the start of corotational elements. Irving, Teran and Fedkiw (2004) made elements survive inversion by working in the diagonal frame of the singular value decomposition, and Barbič and James (2005) extended modal reduction to large deformation. The course notes of Sifakis and Barbič (2012) collected the finite element practice of the field, and Smith, de Goes and Kim (2018) designed a neo-Hookean energy that needs no inversion threshold.

Games asked for methods that never fail at a fixed cost. Meshless shape matching by Müller, Heidelberger, Teschner and Gross (2005) and position based dynamics by Müller, Heidelberger, Hennix and Ratcliff (2007) moved positions directly. Macklin, Müller and Chentanez (2016) added a compliance to make the stiffness independent of the sweep count, and Macklin and coauthors (2019) measured that many substeps with one sweep each beat one step with many sweeps.

At the same time the implicit step was rewritten as an optimization. Liu, Bargteil, O'Brien and Kavan (2013) solved it for springs with a constant matrix, Bouaziz, Martin, Liu, Kavan and Pauly (2014) generalized this to projective dynamics, and Gast, Schroeder, Stomakhin, Jiang and Teran (2015) used Newton's method with a line search. Li and coauthors (2020) added a barrier on distances and capped each step by a collision check in incremental potential contact; Lan, Kaufman, Li, Jiang and Yang (2022) applied it to affine bodies, and Chen, Liu, Yang and Yuksel (2024) minimized the step one vertex at a time on the GPU.

## Further reading

- Eftychios Sifakis and Jernej Barbič, *FEM Simulation of 3D Deformable Solids: A Practitioner's Guide to Theory, Discretization and Model Reduction*, SIGGRAPH 2012 Courses. The deformation gradient, the common material models, corotational and invertible elements and model reduction, with code.
- Javier Bonet and Richard D. Wood, *Nonlinear Continuum Mechanics for Finite Element Analysis*, second edition (2008). The engineering background of strain measures, hyperelastic energies and their linearization.
- Donald H. House and David E. Breen (editors), *Cloth Modeling and Animation* (2000). A survey of particle and spring models of cloth up to the implicit methods.
- David Baraff and Andrew Witkin, *Large Steps in Cloth Simulation*, Proceedings of SIGGRAPH 98. The implicit cloth step, its energies and its filtered solver.
- Robert Bridson, Ronald Fedkiw and John Anderson, *Robust Treatment of Collisions, Contact and Friction for Cloth Animation*, ACM Transactions on Graphics 21(3), 2002. The standard pipeline for cloth contact.
- Minchen Li and coauthors, *Incremental Potential Contact: Intersection- and Inversion-free, Large-Deformation Dynamics*, ACM Transactions on Graphics 39(4), 2020. Barrier contact inside an implicit step.
