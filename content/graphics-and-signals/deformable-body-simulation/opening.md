Cloth that drapes and folds, hair that swings, a rubber toy that squashes on impact, flesh that jiggles under a character's skin: these are deformable bodies, and animation simulates them. A rigid body has six numbers of state. A deformable body has the position of every particle or mesh vertex, often tens of thousands of them, and its forces come from how far each small piece is stretched, sheared, bent or twisted. Two facts shape every method of this chapter. The equations are stiff: cloth resists stretch far more than bending, so an explicit step must be tiny to stay stable. And contact is hard: cloth folds onto itself, and the collision handling often costs more than the elasticity.

The entries answer the first fact in three ways. Some keep explicit steps and correct the positions afterward. Some take a [backward Euler](/backward-euler-method/) step, which is stable for large steps in the cases the entries state but adds numerical damping. Most of the newer methods write that implicit step as the minimization of an energy and differ only in how they minimize it. The entries say plainly which of these properties are proved, which hold only under stated hypotheses, and which are measurements on test scenes.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Particles and springs.** A [particle system](/particle-system/) moves many independent points. The [mass-spring system](/mass-spring-system/) joins them by springs; its explicit step is stable only for $\omega\Delta t < 2$, and its stiffness depends on the mesh. [Provot strain limiting](/provot-strain-limiting/) shortens over-stretched springs after each step.
2. **Continuum elasticity.** The [deformation gradient](/deformation-gradient/) measures local deformation, and a [hyperelastic material](/hyperelastic-material/) turns it into energy and stress. The chapter covers [Terzopoulos's deformable models](/elastically-deformable-models/), the [St. Venant-Kirchhoff](/st-venant-kirchhoff-model/), [neo-Hookean](/neo-hookean-model/) and [stable neo-Hookean](/stable-neo-hookean/) models, [corotational](/corotational-fem/) and [invertible](/invertible-finite-elements/) finite elements, [modal reduction](/modal-reduction/) and [brittle fracture](/brittle-fracture/).
3. **Cloth, shells and rods.** [Cloth simulation](/cloth-simulation/) states the problem and [Baraff-Witkin cloth](/baraff-witkin-cloth/) gives the implicit step that made large steps common. [Discrete shells](/discrete-shells/) and the [quadratic bending model](/quadratic-bending-model/) handle bending; the [Cosserat rod](/cosserat-rod/) and [discrete elastic rods](/discrete-elastic-rods/) handle hair and cables.
4. **Position based methods.** [Position based dynamics](/position-based-dynamics/) projects positions onto constraints. It is not physically exact, and its stiffness depends on the sweep count and the step. [XPBD](/extended-position-based-dynamics/) removes that dependence when the sweeps converge, [small steps](/small-steps-physics/) spends the sweeps on substeps instead, and [meshless shape matching](/meshless-shape-matching/) pulls particles toward a best fit rigid copy.
5. **Implicit steps as optimization.** [Optimization time integration](/optimization-time-integration/) minimizes the backward Euler energy by Newton's method with a line search. [Fast mass-spring](/fast-mass-spring/) and [projective dynamics](/projective-dynamics/) alternate local projections and a solve with a constant matrix, and [vertex block descent](/vertex-block-descent/) updates one vertex at a time over a graph coloring.
6. **Collisions and contact.** [Impact zones](/impact-zones/) and [robust cloth collision](/robust-cloth-collision/) remove all crossings from a cloth step, in exact arithmetic. [Incremental potential contact](/incremental-potential-contact/) adds a barrier to the step energy and is free of intersection if its collision check is conservative, and [affine body dynamics](/affine-body-dynamics/) applies it to stiff bodies.

## A first reading

Start with the mass-spring system and cloth simulation; they show the stiffness problem in its plainest form. Then read the deformation gradient, hyperelastic materials and the neo-Hookean model, which give the language of the rest of the chapter, and corotational finite elements, a common choice for interactive solids. Read Baraff-Witkin cloth for the implicit step, position based dynamics and XPBD for the game engine view, and projective dynamics for the optimization view. Robust cloth collision and incremental potential contact close the first pass.

The rods and shells, the fracture and reduction entries, and the research methods of the last two groups can wait. Each one replaces a single piece, the energy, the solver or the contact step, of a method you will already know.

## Before you start

The time steps use the [symplectic Euler](/symplectic-euler-method/), [backward Euler](/backward-euler-method/) and [Verlet](/verlet-integration/) methods of Volume 12, and the elements come from the [finite element method](/finite-element-method/) there. An implicit step needs [Newton's method for systems](/newton-method-for-systems/) and a linear solver, the [conjugate gradient method](/conjugate-gradient-method/), [Cholesky decomposition](/cholesky-decomposition/) or [Gauss-Seidel](/gauss-seidel-method/) sweeps. Corotational and invertible elements need the [singular value decomposition](/singular-value-decomposition/), and modal reduction the [generalized eigenvalue problem](/generalized-eigenvalue-problem/), all in Volume 12. The optimization view uses the [backtracking line search](/backtracking-line-search/), [coordinate descent](/coordinate-descent/), [Lagrange multipliers](/lagrange-multiplier/) and the [barrier method](/barrier-method/) of Volume 15.

Contact builds on Chapter 28.10: [continuous collision detection](/continuous-collision-detection/) and its [elementary tests](/elementary-ccd-tests/) between moving points, edges and triangles, and the [rigid body simulation problem](/rigid-body-simulation-problem/) that affine bodies replace. Discrete elastic rods use the [rotation minimizing frame](/rotation-minimizing-frame/) of Chapter 28.8, and projective dynamics extends the local and global iteration of [as-rigid-as-possible deformation](/as-rigid-as-possible-deformation/) in Volume 31.
