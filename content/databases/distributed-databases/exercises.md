1. ◦ The relation $\mathrm{Emp}(\mathit{name}, \mathit{dept}, \mathit{salary})$ holds (a, sales, 40), (b, sales, 60), (c, hr, 45), (d, it, 70), (e, hr, 80) and (f, sales, 30). Fragment it [horizontally](/horizontal-fragmentation/) by the simple predicates $p_1: \mathit{dept} = \text{sales}$ and $p_2: \mathit{salary} < 50$. List the four minterm fragments, check that they are complete and disjoint, and say which fragments the query "employees of hr earning less than 50" must read.

2. ◦ The relation $R(K, A, B, C)$ has the key $K$ and holds $(1, x, u, p)$ and $(2, y, u, q)$. Split it [vertically](/vertical-fragmentation/) into $R_1(K, A)$ and $R_2(K, B, C)$ and rebuild it by a join. Then split it into $R_3(A, B)$ and $R_4(B, C)$ instead and join them on $B$. How many rows does each join give, and why does the first split keep $K$ in both parts?

3. ◦ Three attributes are used by the queries $\{A_1, A_2\}$ three times, $\{A_2, A_3\}$ once and $\{A_1, A_3\}$ twice. Build the attribute affinity matrix, with $AA(i, i)$ the total frequency of the queries that use $A_i$. Compute the three bonds and the global affinity measure $AM$ of the orders $A_1A_2A_3$, $A_2A_1A_3$ and $A_1A_3A_2$. Then run the [bond energy algorithm](/bond-energy-algorithm/), inserting $A_3$ into the order $A_1A_2$, and say whether it finds the best order.

4. ◦ A cluster of $p = 8$ nodes joins $R$ with 400 tuples and $S$ with 4000 tuples. Each tuple starts on a node that does not depend on its key. How many tuples does the [parallel hash join](/parallel-hash-join/) send in expectation? How many does a broadcast of $R$ to every node send? Give the condition on $|R|$, $|S|$ and $p$ under which the broadcast sends fewer tuples.

5. ◦ An item has $n$ copies, each up with probability $0.99$, independently. Under plain [read-one-write-all](/read-one-write-all/), what is the probability that a read can run, and that a write can run, for $n = 3$ and for $n = 5$?

6. ◦ A transaction makes $r = 4$ reads and $w = 2$ writes of items with $n = 3$ copies each, all at different primaries, from a home site that holds no copy. Give the bound of [primary copy locking](/primary-copy-locking/) on its messages. What does the bound become if the home site is the primary of the four items it reads?

7. ◦ In the model of [eager and lazy replication](/eager-and-lazy-replication/), by what factor does each rate grow when a system goes from 2 to 8 nodes: eager deadlocks, eager deadlocks when the database grows with the nodes, lazy master deadlocks and lazy group reconciliations? By what factor do eager deadlocks and lazy group reconciliations grow when each transaction makes twice as many updates?

8. ◦ For each guarantee, say whether a system can give it while partitioned, only with sticky sessions, or not at all, by the results of [highly available transactions](/highly-available-transactions/): read committed, monotonic reads, read your writes, causal consistency, item cut isolation, snapshot isolation, prevention of lost updates, linearizability.

9. • Items $x$ and $y$ each have copies at sites $A$ and $B$. Transaction $T_1$ runs $r_1[x_A]\, w_1[y_A]$ and $T_2$ runs $r_2[y_B]\, w_2[x_B]$, each reading the initial value and each writing one copy only. Show that no two operations of the history conflict, so the history is serializable over the copies, but that it is not [one-copy serializable](/one-copy-serializability/). Which rule of read-one-write-all would have stopped it, and how?

10. • Transaction $T_1$ has its home at site 1 and holds a lock on $a$ there; $T_2$ has its home at site 2 and holds a lock on $b$ there. $T_1$ asks for $b$ through an agent at site 2, and $T_2$ asks for $a$ through an agent at site 1. Draw the local and remote wait-for edges of the [Chandy-Misra-Haas](/chandy-misra-haas/) model. If the home process of $T_1$ starts a probe computation, which probes are sent, and after how many message delays is the deadlock declared?

11. • $R_1$ at site 1 has 2000 rows of width 30, and its join attribute $A$ has 500 distinct values. $R_2$ at site 2 has 100 distinct values of $A$. The domain of $A$ has 1000 values of width 4. The answer of $R_1 \bowtie R_2$ is wanted at site 2. Using the estimates of [SDD-1](/sdd1-semijoin-optimization/), compute the cost of shipping $R_1$ whole, and the cost and benefit of the semijoin $R_1 \ltimes_A R_2$ followed by shipping the reduced $R_1$.

12. • Let $R(A, B) = \{(1,1), (2,2), (3,3), (4,5)\}$, $S(B, C) = \{(1,1), (2,4), (3,6), (7,2)\}$ and $T(C, D) = \{(1,7), (4,9), (5,8)\}$. Run the full reducer $S \ltimes R$, $T \ltimes S$, $S \ltimes T$, $R \ltimes S$ and give the relations after each step. Which tuple of $R$ survives the forward pass and is removed only on the way back?

13. • For $n = 3$ copies each up with probability $0.99$, compare the probability that a write can run under plain read-one-write-all, under the available-copies form, and under a majority [quorum](/quorum/) of two copies. Explain why the available-copies figure is not a fair comparison when the network can partition.

14. • One fragment of size 1 may be stored at any of three sites, with storage cost 1 per copy and communication cost 2 between any two different sites. The read rates at the sites are 10, 4 and 1 and each site makes 1 update. With the cost of the [data allocation problem](/data-allocation-problem/), evaluate all seven sets of sites and give the cheapest.

15. • Under [Clock-SI](/clock-si/), a transaction starts at partition $P_1$ when its clock reads 100 and then reads an item at $P_2$, whose clock reads 96. What does $P_2$ do, and why? At $P_2$ another transaction $U$ is prepared with prepare timestamp 98; what must the reader do about $U$? If $U$ prepared at three partitions with timestamps 105, 103 and 110, what is its commit timestamp?

16. • Two doctors are on call, recorded as $x = 1$ and $y = 1$, and the rule is $x + y \ge 1$. Under [Percolator](/percolator-transactions/), $T_1$ reads $x$ and $y$ and sets $x = 0$, and $T_2$ reads both and sets $y = 0$, with overlapping start and commit timestamps. Show that both commit and the rule breaks. How many messages does each transaction send when it runs alone?

17. • Under [RAMP-Fast](/ramp-transactions/), a writer with timestamp 5 writes $x$ and $y$ on two partitions. A reader of $\{x, y\}$ gets, in its first round, the version of $x$ with timestamp 5 and the version of $y$ with timestamp 3. What does it ask for in its second round, why is that version sure to be there, and how many messages does the read send in all?

18. • Say which invariants are [invariant confluent](/invariant-confluence/) under the given transactions, with a merge that combines the effects of both sides: (a) $x + y \ge 0$, where transactions only add positive amounts to $x$ or $y$; (b) $x \ge 0$, where a transaction subtracts 1 from $x$ when it sees $x \ge 1$; (c) each order refers to an existing customer, with inserts of customers and orders only; (d) a post has at least one tag, with transactions that add or remove a tag. Give a counterexample for each one that is not.

19. • Five transactions touch the tuples $\{a, b, c\}$, $\{c, d\}$, $\{d, e\}$, $\{e, f\}$ and $\{d, f\}$. Build the graph of [Schism](/schism-partitioning/) without replication, with one edge of weight 1 for each pair of tuples in a transaction. Find the cut of least weight into two parts of three tuples each. For the split $\{a, b, d\}$ and $\{c, e, f\}$, compare the cut weight with the number of distributed transactions, and explain the gap.

20. ★ Each write of an item goes to $w$ of its $n$ copies and each read to $r$ copies, with version numbers on the copies and strict two-phase locking at each copy. Show that $r + w > n$ makes every read see the newest committed version, and that $2w > n$ makes two writes of one item conflict at some copy. Explain why these two facts give one-copy serializability.

21. ★ Explain why [Calvin](/calvin-deterministic-transactions/) needs no two-phase commit: what would make a participant vote no in a locking system, and why can that not happen when every node runs the same input in the same order? Then take a transaction that updates the account named in a row it reads, and describe how the reconnaissance step of OLLP handles it, including the case where the row changes before the transaction runs.

22. ★ Two replicas of a counter start at 0 and are partitioned. A client at each side runs "read the counter, then write the value read plus 1", and each side must answer without hearing from the other. Show that any such system either loses one of the increments or is not serializable, and so cannot prevent lost updates. Which change of the operation makes the counter safe without coordination?

23. ★ In a parallel hash join on $p = 10$ nodes, one key has $m_R = 10{,}000$ tuples in $R$ and $m_S = 100$ in $S$. How many tuples does its node receive, and how many output tuples does it make? Now split the heavy key: spread its $R$ tuples evenly over all nodes and send each of its $S$ tuples to every node. Give the load and the output per node for that key, the extra tuples sent, and show that the result is still exactly the join.
