A shop keeps its orders, customers and stock in tables. Each evening a manager asks for the ten customers who spent most this month, and the system must find them among millions of rows without reading the disk ten times over. During the day, hundreds of clerks update the same tables at once, and each must see a consistent state, as if working alone. When the power fails, every sale that was confirmed must survive and every half-done one must vanish. And a report that the manager reads every hour should not have to be recomputed from scratch each time. Each of these needs is a problem with its own algorithms: a query must be planned and run, concurrent transactions must be kept serializable, a log must make recovery possible, and a stored result must be kept up to date.

This volume collects those algorithms and the theory under them, from the [relational model](/relational-model/) and its dependencies to join algorithms, query optimization, transactions, recovery and incremental maintenance. It has nine chapters. In this edition chapter 40.1 is written in full; chapters 40.2 to 40.6 and 40.9 are written in part, and the rest of their entries are stubs; chapters 40.7 and 40.8 have stubs only.

## Why databases get a volume of their own

A few ideas run through the whole volume:

- **Queries say what, not how.** A query in [relational algebra](/relational-algebra/) or SQL names the result, and the system chooses the method. Much of the theory asks what can be decided about queries themselves: containment of [conjunctive queries](/conjunctive-query/) is NP-complete, and the [data complexity of Datalog](/datalog/) is P-complete.
- **Constraints shape the schema.** [Functional dependencies](/functional-dependency/) and their relatives decide which schemas repeat data. [Armstrong's axioms](/armstrongs-axioms/) are sound and complete for functional dependencies, and the [chase](/chase-algorithm/) decides implication for the classes of dependencies for which it terminates.
- **The disk sets the cost.** Operators are costed in page reads and writes, so a join that reads its inputs three times can beat one that compares fewer tuples. The [cost model](/cost-model/) and [cardinality estimation](/cardinality-estimation/) of an optimizer are approximations, and their errors are measured, not assumed away.
- **Size bounds decide what a plan can do.** [Join ordering](/join-ordering/) is NP-hard in general, and dynamic programming in the style of System R is exact only for its own search space under its cost model. For the output of a join, the [AGM bound](/agm-bound/) is tight, and worst-case optimal joins run within it.
- **Correctness under concurrency and failure.** [Two-phase locking](/two-phase-locking/) guarantees conflict serializability, and its strict form also gives recoverable, cascadeless schedules. [Snapshot isolation](/snapshot-isolation/) is not serializable, because of write skew, unless it is extended to [serializable snapshot isolation](/serializable-snapshot-isolation/). [ARIES](/aries-recovery/) is correct under [write-ahead logging](/write-ahead-logging/) and its own rules.

## The chapters

**Chapter 40.1, Relational theory**, is written in full. It covers the [relational model](/relational-model/), its algebra and calculus, functional and multivalued dependencies, the normal forms from [first normal form](/first-normal-form/) to [BCNF](/boyce-codd-normal-form/) and beyond, [lossless decompositions](/lossless-join-decomposition/), the chase, the [Chandra-Merlin theorem](/chandra-merlin-theorem/) on containment, [acyclic hypergraphs](/acyclic-hypergraph/) and hypertree width, Datalog with [semi-naive evaluation](/semi-naive-evaluation/), and provenance and [probabilistic databases](/probabilistic-database/).

**Chapter 40.2, Storage and indexing**, is written in part. Its written entries cover the [clustered index](/clustered-index/), covering indexes, [bitmap indexes](/bitmap-index/), [zone maps](/zone-map/), join indexes, bulk loading of B+ trees, database cracking, [learned indexes](/learned-index/) and [index selection](/index-selection/). Buffer management, page and column layouts, compression and further index structures are still stubs.

**Chapter 40.3, Query processing**, is written in part. Its written entries cover join algorithms from the [nested-loop join](/nested-loop-join/) to the [Grace hash join](/grace-hash-join/) and the [hybrid hash join](/hybrid-hash-join/), [semijoins](/semijoin/) and Bloom joins, grouping and the [iterator model](/iterator-model/), joins of many relations with the [Yannakakis algorithm](/yannakakis-algorithm/) and [generic join](/generic-join/), top-k queries with the [threshold algorithm](/threshold-algorithm/), and skylines. Vectorized and compiled execution, parallel operators and online aggregation are still stubs.

**Chapter 40.4, Query planning**, is written in part. Its written entries cover the [query plan](/query-plan/), [selectivity](/selectivity/) and [histograms](/histogram/), [predicate pushdown](/predicate-pushdown/), the cost model, cardinality estimation, join ordering, the [Selinger enumeration](/selinger-join-enumeration/) of System R, the [query optimization problem](/query-optimization/) and [view selection](/view-selection/). Faster join enumeration, optimizer frameworks, learned estimators and adaptive execution are still stubs.

**Chapter 40.5, Transactions and concurrency**, is written in part. Its written entries cover the [ACID properties](/acid-properties/), [serializability](/serializability/) and the [conflict graph](/conflict-graph/), [isolation levels](/isolation-levels/), locking with [strict two-phase locking](/strict-two-phase-locking/), [deadlock prevention](/wait-die-and-wound-wait/), [timestamp ordering](/timestamp-ordering/), [optimistic](/optimistic-concurrency-control/) and [multiversion](/multiversion-concurrency-control/) concurrency control, snapshot isolation and its serializable form, and the [phantom problem](/phantom-problem/). Schedule classes, granular locking and transaction chopping are still stubs.

**Chapter 40.6, Logging and recovery**, is written in part. Its written entries cover [crash recovery](/crash-recovery/), the [steal and force policies](/steal-and-force-policies/), [shadow paging](/shadow-paging/), write-ahead logging, [checkpointing](/checkpointing/), [group commit](/group-commit/) and ARIES. Logging styles, fuzzy checkpoints, media and point-in-time recovery are still stubs.

**Chapter 40.7, Distributed databases**, has stubs only. It will cover the fragmentation and allocation of data across sites, distributed and parallel query processing with semijoins and parallel hash joins, replication with one-copy serializability, and distributed transaction protocols with deadlock detection across sites. Meanwhile, read the [semijoin](/semijoin/) and the [Bloom join](/bloom-join/) of chapter 40.3, [two-phase commit](/two-phase-commit/) and [Paxos](/paxos/) in Volume 21, and [primary-backup replication](/primary-backup-replication/), also in Volume 21.

**Chapter 40.8, Data integration and cleaning**, has stubs only. It will cover schema mappings and data exchange, answering queries using views, repairs and consistent query answering, entity resolution, schema matching, the discovery of dependencies, and truth discovery. Meanwhile, read the [chase](/chase-algorithm/), [tuple-generating dependencies](/tuple-generating-dependency/) and the [query containment problem](/query-containment-problem/) of chapter 40.1, which data exchange and view rewriting build on, and [edit distance](/edit-distance/) in Volume 8 and [MinHash](/minhash/) in Volume 5, which entity resolution uses to compare records.

**Chapter 40.9, Stream and incremental processing**, is written in part. Its written entries cover the [materialized view](/materialized-view/), the [view maintenance problem](/view-maintenance/), [incremental view maintenance](/incremental-view-maintenance/) by delta rules, and the [DRed algorithm](/dred-algorithm/) for recursive views. Continuous queries, windows and watermarks, dataflow systems and stream joins are still stubs.

## How to read this volume

Chapter 40.1 is the base of the rest; read its first groups before anything else. Chapters 40.2, 40.3 and 40.4 follow the path of a query from storage to plan and are best read in that order. Chapters 40.5 and 40.6 form a pair and can be read after 40.1 alone. Chapter 40.9 leans on 40.1 and on the joins of 40.3.

For a first course, take these written entries in order: [relational model](/relational-model/), [relational algebra](/relational-algebra/), [functional dependency](/functional-dependency/), [attribute closure](/attribute-closure-algorithm/), [BCNF](/boyce-codd-normal-form/), [conjunctive query](/conjunctive-query/), [clustered index](/clustered-index/), [relational join](/relational-join/), [block nested-loop join](/block-nested-loop-join/), [sort-merge join](/sort-merge-join/), [Grace hash join](/grace-hash-join/), [query plan](/query-plan/), [selectivity](/selectivity/), [join ordering](/join-ordering/), [ACID properties](/acid-properties/), [conflict serializability](/conflict-serializability/), [two-phase locking](/two-phase-locking/), [write-ahead logging](/write-ahead-logging/) and [materialized view](/materialized-view/). Each chapter's opening page gives a longer first reading.

Each entry stands on its own; its facts box lists what it needs, by entry number.

## What you need first

From Volume 1 you need the [external memory model](/external-memory-model/), which counts the page transfers that most costs in the volume are measured in. From Volume 3 come the [hash table](/hash-table/), the [B+ tree](/b-plus-tree/) and the [Bloom filter](/bloom-filter/), and from Volume 4 [external merge sort](/external-merge-sort/). The theory of chapter 40.1 uses first-order logic and [Horn clauses](/horn-clause/) from Volume 16, and [hypergraphs](/hypergraph/) from Volume 7. Some results are stated as [NP-complete](/np-complete/) problems in the sense of Volume 39.

## The exercises

Each written chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely; you should be able to do them in a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use an idea in a new place; each takes some thought, and some need a short program. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some are results from research papers. Hints for many exercises are in the appendix *Hints to the exercises* at the end of the volume.
