A shop keeps its orders, customers and stock in tables. Each evening a manager asks for the ten customers who spent most this month, and the system must find them among millions of rows without reading the disk ten times over. During the day, hundreds of clerks update the same tables at once, and each must see a consistent state, as if working alone. When the power fails, every sale that was confirmed must survive and every half-done one must vanish. And a report that the manager reads every hour should not have to be recomputed from scratch each time. Each of these needs is a problem with its own algorithms: a query must be planned and run, concurrent transactions must be kept serializable, a log must make recovery possible, and a stored result must be kept up to date.

This volume collects those algorithms and the theory under them, from the [relational model](/relational-model/) and its dependencies to join algorithms, query optimization, transactions, recovery and incremental maintenance. It goes on to distributed databases and to the integration and cleaning of data from many sources. It has nine chapters and 296 entries.

## Why databases get a volume of their own

A few ideas run through the whole volume:

- **Queries say what, not how.** A query in [relational algebra](/relational-algebra/) or SQL names the result, and the system chooses the method. Much of the theory asks what can be decided about queries themselves: containment of [conjunctive queries](/conjunctive-query/) is NP-complete, and the [data complexity of Datalog](/datalog/) is P-complete.
- **Constraints shape the schema.** [Functional dependencies](/functional-dependency/) and their relatives decide which schemas repeat data. [Armstrong's axioms](/armstrongs-axioms/) are sound and complete for functional dependencies, and the [chase](/chase-algorithm/) decides implication for the classes of dependencies for which it terminates.
- **The disk sets the cost.** Operators are costed in page reads and writes, so a join that reads its inputs three times can beat one that compares fewer tuples. The [cost model](/cost-model/) and [cardinality estimation](/cardinality-estimation/) of an optimizer are approximations, and their errors are measured, not assumed away.
- **Size bounds decide what a plan can do.** [Join ordering](/join-ordering/) is NP-hard in general, and dynamic programming in the style of System R is exact only for its own search space under its cost model. For the output of a join, the [AGM bound](/agm-bound/) is tight, and worst-case optimal joins run within it.
- **Correctness under concurrency and failure.** [Two-phase locking](/two-phase-locking/) guarantees conflict serializability, and its strict form also gives recoverable, cascadeless schedules. [Snapshot isolation](/snapshot-isolation/) is not serializable, because of write skew, unless it is extended to [serializable snapshot isolation](/serializable-snapshot-isolation/). [ARIES](/aries-recovery/) is correct under [write-ahead logging](/write-ahead-logging/) and its own rules.
- **Many sites and many sources.** Over many nodes the same goals cost messages, and some guarantees cannot survive a partition. Data from many sources is incomplete and inconsistent, so the theory asks for the certain answers, and many methods for cleaning and matching are heuristics.

## The chapters

**Chapter 40.1, Relational theory** (53 entries), covers the [relational model](/relational-model/), its algebra and calculus, functional and multivalued dependencies, the normal forms from [first normal form](/first-normal-form/) to [BCNF](/boyce-codd-normal-form/) and beyond, [lossless decompositions](/lossless-join-decomposition/), the chase, the [Chandra-Merlin theorem](/chandra-merlin-theorem/) on containment, [acyclic hypergraphs](/acyclic-hypergraph/) and hypertree width, Datalog with [semi-naive evaluation](/semi-naive-evaluation/), and provenance and [probabilistic databases](/probabilistic-database/).

**Chapter 40.2, Storage and indexing** (31 entries), covers the [slotted page](/slotted-page/), heap files, row, column and [PAX](/pax-page-layout/) layouts, dictionary and frame-of-reference encoding, the [buffer pool](/buffer-pool/) and the [five-minute rule](/five-minute-rule/). Its indexes run from the [clustered index](/clustered-index/), covering and partial indexes to the prefix B-tree, the [Bw-tree](/bw-tree/), [GiST](/generalized-search-tree/), [bitmap indexes](/bitmap-index/) with WAH compression, [zone maps](/zone-map/) and join indexes. It ends with [database cracking](/database-cracking/), [learned indexes](/learned-index/) such as ALEX and the PGM-index, and [index selection](/index-selection/) with what-if analysis.

**Chapter 40.3, Query processing** (40 entries), covers join algorithms from the [nested-loop join](/nested-loop-join/) to the [Grace hash join](/grace-hash-join/), the [hybrid hash join](/hybrid-hash-join/) and the radix hash join, [semijoins](/semijoin/) and Bloom joins, and aggregation by hashing and sorting. It treats execution with the [iterator model](/iterator-model/), the exchange operator, [vectorized execution](/vectorized-query-execution/), [data-centric compilation](/data-centric-query-compilation/) and morsel-driven parallelism. It covers joins of many relations with the [Yannakakis algorithm](/yannakakis-algorithm/), the AGM bound, [generic join](/generic-join/), leapfrog triejoin, then top-k queries with the [threshold algorithm](/threshold-algorithm/), skylines, the data cube and online aggregation.

**Chapter 40.4, Query planning** (35 entries), covers the [query plan](/query-plan/), [selectivity](/selectivity/), [histograms](/histogram/) and cardinality estimation, learned and pessimistic. It treats the cost model, join ordering and its NP-hardness, the [Selinger enumeration](/selinger-join-enumeration/) of System R with interesting orders, [DPccp](/dpccp/), DPhyp, [IKKBZ](/ikkbz-algorithm/) and greedy ordering. Rewrites such as [predicate pushdown](/predicate-pushdown/) and subquery unnesting follow, then the [Volcano](/volcano-optimizer-generator/) and [Cascades](/cascades-framework/) frameworks, mid-query reoptimization, [eddies](/eddies/), [view selection](/view-selection/) and learned optimization.

**Chapter 40.5, Transactions and concurrency** (41 entries), covers the [ACID properties](/acid-properties/), schedules, [serializability](/serializability/) and the [conflict graph](/conflict-graph/), recoverable and strict schedules, and anomalies and [isolation levels](/isolation-levels/). On the protocol side it covers [strict two-phase locking](/strict-two-phase-locking/) and its variants, granular and key-range locking, the [phantom problem](/phantom-problem/), [deadlock prevention](/wait-die-and-wound-wait/), [timestamp ordering](/timestamp-ordering/), [optimistic](/optimistic-concurrency-control/) and [multiversion](/multiversion-concurrency-control/) concurrency control, and snapshot isolation with its serializable form. It ends with sagas, escrow, [transaction chopping](/transaction-chopping/) and Silo.

**Chapter 40.6, Logging and recovery** (21 entries), covers [crash recovery](/crash-recovery/), the [steal and force policies](/steal-and-force-policies/), [shadow paging](/shadow-paging/), write-ahead and undo-redo logging, log sequence numbers and compensation records, ARIES, [checkpointing](/checkpointing/) with fuzzy checkpoints, [group commit](/group-commit/) and savepoints. It compares physiological, logical and command logging, and treats [media recovery](/media-recovery/), point-in-time recovery, write-behind logging and instant restart.

**Chapter 40.7, Distributed and replicated databases** (20 entries), covers the [shared-nothing architecture](/shared-nothing-architecture/), [horizontal](/horizontal-fragmentation/) and vertical fragmentation, the bond energy algorithm, data allocation and Schism, then distributed queries with SDD-1 and the [parallel hash join](/parallel-hash-join/). For replicated data it treats [one-copy serializability](/one-copy-serializability/), eager and lazy replication, primary copy locking and read-one-write-all. For transactions across nodes it covers distributed deadlock detection, [Calvin](/calvin-deterministic-transactions/), Clock-SI and Percolator, and it ends with what can be done without coordination: [highly available transactions](/highly-available-transactions/), RAMP transactions and invariant confluence. Consensus and atomic commit are in Volume 21.

**Chapter 40.8, Data integration and cleaning** (30 entries), covers [schema matching](/schema-matching/) and [schema mappings](/schema-mapping/) in the GAV and LAV styles, [answering queries using views](/answering-queries-using-views/) with the bucket algorithm, [MiniCon](/minicon-algorithm/) and inverse rules, and data exchange with [universal solutions](/universal-solution/), [certain answers](/certain-answers/), weak acyclicity, the core and composition. It treats [entity resolution](/entity-resolution/) with the [Fellegi-Sunter model](/fellegi-sunter-model/), blocking and R-Swoosh. It ends with conditional functional and denial constraints, [repairs](/database-repair/) and consistent query answering, HoloClean, the discovery of functional dependencies with TANE, and truth discovery.

**Chapter 40.9, Stream and incremental processing** (25 entries), covers the [materialized view](/materialized-view/) and the [view maintenance problem](/view-maintenance/), [incremental](/incremental-view-maintenance/), deferred and self-maintainable maintenance, delta rules, the [DRed algorithm](/dred-algorithm/) for recursive views, higher-order IVM and DBSP. For streams it treats [continuous queries](/continuous-query/), the dataflow model with [event-time watermarks](/event-time-watermark/) and punctuation, the [symmetric hash join](/symmetric-hash-join/) and XJoin, complex event processing, window aggregation with panes and slicing, timely and differential dataflow, and asynchronous barrier snapshotting.

## How to read this volume

Chapter 40.1 is the base of the rest; read its first groups before anything else. Chapters 40.2, 40.3 and 40.4 follow the path of a query from storage to plan and are best read in that order. Chapters 40.5 and 40.6 form a pair and can be read after 40.1 alone. Chapter 40.7 needs the joins of 40.3, the transactions of 40.5 and the consensus material of Volume 21. Chapter 40.8 leans on the dependencies and queries of 40.1 alone, and chapter 40.9 on 40.1 and the joins of 40.3.

For a first course, take these entries in order: [relational model](/relational-model/), [relational algebra](/relational-algebra/), [functional dependency](/functional-dependency/), [attribute closure](/attribute-closure-algorithm/), [BCNF](/boyce-codd-normal-form/), [conjunctive query](/conjunctive-query/), [clustered index](/clustered-index/), [relational join](/relational-join/), [block nested-loop join](/block-nested-loop-join/), [sort-merge join](/sort-merge-join/), [Grace hash join](/grace-hash-join/), [query plan](/query-plan/), [selectivity](/selectivity/), [join ordering](/join-ordering/), [ACID properties](/acid-properties/), [conflict serializability](/conflict-serializability/), [two-phase locking](/two-phase-locking/), [write-ahead logging](/write-ahead-logging/) and [materialized view](/materialized-view/). A second course can add [horizontal fragmentation](/horizontal-fragmentation/), [one-copy serializability](/one-copy-serializability/), [read-one-write-all](/read-one-write-all/), [schema mapping](/schema-mapping/), [certain answers](/certain-answers/) and [entity resolution](/entity-resolution/). Each chapter's opening page gives a longer first reading.

Each entry stands on its own; its facts box lists what it needs, by entry number.

## What you need first

From Volume 1 you need the [external memory model](/external-memory-model/), which counts the page transfers that most costs in the volume are measured in. From Volume 3 come the [hash table](/hash-table/), the [B+ tree](/b-plus-tree/) and the [Bloom filter](/bloom-filter/), and from Volume 4 [external merge sort](/external-merge-sort/). The theory of chapter 40.1 uses first-order logic and [Horn clauses](/horn-clause/) from Volume 16, and [hypergraphs](/hypergraph/) from Volume 7. Some results are stated as [NP-complete](/np-complete/) problems in the sense of Volume 39.

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely; you should be able to do them in a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use an idea in a new place; each takes some thought, and some need a short program. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some are results from research papers. Hints for many exercises are in the appendix *Hints to the exercises* at the end of the volume.
