A query plan is a tree of operators, and this chapter is about the operators and how plans run. Each operator takes relations in and gives a relation out: a join, a semijoin, a grouping, a top-k selection, a skyline. The planner of the next chapter chooses which operators to use and in which order; the entries here say how each operator works, what it costs and when it is the right choice.

Most costs in the chapter are counted in page reads and writes, as in the [external memory model](/external-memory-model/), with $M$ and $N$ pages for the two inputs and $B$ pages of memory. The textbook rules are simple: nested loops that rescan the inner relation for each outer tuple cost $M + pMN$ page reads with $p$ tuples per page, a join with enough memory about $3(M + N)$, and a join whose smaller input fits in memory $M + N$. The later entries count tuples or processor work instead, because there the danger is the size of what is built from the inputs, or the cost per tuple.

## What the chapter covers

1. **The join and nested loops.** The [relational join](/relational-join/) needs at least $M + N$ reads. The [nested-loop join](/nested-loop-join/) works for any condition. The [block nested-loop join](/block-nested-loop-join/) scans the inner relation once per block of $B - 2$ outer pages, and the [index nested-loop join](/index-nested-loop-join/) replaces the scan by index probes.
2. **Sort and hash joins.** The [sort-merge join](/sort-merge-join/) sorts both inputs and merges them. The [hash join](/hash-join/) builds a table on the smaller input; the [Grace hash join](/grace-hash-join/) first splits both inputs into partitions that fit, and the [hybrid hash join](/hybrid-hash-join/) keeps one partition in memory. The [radix hash join](/radix-hash-join/) partitions in memory, in passes, until each part fits in the cache.
3. **Semijoins.** The [semijoin](/semijoin/) $R \ltimes S$ keeps the tuples of $R$ with a partner in $S$. The [Bloom join](/bloom-join/) sends a Bloom filter of the keys instead of the keys.
4. **Aggregation and the data cube.** [Group-by aggregation](/group-by-aggregation/) is the problem; [hash aggregation](/hash-aggregation/) solves it with one partial state per group, and [sort-based aggregation](/sort-based-aggregation/) by sorting and combining equal keys inside the runs. The [data cube](/data-cube/) holds the aggregates of every subset of the grouping attributes, and [BUC](/bottom-up-cube/) computes the cells with enough rows.
5. **Running a plan.** The [iterator model](/iterator-model/) runs a plan through open, next and close, and [pipelining and materialization](/pipelining-and-materialization/) explains where it must stop and store. The [exchange operator](/exchange-operator/) runs a plan in parallel streams. [Data-centric compilation](/data-centric-query-compilation/) turns each pipeline into one loop, [vectorized execution](/vectorized-query-execution/) passes batches of column values, and [morsel-driven parallelism](/morsel-driven-parallelism/) hands small pieces of input to worker threads.
6. **Joins of many relations.** A [multiway join](/multiway-join/) joins three or more relations at once. [Acyclic joins](/acyclic-join-evaluation/) run in time linear in input plus output by the [Yannakakis algorithm](/yannakakis-algorithm/). The [AGM bound](/agm-bound/) limits the output of any join, and [generic join](/generic-join/) and [leapfrog triejoin](/leapfrog-triejoin/) run within it. A [factorized representation](/factorized-representation/) stores a join result in less space than its tuples, and the [holistic twig join](/holistic-twig-join/) matches tree patterns in XML.
7. **Top-k queries and skylines.** A [top-k query](/top-k-query/) combines grades from sorted lists. [Fagin's algorithm](/fagin-algorithm/), the [threshold algorithm](/threshold-algorithm/) and the [no-random-access algorithm](/no-random-access-algorithm/) read only a prefix of each list, and the [rank join](/rank-join/) does the same for a join. The [BNL skyline](/bnl-skyline/) filters tuples through a window, the [sort-filter skyline](/sort-filter-skyline/) presorts so each window tuple is output at once, and the [branch-and-bound skyline](/branch-and-bound-skyline/) reads only the R-tree nodes that may hold a skyline point.
8. **Online aggregation.** [Online aggregation](/online-aggregation/) reads rows in random order and shows a running estimate with a confidence interval; the [ripple join](/ripple-join/) does this for a join.

## A first reading

Read the relational join, the nested-loop join, the block nested-loop join and the index nested-loop join, then the sort-merge join, the hash join and the Grace hash join. These seven give the cost rules the rest of the volume relies on. Then read the semijoin, group-by aggregation, the iterator model and pipelining and materialization. Of the later groups, the exchange operator is the entry into parallel plans, the multiway join and the Yannakakis algorithm into joins of many relations, and the threshold algorithm into top-k queries. The radix hash join, compilation, vectorization, morsels, the AGM bound, generic join, leapfrog triejoin and factorized representations can wait.

The data structures are the [hash table](/hash-table/), the [B+ tree](/b-plus-tree/), the [Bloom filter](/bloom-filter/), the [trie](/trie/), the [stack](/stack/), the [priority queue](/priority-queue/) and the [R-tree](/r-tree/) of Volume 3. Leapfrog triejoin uses [binary search](/binary-search/) from Volume 5 and [sorted array intersection](/sorted-array-intersection/) from Volume 2. The AGM bound is proved with [Shannon entropy](/shannon-entropy/) from Volume 24 and a [linear program](/linear-programming-problem/) from Volume 15. The skyline entries use [Pareto dominance](/pareto-dominance/) from Volume 15, online aggregation uses [random sampling](/random-sampling/) from Volume 17 and [confidence intervals](/confidence-interval/) from Volume 33, and the exchange operator uses [hash partitioning](/hash-partitioning/) from Volume 26. The iterator model runs a [query plan](/query-plan/) from the query planning chapter.
