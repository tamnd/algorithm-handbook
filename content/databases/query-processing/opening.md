A query plan is a tree of operators, and this chapter is about the operators. Each one takes relations in and gives a relation out: a join, a semijoin, a grouping, a top-k selection, a skyline. The planner of the next chapter chooses which operators to use and in which order; the entries here say how each operator works, what it costs, and when it is the right choice.

Most costs in the chapter are counted in page reads and writes, as in the [external memory model](/external-memory-model/), with $M$ and $N$ pages for the two inputs and $B$ pages of memory. The textbook rules are simple: nested loops that rescan the inner relation for each outer tuple cost $M + pMN$ page reads with $p$ tuples per page, a join with enough memory about $3(M + N)$, and a join whose smaller input fits in memory $M + N$. The later entries count tuples instead, because there the danger is not the reading of the inputs but the size of what is built from them.

## What the chapter covers

1. **The join and nested loops.** The [relational join](/relational-join/) needs at least $M + N$ reads. The [nested-loop join](/nested-loop-join/) works for any condition. The [block nested-loop join](/block-nested-loop-join/) scans the inner relation once per block of $B - 2$ outer pages, and the [index nested-loop join](/index-nested-loop-join/) replaces the scan by index probes.
2. **Sort and hash joins.** The [sort-merge join](/sort-merge-join/) sorts both inputs and merges them. The [hash join](/hash-join/) builds a table on the smaller input; the [Grace hash join](/grace-hash-join/) first splits both inputs into partitions that fit, and the [hybrid hash join](/hybrid-hash-join/) keeps one partition in memory.
3. **Semijoins.** The [semijoin](/semijoin/) $R \ltimes S$ keeps the tuples of $R$ with a partner in $S$. The [Bloom join](/bloom-join/) sends a Bloom filter of the keys instead of the keys.
4. **Aggregation and the iterator model.** [Group-by aggregation](/group-by-aggregation/) is the problem; [hash aggregation](/hash-aggregation/) solves it with one partial state per group. The [iterator model](/iterator-model/) runs a plan through open, next and close.
5. **Joins of many relations.** A [multiway join](/multiway-join/) joins three or more relations at once. [Acyclic joins](/acyclic-join-evaluation/) run in time linear in input plus output by the [Yannakakis algorithm](/yannakakis-algorithm/). The [AGM bound](/agm-bound/) limits the output of any join, and [generic join](/generic-join/) and [leapfrog triejoin](/leapfrog-triejoin/) run within it.
6. **Top-k queries.** A [top-k query](/top-k-query/) combines grades from sorted lists. [Fagin's algorithm](/fagin-algorithm/), the [threshold algorithm](/threshold-algorithm/) and the [no-random-access algorithm](/no-random-access-algorithm/) read only a prefix of each list.
7. **Skylines.** The [BNL skyline](/bnl-skyline/) algorithm filters tuples through a window; the [sort-filter skyline](/sort-filter-skyline/) presorts by a monotone score so that each window tuple can be output at once.

## A first reading

Read the relational join, the nested-loop join, the block nested-loop join and the index nested-loop join, then the sort-merge join, the hash join and the Grace hash join. These seven give the cost rules that the rest of the chapter, and the query planning chapter, rely on. Then read the semijoin, group-by aggregation and the iterator model. Of the later groups, the multiway join and the Yannakakis algorithm are the best entry into joins of many relations, and the threshold algorithm into top-k queries. The AGM bound, generic join and leapfrog triejoin are research topics and can wait.

## Before you start

You need the [relational algebra](/relational-algebra/) of chapter 40.1 and, for the many-relation joins, the [hypergraph](/hypergraph/) of a query. The data structures are the [hash table](/hash-table/), the [B+ tree](/b-plus-tree/), the [Bloom filter](/bloom-filter/), the [trie](/trie/) and the [priority queue](/priority-queue/) of Volume 3. The sort-merge join uses [external merge sort](/external-merge-sort/) and the top-k entries [top-k selection](/top-k-selection/), both from Volume 4. Leapfrog triejoin uses [binary search](/binary-search/) from Volume 5 and [sorted array intersection](/sorted-array-intersection/) from Volume 2. The AGM bound is proved with [Shannon entropy](/shannon-entropy/) from Volume 24 and a [linear program](/linear-programming-problem/) from Volume 15. The skyline entries use [Pareto dominance](/pareto-dominance/) from Volume 15. The iterator model runs a [query plan](/query-plan/) from the query planning chapter.
