## History

The join methods of the first two groups come from System R and the database machines of the early 1980s. Selinger and her coauthors (1979) costed nested loops and merging scans in the System R optimizer. Kitsuregawa, Tanaka and Moto-Oka (1983) partitioned both inputs in the GRACE database machine, and DeWitt, Katz, Olken, Shapiro, Stonebraker and Wood (1984) found the hybrid hash join best over a wide range of memory sizes. When memory became large and caches the bottleneck, Shatdal, Kant and Naughton (1994) partitioned joins to fit the cache, and Boncz, Manegold and Kersten (1999) added the multi-pass radix cluster. Bitton and DeWitt (1983) combined duplicates inside the runs of a sort, the idea behind sort-based aggregation.

Semijoins came from distributed databases: Bernstein and Chiu (1981) used them to reduce relations before a join and showed that tree queries have full reducers. Bloom's filter of 1970 gave the cheaper, inexact form that Mackert and Lohman measured in the R* system in 1986. Yannakakis (1981) gave the algorithm for acyclic joins, and Beeri, Fagin, Maier and Yannakakis (1983) showed that acyclicity, join trees and full reducers are the same condition. Cyclic joins had to wait: Grohe and Marx bounded join sizes by fractional edge covers in 2006, Atserias, Grohe and Marx (2008) showed the bound tight, Ngo, Porat, Ré and Rudra (2012) gave the first algorithm within it, Veldhuizen proved the same for leapfrog triejoin, and Ngo, Ré and Rudra (2013) described generic join. Olteanu and Závodný introduced factorized representations in 2012, and Bruno, Koudas and Srivastava gave TwigStack at SIGMOD 2002.

The way plans run changed with hardware. Graefe's Volcano system made open, next and close standard, and his exchange operator (1990) let the same serial operators run in parallel, in systems of the kind DeWitt and Gray surveyed in 1992. Boncz, Zukowski and Nes (2005) found that tuple-at-a-time engines use modern processors poorly and passed vectors of values in MonetDB/X100. Neumann (2011) compiled each pipeline into one loop in HyPer, and Leis, Boncz, Kemper and Neumann (2014) added morsel-driven scheduling for many cores.

Gray, Bosworth, Layman and Pirahesh proposed the CUBE operator at ICDE 1996 and Beyer and Ramakrishnan (1999) gave BUC for sparse and iceberg cubes. Hellerstein, Haas and Wang (1997) proposed online aggregation, and Haas and Hellerstein (1999) extended it to joins with the ripple join.

Fagin presented his algorithm at PODS 1996 for the Garlic middleware system, and Fagin, Lotem and Naor (2001) gave the threshold and no-random-access algorithms and proved them instance optimal; the paper received the Gödel Prize in 2014. Ilyas, Aref and Elmagarmid (2003) carried the stopping rule into joins with HRJN. Börzsönyi, Kossmann and Stocker (2001) brought the maxima of vectors, studied by Kung, Luccio and Preparata in 1975, into databases as the skyline with the BNL algorithm. Chomicki, Godfrey, Gryz and Liang (2003) added presorting with SFS, and Papadias, Tao, Fu and Seeger (2003) gave BBS on an R-tree.

## Further reading

- Raghu Ramakrishnan and Johannes Gehrke, *Database Management Systems*, third edition, McGraw-Hill (2003). Chapters 12 to 14 give the cost formulas for selections, sorting and joins used throughout the chapter.
- Goetz Graefe, Query evaluation techniques for large databases, *ACM Computing Surveys* 25(2) (1993). The standard survey of join, aggregation and sorting methods, the iterator interface and parallel execution.
- Timo Kersten, Viktor Leis, Alfons Kemper, Thomas Neumann, Andrew Pavlo and Peter Boncz, Everything you always wanted to know about compiled and vectorized queries but were afraid to ask, *Proceedings of the VLDB Endowment* 11(13) (2018). A side-by-side measurement of the two engine designs.
- Hung Q. Ngo, Christopher Ré and Atri Rudra, Skew strikes back: new developments in the theory of join algorithms, *SIGMOD Record* 42(4) (2013). A short account of the AGM bound and worst-case optimal joins.
- Ronald Fagin, Amnon Lotem and Moni Naor, Optimal aggregation algorithms for middleware, *Journal of Computer and System Sciences* 66(4) (2003). The threshold and no-random-access algorithms and instance optimality.
- Ihab F. Ilyas, George Beskales and Mohamed A. Soliman, A survey of top-k query processing techniques in relational database systems, *ACM Computing Surveys* 40(4) (2008). Top-k selection, rank joins and their place in the optimizer.
