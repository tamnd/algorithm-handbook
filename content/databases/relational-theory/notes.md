## History

Edgar F. Codd proposed the relational model in 1970, in *A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks*, with an algebra of operations on relations. In 1972 he defined the relational calculus and proved that the algebra can express every query of the calculus, the result now called Codd's theorem. Design theory began at the same time. Codd defined the second and third normal forms, and with Raymond Boyce the stronger form now called BCNF. Peter Chen's entity-relationship model of 1976 gave designers a picture to draw before choosing tables.

Dependency theory grew fast in the second half of the 1970s. William Armstrong gave his sound and complete axioms for functional dependencies in 1974. Ronald Fagin introduced multivalued dependencies and fourth normal form in 1977, and the projection-join normal form, now called 5NF, in 1979. Philip Bernstein gave the synthesis of third normal form in 1976, and Catriel Beeri and Bernstein the linear-time closure algorithm in 1979. In the same year Alfred Aho, Beeri and Jeffrey Ullman gave the tableau test for lossless joins, and David Maier, Alberto Mendelzon and Yehoshua Sagiv turned it into the chase. Cláudio Lucchesi and Sylvia Osborn showed in 1978 that deciding whether an attribute is prime is NP-complete. In the 1980s the dependencies were unified as equality-generating and tuple-generating dependencies, and inclusion dependencies showed that implication can become undecidable.

Query reasoning has its own line. Ashok Chandra and Philip Merlin showed in 1977 that containment of conjunctive queries is the existence of a homomorphism, and that each query has a unique minimal equivalent. Sagiv and Mihalis Yannakakis extended containment to unions in 1980. Moshe Vardi separated data complexity from combined complexity in 1982. Acyclic schemas were studied by Marc Graham and by Clement Yu and Meral Ozsoyoglu in 1979, and Beeri, Fagin, Maier and Yannakakis collected the equivalent conditions in 1983. Georg Gottlob, Nicola Leone and Francesco Scarcello defined hypertree width in 2002, and Martin Grohe and Dániel Marx fractional hypertree width in 2006.

Datalog joined logic programming and databases. Maarten van Emden and Robert Kowalski gave the least fixed-point semantics of Horn clauses in 1976. François Bancilhon described semi-naive evaluation in 1986, the year Bancilhon, Maier, Sagiv and Ullman introduced magic sets. Mendelzon and Peter Wood studied regular path queries over graphs in 1995. Provenance came later: Peter Buneman, Sanjeev Khanna and Wang-Chiew Tan defined why- and where-provenance in 2001, and Todd Green, Grigoris Karvounarakis and Val Tannen unified the annotations as semirings in 2007. Nilesh Dalvi and Dan Suciu proved the dichotomy for self-join-free queries on probabilistic databases in 2004, and for unions of conjunctive queries in 2012.

## Further reading

- Serge Abiteboul, Richard Hull and Victor Vianu, *Foundations of Databases* (1995). The standard text on the theory: query languages, conjunctive queries, acyclicity, dependencies, the chase and Datalog, with proofs.
- Jeffrey D. Ullman, *Principles of Database and Knowledge-Base Systems*, Volume I (1988). Dependency theory and normalization worked out in detail, with many examples of closures and decompositions.
- Hector Garcia-Molina, Jeffrey D. Ullman and Jennifer Widom, *Database Systems: The Complete Book*, second edition (2009). A textbook account of design theory and SQL, including NULLs, for readers who want the practice first.
- Evgeny Dantsin, Thomas Eiter, Georg Gottlob and Andrei Voronkov, *Complexity and Expressive Power of Logic Programming*, ACM Computing Surveys 33(3), 2001. The complexity of Datalog and its extensions in one survey.
- Dan Suciu, Dan Olteanu, Christopher Ré and Christoph Koch, *Probabilistic Databases* (2011). Tuple-independent databases, lineage, safe plans and the dichotomy.
