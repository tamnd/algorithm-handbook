1. ◦ Four relations form a chain query $R_1 - R_2 - R_3 - R_4$, with a join predicate only between neighbors. List the left-deep [join orders](/join-ordering/) that never form a cross product. How many of the $4! = 24$ orders are left out?

2. ◦ A table has 30,000 rows and no statistics, so the optimizer uses the System R default [selectivities](/selectivity/). Estimate the rows that pass `city = 'Lyon' AND age > 40 AND salary BETWEEN 10 AND 20`, assuming independence. Then estimate `city = 'Lyon' OR age > 40` with the rule for OR.

3. ◦ Two predicates on a table of 1000 rows have selectivities 0.7 and 0.6. Give the Fréchet lower and upper bounds on the number of rows that satisfy both, and the estimate under independence. Describe a table for which each bound is the true count.

4. ◦ Relation $R$ has 2000 rows and $S$ has 600. The join attribute has 150 distinct values in $R$ and 120 in $S$. Estimate the size of the equijoin $R \bowtie_{R.a = S.a} S$ with the rule $|R|\,|S| / \max(V(R, a), V(S, a))$, and name the assumption that makes this rule exact.

5. ◦ An equi-width [histogram](/histogram/) on $\mathit{age}$ over 10,000 rows has the buckets $[0, 20)$ with 1000 rows, $[20, 40)$ with 4000, $[40, 60)$ with 3000 and $[60, 80)$ with 2000. Estimate the number of rows with $30 \le \mathit{age} < 50$, assuming values are spread evenly inside each bucket. Estimate it again for the same predicate combined by AND with a predicate of selectivity $0.1$, and name the assumption you used.

6. ◦ Using the PostgreSQL rule of the [cost model](/cost-model/) entry, with `seq_page_cost` = 1, `cpu_tuple_cost` = 0.01 and `cpu_operator_cost` = 0.0025, find the estimated cost of a sequential scan of a table with 50,000 rows on 1200 pages, without and with one `WHERE` condition. Why does the selectivity of the condition not change the cost?

7. ◦ A query computes $R \mathbin{⟕} S$ on $R.a = S.a$, a left outer join that keeps every row of $R$. For each filter in the `WHERE` clause, say whether [predicate pushdown](/predicate-pushdown/) may move it below the join, and to which side: (a) $R.b = 5$; (b) $S.c = 7$; (c) $S.c \text{ IS NULL}$.

8. ◦ The q-error of an estimate $\hat{c}$ of a true count $c$ is $\max(\hat{c}/c, c/\hat{c})$. Find the q-errors of the estimates 25, 1000 and 300 for the true counts 100, 200 and 300. Why does [cardinality estimation](/cardinality-estimation/) use this ratio and not the difference?

9. ◦ A table of 2000 flights has 200 rows with `city = 'Denver'` and 180 rows with `airport = 'DEN'`, and every DEN row is a Denver row. Estimate the rows that satisfy both predicates under [attribute value independence](/attribute-value-independence/), and give the true count and the ratio. What is the largest ratio of truth to estimate that any table with these two selectivities can have, and what does a [learned estimator](/learned-cardinality-estimation/) whose model equals the joint distribution of the two columns return?

10. ◦ A relation has $T = 80{,}000$ tuples on $N = 2000$ pages. It has an unclustered B+ tree on $a$ and a clustered B+ tree on $b$, each of height 3, with 300 and 500 leaf pages. With the costs of [access path selection](/access-path-selection/), find the cost of the full scan, of the index on $a$ for a predicate with $F = 1/2000$ (round the leaf pages up to a whole page), and of the index on $b$ for a predicate with $F = 1/5$. For which $F$ does the unclustered index beat the full scan?

11. ◦ A column has $n = 10{,}000$ rows. A uniform sample of $r = 100$ rows holds 30 values once, 10 values twice, 5 values four times and one value 30 times. Compute the estimate of the [guaranteed-error estimator](/guaranteed-error-estimator/), and the lower bound on the true number $D$ of distinct values that its guarantee $\hat D \le \sqrt{n/r}\, D$ gives. Is that bound useful here?

12. • A chain query joins $R$ with 1000 tuples, $S$ with 100 and $T$ with 10. The join of $R$ and $S$ has selectivity $1/100$ and that of $S$ and $T$ has selectivity $1/10$, and there is no predicate between $R$ and $T$. Compute $C_{\mathrm{out}}$, the sum of the sizes of the join results, for $(R \bowtie S) \bowtie T$, $(S \bowtie T) \bowtie R$ and $(R \times T) \bowtie S$. Which order is cheapest under $C_{\mathrm{out}}$?

13. • A cycle query joins $R_1, \ldots, R_5$ with predicates $R_i - R_{i+1}$ and $R_5 - R_1$. Count the connected sets of relations and the csg-cmp pairs that [DPccp](/dpccp/) enumerates, and compare with the 20 pairs of a chain and the 90 of a clique on 5 relations. Then find a closed form for the number of pairs of a cycle of $n$ relations.

14. • The frequencies of seven consecutive values are $(1, 1, 9, 10, 2, 2, 3)$. Find the V-optimal histogram with three buckets of consecutive values, the one that minimizes the sum over buckets of the squared deviations from the bucket mean. Give its sum of squares.

15. • A data cube has the views $ab$ with 100 rows, $a$ with 50, $b$ with 40 and $\emptyset$ with 1, where each view can be computed from any view above it in the lattice and the cost of a query is the size of the view it reads. The top view $ab$ is always stored. Run the [HRU greedy algorithm](/hru-greedy-view-selection/) of [view selection](/view-selection/) for two more views, and check its choice against all pairs.

16. • Relations $R$, $S$ and $T$ join on one attribute $A$, and $T$ is stored sorted on $A$. For $\{R, S\}$ a hash join costs 100, and a merge join costs 120 and returns its output sorted on $A$. Joining the result with $T$ by a merge costs 50 if the input is sorted on $A$; unsorted input must first be sorted at a cost of 100. Which plan does a dynamic program that keeps only the cheapest plan for each set return, and which plan is best? How do interesting orders fix this?

17. • Show that $\sigma_p(R \bowtie S) = \sigma_p(R) \bowtie S$ in bag semantics when $p$ uses only attributes of $R$, one of the [equivalences](/relational-algebra-equivalences/) that hold for bags: compare the multiplicity of each output tuple on the two sides. Then give an instance showing that replacing the filter $R.b > 5 \lor S.c > 5$ above the join by $\sigma_{R.b > 5}(R) \bowtie S$ loses rows.

18. • A chain query $R_1 - R_2 - R_3$ has $|R_1| = |R_3| = 10$, $|R_2| = 10{,}000$ and both join selectivities equal to $1/100$. Compare $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ for $(R_1 \bowtie R_2) \bowtie R_3$ and for $(R_1 \times R_3) \bowtie R_2$. What does this say about [query optimization](/query-optimization/) that never considers cross products?

19. • A chain query $A - B - C - D$ has $|A| = 50$, $|B| = 10$, $|C| = 1000$ and $|D| = 20$, with selectivities $1/10$ for $A - B$, $1/100$ for $B - C$ and $1/100$ for $C - D$. Run [greedy operator ordering](/greedy-operator-ordering/), which always joins the two subtrees with the smallest result, and give its plan and its $C_{\mathrm{out}}$. Then find the best bushy plan without cross products and the ratio of the two costs.

20. • Take `Dept(d, region, min_staff)` with rows (1, N, 3), (2, N, 1), (3, S, 1), and `Emp(e, d, salary)` with three employees in department 1 earning 50, 60 and 70, and two in department 3 earning 40 and 30. (a) Find the departments with `min_staff > (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Emp WHERE Emp.d = Dept.d)`, then the answer of the rewrite that groups `Emp` by `d` and joins the counts to `Dept`. Give a [subquery unnesting](/subquery-unnesting/) that is correct. (b) For `SELECT region, SUM(salary), COUNT(*)` over `Emp` joined with `Dept` and grouped by region, apply [eager aggregation](/eager-aggregation/) on `Emp`: how many rows enter the join, and what are the sums and counts per region?

21. • Two queries $Q_1 = A \bowtie B \bowtie C$ and $Q_2 = A \bowtie B \bowtie D$ run together. Computing $A \bowtie B$ costs 100, joining it with $C$ costs 50 and with $D$ costs 60, and the best plan for $Q_2$ alone, $A \bowtie (B \bowtie D)$, costs 140. Writing a result to disk costs 20 and reading it back costs 10. Find the cost of the best plans run separately, and of the plan of [multiple-query optimization](/multiple-query-optimization/) that materializes $A \bowtie B$ once. Why does combining the best plan of each query miss the saving?

22. • Three plans for a query have costs $5c$, $20{,}000 + c$ and $30{,}000 + 0.2c$, where $c$ is the size of an intermediate result. Find the range of $c$ on which each plan is cheapest, as [parametric query optimization](/parametric-query-optimization/) would. The optimizer estimates $c = 800$; give the validity range of its plan that [mid-query re-optimization](/mid-query-reoptimization/) would check. If the true $c$ is 20,000, how much more does the chosen plan cost than the best one?

23. • Relations $R(a, b)$ and $S(b, c)$ have 1000 and 2000 tuples. Each value of $b$ occurs at most 10 times in $R$ and at most 50 times in $S$. Prove that $|R \bowtie S| \le \min(|R| \cdot 50, |S| \cdot 10)$, as in [pessimistic cardinality estimation](/pessimistic-cardinality-estimation/), and give instances of $R$ and $S$ for which the join has exactly this many rows.

24. • Take $R(a, b)$ with rows (1, x), (2, x), (3, y), (4, z), and $S(b, c)$ with rows (x, 10), (y, 20), (y, 30), (y, 40), both with an index on $b$. For COUNT over $R \bowtie S$, list the possible values of one [wander join](/wander-join/) walk in the order $R, S$ and in the order $S, R$, with their probabilities. Check that both means equal the true count, and find the variances. About how many walks does each order need for a standard error of at most 0.5?

25. ★ In a chain query $R_1 - \cdots - R_n$, show that every connected set of relations is an interval $\{R_i, \ldots, R_j\}$. Give a dynamic program over intervals that finds the best bushy plan without cross products under $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ in $O(n^3)$ time, and relate it to [matrix chain multiplication](/matrix-chain-multiplication/). Why does this not contradict the [NP-hardness of join ordering](/join-ordering-np-hardness/)?

26. ★ A star query has a center $R_0$ and satellites $R_1, \ldots, R_m$, where $R_i$ has $n_i$ rows and its join with $R_0$ has selectivity $f_i$. Among left-deep plans that start with $R_0$, prove by an exchange argument that $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ is smallest when the satellites are joined in increasing order of $n_i f_i$, the order of the ranks of the [IKKBZ algorithm](/ikkbz-algorithm/) on a star.

27. ★ A chain of $n$ relations is estimated by multiplying $n - 1$ join selectivities, each with q-error at most $q$, while the base table sizes are exact. Prove that the estimate of the full join has q-error at most $q^{n-1}$, an instance of [error propagation](/estimation-error-propagation/),, and give an instance where this bound is reached.

28. ★ For a chain query on $n$ relations, show that the memo of the [Volcano optimizer generator](/volcano-optimizer-generator/), when cross products are not allowed, has $n(n+1)/2$ groups and $(n^3 - n)/3$ join expressions, counting $X \bowtie Y$ and $Y \bowtie X$ as two. Compare with the $2^n - 1$ groups of a clique, and say what this means for the work of [Cascades](/cascades-framework/) on a chain.

29. ★ Four relations $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ have the simple predicates $A - B$ and $B - C$ and one complex predicate $A.x + C.y = D.z$, the hyperedge $\{\{A, C\}, \{D\}\}$. List the connected sets and the csg-cmp pairs that [DPhyp](/dphyp/) uses, and prove that every plan without cross products joins $D$ last. If the hyperedge were replaced by the simple edges $A - D$ and $C - D$, how many pairs would DPccp enumerate, and what goes wrong with the plans it builds?

30. ★ An [eddy](/eddies/) runs $R \bowtie S$ with one SteM, a hash table, per relation. Each tuple is built into its own SteM when it arrives, and probes the other SteM later, when the eddy routes it; a probe returns only tuples with a smaller arrival timestamp. Prove that every result is output exactly once, for every interleaving of arrivals and routing. Then drop the timestamp test and give an interleaving in which every result comes out twice, and show that no result comes out more than twice.
