## History

Query optimization began with the first relational systems. In 1979 Patricia Selinger and her colleagues at IBM described the optimizer of System R in *Access Path Selection in a Relational Database Management System*. The paper set the pattern that most optimizers still follow: estimate the selectivity of each predicate from a few statistics or from fixed defaults, add up a cost in page reads and CPU work, and search the left-deep join orders by dynamic programming, keeping one plan for each set of relations and each interesting order. Won Kim classified nested SQL queries and their rewrites as joins in 1982, and Richard Ganski and Harry Wong found the COUNT bug in such rewrites in 1987. Weipeng Yan and Per-Åke Larson gave eager and lazy aggregation in 1995.

Better statistics came next. Robert Kooi proposed histograms for selectivity estimation in his 1980 thesis, and Gregory Piatetsky-Shapiro and Charles Connell studied equi-depth histograms in 1984. Yannis Ioannidis and Stavros Christodoulakis showed in 1991 that estimation errors grow exponentially with the number of joins. Ioannidis and Viswanath Poosala introduced V-optimal histograms in 1995, and H. V. Jagadish and colleagues gave the dynamic program that builds them in 1998. In 2000 Moses Charikar, Surajit Chaudhuri, Rajeev Motwani and Vivek Narasayya gave the guaranteed-error estimator for distinct counts, with a lower bound for every estimator that reads a sample. Guido Moerkotte, Thomas Neumann and Gabriele Steidl made the q-error the standard measure in 2009.

The search problem was studied in parallel. Toshihide Ibaraki and Tiko Kameda showed in 1984 that finding the best join order is NP-hard in general, and gave a polynomial algorithm for tree queries, which Ravi Krishnamurthy, Haran Boral and Carlo Zaniolo generalized to ASI costs in 1986. Timos Sellis posed multiple-query optimization in 1988, and Yannis Ioannidis, Raymond Ng, Kyuseok Shim and Sellis studied parametric optimization in 1992. Goetz Graefe and William McKenna turned rules and costs into a search engine with the Volcano optimizer generator in 1993, and Graefe's Cascades framework followed in 1995. Leonidas Fegaras gave greedy operator ordering in 1998. Moerkotte and Neumann gave DPccp in 2006 and DPhyp for hypergraphs in 2008.

Venky Harinarayan, Anand Rajaraman and Jeffrey Ullman gave the greedy algorithm for choosing views of a data cube in 1996. Navin Kabra and David DeWitt re-optimized queries in the middle of their run in 1998, and Volker Markl and colleagues added validity ranges with progressive optimization in 2004. Ron Avnur and Joseph Hellerstein gave eddies in 2000, and Feifei Li, Bin Wu, Ke Yi and Zhuoyue Zhao gave wander join in 2016.

The latest work questions the estimates. In 2015 Viktor Leis and his colleagues built the Join Order Benchmark on real data and measured that cardinality errors, not the cost model or the search, cause most bad plans in current systems. Walter Cai, Magdalena Balazinska and Dan Suciu answered with pessimistic bounds in 2019. Learned estimators such as MSCN of Andreas Kipf and colleagues and Naru of Zongheng Yang and colleagues appeared in 2019, and Ryan Marcus and colleagues built the learned optimizers Neo in 2019 and Bao in 2021.

## Further reading

- Patricia G. Selinger, Morton M. Astrahan, Donald D. Chamberlin, Raymond A. Lorie and Thomas G. Price, *Access Path Selection in a Relational Database Management System*, SIGMOD 1979. The source of the cost model, interesting orders and the dynamic program.
- Surajit Chaudhuri, *An Overview of Query Optimization in Relational Systems*, PODS 1998. A short survey of rewrites, statistics and search.
- Goetz Graefe, *The Cascades Framework for Query Optimization*, IEEE Data Engineering Bulletin, 1995. The top-down memo search that many commercial optimizers use.
- Guido Moerkotte and Thomas Neumann, *Analysis of Two Existing and One New Dynamic Programming Algorithm for the Generation of Optimal Bushy Join Trees without Cross Products*, VLDB 2006. DPccp and the counts of csg-cmp pairs.
- Venky Harinarayan, Anand Rajaraman and Jeffrey D. Ullman, *Implementing Data Cubes Efficiently*, SIGMOD 1996. The view lattice and the greedy selection.
- Viktor Leis, Andrey Gubichev, Atanas Mirchev, Peter Boncz, Alfons Kemper and Thomas Neumann, *How Good Are Query Optimizers, Really?*, PVLDB 9(3), 2015. Measurements of estimates, cost models and plans.
