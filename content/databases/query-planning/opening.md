A query in SQL says what answer it wants, not how to compute it. The system must choose how: in which order to join the tables, which join algorithm to use for each pair, whether to read a table in full or through an index, where to apply each filter, and whether to rewrite a subquery as a join. These choices are the plan, and for one query the cheapest and the most expensive plans can differ by several orders of magnitude. Query optimization is the part of a database system that makes the choice.

The optimizer never knows the true cost of a plan before it runs it. It estimates the size of each intermediate result from statistics, prices each plan with a cost model, and searches a space of plans that grows faster than exponentially with the number of tables. Each of the three steps has its own algorithms, and each can fail in its own way. When the estimates are bad, a last line of defence is to change the plan while it runs.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Plans, shapes and rewrites.** What a [query plan](/query-plan/) is, how the [selectivity](/selectivity/) of a predicate gives the size of a result, and how many [left-deep and bushy trees](/left-deep-and-bushy-join-trees/) there are. The rewrites: [predicate pushdown](/predicate-pushdown/), [subquery unnesting](/subquery-unnesting/) with its COUNT bug, and [eager aggregation](/eager-aggregation/), all justified by the [equivalences of relational algebra](/relational-algebra-equivalences/), under set or bag semantics.
2. **Costs and the search problems.** The [cost model](/cost-model/), from System R's page fetches to $C_{\mathrm{out}}$. The [join ordering problem](/join-ordering/), the wider [query optimization problem](/query-optimization/), and the [proofs that join ordering is NP-hard](/join-ordering-np-hardness/).
3. **Statistics and size estimates.** The [histogram](/histogram/), [cardinality estimation](/cardinality-estimation/) for joins and the [independence assumption](/attribute-value-independence/) behind it, and [how errors propagate](/estimation-error-propagation/) through a plan. Then three ways past the classic estimate: [GEE](/guaranteed-error-estimator/) for distinct counts from a sample, [learned estimators](/learned-cardinality-estimation/) and [pessimistic bounds](/pessimistic-cardinality-estimation/).
4. **Join enumeration.** [System R join enumeration](/selinger-join-enumeration/) with [interesting orders](/interesting-orders/) and [access path selection](/access-path-selection/). The exact bushy programs [DPccp](/dpccp/) and [DPhyp](/dphyp/), the [IKKBZ algorithm](/ikkbz-algorithm/) for tree queries, and [greedy operator ordering](/greedy-operator-ordering/) for queries too large for any exact method.
5. **Optimizer frameworks.** The [Volcano optimizer generator](/volcano-optimizer-generator/) and the [Cascades framework](/cascades-framework/), which search a memo of equivalent expressions top down, and [learned query optimization](/learned-query-optimization/), which learns from measured running times.
6. **Plans that adapt.** [Parametric optimization](/parametric-query-optimization/) for unknown parameters, [mid-query re-optimization](/mid-query-reoptimization/), [eddies](/eddies/) that route each tuple, and [wander join](/wander-join/) for approximate answers over joins.
7. **Many queries and stored views.** [View selection](/view-selection/), the [HRU greedy algorithm](/hru-greedy-view-selection/) on a data cube, and [multiple-query optimization](/multiple-query-optimization/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these ten entries in order: the query plan, selectivity, predicate pushdown, the cost model, the join ordering problem, cardinality estimation, the attribute value independence assumption, System R join enumeration, interesting orders and the Volcano optimizer generator. They show what is chosen, how each choice is priced, how the best order is found exactly, and why the result is still only as good as the estimates.

Read DPccp next if you build an optimizer, and the error propagation entry if you want to know why plans go wrong. The NP-hardness proofs, IKKBZ, DPhyp and Cascades can wait until the search itself interests you. The learned methods, eddies and wander join are research topics; read them after the classic ones they replace. View selection and its HRU algorithm belong to data warehouses and can wait until you have read the [materialized view](/materialized-view/) in Chapter 40.9.

## Before you start

You need [relational algebra](/relational-algebra/) from Chapter 40.1, since every plan is an algebra expression, with [SQL null semantics](/sql-null-semantics/) for the rewrites of subqueries and [functional dependencies](/functional-dependency/) for the rules on orders. From Chapter 40.3 you need the join algorithms: the [nested-loop join](/nested-loop-join/), the [index nested-loop join](/index-nested-loop-join/), the [sort-merge join](/sort-merge-join/) and the [hash join](/hash-join/), with the [semijoin](/semijoin/), [grouping and aggregation](/group-by-aggregation/) and the [iterator model](/iterator-model/) that runs a plan. The adaptive methods build on the [symmetric hash join](/symmetric-hash-join/) and [online aggregation](/online-aggregation/). The cost formulas count page reads, so the [external memory model](/external-memory-model/) of Volume 1 helps, and the [B+ tree](/b-plus-tree/) of Volume 3 is the index the access paths use.

The dynamic programs of this chapter rest on the [principle of optimality](/principle-of-optimality/) and are close to the [matrix chain multiplication](/matrix-chain-multiplication/) program of Volume 2. The hardness results use [NP-hardness](/np-hard/) and the [clique problem](/clique-problem/) of Volume 39, and DPhyp works on a [hypergraph](/hypergraph/). Estimates of distinct counts and join sizes use the [distinct elements problem](/distinct-elements-problem/), [HyperLogLog](/hyperloglog/) and the [join size estimation problem](/join-size-estimation-problem/) of Volume 19, and the view selection guarantee comes from [submodular functions](/submodular-function/).
