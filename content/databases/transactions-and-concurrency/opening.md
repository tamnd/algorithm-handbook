A database serves many users at once. Each of them runs transactions, groups of reads and writes that should act as one step: a transfer debits one account and credits another, and nobody should see the money in neither or in both. If the system ran the transactions one after another, this would be easy, but slow. It interleaves their actions instead, and the interleaving can produce results that no one-at-a-time run could give: a lost update, a read of data that is later rolled back, or two checks that each pass while their combination breaks a rule.

This chapter is about the theory that says which interleavings are correct and the schedulers that produce only those. The theory turns a schedule into a graph and asks whether the graph has a cycle. The schedulers lock items, compare timestamps, validate at commit, or keep old versions. Real systems also offer weaker isolation levels for speed, and the chapter says exactly what each of them allows.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Transactions and what correct means.** The [ACID properties](/acid-properties/), the [schedule](/transaction-schedule/) as an object, and [serializability](/serializability/), with its three nested classes from final-state, view and conflict equivalence.
2. **Schedules that survive an abort.** [Recoverable](/recoverable-schedule/), [cascadeless](/cascadeless-schedule/) and [strict](/strict-schedule/) schedules, each class strictly inside the one before.
3. **Testing a schedule.** The [conflict graph](/conflict-graph/), [conflict serializability](/conflict-serializability/) and the [serializability theorem](/serializability-theorem/), which says it holds exactly when that graph is acyclic. [View serializability](/view-serializability/) is larger, and [testing it is NP-complete](/view-serializability-np-completeness/).
4. **Locking.** The [concurrency control problem](/concurrency-control/), [two-phase locking](/two-phase-locking/) and the [theorem](/two-phase-locking-theorem/) that its schedules are conflict serializable; [strict](/strict-two-phase-locking/) and [conservative](/conservative-two-phase-locking/) two-phase locking; the [lock table](/lock-table/) that implements them; deadlock prevention by [wait-die and wound-wait](/wait-die-and-wound-wait/); [multiple granularity locking](/multiple-granularity-locking/) and the [tree protocol](/tree-locking-protocol/).
5. **Phantoms.** The [phantom problem](/phantom-problem/), its exact cure by [predicate locking](/predicate-locking/), and [key-range locking](/key-range-locking/), the form that real systems use on an index.
6. **Schedulers without locks.** [Optimistic concurrency control](/optimistic-concurrency-control/) and its multicore form [Silo](/silo-concurrency-control/), [timestamp ordering](/timestamp-ordering/) with the [Thomas write rule](/thomas-write-rule/), and [multiversion concurrency control](/multiversion-concurrency-control/) with its theory, [multiversion serializability](/multiversion-serializability/).
7. **Weak isolation.** The [isolation levels](/isolation-levels/) of SQL and the [anomalies](/transaction-anomalies/) that define them; [write skew](/write-skew/) and [snapshot isolation](/snapshot-isolation/), which allows it; [Adya's levels](/adya-isolation-levels/), defined by cycles in a dependency graph; the [dangerous structure theorem](/snapshot-isolation-dangerous-structure/); [serializable snapshot isolation](/serializable-snapshot-isolation/); and [robustness](/robustness-against-weak-isolation/), the question of when a weak level is safe for a given workload.
8. **Beyond the classical model.** [Commitment ordering](/commitment-ordering/), [transaction chopping](/transaction-chopping/), [sagas](/sagas/) and the [escrow method](/escrow-transactional-method/), which use the order of commits, the shape of known programs, compensation or commuting updates to get more concurrency than plain serializability allows.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these ten entries in order: ACID properties, serializability, strict schedules, the conflict graph, conflict serializability, two-phase locking, strict two-phase locking, isolation levels, snapshot isolation and write skew. They give the definition of correctness, the test for it, the scheduler that most systems use, and the weaker guarantee that many systems give by default, with the anomaly it lets through.

Then read timestamp ordering and multiversion concurrency control for the other main family of schedulers, key-range locking for phantoms, and serializable snapshot isolation to see how the gap left by snapshot isolation is closed. The theorems of groups 3 and 4, the NP-completeness proof, Adya's levels, robustness and the four entries of the last group can wait until the basic picture is clear.

## Before you start

The tests on schedules are graph algorithms: a schedule is conflict serializable when its graph has no cycle, found by [cycle detection with DFS](/dfs-cycle-detection/), and an equivalent serial order is a [topological sort](/topological-sort/), both from Volume 7. The NP-completeness of view serializability uses [NP-hardness](/np-hard/) from Volume 39.

Concurrency in a single program is the subject of Volume 22, and much of it carries over. Shared and exclusive locks are the [readers-writer lock](/readers-writer-lock/); deadlocks are found with a [wait-for graph](/wait-for-graph/) and [deadlock detection](/deadlock-detection/), or avoided by [deadlock prevention](/deadlock-prevention/). [Software transactional memory](/software-transactional-memory/) applies optimistic validation and multiple versions to memory instead of disk, and [TL2](/tl2/) is a close relative of optimistic concurrency control. Recovery after a crash, which the atomicity and durability of ACID need, is the subject of Chapter 40.6. Key-range locking works on the [B-tree](/b-tree/) of Volume 3, and the tree protocol generalizes the [lock coupling](/lock-coupling/) of Volume 22. Transactions across several sites, and the [one-copy serializability](/one-copy-serializability/) of replicated data, are in Chapter 40.7.
