## History

The radar equation took its form in the radar work of the Second World War, and so did the
[matched filter](/matched-filter/), which D. O. North described in a report of 1943. Marcum and Swerling
then worked out the probability of detection for steady and fluctuating targets; their studies appeared in
the IRE Transactions on Information Theory in 1960, and the four Swerling cases are still the standard
models. P. M. Woodward's book of 1953 introduced the ambiguity function and its view of a radar as a
system that extracts information, and J. Siebert showed in 1956 that its volume is fixed, which is often
called the radar uncertainty principle. R. H. Barker gave his binary codes in 1953, and R. Turyn and
J. Storer proved in 1961 that none of odd length above 13 exists.

The paper of Klauder, Price, Darlington and Albersheim in the
Bell System Technical Journal in 1960 made it the standard way to compress a pulse, with its spectrum by
stationary phase and its range-Doppler coupling. E. N. Fowle showed in 1964 how to design a nonlinear sweep
for a given spectrum. R. L. Frank published his polyphase code in 1963, and J. P. Costas proposed the
frequency hopping patterns now called Costas arrays in 1984; in the same year S. W. Golomb and H. Taylor
described the Welch and Lempel-Golomb constructions and their properties.

For detection, H. M. Finn and R. S. Johnson gave cell-averaging CFAR in 1968, and H. Rohling gave
ordered-statistic CFAR in 1983. M. H. Ackroyd and F. Ghani found the least squares mismatched filter in
1973, and S. Zoraster the minimum peak filter by linear programming in 1980. W. J. Albersheim fitted his
formula to Robertson's detection curves in a letter of 1981. L. E. Brennan and I. S. Reed gave the theory of
adaptive radar over antennas and pulses in 1973, and Reed, Mallett and Brennan the loss of the sample
matrix weight in 1974; J. Ward's 1994 report set out the clutter model of space-time adaptive processing.
The [fast Fourier transform](/fast-fourier-transform/) of Cooley and Tukey (1965) made range-Doppler
processing digital. C. Sturm and W. Wiesbeck's paper of 2011 gave the OFDM radar processing that most work
on joint sensing and communication uses.

## Further reading

- M. I. Skolnik, *Introduction to Radar Systems*, third edition, McGraw-Hill, 2001. The standard
  textbook on the whole radar system: the range equation, its losses, MTI and detection.
- M. A. Richards, *Fundamentals of Radar Signal Processing*, McGraw-Hill, 2005; second edition 2014. The
  signal processing of this chapter: waveforms, Doppler processing, detection, CFAR and an introduction to
  space-time adaptive processing.
- N. Levanon and E. Mozeson, *Radar Signals*, Wiley, 2004. Waveforms and their ambiguity functions: chirps,
  nonlinear FM, phase codes, Costas arrays and stepped frequency, with many plots.
- P. M. Woodward, *Probability and Information Theory, with Applications to Radar*, Pergamon, 1953. The
  short book that introduced the ambiguity function; still clear.
- D. R. Wehner, *High-Resolution Radar*, second edition, Artech House, 1994. Stepped frequency and other
  wideband waveforms.
- J. Ward, *Space-Time Adaptive Processing for Airborne Radar*, Technical Report 1015, MIT Lincoln
  Laboratory, 1994. The clutter ridge, its rank and the processing architectures.
