## History

In 1949 Shannon showed that the capacity of a channel with
colored noise is reached by pouring power over the spectrum like water into a vessel, Marginal analysis, the greedy rule of
adding one unit where it buys the most, was studied by Fox in 1966 for separable allocation problems with
convex costs. The two met in multicarrier modulation. Hughes-Hartogs's patent for Telebit (filed in 1985, issued in
1987) loaded bits one at a time onto the cheapest tone, and Chow, Cioffi and Bingham gave in 1995 the fast
practical loader used for discrete multitone in ADSL. Campello (1998 and 1999) and Levin (2001) then
gave the efficiency and tightness conditions that let an exact loader start from any integer
loading.

Adaptation over time on a fading channel came next. Goldsmith and Varaiya (1997) found the capacity of a
fading channel with side information at both ends, together with truncated channel inversion, and
Goldsmith and Chua (1997) turned the result into variable-rate, variable-power QAM with the SNR gap as
the link between theory and constellations. Retransmission had its own line. Lin and Yu proposed hybrid
ARQ with parity retransmission in 1982, Chase gave code combining in 1985, Hagenauer's rate-compatible
punctured codes of 1988 made incremental redundancy easy to build, and Caire and Tuninetti (2001)
analysed the throughput of both kinds. Exponential effective SNR mapping was proposed in a 3GPP contribution by Ericsson in 2003,
and Pedersen and coauthors described the outer loop for LTE in 2007.

Power control under interference began with Zander's 1992 analysis of the best common SIR, which is set by
the Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue of the gain matrix. Foschini and Miljanic (1993) gave the distributed
update that each link can run on its own measurement, and Yates (1995) found the three axioms of a
standard interference function that make every such update converge. Knopp and Humblet (1995) showed that
in the uplink the sum capacity is reached by serving the strongest user alone, and Viswanath, Tse and
Laroia (2002) showed how to create the fluctuations that this multiuser diversity needs.
Wong and coauthors (1999) and Jang and Lee (2003) set out the subcarrier, bit and power problem of
OFDMA.

The 2000s brought optimization theory into the subject. Kandukuri and Boyd and Julian and coauthors (both
2002) wrote power control problems as geometric programs, and Chiang and coauthors gave
the full treatment in 2007. Luo and Zhang (2008) proved that weighted sum rate over interfering links is
NP-hard, and Shi and coauthors (2011) gave the WMMSE method that reaches stationary points. Cadambe and
Jafar (2008) showed that, on channels that vary in time or frequency, alignment over ever longer symbol
extensions gives $K$ interfering users $K/2$ degrees of freedom in the limit. Energy efficiency, a
ratio of rate to power, falls to Dinkelbach's 1967 method. Spectrum sensing has a separate history: Urkowitz analysed the energy detector in
1967, Gardner and others built the theory of cyclostationary signals in the 1970s and
1980s, and Tandra and Sahai (2008)
proved the SNR wall that limits detectors that do not know the noise level.

## Further reading

- Andrea Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (Cambridge University Press, 2005). Adaptive modulation,
  the SNR gap, truncated inversion and fading capacity in one notation.
- David Tse and Pramod Viswanath, *Fundamentals of Wireless Communication* (Cambridge University Press,
  2005). Multiuser diversity, scheduling and hybrid ARQ from the information theory side.
- Roy D. Yates, "A framework for uplink power control in cellular radio systems", *IEEE Journal on
  Selected Areas in Communications* 13 (1995). The short paper behind every fixed-point power control
  proof.
- Mung Chiang, Chee Wei Tan, Daniel P. Palomar, Daniel O'Neill and David Julian, "Power control by
  geometric programming", *IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications* 6 (2007). Convex power control
  and its limits at low SINR.
- Alessio Zappone and Eduard Jorswieck, *Energy Efficiency in Wireless Networks via Fractional
  Programming Theory* (Now Publishers, 2015). Dinkelbach's method and its relatives applied to radio.
- Tevfik Yücek and Hüseyin Arslan, "A survey of spectrum sensing algorithms for cognitive radio
  applications", *IEEE Communications Surveys and Tutorials* 11 (2009). Energy, matched filter and
  feature detection compared.
