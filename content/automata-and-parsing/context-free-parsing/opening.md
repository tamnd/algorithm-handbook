A compiler reads `x = a + b * c;` as a string of characters. Before it can produce code, it must know that `b * c` is computed first, that its result is added to `a`, and that the sum is stored in `x`. A finite automaton cannot find this structure: expressions nest to any depth, and an automaton cannot count open parentheses. A context-free grammar can. It says, in a few rules, that an expression is a sum of terms, a term is a product of factors, and a factor is a name, a number or an expression in parentheses. Parsing is the inverse problem: given the rules and a string, find the tree of rules that produces the string.

Every programming language, data format and query language has a grammar of this kind, and every compiler, interpreter and configuration loader has a parser. The chapter covers the theory of the languages that grammars describe and the algorithms that parse them. These range from cubic-time methods that handle every grammar to linear-time methods that handle the grammars people write for programming languages. Chapter 9.4 turns the linear-time methods into tools that build parsers from grammars.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Grammars, derivations and trees.** The [context-free grammar](/context-free-grammar/), [derivations](/derivation/), [parse trees](/parse-tree/), [abstract syntax trees](/abstract-syntax-tree/) and [ambiguity](/ambiguity/).
2. **Cleaning grammars and normal forms.** [Nullable symbols](/nullable-symbol/), the removal of useless symbols and $\varepsilon$-productions, [Chomsky normal form](/chomsky-normal-form/), [left recursion](/left-recursion/) and [Greibach normal form](/greibach-normal-form/).
3. **Pushdown automata and the theory of context-free languages.** The [pushdown automaton](/pushdown-automaton/), [deterministic languages](/deterministic-context-free-language/), the [pumping lemma](/cfl-pumping-lemma/), [closure properties](/cfl-closure-properties/), and the theorems of [Parikh](/parikh-theorem/) and [Chomsky and Schützenberger](/chomsky-schutzenberger-theorem/).
4. **Decision problems.** [Emptiness](/cfg-emptiness-problem/) and [membership](/cfg-membership-problem/), which are decidable, and [ambiguity](/cfg-ambiguity-undecidable/) and [equivalence](/cfg-equivalence-undecidable/), which are not.
5. **Top-down parsing.** [Recursive descent](/recursive-descent-parsing/), [LL grammars](/ll-grammar/), the [LL parse table](/ll-parse-table/) and [LL(1) parsing](/ll1-parsing/).
6. **Shift-reduce parsing and precedence.** [Handles](/handle/), [shift-reduce parsing](/shift-reduce-parsing/), conflicts, [operator precedence](/operator-precedence-parsing/), the [shunting-yard algorithm](/shunting-yard-algorithm/) and [Pratt parsing](/pratt-parsing/).
7. **LR parsing.** [LR items](/lr-item/), [LR(0)](/lr0-parsing/), [SLR](/slr-parsing/), [LALR](/lalr-parsing/) and [canonical LR](/canonical-lr-parsing/) parsing, and [Knuth's theorem](/knuth-lr-theorem/).
8. **General parsing.** The [CYK algorithm](/cyk-algorithm/), the [Earley parser](/earley-parser/), [Valiant's algorithm](/valiant-algorithm/), [GLR parsing](/glr-parsing/) and [shared packed parse forests](/shared-packed-parse-forest/).
9. **Probabilistic grammars.** [Probabilistic grammars](/probabilistic-context-free-grammar/), [probabilistic CKY](/probabilistic-cky/) and the [inside-outside algorithm](/inside-outside-algorithm/).
10. **Meaning, errors and other formalisms.** [Syntax-directed translation](/syntax-directed-translation/), [attribute grammars](/attribute-grammar/), [error recovery](/panic-mode-error-recovery/), [parsing expression grammars](/parsing-expression-grammar/) and [packrat parsing](/packrat-parsing/).

## Before you start

You need the [regular languages](/regular-language/), the [NFA](/nondeterministic-finite-automaton/) and the [DFA](/deterministic-finite-automaton/) of Chapter 9.1; LR parsing builds a DFA over items with the [subset construction](/subset-construction/). A pushdown automaton adds a [stack](/stack/), and recursive descent uses [recursion](/recursion/). The general parsers solve [overlapping subproblems](/overlapping-subproblems/) over spans of the input, and packrat parsing uses [memoization](/memoization/), and Valiant's algorithm uses [Boolean matrix multiplication](/boolean-matrix-multiplication/). The undecidability results reduce from [Post's correspondence problem](/post-correspondence-problem/).
