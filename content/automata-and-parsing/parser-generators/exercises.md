1. ◦ For the grammar $S \to A\,B$, $A \to a\,A \mid \varepsilon$, $B \to b\,B \mid c$, find the nullable nonterminals and the sets FIRST and FOLLOW of $S$, $A$ and $B$. Is the grammar LL(1)?

2. ◦ Show that $S \to A\,b \mid c$, $A \to b\,A \mid \varepsilon$ is not LL(1) by finding a cell of the LL(1) table with two productions. Describe its language and give an LL(1) grammar for it.

3. ◦ Augment $E \to E + T \mid T$, $T \to \mathit{id}$ with $E' \to E$. How many LR(0) items does the grammar have, and how many states does its LR(0) automaton have?

4. ◦ The grammar $S \to a\,S\,b \mid c$ is an operator grammar. List all its precedence relations, including those with the end marker $\$$, and decide whether it is an operator precedence grammar.

5. ◦ A yacc file declares `%left '+'`, then `%left '*'`, then `%right '^'`, for the grammar $E \to E + E \mid E * E \mid E \wedge E \mid \mathit{num}$. What does yacc do in the state with the complete item $E \to E + E\,\cdot$ on the lookahead `*`, with $E \to E * E\,\cdot$ on `+`, and with $E \to E \wedge E\,\cdot$ on `^`? What value does the parser give to `2 ^ 3 ^ 2`?

6. ◦ An LR parser has 400 states, 80 terminals plus the end marker, and 60 nonterminals besides the added start symbol. How many cells do its ACTION and GOTO tables have, and how many bytes do they take at two bytes per cell without compression?

7. ◦ For $S \to A\,a \mid B\,b$, $A \to c$, $B \to c$, find the least $k$ for which the grammar is LL(k) and the least $k$ for which it is LR(k).

8. • Take the productions $A_1 \to A_2$, $A_2 \to A_3$, …, $A_{n-1} \to A_n$, $A_n \to a$, in this order, and compute FIRST by passes over the productions in this order until a pass changes nothing. How many passes are made? How many are made if the productions are taken in the reverse order?

9. • Prove that a grammar without useless symbols is LL(1) if and only if it is strong LL(1). Then explain in one or two sentences why the same argument fails for $k = 2$.

10. • Remove the left recursion from $E \to E - T \mid T$, $T \to \mathit{num}$, and give the PREDICT sets of the new grammar. If each nonterminal returns a value computed only from its children, what value does the new grammar give to `5 - 3 - 1`? How must the parser pass values so that it gives the right one?

11. • Find precedence functions $f$ and $g$ for the relations of exercise 4, with $f(a) < g(b)$ for $a \lessdot b$, $f(a) = g(b)$ for $a \doteq b$ and $f(a) > g(b)$ for $a \gtrdot b$, taking the smallest nonnegative values.

12. • Build the canonical LR(1) collection and the LR(0) automaton of $S \to a\,S\,b \mid c$, both augmented with $S' \to S$. How many states does each have, and which LR(1) states does LALR(1) merge?

13. • Give yacc the grammar $E \to E + E \mid E * E \mid \mathit{id}$ with no precedence declarations. How many shift-reduce conflicts does it report? With every conflict settled by shifting, how are `id + id * id` and `id * id + id` grouped?

14. • A yacc grammar has $E \to E < E \mid E + E \mid \mathit{id}$ with `%nonassoc '<'` declared before `%left '+'`. How are `a < b + c` and `a + b < c` grouped, and what happens on `a < b < c`?

15. • In a parser combinator library with list-of-successes semantics, `many(p)` is defined as `p` followed by `many(p)`, or else the empty parser, and every alternative is kept. How many results does `many(a)` followed by `many(a)` return on the input `aaa`, and how many of them consume the whole input?

16. • Prove that every LR(k) grammar is unambiguous.

17. • Consider $S \to A\,x \mid B\,y$, $A \to a\,A \mid a$, $B \to a\,B \mid a$ and the input $a^n x$. Show that the grammar is LR(1), and that replacing the last token $x$ by $y$ forces any incremental parser that outputs the new parse tree to create $\Theta(n)$ nodes. Give an LR(1) grammar for the same language for which the same edit changes $O(1)$ nodes.

18. ★ Show that $S \to (\,X \mid E\,] \mid F\,)$, $X \to E\,) \mid F\,]$, $E \to A$, $F \to A$, $A \to \varepsilon$, where $($, $)$ and $]$ are terminals, is LL(1) but not LALR(1).

19. ★ Prove that every LL(1) grammar without useless symbols is LR(1).

20. ★ Given the LR(0) automaton of a grammar $G$, build the grammar $G'$ whose nonterminals are the nonterminal transitions $[p, A]$, with a production $[p, A] \to [p_0, X_1][p_1, X_2] \cdots [p_{m-1}, X_m]$ for each $A \to X_1 \cdots X_m$, where $p_0 = p$ and $p_i = \mathrm{GOTO}(p_{i-1}, X_i)$, a terminal $a$ stands for itself, and the start symbol is $[q_0, S]$ for the start state $q_0$. Prove that DeRemer and Pennello's $\mathrm{Follow}(p, A)$ equals $\mathrm{FOLLOW}([p, A])$ in $G'$.

21. ★ Prove that every language generated by an LR(k) grammar is generated by an LR(1) grammar.
