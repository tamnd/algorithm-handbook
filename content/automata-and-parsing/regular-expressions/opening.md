A server log has a million lines, and you want those that record an error from one of a few hosts. You type a short pattern such as `ERROR.*host-(12|17|40)` and a tool prints the matching lines a moment later. The pattern is a regular expression: a few symbols joined by choice, sequence and repetition. The same notation describes the tokens of a programming language, the fields of a form, and the paths a firewall should block. It looks like a small language for searching text, and it is one, but behind it sit the finite automata of Chapter 9.1, and the cost of every regex tool depends on how it turns the pattern into an automaton and runs it.

Two kinds of engine answer the same question in very different ways. One builds an automaton from the pattern and runs all of its states at once; it takes time proportional to the pattern size times the text length, whatever the pattern. The other tries the choices of the pattern one by one and backs up when one fails; it is simple and supports more features, but some short patterns make it run for exponential time. Most of the chapter is about this split, and about the questions, such as equivalence of two patterns, that no engine answers quickly.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Expressions and what they denote.** The [Kleene star](/kleene-star/), the [regular expression](/regular-expression/) itself, the [extended syntax](/extended-regular-expressions/) of POSIX tools, and [bounded repetition](/bounded-repetition/).
2. **The basic problems.** [Emptiness](/regex-emptiness/), [matching](/regex-matching/), [search](/regex-search/), and conversion [from expressions to NFAs](/regex-to-nfa-conversion/) and [back](/dfa-to-regex-conversion/).
3. **Converting between expressions and automata.** [Thompson's construction](/thompson-construction/), the [Glushkov automaton](/glushkov-automaton/), the [exponential DFA blowup](/regex-dfa-state-blowup/), [state elimination](/state-elimination/) and [Arden's lemma](/arden-lemma/).
4. **Matching with automata.** [Thompson's NFA simulation](/thompson-nfa-simulation/) in $O(mn)$ time, [grep](/grep/), the [lazy DFA](/lazy-dfa-construction/), [bit-parallel matching](/bit-parallel-regex-matching/) and [one-pass matching](/one-pass-regex-matching/).
5. **Derivatives.** The [derivative of an expression](/regex-derivative/), the [finiteness theorem](/brzozowski-finiteness-theorem/) behind it, [derivative-based matching](/brzozowski-derivatives/) and [partial derivatives](/antimirov-derivatives/).
6. **Backtracking and what it costs.** [Backtracking matching](/backtracking-regex-matching/), [ReDoS](/redos/), [ambiguity](/regex-ambiguity-detection/), [backreferences](/backreference/) and [lookaround](/regex-lookaround/).
7. **Which match, and its parts.** [Greedy and lazy quantifiers](/greedy-and-lazy-quantifiers/), [POSIX semantics](/posix-regex-semantics/), [submatch extraction](/submatch-extraction/) and the [Pike VM](/pike-vm/).
8. **Deciding questions about expressions.** [Equivalence](/regex-equivalence/) and its [PSPACE-completeness](/regex-equivalence-pspace-completeness/), [minimization](/regex-minimization/), the [star height problem](/star-height-problem/) and the [Backurs-Indyk lower bound](/backurs-indyk-regex-hardness/).
9. **The algebra of regular expressions.** [Kleene algebra](/kleene-algebra/), [Kleene algebra with tests](/kleene-algebra-with-tests/) and [star-free expressions](/star-free-expressions/).
