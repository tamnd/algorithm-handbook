## History

Paging was studied by simulation long before it had a theory. Belady (1966) compared replacement rules for virtual storage at IBM and used the rule that evicts the page needed furthest in the future as the optimum. Belady, Nelson and Shedler (1969) found that FIFO can fault more with a larger memory, and Mattson, Gecsei, Slutz and Traiger (1970) explained why LRU and the optimal rule cannot: they are stack algorithms, and one pass gives their faults for all cache sizes. List update has a similar early history. McCabe (1965) analysed move-to-front for requests drawn from a fixed distribution, and Rivest (1976) compared it with transpose.

The worst-case view came in 1985. Sleator and Tarjan proved that move-to-front costs at most twice the optimum on every sequence and that LRU and FIFO are within $k/(k - h + 1)$ of an optimum with $h$ pages. Karlin, Manasse, Rudolph and Sleator (1988) named such bounds competitive. Fiat, Karp, Luby, McGeoch, Sleator and Young (1991) defined phases and marking algorithms, gave randomized marking with ratio $2H_k$, and proved the lower bound $H_k$ through Yao's principle. McGeoch and Sleator (1991) reached $H_k$ with the Partition algorithm, and Achlioptas, Chrobak and Noga (2000) gave the simpler Equitable and showed that the exact ratio of randomized marking is $2H_k - 1$. Irani (1991) credits the deterministic lower bound $2n/(n + 1)$ for list update to Karp and Raghavan.

Manasse, McGeoch and Sleator introduced the $k$-server problem at STOC 1988 and conjectured that ratio $k$ is possible on every metric. Fiat, Rabani and Ravid (1990) gave the first ratio that depends only on $k$. Chrobak, Karloff, Payne and Vishwanathan (1991) found double coverage for the line, and Chrobak and Larmore (1991) extended it to trees. Koutsoupias and Papadimitriou proved in 1994 that the work function algorithm is $(2k - 1)$-competitive, and Bartal and Koutsoupias (2004) proved ratio $k$ on special metrics. The randomized version of the conjecture, ratio $O(\log k)$, was refuted by Bubeck, Coester and Rabani at STOC 2023.

Pages of different costs and sizes came with the web. Young (1994) gave GreedyDual for weighted paging, Cao and Irani (1997) adapted it to web caches as GreedyDual-Size, and Young (1998, journal 2002) gave Landlord and its analysis for any sizes and costs. Practical rules developed alongside: tree PLRU in four-way processor caches of the late 1980s, and 2Q, which Johnson and Shasha presented at VLDB in 1994. Lykouris and Vassilvitskii (2018, journal 2021) added predictions to marking, and Rohatgi (2020) improved their bound.

## Further reading

- Allan Borodin and Ran El-Yaniv, *Online Computation and Competitive Analysis* (1998). The standard text; its chapters on paging, list update and the $k$-server problem cover most of this chapter with full proofs.
- Amos Fiat and Gerhard J. Woeginger (eds.), *Online Algorithms: The State of the Art*, LNCS 1442 (1998). Survey chapters on paging, list update, the $k$-server problem and weighted caching.
- Daniel D. Sleator and Robert E. Tarjan, "Amortized efficiency of list update and paging rules", *Communications of the ACM* 28(2) (1985). The paper that started competitive analysis, short and readable.
- Elias Koutsoupias, "The $k$-server problem", *Computer Science Review* 3(2) (2009). A survey of the conjecture, the work function algorithm and the special metrics.
- Shahin Kamali and Alejandro López-Ortiz, "A survey of algorithms and models for list update", in *Space-Efficient Data Structures, Streams, and Algorithms*, LNCS 8066 (2013). Covers the deterministic and randomized rules and the models of locality.
