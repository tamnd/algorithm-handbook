Skis cost 8 to buy or 1 a day to rent, and nobody knows how many days the season will last. If you rent for 7 days and buy on the eighth, a season of 3 days costs you 3, the same as for someone who knew the length in advance. A season of 8 days costs you $7 + 8 = 15$, while buying at the start would have cost 8. A season of 100 days also costs you 15, against 8. So you never pay more than $15/8$ times the best cost in hindsight. Buy on any other day and some season length makes the factor larger.

The rule is an [online algorithm](/online-algorithm/): it decides each day before it sees the next one, and it cannot take a decision back. The best cost in hindsight is the [offline optimum](/offline-optimum/), and the worst factor between the two, over all inputs, is the [competitive ratio](/competitive-ratio/). The [ski rental problem](/ski-rental-problem/) is the smallest problem where all three ideas can be seen at once. Its answer, $2 - 1/B$ for a price of $B$ days of rent, falls to at most $e/(e - 1) \approx 1.58$ when the buying day is drawn at random. The rest of the chapter asks the same question of other problems: how much does ignorance of the future cost in the worst case, and which rule keeps the cost small?

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Online and offline.** The definitions: [online](/online-algorithm/) and [offline](/offline-algorithm/) algorithms, the offline optimum, and the competitive ratio with its additive constant. [Frequency count](/frequency-count/) is a natural rule whose ratio grows with the length of the list.
2. **Randomization and adversaries.** A [randomized online algorithm](/randomized-online-algorithm/) faces an [oblivious adversary](/oblivious-adversary/), an [adaptive adversary](/adaptive-adversary/) or an [adaptive offline adversary](/adaptive-offline-adversary/). The [request-answer game](/request-answer-game/) shows that against the last one randomness gains nothing.
3. **Guessing an unknown length.** Ski rental and the [break-even strategy](/break-even-strategy/), the [take-the-stairs strategy](/take-the-stairs-strategy/), the [Bahncard problem](/bahncard-problem/), [TCP acknowledgment](/tcp-acknowledgment-problem/) and the [linear search problem](/linear-search-problem/), where doubling gives 9.
4. **Stopping and selection.** The [secretary problem](/secretary-problem/) and its [1/e rule](/one-over-e-stopping-rule/), the [prophet inequality](/prophet-inequality/) and the [online hiring problem](/online-hiring-problem/).
5. **Regret and online learning.** [Regret](/regret/) against the best fixed action, the [expert problem](/expert-problem/), [weighted majority](/weighted-majority/), [multiplicative weights](/multiplicative-weights-update/), [follow the leader](/follow-the-leader/) and [online gradient descent](/online-gradient-descent/).
6. **Metrical task systems.** The general model of [metrical task systems](/metrical-task-systems/), with ratio $2n - 1$, the [work function](/work-function/), [service systems](/metrical-service-systems/) and [page migration](/page-migration-problem/).
7. **Matching and packing.** [Online bipartite matching](/online-bipartite-matching/), where [greedy](/greedy-online-matching/) gets $1/2$ and [Ranking](/ranking-algorithm/) gets $1 - 1/e$, then [Balance](/balance-algorithm/), [adwords](/online-adwords-problem/) and [online knapsack](/online-knapsack-problem/).
8. **Covering and network design.** The [online primal-dual method](/online-primal-dual/), [set cover](/online-set-cover-problem/), [vertex cover](/online-vertex-cover-problem/), [Steiner trees](/online-steiner-tree-problem/), [spanning trees](/online-minimum-spanning-tree-problem/) and [facility location](/online-facility-location-problem/).
9. **Graphs and routing.** [Colouring](/online-graph-coloring-problem/), [independent set](/online-independent-set-problem/), [graph exploration](/online-graph-exploration-problem/), the [online travelling salesman](/online-traveling-salesman-problem/) and [dial-a-ride](/online-dial-a-ride-problem/).
10. **Predictions and advice.** [Learning-augmented algorithms](/learning-augmented-algorithms/) and the [advice model](/advice-model/).

## A first reading

Read the four entries of the first group in order: [online algorithm](/online-algorithm/), [offline algorithm](/offline-algorithm/), [offline optimum](/offline-optimum/) and [competitive ratio](/competitive-ratio/). Then read the [ski rental problem](/ski-rental-problem/) and the [break-even strategy](/break-even-strategy/), and work out by hand the worst season for each buying day with a small price. Next read [randomized online algorithm](/randomized-online-algorithm/) and [oblivious adversary](/oblivious-adversary/), which explain why a random buying day does better. Then read the [linear search problem](/linear-search-problem/), the [secretary problem](/secretary-problem/), the [expert problem](/expert-problem/) with [weighted majority](/weighted-majority/), and [online bipartite matching](/online-bipartite-matching/) with [greedy online matching](/greedy-online-matching/). That is a first unit of fourteen entries, and it is enough to start Chapter 19.1 on paging.

The adaptive adversaries and the request-answer game can wait until randomized lower bounds matter. Metrical task systems and the work function are hard going, but they are the background for the [k-server problem](/k-server-problem/). The covering group, the graph group and the last group are research-level; each entry there can be read alone once the first two groups are known.
