## History

Several problems of this chapter are older than the competitive ratio. Bellman posed the [linear search problem](/linear-search-problem/) in 1963, and Beck and Newman proved in 1970 that 9 is the best factor on the line. Lindley (1961) and Dynkin (1963) solved the [secretary problem](/secretary-problem/), and Krengel and Sucheston proved the [prophet inequality](/prophet-inequality/) in 1977. Graham's analysis of list scheduling in 1966 is, in modern terms, a competitive analysis. On the learning side, Hannan studied [regret](/regret/) in repeated games in 1957.

The general method came from data structures. Sleator and Tarjan compared move-to-front and LRU with the best offline algorithm on every sequence in 1985. Karlin, Manasse, Rudolph and Sleator gave the factor its name, the [competitive ratio](/competitive-ratio/), in their work on snoopy caching (1986, journal 1988), where the rent-or-buy argument behind the [break-even strategy](/break-even-strategy/) first appears. Borodin, Linial and Saks defined [metrical task systems](/metrical-task-systems/) in 1987 and proved the bound $2n - 1$. Ben-David, Borodin, Karp, Tardos and Wigderson sorted out the [adversaries](/adaptive-adversary/) for randomized algorithms (1990, journal 1994), and Karp, Vazirani and Vazirani gave [Ranking](/ranking-algorithm/) for matching in 1990. Imase and Waxman settled the [online Steiner tree problem](/online-steiner-tree-problem/) in 1991.

In learning, Littlestone and Warmuth gave [weighted majority](/weighted-majority/) in 1989, and Freund and Schapire's Hedge (1997) made multiplicative weights the standard tool. Zinkevich defined [online convex optimization](/online-convex-optimization/) in 2003. In the same years Alon, Awerbuch, Azar, Buchbinder and Naor gave the first good algorithm for [online set cover](/online-set-cover-problem/), and Buchbinder and Naor turned their method into the [online primal-dual](/online-primal-dual/) framework. Mehta, Saberi, Vazirani and Vazirani brought online matching to ad allocation with the [adwords problem](/online-adwords-problem/).

The newest models ask how much knowledge of the future is worth. The [advice model](/advice-model/) of Dobrev, Královič and Pardubská (2008) and of Böckenhauer and coauthors (2009) counts bits of perfect information. [Learning-augmented algorithms](/learning-augmented-algorithms/), set up by Lykouris and Vassilvitskii and by Purohit, Svitkina and Kumar in 2018, use predictions that may be wrong. Several old questions are still open: the deterministic ratio of [graph exploration](/online-graph-exploration-problem/), the best ratio of [page migration](/page-migration-problem/) on general metrics, and the [k-server conjecture](/k-server-conjecture/) of Chapter 19.1.

## Further reading

- Allan Borodin and Ran El-Yaniv, *Online Computation and Competitive Analysis* (1998). The standard textbook, with the adversary models, metrical task systems and many problems of this chapter.
- Amos Fiat and Gerhard J. Woeginger (eds.), *Online Algorithms: The State of the Art* (1998). Survey chapters on the main problems, from paging to scheduling and network design.
- Nicolò Cesa-Bianchi and Gábor Lugosi, *Prediction, Learning, and Games* (2006). The theory of experts, regret and multiplicative weights in one book.
- Elad Hazan, *Introduction to Online Convex Optimization* (2016). A short course on online gradient descent and its relatives.
- Niv Buchbinder and Joseph Naor, *The Design of Competitive Online Algorithms via a Primal-Dual Approach* (2009). The online primal-dual method, with ski rental, set cover and adwords as examples.
- Aranyak Mehta, *Online Matching and Ad Allocation* (2013). Greedy, Ranking, Balance and the adwords problem in one survey.
