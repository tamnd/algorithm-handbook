A router sees one bit per packet, 1 when the packet is dropped, and the operator wants the number of drops among the last million packets, updated after every packet. A counter of all drops since the start is not the answer, because old drops must stop counting. Keeping the last million bits works, but it is a million bits for one number. With an error of at most 1% allowed, the [DGIM algorithm](/dgim-algorithm/) keeps at most 765 buckets of 20 bits each, about 15,000 bits, and no method can do much better. The window is what makes the problem hard: items leave it without a message, and the algorithm has to forget them anyway.

This chapter is about such windows. In the [sliding window model](/sliding-window-model/) the queries ask about the last $W$ items, or the items of the last $W$ time units. Some questions have exact answers in small time once the window is stored: the maximum, a sum, any associative aggregate. Others need less space than the window, and then the answers are approximate, with bounds that are often a factor $\log W$ above those of the whole stream. A third approach drops the window altogether and gives old items a weight that decays.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **The window model.** The [sliding window model](/sliding-window-model/) and its two kinds of window, the [count-based window](/count-based-window/) and the [time-based window](/time-based-window/). The [landmark window](/landmark-window/) only grows.
2. **Windows in stream processing systems.** [Tumbling](/tumbling-window/), [hopping](/hopping-window/) and [session windows](/session-window/), the windows that systems such as Flink and Dataflow compute aggregates over.
3. **Averages and decay.** The [moving average](/moving-average/), the [exponential moving average](/exponential-moving-average/), [exponential decay](/exponential-decay/) and [forward decay](/forward-decay/).
4. **Exact answers over a stored window.** The [sliding window maximum](/sliding-window-maximum/) with a monotone deque, [sliding-window aggregation](/sliding-window-aggregation/) with the [two-stacks queue](/two-stacks-queue/) and the [DABA queue](/daba-queue/), the [sliding window median](/sliding-window-median/) and the [sliding window join](/sliding-window-join/).
5. **Counting with exponential histograms.** [Basic counting](/basic-counting/), the DGIM algorithm, the [exponential histogram](/exponential-histogram/) for sums, and the [space bound](/exponential-histogram-bound/) $\Theta(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log^2 W)$.
6. **Sampling a window.** [Sliding window sampling](/sliding-window-sampling/) with priorities and blocks, and [chain sampling](/chain-sampling/).
7. **Smooth functions.** The [smooth function](/smooth-function/) and the [smooth histogram](/smooth-histogram/), which turns an insertion-only algorithm into a window algorithm.
8. **Frequencies, distinct items and quantiles.** [Heavy hitters](/sliding-window-heavy-hitters/), the [distinct count](/sliding-window-distinct-count/), [Sliding HyperLogLog](/sliding-hyperloglog/) and [quantiles](/sliding-window-quantiles/) over a window.

## A first reading

Start with the sliding window model and the count-based and time-based windows; they fix the conventions that the rest of the chapter uses. Then read the moving average and the exponential moving average, which show the two ways to forget: subtract the item that leaves, or shrink every weight a little at each step. Next read the sliding window maximum and the two-stacks queue, which answer exactly in $O(1)$ amortized time. For the small-space side, read basic counting and the DGIM algorithm, and run DGIM by hand on a dozen bits; the bucket rules are easier to follow on paper than in prose. Finish with chain sampling. That is a first unit of ten entries.

The exponential histogram and its space bound come next, and then the smooth function and the smooth histogram, which give the general method. The DABA queue, forward decay, the join and the entries of the last group are research-level and can wait. The stream system windows of the second group are short and stand apart; read them when you need them.
