Two identical machines receive jobs one at a time, and each job must go to a machine before the next one is shown. Two jobs of size 1 arrive. If the scheduler puts them on the same machine and the list ends, its makespan is 2, while the best schedule ends at 1. If it puts them on different machines, a job of size 2 may come next. Then one machine ends at 3, while the best schedule puts the two small jobs together and ends at 2. Either way the scheduler loses a factor $\frac32$, and list scheduling, which always picks the machine of least load, loses no more. Online scheduling asks how large this loss is for each kind of machine, job and objective, and which rules keep it small.

The first such bound is older than the name. Graham proved in 1966 that list scheduling on $m$ machines is never worse than $2 - \frac1m$ times the optimum, and Schrage proved in 1968 that the rule of shortest remaining processing time gives the least total flow time on one machine. The definitions come from Chapter 19.3: an [online algorithm](/online-algorithm/), the [offline optimum](/offline-optimum/) and the [competitive ratio](/competitive-ratio/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Machines and notation.** [Graham's three-field notation](/graham-three-field-notation/) names each problem and says whether jobs come in a list or over time. [Identical](/identical-machines/), [uniform](/uniform-machines/) and [unrelated machines](/unrelated-machines/) and [restricted assignment](/restricted-assignment/) are the machine models.
2. **Jobs over time.** A [release time](/release-time/) is when a job becomes known, and a [deadline](/deadline/) is when it must end. [Preemption](/preemptive-scheduling/), [migration](/job-migration/) and [clairvoyance](/clairvoyant-scheduling/) say what the scheduler may do and what it knows.
3. **Makespan over a list.** [List scheduling is $(2 - \frac1m)$-competitive](/list-scheduling-competitive-ratio/), and that is optimal for $m \le 3$. [Online makespan minimization](/online-makespan-minimization/) gets about 1.9201 for large $m$, and the [lower bound](/online-makespan-lower-bound/) is about 1.88.
4. **Restricted assignment.** In [online restricted assignment](/online-restricted-assignment-load-balancing/), [greedy](/greedy-restricted-assignment/) is $(\lceil \log_2 m \rceil + 1)$-competitive, and the [lower bound](/online-restricted-assignment-lower-bound/) is $\Omega(\log m)$, with or without randomness.
5. **Flow time and SRPT.** [Flow time](/flow-time/) measures how long a job stays in the system. [SRPT](/srpt/) is [optimal](/srpt-optimality/) on one machine. [Online flow time minimization](/online-flow-time-minimization/) and [online total completion time](/online-total-completion-time/) cover the other models.
6. **Unknown sizes and stretch.** [Non-clairvoyant flow time minimization](/non-clairvoyant-flow-time-minimization/) studies rules that do not know the sizes. [Stretch](/stretch/) divides the flow time by the size, and [online maximum stretch](/online-max-stretch/) is open between $\Delta^{1/3}$ and $\sqrt\Delta$.
7. **Deadlines, value and machines.** [Throughput maximization](/online-throughput-maximization/) earns the value of each job that meets its deadline. [Online interval scheduling](/online-interval-scheduling/) and [online admission control](/online-admission-control/) accept or reject requests at once, and [online machine minimization](/online-machine-minimization/) meets every deadline with few machines.
8. **Speed scaling.** In [online speed scaling](/online-speed-scaling/) the energy is $\int s^\alpha\,dt$. The rules are [Average Rate](/avr/), with its [competitive ratio](/avr-competitive-ratio/), [Optimal Available](/optimal-available/) and [BKP](/bkp-speed-scaling/).

## A first reading

Start with [Graham's notation](/graham-three-field-notation/) and [identical machines](/identical-machines/), then read the proof that [list scheduling is $(2 - \frac1m)$-competitive](/list-scheduling-competitive-ratio/) and the [adversary for two and three machines](/online-makespan-lower-bound/). Run list scheduling by hand on a list of seven jobs; it shows where the factor $1 - \frac1m$ comes from. Next read [restricted assignment](/restricted-assignment/) and [greedy restricted assignment](/greedy-restricted-assignment/), the model case of a logarithmic ratio. Then turn to jobs over time: [release time](/release-time/), [flow time](/flow-time/), [SRPT](/srpt/) and [its optimality](/srpt-optimality/). End with [online speed scaling](/online-speed-scaling/) and [Optimal Available](/optimal-available/). That is a first unit of twelve entries.

The machine models with speeds, migration and clairvoyance can be read when a later entry needs them. The non-clairvoyant group, the maximum stretch, machine minimization and BKP are research-level and can wait. The admission control entry is about networks more than machines, and it reads best next to the online knapsack problem of Chapter 19.3.
