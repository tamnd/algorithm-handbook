3. The jobs go to machines 1, 2, 3, 2, 2, 3, 1, and the loads are $(10, 8, 5)$, so the makespan is 10. The optimum is 8, for example $\{6, 2\}$, $\{5, 3\}$, $\{4, 2, 1\}$. The lower bounds are $\frac{23}3$ and 6, and the upper bound is $\frac{23}3 + \frac23 \cdot 6 = \frac{35}3$. 5. SRPT completes C at 3, B at 5, E at 7, D at 11 and A at 16. The flow times of A to E are 16, 4, 1, 7 and 2, with total 30 and maximum 16. It preempts twice: A at time 1 and B at time 2. $N(t)$ is 1 on $[0, 1)$, 2 on $[1, 2)$, 3 on $[2, 3)$, 2 on $[3, 4)$, 3 on $[4, 5)$, 3 on $[5, 7)$, 2 on $[7, 11)$ and 1 on $[11, 16)$, which also sums to 30. 9. With $\mu = 32$ and $u = 5$, the price at load $k$ is $32^{k/5} - 1 = 2^k - 1$: 0, 1, 3, 7 and 15. The rule accepts the profits 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16, for 31, and then the edge is full. The optimum takes 16, 8, 5, 4 and 2, for 35. Accepting while there is room takes the first five requests, for 9. The requests are small, because $u/\log_2 \mu = 5/5 = 1$.

11. EDF runs A and B on $[0, 1)$ and then C on $[1, 4)$, past its deadline 3. A feasible schedule runs C alone on one machine and A then B on the other. On $[0, 3]$ the work 5 must fit into 3 time units, so one machine is not enough. Least laxity first starts C, whose laxity is 0, and keeps all deadlines. At speed $s$, EDF ends C at $4/s$, so it needs $s \ge \frac43$. The speed $\frac32 = 2 - \frac12$ is enough, and then C ends at $\frac83$.

13. The first round puts the four jobs on machines 1, 3, 5 and 7. The second has the jobs $\{1, 3\}$ and $\{5, 7\}$, which go to 1 and 5. The third has $\{1, 5\}$, which goes to 1, and the last has $\{1\}$. The loads are $(4, 0, 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 0)$, so greedy has makespan 4. The optimum is 1, since every job can go to the other machine of its pair and the last job to machine 1. This meets the greedy bound $\lceil \log_2 8 \rceil + 1 = 4$, so greedy's bound is tight. In the randomized proof, the hidden machine gets expected load $H_8 = \frac{761}{280} \approx 2.718$, above $\ln 8 \approx 2.079$.

15. At time 0 the only job has size 6, so the algorithm waits until 1. At time 1 the shortest job has size 3, so it waits until 2. Then it runs C on $[2, 3)$, B to 6, E to 8, D to 12 and A to 18, so $\sum C_j = 47$. SRPT has $\sum C_j = 30 + 12 = 42$, a lower bound. The best schedule without preemption waits until 1 and runs B, C, E, D, A, done at 4, 5, 7, 11 and 17, for 44. The ratio is $\frac{47}{44}$. With $S = \frac12$ the bound is $\frac{3(n + 1)}{n + 3}$. It exceeds 2 when $n \ge 4$, where it is $\frac{15}7$, and it tends to 3.

17. The unit jobs alone keep the machine busy from 1 to $k + 1$. If the long job yields to every unit job, it ends at $9 + k$, for stretch $1 + \frac{k}9$, and the unit jobs have stretch 1. If it runs first, the unit job released at 1 ends at 10, for stretch 9. Schedules between the two do no better. SRPT has 2, 4 and 11, FIFO has 9 each time, and the optimum is 2, 4 and 9. FIFO minimizes the largest flow time. If OPT has maximum stretch $S^*$, every flow time in it is at most $S^* p_{\max}$, so FIFO's maximum flow time is at most $S^* p_{\max}$, and its stretch is at most $S^* p_{\max}/p_{\min} = \Delta S^*$. For SRPT the ratio $(1 + \frac{k}9)/9$ grows without bound in $k$.

19. The offline optimum runs at $\frac75$ on $[0, 5)$, since the whole interval has the largest density. Its energy is $\frac{49}5$ for $\alpha = 2$ and $\frac{343}{25}$ for $\alpha = 3$. 21. $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}] = \frac12(1 + 2) = \frac32$. A deterministic algorithm that puts the first two jobs on different machines has makespan 1 and then 3; one that puts them together has 2 and 2. Either way the expectation is 2, so the ratio is at least $\frac2{3/2} = \frac43$. Put the second job with the first with probability $q$. On $(1, 1)$ the expected ratio is $1 + q$. On $(1, 1, 2)$ it is $q + (1 - q)\frac32$. Both equal $\frac43$ when $q = \frac13$.

22. Sort the sizes so that $p_1 \le \cdots \le p_n$. While job $i < j$ runs, job $j$ receives the same service as job $i$. So $C_j = \sum_{i < j} p_i + (n - j + 1) p_j$, and $\sum C_j = \sum_j (2n - 2j + 1) p_j$. Since $\mathrm{OPT} = \sum_j (n - j + 1) p_j$, this equals $2\,\mathrm{OPT} - \sum p_j$. The weights $n - j + 1$ decrease while the $p_j$ increase, so by Chebyshev's sum inequality $\mathrm{OPT} \le \frac{n + 1}2 \sum p_j$. Then the ratio is $2 - \sum p_j/\mathrm{OPT} \le 2 - \frac2{n + 1}$, with equality when all sizes are equal. Four unit jobs give 16 against 10, which is $\frac85$.

23. If nothing else arrives, OPT is $2^{1 - \alpha}$ as before. With the job $(1, 2, 2)$, the densest interval is $[1, 2]$, so OPT runs at 2 there and at 1 on $[0, 1)$, with energy $1 + 2^\alpha$. The algorithm's ratios are $2^{\alpha - 1}(x^\alpha + (1 - x)^\alpha)$ and $(x^\alpha + (3 - x)^\alpha)/(1 + 2^\alpha)$. For $\alpha = 2$ they meet where $18x^2 - 14x + 1 = 0$, at $x = \frac{7 + \sqrt{31}}{18} \approx 0.698$, and the bound is $\frac{116 - 4\sqrt{31}}{81} \approx 1.157$, above $\frac{10}9$. For $\alpha = 3$ it is about 1.413, above $\frac43$. Both stay far below $e^{\alpha - 1}/\alpha$, which is about 1.359 and 2.463.

24. With the common deadline 1, OA runs at $s = W/(1 - t)$, where $W$ is the remaining work. The balance $W' = (1 - t)^{-1/\alpha} - s$ then becomes $s'(1 - t) = (1 - t)^{-1/\alpha}$, which the given speed satisfies with $s(0) = 0$. Substitute $u = 1 - t$ and $L = \ln(1/\varepsilon)$. For $\alpha = 2$ the energy is $4(L - 3 + 4\sqrt\varepsilon - \varepsilon)$, and for $\alpha = 3$ it is $27(L - 5.5 + 9\varepsilon^{1/3} - 4.5\varepsilon^{2/3} + \varepsilon)$. The optimum can run each piece of work as it arrives, at speed $(1 - t)^{-1/\alpha}$, with energy $L$. With $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$, $L \approx 13.82$, the energies are about 43.28 and 226.94, and the ratios are at least 3.13 and 16.43. As $\varepsilon \to 0$ the bounds tend to $\alpha^\alpha$, which is 4 and 27.

25. Until the job ends, the largest window in the BKP rule is the one that ends at the deadline, and it gives speed $w/(d - t)$. Then $\int_0^T \frac{w}{d - t}\,dt = w$ gives $\ln\frac{d}{d - T} = 1$, so $T = (1 - \frac1e)d$. The speed there is $ew/d$, while the optimum runs at $w/d$. The energy is $\int_0^T (\frac{w}{d - t})^\alpha dt = \frac{w^\alpha d^{1 - \alpha}(e^{\alpha - 1} - 1)}{\alpha - 1}$, and the optimum uses $w^\alpha d^{1 - \alpha}$. The ratio is $e - 1 \approx 1.718$ for $\alpha = 2$ and $\frac{e^2 - 1}2 \approx 3.195$ for $\alpha = 3$. AVR and OA both run at $w/d$ and are optimal here.
