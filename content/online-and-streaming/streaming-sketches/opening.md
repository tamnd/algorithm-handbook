A web server writes one billion log lines a day, from about 300 million distinct visitors. To count the visitors exactly, a program must remember every visitor it has seen, which at 8 bytes an address is about 2.4 GB. [HyperLogLog](/hyperloglog/) with 2048 registers of 5 bits, 1280 bytes in all, reads the log once and gives the count with a typical error of about 2.3%. A [Misra-Gries summary](/misra-gries-summary/) with 99 counters reads the same log and returns a list that contains every visitor with more than 1% of the lines. Neither can answer exactly, and neither can be made exact in small memory: an exact distinct count needs memory linear in the number of possible visitors, whatever the algorithm.

That trade, a small error for a huge saving in memory, is the subject of this chapter. A [streaming algorithm](/streaming-model/) reads its input once, in order, and keeps a summary of polylogarithmic size. The input is described by its [frequency vector](/frequency-vector/), the count of each item, and each problem asks for one function of that vector: the largest entries, the number of nonzero entries, a sum of powers, or the ranks of the items in sorted order. Answers hold with an [(ε, δ) guarantee](/epsilon-delta-guarantee/), and the [median trick](/median-trick/) turns a weak guarantee into a strong one at a cost of $O(\log(1/\delta))$ copies.

Several structures of this chapter appear elsewhere in the book. Volume 3 treats the [Bloom filter](/bloom-filter/), the [count-min error bound](/count-min-error-bound/) and the [Flajolet-Martin sketch](/flajolet-martin-sketch/) as probabilistic structures with their error bounds. Volume 17 supplies the tools: a [universal hash family](/universal-hash-family/), [pairwise](/pairwise-independence/) and [k-wise independence](/k-wise-independence/), and [reservoir sampling](/reservoir-sampling/). Here the same ideas are treated as streaming algorithms: what problem each one solves, in which stream model, with how much memory, and why no algorithm can do much better.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **The streaming model.** The [cash register model](/cash-register-model/) allows only insertions, and the [turnstile model](/turnstile-model/) also allows deletions. The [(ε, δ) guarantee](/epsilon-delta-guarantee/) and the [median trick](/median-trick/) set the terms of every result, and a [linear sketch](/linear-sketch/) is the form that handles deletions.
2. **Problems on a stream.** The [majority](/majority-element-problem/), [approximate counting](/approximate-counting-problem/), [frequent items](/frequent-items-problem/), [distinct elements](/distinct-elements-problem/), [frequency moments](/frequency-moment-problem/), [join size](/join-size-estimation-problem/) and [quantiles](/quantile-summary-problem/), each with its exact and approximate bounds.
3. **Counter summaries.** The [Boyer-Moore vote](/boyer-moore-majority/), the [Morris counter](/morris-counter/), [Misra-Gries](/misra-gries-summary/), [Space-Saving](/space-saving/), [lossy counting](/lossy-counting/), [sticky sampling](/sticky-sampling/) and [sample and hold](/sample-and-hold/), and [mergeable summaries](/mergeable-summaries/) to combine them.
4. **Linear sketches of frequencies.** [Dyadic intervals](/dyadic-intervals/), the [count-min sketch](/count-min-sketch/), the [count sketch](/count-sketch/), the [AMS sketch](/ams-sketch/), [p-stable laws](/p-stable-distribution/) and the [Indyk sketch](/indyk-p-stable-sketch/) for $\ell_p$ norms, and [entropy estimation](/entropy-estimation-problem/).
5. **Counting distinct items.** From [Flajolet-Martin](/flajolet-martin-algorithm/), [linear counting](/linear-counting/) and the [KMV sketch](/kmv-sketch/) through [PCSA](/pcsa/) and [LogLog](/loglog-counting/) to HyperLogLog and [HyperLogLog++](/hyperloglog-plus-plus/).
6. **Quantile summaries.** The [Munro-Paterson algorithm](/munro-paterson-algorithm/), [Greenwald-Khanna](/greenwald-khanna-summary/), the [q-digest](/q-digest/), the [KLL sketch](/kll-sketch/) and the [t-digest](/t-digest/).
7. **Sampling under deletions and graph streams.** The [L0 sampling problem](/l0-sampling-problem/) and the [L0 sampler](/l0-sampler/), [connectivity](/stream-connectivity-problem/) and the [AGM sketch](/agm-graph-sketch/), [matching](/streaming-maximal-matching-problem/) with the [greedy algorithm](/greedy-streaming-matching/), and [triangle counting](/streaming-triangle-counting-problem/) by [neighbourhood sampling](/streaming-triangle-counting/).
8. **Lower bounds.** The [INDEX bound](/index-problem-lower-bound/) for one pass and the [frequency moment lower bounds](/frequency-moment-lower-bounds/) from set disjointness.

## A first reading

Start with the [streaming model](/streaming-model/) and the [frequency vector](/frequency-vector/), then the [(ε, δ) guarantee](/epsilon-delta-guarantee/) and the [median trick](/median-trick/). Read the [Boyer-Moore vote](/boyer-moore-majority/) and [Misra-Gries](/misra-gries-summary/) together, since the second is the first with more counters. Then read the [count-min sketch](/count-min-sketch/) and the [AMS sketch](/ams-sketch/), the two basic linear sketches. For distinct counts read [Flajolet-Martin](/flajolet-martin-algorithm/) and then [HyperLogLog](/hyperloglog/). For quantiles read the [problem page](/quantile-summary-problem/) and [Greenwald-Khanna](/greenwald-khanna-summary/). Finish with the [INDEX bound](/index-problem-lower-bound/), which explains most of the lower bounds in the chapter. That is twelve entries.

The rest can wait. The graph streams of group 7, the p-stable sketches, the t-digest, HyperLogLog++ and the frequency moment lower bounds build on the first reading and can be read in any order after it.

## Before you start

The analyses use [expected values](/expected-value/), [linearity of expectation](/linearity-of-expectation/), and the inequalities of [Markov](/markov-inequality/), [Chebyshev](/chebyshev-inequality/) and [Chernoff](/chernoff-bound/). The sketches hash items with a [universal hash family](/universal-hash-family/), and several proofs need only [pairwise independence](/pairwise-independence/) or 4-wise independence. The summaries store their counters in a [hash table](/hash-table/) or a [binary heap](/binary-heap/). Some estimators pick a position of the stream by [reservoir sampling](/reservoir-sampling/).
