## History

The oldest summaries in the chapter came before the word streaming was used. Robert Morris published his [counter](/morris-counter/) for 8-bit registers in 1978, and [Munro and Paterson](/munro-paterson-algorithm/) studied selection with little memory and few passes in the same year, with the journal version in 1980. Boyer and Moore wrote up the [majority vote](/boyer-moore-majority/) in a 1981 report, and [Misra and Gries](/misra-gries-summary/) generalized it to $k - 1$ counters in 1982. Flajolet and Martin presented [probabilistic counting](/flajolet-martin-algorithm/) at FOCS in 1983, to estimate the number of distinct values in a database column, and the full paper with [stochastic averaging](/pcsa/) followed in 1985.

The theory began with Alon, Matias and Szegedy at STOC 1996. Their paper estimated the [frequency moments](/frequency-moment-problem/) with the sampling estimator and the [tug-of-war sketch](/ams-sketch/), used the median of means, and proved the first space [lower bounds](/frequency-moment-lower-bounds/) for streams, through communication complexity. The authors received the Gödel Prize in 2005. Over the next decade the gap between the upper and lower bounds for $F_k$ closed: Bar-Yossef, Jayram, Kumar and Sivakumar introduced information complexity in 2002, Chakrabarti, Khot and Sun proved the one-way bound in 2003, and Indyk and Woodruff gave the matching algorithm at STOC 2005. [Indyk's stable sketches](/indyk-p-stable-sketch/) of 2000 extended the tug of war to every $p \le 2$.

Around 2000 the summaries moved into networking and databases. Greenwald and Khanna gave their deterministic [quantile summary](/greenwald-khanna-summary/) in 2001, Charikar, Chen and Farach-Colton the [count sketch](/count-sketch/) in 2002, and Manku and Motwani [lossy counting](/lossy-counting/) and [sticky sampling](/sticky-sampling/) in the same year, while Estan and Varghese proposed [sample and hold](/sample-and-hold/) for traffic accounting. Cormode and Muthukrishnan presented the [count-min sketch](/count-min-sketch/) at LATIN 2004, and Metwally, Agrawal and El Abbadi gave [Space-Saving](/space-saving/) in 2005. Muthukrishnan's survey of 2005 named the [cash register](/cash-register-model/) and [turnstile](/turnstile-model/) models.

Counting distinct items followed a separate line through [LogLog](/loglog-counting/) (Durand and Flajolet 2003) to [HyperLogLog](/hyperloglog/) (Flajolet, Fusy, Gandouet and Meunier 2007) and its [engineered form](/hyperloglog-plus-plus/) at Google (Heule, Nunkesser and Hall 2013). Kane, Nelson and Woodruff found the optimal space for the [problem](/distinct-elements-problem/) in 2010. In 2012 Agarwal and five coauthors defined [mergeable summaries](/mergeable-summaries/) and showed that Misra-Gries and Space-Saving are the same summary, and Ahn, Guha and McGregor gave the [AGM sketch](/agm-graph-sketch/), which adds [L0 samplers](/l0-sampler/) over vertex sets to find a spanning forest of a graph with deletions. Karnin, Lang and Liberty closed the space question for [randomized quantiles](/kll-sketch/) in 2016, and Nelson and Yu proved in 2019 that the graph sketch is optimal.

## Further reading

- S. Muthukrishnan, *Data Streams: Algorithms and Applications* (2005). The survey that fixed the vocabulary of the field, with the stream models and many early sketches.
- Graham Cormode and Ke Yi, *Small Summaries for Big Data* (2020). A book on the summaries of this chapter as data structures, with pseudocode, merging and practical advice.
- Noga Alon, Yossi Matias and Mario Szegedy, *The Space Complexity of Approximating the Frequency Moments* (1996). The paper that started the theory, short and still readable.
- Philippe Flajolet, Éric Fusy, Olivier Gandouet and Frédéric Meunier, *HyperLogLog: the analysis of a near-optimal cardinality estimation algorithm* (2007). The analysis of the distinct counter that is now built into most databases.
- Andrew McGregor, *Graph Stream Algorithms: A Survey* (2014). An overview of connectivity, matching, triangle counting and the sketches for graphs with deletions.
