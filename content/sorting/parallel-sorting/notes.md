## History

Parallel sorting began with fixed networks of simple processors. Kenneth Batcher's sorting networks of 1968 gave depth $O(\log^2 n)$, and A. N. Habermann proved in a technical report of 1972 that odd-even transposition sort needs only $n$ rounds on a line. C. D. Thompson and H. T. Kung sorted on a $\sqrt n \times \sqrt n$ mesh in $O(\sqrt n)$ steps in 1977. In 1986 Isaac Scherson, Sandeep Sen and Adi Shamir published the simpler but slower shearsort, and Claus Schnorr and Adi Shamir gave a mesh sort of $3\sqrt n + o(\sqrt n)$ steps with a matching lower bound. Tom Leighton introduced columnsort in 1985, in a paper on tight bounds for parallel sorting.

The question of how fast sorting can be in principle was set by Leslie Valiant in 1975. He defined the parallel comparison model, which counts only comparisons, and showed that with $n$ processors merging and finding the maximum take $O(\log \log n)$ rounds. Allan Borodin and John Hopcroft proved in 1985 that merging needs this many rounds. On the PRAM, Franco Preparata sorted in time $O(\log n)$ with $n \log n$ processors in 1978, and Yossi Shiloach and Uzi Vishkin gave in 1981 a merge and a sort that count all steps, not only comparisons. Stephen Cook, Cynthia Dwork and Rüdiger Reischuk proved in 1986 that the CREW PRAM needs $\Omega(\log n)$ time even to compute the OR of $n$ bits, and Paul Beame and Johan Håstad proved the bound $\Omega(\log n / \log \log n)$ for the CRCW PRAM in 1989.

Miklós Ajtai, János Komlós and Endre Szemerédi built a sorting network of depth $O(\log n)$ in 1983, but with a huge constant. Richard Cole gave a PRAM sort in time $O(\log n)$ with $n$ processors and moderate constants at FOCS in 1986, published in 1988. It pipelines the merges of merge sort through the tree.

On real machines a different set of sorts won. W. D. Frazer and A. C. McKellar described samplesort in 1970 as a sequential sort, and the parallel form with oversampling was analysed in the 1980s, among others by John Reif and Leslie Valiant with flashsort in 1987. Bruce Wagar presented hyperquicksort in 1987. In 1991 Guy Blelloch and five coauthors compared bitonic sort, radix sort and sample sort on the Connection Machine CM-2 and found sample sort the fastest for large inputs, and Marco Zagha and Blelloch gave the radix sort with a histogram per processor that GPU sorts still use. Hanmao Shi and Jonathan Schaeffer published sorting by regular sampling in 1992.

Two later ideas shaped practice on multicores and GPUs. The textbook of Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest and Stein added a chapter on multithreaded algorithms in 2009, with the fork-join merge sort of span $\Theta(\log^3 n)$. In 2012 Saher Odeh, Oded Green and three coauthors gave merge path, which cuts a merge into equal pieces by a search along diagonals, and Green, Robert McColl and David Bader adapted it to GPUs in the same year.

## Further reading

- Joseph JaJa, *An Introduction to Parallel Algorithms* (Addison-Wesley, 1992). Chapter 4 covers merging and sorting on the PRAM, including Cole's merge sort, with full proofs.
- F. Thomson Leighton, *Introduction to Parallel Algorithms and Architectures: Arrays, Trees, Hypercubes* (Morgan Kaufmann, 1992). The standard text on sorting on linear arrays, meshes and hypercubes, with odd-even transposition sort, shearsort and the zero-one principle.
- Ananth Grama, Anshul Gupta, George Karypis and Vipin Kumar, *Introduction to Parallel Computing*, second edition (Addison-Wesley, 2003). Chapter 9 gives the message-passing sorts, including parallel quicksort on the hypercube and sample sort, with their costs.
- Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest and Clifford Stein, *Introduction to Algorithms*, fourth edition (MIT Press, 2022). Chapter 26 gives the work-span model and the parallel merge sort of this chapter.
- Guy E. Blelloch, Charles E. Leiserson, Bruce M. Maggs, C. Greg Plaxton, Stephen J. Smith and Marco Zagha, *A comparison of sorting algorithms for the Connection Machine CM-2*, Proceedings of the 3rd ACM Symposium on Parallel Algorithms and Architectures (SPAA), 1991. A careful comparison of bitonic sort, radix sort and sample sort on one machine.
