## What to remember

- **Partitions and their counts.** A partition of $n$ is a non-increasing sequence of positive parts with sum $n$; a composition drops the order condition, and there are $2^{n-1}$ of them. The partition function satisfies $\sum p(n) x^n = \prod_{k \ge 1} 1/(1 - x^k)$, Euler's pentagonal recurrence computes $p(0), \ldots, p(n)$ in $O(n^{3/2})$ additions, and $p(n) \sim e^{\pi\sqrt{2n/3}}/(4n\sqrt3)$.
- **The Ferrers diagram proves identities.** Transposing it gives the conjugate partition, so partitions into at most $k$ parts match partitions with parts at most $k$. Euler's theorem, as many partitions into distinct parts as into odd parts, has a proof by splitting even parts, and the Durfee square cuts a diagram into a square and two smaller partitions.
- **Algorithm P** lists the partitions of $n$ in reverse lexicographic order. It splits the last part larger than 1 and spreads the rest. Each step costs $O(1)$ amortized and $\Theta(n)$ at worst.
- **Standard Young tableaux** of shape $\lambda$ number $f^\lambda = n!/\prod h(c)$, the hook length formula, and $\sum_\lambda (f^\lambda)^2 = n!$. A tableau is a path in Young's lattice from the empty diagram to $\lambda$, so the tableaux are generated by removing $n$ from each corner in turn.
- **Set partitions** of $[n]$ are coded by restricted growth strings $a_1 \cdots a_n$ with $a_1 = 0$ and $a_{i} \le 1 + \max(a_1, \ldots, a_{i-1})$. There are $B_n$ of them, and those with $k$ blocks number $S(n, k)$, with $S(n,k) = k\,S(n-1,k) + S(n-1,k-1)$. The lexicographic successor runs in constant amortized time.
- **Dyck words** of $n$ pairs, $C_n = \binom{2n}{n}/(n+1)$ of them, code binary trees with $n$ nodes and ordered trees with $n+1$ nodes. The successor finds the last place where an opening bracket can become a closing one and rewrites the suffix in its smallest form.
- **Ranking and unranking** need only a table of completion counts: the rank adds, at each position, the number of words that branch off with a smaller symbol. For Dyck words the table has $\Theta(n^2)$ entries and both maps take $\Theta(n)$ lookups.
- **Labeled trees** on $[n]$ are the $n^{n-2}$ Prüfer codes, and decoding takes $\Theta(n)$ time with a pointer that only moves forward. **Unlabeled trees** need a canonical code: the largest level sequence for a rooted tree, and for a free tree the level sequence rooted at its center or centroid.
- **Graphs up to isomorphism** have no cheap canonical code. Orderly generation and canonical augmentation each keep one member of a class by a canonicity test at every node of the search tree, and neither stores the list.

## Choosing a method

- For every partition of $n$, use Algorithm P. For partitions with restricted parts, prune the same recursion; do not filter compositions, which are exponentially more numerous.
- For every set partition, step restricted growth strings in lexicographic order. For a fixed number of blocks, keep the maximum in the string and cut branches that cannot reach $k$ blocks.
- For binary or ordered trees, generate Dyck words and convert only when the caller needs a linked tree. Use ranking and unranking to split the list among workers or to draw a uniform tree.
- For labeled trees, step the Prüfer code like an odometer and decode each code in $\Theta(n)$.
- For unlabeled rooted or free trees, use the level sequence generators. They need no table of trees already seen.
- For graphs, use an existing canonical augmentation program, such as geng, rather than writing a new one.
