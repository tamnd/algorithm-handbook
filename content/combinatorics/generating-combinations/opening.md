A program that must try every choice of items needs a way to walk through the choices. A search for the best team of 5 from 20 players looks at $\binom{20}{5} = 15\,504$ teams. A poker program that deals every five-card hand meets $\binom{52}{5} = 2\,598\,960$ of them. A dynamic program over subsets, a meet-in-the-middle search such as the [Horowitz-Sahni algorithm](/horowitz-sahni-algorithm/), and a test that a property holds for all small sets each start with a loop over subsets. This chapter is about how to write that loop: in what order, at what cost per subset, and how to jump straight to the $r$-th subset.

The chapter also sets the terms for the rest of the volume. A generator *visits* each object once and is charged for the work between visits. It runs in *constant amortized time* when the total work is $O(1)$ per object, and it is *loopless* when every single step takes $O(1)$ time. Subsets are the simplest objects for which these ideas can be seen clearly, and the methods return in the chapters on partitions, trees and Gray codes.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in four groups, read in order.

1. **The problems.** [Combinatorial generation](/combinatorial-generation/) sets the rules and the measures of cost. Then come the problems: the [power set](/power-set-enumeration/), the [$k$-subsets](/k-subset-enumeration/) of an $n$-set, and the same $k$-subsets as [bit strings of fixed weight](/fixed-weight-binary-strings/).
2. **Simple generators and their cost.** A [binary counter or a recursion](/power-set-by-recursion/) for all subsets, a [take-or-skip recursion](/recursive-combination-generation/) and the [lexicographic successor](/lexicographic-combinations/) for $k$-subsets, and the notion of [constant amortized time](/constant-amortized-time-generation/) that measures them.
3. **Colex order, ranks and multisets.** [Colexicographic order](/colexicographic-order/), the [combinatorial number system](/combinatorial-number-system/) that ranks it, [ranking and unranking](/combinatorial-ranking-and-unranking/) in four common orders, and [combinations with repetition](/combinations-with-repetition/), which reduce to $k$-subsets.
4. **Loopless generation.** What [loopless generation](/loopless-generation/) asks for, and two loopless generators of combinations: [Ehrlich's loopless combination generation](/ehrlich-loopless-combinations/) in the revolving door order, and [cool-lex order](/cool-lex-order/), which rotates a short prefix of a bit string at each step.
