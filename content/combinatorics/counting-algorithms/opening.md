Counting asks how many objects of a kind there are, without listing them. There are $2^{45}$ labelled graphs on 10 vertices, $115\,975$ ways to split 10 data points into clusters, $190\,569\,292$ partitions of the number 100, and $12\,988\,816$ ways to tile a chessboard with dominoes. A formula, a short table or one determinant gives each of these numbers, while a program that lists the objects one at a time needs hours for the first and is soon out of reach as the size grows. Exact counts tell a search how large its space is, give the probabilities of a random structure, fix the size of a dynamic programming table, and check a generator: a program that lists partitions must list $p(n)$ of them.

The chapter turns a description of a class of objects into a number. The answer can be a closed formula, a recurrence filled as a table, a coefficient of a power series, a determinant, or, for the hard problems, an estimate from random samples. The cost of a count is measured as for any other algorithm, with one difference: the answers grow fast, so the size of the numbers matters. The central binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{n/2}$ has about $n$ bits and $p(n)$ has about $3.70\sqrt n$, and many entries also give the method modulo a prime, where every number fits in a word.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **The rules of counting.** The [rule of sum](/rule-of-sum/), the [rule of product](/rule-of-product/), the [counting problem](/counting-problem/) as an algorithmic task, and three ways of proof: [bijections](/bijective-proof/), [double counting](/double-counting/) and the [pigeonhole principle](/pigeonhole-principle/).
2. **Binomial coefficients.** The [binomial coefficient](/binomial-coefficient/) and its identities, its computation by [Pascal's triangle](/pascal-triangle-dp/) or by the [product formula and residues](/binomial-coefficient-computation/), its [bounds](/binomial-coefficient-bounds/), and [stars and bars](/stars-and-bars/) for distributions of identical items.
3. **Inclusion-exclusion and Möbius inversion.** The [inclusion-exclusion principle](/inclusion-exclusion-principle/) with its standard examples, the [derangements](/derangement/) and the [bounded solutions](/bounded-integer-solutions-counting/) of an equation, and [Möbius inversion](/mobius-inversion/) over divisors.
4. **Lattice paths, Catalan numbers and tableaux.** [Lattice paths](/counting-lattice-paths/), the [reflection principle](/reflection-principle/), the [Catalan numbers](/catalan-numbers/), the [ballot theorem](/ballot-theorem/) and the [cycle lemma](/cycle-lemma/), then non-crossing paths by the [Lindström-Gessel-Viennot lemma](/lindstrom-gessel-viennot-lemma/) and tableaux by the [hook length formula](/hook-length-formula/).
5. **Stirling, Bell and Eulerian numbers.** Set partitions by [Stirling numbers of the second kind](/stirling-numbers-second-kind/) and [Bell numbers](/bell-numbers/), permutations by cycles with [Stirling numbers of the first kind](/stirling-numbers-first-kind/) and by descents with [Eulerian numbers](/eulerian-numbers/).
6. **Generating functions.** [Generating functions](/generating-functions/), the table methods for [making change](/coin-change-counting/) and [subset sums](/counting-subset-sum/), and the [transfer-matrix method](/transfer-matrix-method/) for strings and tilings.
7. **Integer partitions.** The [partition function](/integer-partition-counting/), [Euler's pentagonal recurrence](/euler-pentagonal-recurrence/) and the [Hardy-Ramanujan formula](/hardy-ramanujan-asymptotics/).
8. **Labelled structures.** [Exponential generating functions](/exponential-generating-functions/), [labelled graphs](/counting-labeled-graphs/) and the [connected ones](/counting-connected-labeled-graphs/), [Cayley's formula](/cayley-formula/) and the [symbolic method](/symbolic-method/).
9. **Counting up to symmetry.** [Burnside's lemma](/burnside-lemma/), [necklaces](/necklace-counting/) and the [Pólya enumeration theorem](/polya-enumeration-theorem/).
10. **Permanents, hard counts and approximation.** The [permanent](/permanent-computation/) by [Ryser's formula](/ryser-formula/), [perfect matchings](/counting-perfect-matchings/) of planar graphs by [Kasteleyn's method](/kasteleyn-method/), the [FPRAS](/fpras/) with the [Karp-Luby algorithm](/karp-luby-algorithm/) for DNF formulas, and [lattice points in polytopes](/lattice-point-counting/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fourteen entries in order: [rule of sum](/rule-of-sum/), [rule of product](/rule-of-product/), [binomial coefficient](/binomial-coefficient/), [Pascal's triangle](/pascal-triangle-dp/), [stars and bars](/stars-and-bars/), [inclusion-exclusion](/inclusion-exclusion-principle/), [derangements](/derangement/), [lattice paths](/counting-lattice-paths/), [Catalan numbers](/catalan-numbers/), [Stirling numbers of the second kind](/stirling-numbers-second-kind/), [generating functions](/generating-functions/), [the partition function](/integer-partition-counting/), [exponential generating functions](/exponential-generating-functions/) and [Burnside's lemma](/burnside-lemma/). The proof techniques of the first group can be read when an entry uses them. The tableaux at the end of the fourth group, the Hardy-Ramanujan formula and the symbolic method are advanced and can wait. In the last group, read the permanent, Ryser's formula and the FPRAS, and leave Latin squares, linear extensions and Barvinok's algorithm for a second pass.
