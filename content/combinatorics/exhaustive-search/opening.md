Eight queens can stand on a chessboard in $\binom{64}{8} = 4\,426\,165\,368$ ways, and only 92 of them leave every queen unattacked. A program that puts one queen in each row and drops a placement as soon as two queens attack finds all 92 after looking at 2,057 partial boards. Nobody knows a formula for the number of solutions on an $n \times n$ board, and every known count, such as the 14,772,512 solutions for $n = 16$, comes from a search of this kind. The same holds for the shortest Golomb rulers, for the number of polyominoes with $n$ cells, and for many puzzles: the answer is a fact about a finite set, and the only known way to get it is to look.

Exhaustive search is the method of last resort, and it is often the only exact method there is. The earlier chapters of this volume list every object of a class, and their generators are the inner loops of a search. This chapter is about the search itself: how large the space is, how fast it grows, how to cut it down, and how to list every solution, rather than only one, at a cost that depends on the number of solutions. It also gives the exact algorithms that beat brute force by a smaller base of the exponent, such as $1.38^n$ in place of $2^n$.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Search spaces and brute force.** The [search space](/search-space/) and its size, the [combinatorial explosion](/combinatorial-explosion/) that makes the size grow faster than any machine, [brute-force search](/brute-force-search/), [generate-and-test](/generate-and-test/), and [exact exponential-time algorithms](/exact-exponential-algorithms/), which measure an algorithm by the base $c$ in $O^*(c^n)$.
2. **Two classic searches.** The [n-queens problem](/n-queens-problem/), with its counts, its bitwise backtracking program and its explicit constructions, and the [Golomb ruler problem](/golomb-ruler-problem/), whose optima are known only up to 28 marks.
3. **Exact cover.** The [exact cover problem](/exact-cover/) and its use as a common form for puzzles, the [dancing links](/dancing-links/) that undo a deletion from a doubly linked list in $O(1)$ time, and Knuth's [Algorithm X](/algorithm-x/), which combines them with the rule "branch on the item with the fewest options".
4. **Listing all solutions.** The [enumeration problem](/enumeration-problem/), the cost measures of [output-sensitive enumeration](/output-sensitive-enumeration/) (total time, incremental time and delay), and [reverse search](/reverse-search/), which walks a tree of parent links with no memory of the objects already seen.
5. **Polyominoes and walks.** [Polyomino enumeration](/polyomino-enumeration/), [Redelmeier's algorithm](/redelmeier-algorithm/) for the fixed polyominoes, and [self-avoiding walk enumeration](/self-avoiding-walk-enumeration/).
6. **Independent sets and transversals.** [Maximal independent set enumeration](/maximal-independent-set-enumeration/) with the Moon-Moser bound $3^{n/3}$, the [Tsukiyama algorithm](/tsukiyama-algorithm/), [minimal transversal enumeration](/minimal-transversal-enumeration/), and the [Fredman-Khachiyan algorithm](/fredman-khachiyan-algorithm/), whose quasi-polynomial bound is the best known for that problem.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: search space, combinatorial explosion, brute-force search, generate-and-test, the n-queens problem, the exact cover problem, dancing links, Algorithm X, the enumeration problem, output-sensitive enumeration, reverse search and polyomino enumeration. They show the whole arc, from the size of the space to listing solutions with bounded delay. Redelmeier's algorithm and self-avoiding walks come next if you like counting problems. The Golomb ruler problem is short and can be read at any time. Exact exponential-time algorithms are a bridge to the branching and subset methods of Volume 2 and can wait. The last group is the hardest: read maximal independent set enumeration and the Tsukiyama algorithm on a second pass, and leave minimal transversals and the Fredman-Khachiyan algorithm, which are research topics, for last.
