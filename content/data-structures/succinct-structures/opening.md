A tree of a million nodes, stored with three 32-bit pointers per node, takes 96 million bits. Yet there are only about $4^n$ trees with $n$ ordered nodes, so about two bits per node are enough to tell them apart. The structures of this chapter close that gap. A [succinct data structure](/succinct-data-structures/) stores an object from a class of $N$ objects in $\lg N + o(\lg N)$ bits, close to the [information-theoretic minimum](/information-theoretic-bound/), and still answers queries fast, often in constant time on the [word RAM](/word-ram/). A [compressed data structure](/compressed-data-structure/) goes further and uses less space for objects that are easy to compress.

Almost everything here is built on one base: a [bit vector](/bit-vector/) with two queries. $\text{rank}_1(i)$ counts the 1s before position $i$, and $\text{select}_1(j)$ finds the position of the $j$-th 1. With an index of $o(n)$ extra bits, both take $O(1)$ time. Rank turns a position in one sequence into a position in another, and select goes back, and with these two moves a tree, a set of numbers, a string or a permutation can be packed into a few bit vectors and still be navigated. The structures are static, built once and then queried, with one dynamic exception. They are the base of compressed text indexes such as the [FM-index](/fm-index/), of succinct tries, and of large graphs and trees kept in main memory.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in five groups, read in order.

1. **Bits, space and the rank and select problem.** The bit vector, the [rank and select problem](/rank-select-problem/), and the two measures of space: succinct, against the size of the class, and compressed, against the entropy of the object.
2. **Rank and select in little extra space.** [Jacobson's rank](/jacobson-rank/) structure with superblocks, blocks and a table, the [rank/select dictionary](/rank-select-dictionary/) with constant-time select, [Pătraşcu's lower bounds](/patrascu-succinct-lower-bound/) on the extra space, and the [dynamic bit vector](/dynamic-bit-vector/).
3. **Compressed bit vectors and sparse sets.** The [fully indexable dictionary](/fully-indexable-dictionary/), the [RRR bit vector](/rrr-bitvector/) with its class and offset blocks, [Elias-Fano encoding](/elias-fano-encoding/) of sorted integers, and the [SD-array](/sd-array/) built on it.
4. **Trees in 2n bits.** The [ordinal tree](/ordinal-tree/) type, [parenthesis matching](/parenthesis-matching/), and three encodings: [LOUDS](/louds-tree/), [balanced parentheses](/balanced-parentheses-representation/) and [DFUDS](/dfuds/). The [range min-max tree](/range-min-max-tree/) answers the excess searches that the last two need.
5. **Sequences and permutations.** The [static sequence](/static-sequence/) type over a larger alphabet, the [wavelet tree](/wavelet-tree/), the [wavelet matrix](/wavelet-matrix/), and the [succinct permutation](/succinct-permutation/), which gives the inverse in little extra space.
