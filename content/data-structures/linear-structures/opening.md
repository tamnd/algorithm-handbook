A linear structure holds items in a row. Each item has a position, a first item and a last item, and a next item after each one but the last. The text in an editor, the jobs waiting for a printer, the calls in progress on a thread, the undo history of a program and the rows of a matrix are all such rows. The questions are simple: how fast can a program read the item at position $i$, add an item, remove one, or find one by its key? The answers depend on how the row is laid out in memory, and there are two basic layouts. An [array](/array/) puts the items in consecutive cells, so position $i$ is one address computation away but an insert in the middle moves every later item. A [linked list](/linked-list/) puts each item in its own node with a [pointer](/pointer/) to the next, so an insert after a held node changes one pointer but position $i$ is $i$ steps away. Almost every structure in the rest of this volume is built from these two, or from a mix of them.

The chapter also defines the abstract types that restrict where items enter and leave. A [stack](/stack/) adds and removes at one end, a [queue](/queue/) adds at one end and removes at the other, and a [deque](/deque/) does both at both ends. These restrictions are what make each operation take $O(1)$ time, and they match the order in which many algorithms need their work: last in, first out for recursion and depth-first search, first in, first out for breadth-first search and buffers.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Memory, arrays and pointers.** The [array](/array/), with $\Theta(1)$ access by index, the two layouts of a matrix in [row-major and column-major order](/row-major-order/), and the [pointer](/pointer/).
2. **Collections defined by their operations.** The [list](/list/), the [bag](/bag/), the [stack](/stack/), the [queue](/queue/), the [deque](/deque/) and their relatives, each given by its operations and the laws they obey.
3. **Arrays that grow and wrap.** The [dynamic array](/dynamic-array/), which doubles when full and so appends in $O(1)$ amortized time, the general rule of [resizing](/dynamic-array-resizing/), the [circular buffer](/circular-buffer/) and the [gap buffer](/gap-buffer/) of text editors.
4. **Linked lists.** Singly and [doubly linked lists](/doubly-linked-list/), the [sentinel node](/sentinel-node/) that removes special cases, circular, XOR and unrolled lists, and the left-child right-sibling form of a tree.
5. **Walking and rewiring lists.** [List reversal](/reverse-linked-list/) in place, and [cycle detection](/floyd-cycle-detection/) with two pointers.
6. **Stacks and expressions.** [Reverse Polish notation](/reverse-polish-notation/), its evaluation with one stack, the conversion from infix, and [balanced parentheses](/balanced-parentheses/).
7. **Stacks and queues with more to say.** The [stack with minimum](/min-stack/), the [queue with minimum](/min-queue/) built from two such stacks, and the [monotonic stack](/monotonic-stack/) and [monotonic deque](/monotonic-deque/), which answer nearest-larger and sliding-window questions in linear time.
8. **Symbol tables on lists and arrays.** The [sequential search](/sequential-search-symbol-table/) and [binary search](/binary-search-symbol-table/) symbol tables, the baselines for hashing and search trees, and the [sparse vector](/sparse-vector/).
9. **Lists that reorder themselves.** The [list access problem](/list-access-problem/) and the [move-to-front](/move-to-front-heuristic/) rule, which is 2-competitive.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: [array](/array/), [pointer](/pointer/), [stack](/stack/), [queue](/queue/), [deque](/deque/), [dynamic array](/dynamic-array/), [circular buffer](/circular-buffer/), [linked list](/linked-list/), [doubly linked list](/doubly-linked-list/), [in-place list reversal](/reverse-linked-list/), [postfix evaluation](/postfix-evaluation/) and [monotonic stack](/monotonic-stack/). They are all marked *Intro*. The variants of groups 2 and 4, such as the steque, the XOR linked list and the unrolled list, can wait, and so can the last group, which needs the idea of an online algorithm. Read the amortized analysis of the dynamic array closely: the same argument comes back for hash tables, heaps and many other structures later in the volume.
