1. ◦ A trial run of [simulated annealing](/simulated-annealing/) proposes four worse moves, with increases 2, 4, 4 and 6. Use the rule of [cooling schedule](/cooling-schedule/) to set the start temperature $T_0$ for an acceptance rate $\chi_0 = 0.8$. With what probability does the [Metropolis criterion](/metropolis-criterion/) accept the increase of 6 at $T_0$? With the geometric schedule $T_{j+1} = 0.9\,T_j$, after how many reductions is the temperature first below $0.1$?

2. ◦ The current solution has $f(x) = 20$, and five candidates have $f(y) = 18, 20, 21.5, 22, 25$. Which of them does [threshold accepting](/threshold-accepting/) with $\tau = 2$ accept? Which does the [great deluge algorithm](/great-deluge-algorithm/) accept with level $B = 21$? If $B_0 = 21$, $\rho = 0.25$ and the least value of $f$ is 15, at most how many moves can the great deluge accept in a whole run?

3. ◦ Run [late acceptance hill climbing](/late-acceptance-hill-climbing/) with $L = 2$ from a solution of cost 10, on candidates whose costs are, in order, 12, 9, 10, 11, 8, 9. At which iterations is the candidate accepted, and which of these accept a worse solution? Give the history list, the current cost and the best cost at the end. Do the same with $L = 1$.

4. ◦ One run of hill climbing from a random start reaches the global optimum with probability $p = 0.05$. How many independent runs of [random-restart hill climbing](/random-restart-hill-climbing/) are needed for success with probability at least $0.99$? How many does the bound $R \ge \ln(1/\delta)/p$ ask for? What is the expected number of runs up to the first success?

5. ◦ A population of four members has fitness 1, 2, 3 and 4, to be maximized. Give the selection probabilities of [roulette-wheel selection](/roulette-wheel-selection/), and the expected number of copies of each member in four spins. Repeat after adding 10 to every fitness. With the original fitness, what is the probability that four spins never pick the best member? Give the probabilities of binary [tournament selection](/tournament-selection/) with replacement, and say whether they change when 10 is added.

6. ◦ Standard bit [mutation](/mutation/) with rate $p = 1/n$ acts on a string of length $n = 10$. Find the probability that the child equals the parent, that exactly one bit flips, and that only bit 3 flips. Compare the first and the last with $1/e$ and $1/(en)$.

7. ◦ Take strings of length $l = 8$ and the schema $H = 1{*}{*}0{*}1{*}{*}$. Give its order and its defining length. In the bound of the [schema theorem](/schema-theorem/) with one-point crossover, $p_c = 0.7$ and $p_m = 0.01$, what factor multiplies $m(H, t)\, f(H, t)/\bar f(t)$? How large must $f(H, t)/\bar f(t)$ be for the bound to promise growth?

8. ◦ An ant of [ant colony optimization](/ant-colony-optimization/) stands at city 1. Cities 2, 3 and 4 are unvisited, at distances 2, 4 and 1, with pheromone values 1, 2 and 1 on the three edges. With $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 2$, give the probability of each next city as exact fractions. What are they with $\alpha = 0$?

9. ◦ A tour of five cities is a local minimum of 2-opt, and its edges have lengths 9, 8, 6, 5 and 4. Run [guided local search](/guided-local-search/) with $a = 0.2$, and suppose the local search never leaves this tour. Find $\lambda$, the edge that receives a penalty in each of the first five iterations, and the augmented cost $h$ of the tour after the fifth.

10. ◦ For [particle swarm optimization](/particle-swarm-optimization/) with $c_1 = c_2$, check each setting against the first-order region $|w| < 1$, $0 < c_1 + c_2 < 4(1 + w)$ and against Poli's second-order region $0 < c_1 + c_2 < 24(1 - w^2)/(7 - 5w)$: (a) $w = 0.5$, $c_1 = c_2 = 1.8$; (b) $w = 0.9$, $c_1 = c_2 = 2$; (c) $w = 1$, $c_1 = c_2 = 1$; (d) $w = -0.5$, $c_1 = c_2 = 0.5$.

11. • Take the solutions $1, \ldots, N$ with $N \ge 2$ on a path, where the neighbors of $i$ are $i - 1$ and $i + 1$ when they exist, and give them independent random values from one continuous distribution. Show that the expected number of [local minima](/local-optimum/) is $(N + 1)/3$. What fraction of the solutions is this for large $N$, and how does it compare with the fraction $1/(n + 1)$ for single flips on $\{0,1\}^n$?

12. • States $0, \ldots, 6$ lie on a path with values $V = (2, 4, 1, 4, 0, 6, 3)$, and each state's neighbors are the states next to it. Find the local minima that are not global, their depths in the sense of [simulated annealing convergence](/simulated-annealing-convergence/), and $d^*$. For the schedule $T_k = c/\ln(k + 1)$ with the smallest $c$ allowed by Hajek's theorem, at which step $k$ does the temperature first reach $0.5$? Show that a geometric schedule $T_k = T_0 \alpha^k$ with $0 < \alpha < 1$ fails the condition of the theorem on this path.

13. • Randomized local search on OneMax flips one bit chosen uniformly at random and keeps the result if the number of ones does not fall. Show that from a string with $z$ zeros the expected number of iterations to reach all ones is exactly $n H_z$, where $H_z = 1 + 1/2 + \cdots + 1/z$. Evaluate it for $n = 10$ from all zeros, and compare with the bound $e n H_n$ of the $(1+1)$ EA in [genetic algorithm](/genetic-algorithm/).

14. • Two parents differ in $d \ge 1$ positions, and uniform [crossover](/crossover/) takes each gene from either parent with probability $1/2$. Show that the child equals one of the parents with probability $2^{1-d}$. For $d = 4$, find the probability that the child is at Hamming distance exactly 2 from both parents.

15. • On three bits, let $u(x)$ be the number of ones and let $f$ be 4, 2, 0 and 5 for $u = 0, 1, 2, 3$, to be maximized. Find the average of $f$ over the schemata $1{*}{*}$, $0{*}{*}$, $11{*}$, $00{*}$, $10{*}$ and $01{*}$. In each competition, $1{*}{*}$ against $0{*}{*}$ and $11{*}$ against $00{*}$, which schema has the larger average? What does this say about the [building block hypothesis](/building-block-hypothesis/) on this function, and which string is a local maximum for single flips besides the optimum?

16. • Let $X = \{1, \ldots, N\}$ with $3 \le N \le 10$ and $Y = \{0, 1, \ldots, 9\}$. Which of these sets of functions $X \to Y$ are closed under permutation, so that the [no free lunch theorem](/no-free-lunch-theorem/) applies to them: (a) the functions with exactly one global minimum; (b) the functions whose minimum is at $x = 1$; (c) the functions with at most two distinct values; (d) the functions that strictly decrease and then strictly increase along $1, \ldots, N$; (e) the functions $g \circ \sigma$ for one fixed $g$ and all bijections $\sigma$ of $X$?

17. • Let $g$ be strictly increasing. Which of these make exactly the same random choices on $g \circ f$ as on $f$, given the same random numbers: roulette-wheel selection, tournament selection, the $(1+1)$-ES with the 1/5 rule of [evolution strategies](/evolution-strategies/), and simulated annealing with a fixed schedule? For each that does not, give a small example with $g(v) = v^2$ on positive values.

18. • Let $x = 0110100$ and $y = 1100111$. Find their Hamming distance $h$. How many shortest paths of single flips lead from $x$ to $y$, and how many distinct strings lie on them, the ends included? Prove both counts for general $h$. How many candidates does the greedy walk of [path relinking](/path-relinking/) evaluate, and how many solutions strictly between $x$ and $y$ does it visit?

19. • The [cross-entropy method](/cross-entropy-method/) maximizes the number of ones on four bits with independent Bernoulli sampling. Start from $v = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$, draw the six samples 1111, 1011, 0110, 0001, 1100 and 0000, keep the $\lceil m/3 \rceil$ best, and use smoothing $\alpha = 0.7$. Give the new $v$. What happens with $\alpha = 1$ to a coordinate whose elite samples are all 1, and why can this not happen in finitely many steps with $\alpha < 1$ from a value below 1?

20. • One run of hill climbing takes 1 second and succeeds with probability $0.02$. One run of simulated annealing takes 30 seconds and succeeds with probability $0.5$. Runs are independent. Compare the two by the expected time until the first success, and by the probability of at least one success in 300 seconds. Explain why the two criteria disagree.

21. • A generation draws $N$ parents by tournaments of size $k$. Show that if each tournament draws $k$ distinct members, the best member gets $k$ copies on average. Compare with the expected number with replacement, $N(1 - (1 - 1/N)^k)$, for $N = 10$ and $k = 2, 3$.

22. ★ A proposal picks a uniform random neighbor, $R(x, y) = 1/|N(x)|$, and the neighborhood is symmetric but the sizes $|N(x)|$ differ. Show that with the Metropolis criterion the stationary distribution is proportional to $|N(x)|\, e^{-f(x)/T}$, not to $e^{-f(x)/T}$. Work it out for the path $a - b - c$ with $f$ constant. Which acceptance rule, in the form of [Metropolis-Hastings](/metropolis-hastings/), restores the Boltzmann distribution?

23. ★ Let $S^*$ be the set of global minima of $f$ on a finite set $S$, and let $\delta > 0$ be the gap between $f^*$ and the next value of $f$. Show that the Boltzmann distribution at temperature $T$ gives $S^*$ probability at least $1/(1 + (|S|/|S^*|)\, e^{-\delta/T})$. For $|S| = 2^{20}$, one global minimum and $\delta = 1$, how low must $T$ be for this bound to reach $0.99$?

24. ★ A point $x$ of $\{0,1\}^n$ is such that every better point lies at Hamming distance at least $m$, and some better point lies at distance exactly $m$. Show that the elitist $(1+1)$ EA with mutation rate $1/n$ leaves $x$ in expected at most $e n^m$ iterations. Show that the rate $p = m/n$ maximizes the probability of producing one given point at distance $m$, and find the limit, as $n \to \infty$, of the ratio of the expected waiting times $1/(p^m (1 - p)^{n - m})$ for that point with $p = 1/n$ and with $p = m/n$. Evaluate it for $m = 2$ and $m = 3$.

25. ★ A randomized search, run alone, stops with the optimum after a random time $T$. A restart rule stops each run at the cutoff $t_0$ if it has not finished and starts an independent new one. Let $q = \Pr[T \le t_0] > 0$. Show that the expected total time is $\mathrm{E}[\min(T, t_0)]/q$. For $\Pr[T = 1] = 0.1$ and $\Pr[T = 100] = 0.9$, compare the expected time with no restarts and with $t_0 = 1$.

26. ★ Call $f : \{1, \ldots, N\} \to \mathbb{R}$ unimodal if its values are distinct and it strictly decreases and then strictly increases (either part may be empty). Give a deterministic algorithm that finds the minimizer of every unimodal $f$ with $O(\log N)$ evaluations. Every non-repeating algorithm needs $(N + 1)/2$ evaluations on average to evaluate the minimizer when all orderings of $N$ distinct values count equally. Explain why this does not contradict the no free lunch theorem, and check the reason directly for $N = 3$.
