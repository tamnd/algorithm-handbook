Many optimization problems are too hard to solve exactly and too irregular for calculus. A school needs a timetable with no clashes and few free periods. A delivery firm needs short routes for a fleet of vans. An engineer needs the shape of a part that a simulation scores but cannot differentiate. For problems like these, the practical method is to start from some solution, change it a little, keep the change if it helps, and add a rule that stops the search from getting stuck. A *metaheuristic* is such a rule: a general strategy that guides a problem-specific search, and that works the same way on timetables, routes and shapes.

The chapter covers the main strategies of this kind. None of them can prove that its answer is optimal, and most have no useful bound on their running time. What they have instead are exact statements about what they can and cannot do, and the entries state these with care: when simulated annealing converges, why a tabu list stops short cycles, what the schema theorem really says, and why no method beats every other on average.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Search spaces and their optima.** A [neighborhood structure](/neighborhood-structure/) says which solutions are close. It defines a [local optimum](/local-optimum/), a solution with no better neighbor, and the [global optimization](/global-optimization/) problem asks for the best of all. [Black-box optimization](/black-box-optimization/) is the setting where the function can only be evaluated.
2. **Local search and the idea of a metaheuristic.** [Local search](/local-search/) and [hill climbing](/hill-climbing/) stop in the first local optimum they reach. [Random restarts](/random-restart-hill-climbing/) are the simplest escape, and a [metaheuristic](/metaheuristic/) is the general name for the others.
3. **Accepting worse moves.** [Simulated annealing](/simulated-annealing/) accepts a worse neighbor with the probability given by the [Metropolis criterion](/metropolis-criterion/), and [Hajek's theorem](/simulated-annealing-convergence/) says how slowly the temperature must fall for it to find a global minimum. [Threshold accepting](/threshold-accepting/), the [great deluge](/great-deluge-algorithm/) and [late acceptance](/late-acceptance-hill-climbing/) use simpler tests.
4. **Memory, perturbation and restarts.** [Tabu search](/tabu-search/) forbids recent moves. [Iterated local search](/iterated-local-search/), [variable neighborhood search](/variable-neighborhood-search/), [GRASP](/grasp/) and [path relinking](/path-relinking/) restart local search from well-chosen points.
5. **Evolutionary algorithms.** A population changes by [selection](/tournament-selection/), [crossover](/crossover/) and [mutation](/mutation/). Together these make the [genetic algorithm](/genetic-algorithm/); with local search added, the [memetic algorithm](/memetic-algorithm/).
6. **Real vectors and learned distributions.** [Evolution strategies](/evolution-strategies/), [CMA-ES](/cma-es/) and [differential evolution](/differential-evolution/) work on $\mathbb{R}^n$. The [cross-entropy method](/cross-entropy-method/) fits a sampling distribution to the best samples.
7. **Swarms and metaphors.** [Ant colony optimization](/ant-colony-optimization/) and [particle swarm optimization](/particle-swarm-optimization/), and five newer methods that turn out to be close to older ones.
8. **What theory says.** The [schema theorem](/schema-theorem/), the [no free lunch theorem](/no-free-lunch-theorem/), and [hyper-heuristics](/hyper-heuristic/), which choose among heuristics during the search.

## Before you start

The chapter assumes the [combinatorial optimization problem](/combinatorial-optimization-problem/) and its standard examples, the [traveling salesman problem](/traveling-salesman-problem/) and the [knapsack problem](/knapsack-problem/), from the previous chapter. Simulated annealing rests on [Markov chains](/markov-chain/) and their [stationary distributions](/stationary-distribution/). The analyses of random restarts and mutation use the [geometric distribution](/geometric-distribution/) and the [binomial distribution](/binomial-distribution/), and bit strings are compared by [Hamming distance](/hamming-distance/). For the continuous methods, [gradient descent](/gradient-descent/) is the method they are measured against, and CMA-ES needs the [covariance matrix](/covariance-matrix/).
