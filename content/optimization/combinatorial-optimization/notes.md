## History

The problems of the chapter came from operations research in the 1950s. George Dantzig, Ray Fulkerson and Selmer Johnson wrote the traveling salesman problem as an integer program with subtour elimination constraints in 1954, adding the constraints only as needed, which is still the method of exact TSP codes. Dantzig gave the ratio rule for the fractional knapsack in 1957. In the same year Tjalling Koopmans and Martin Beckmann posed the quadratic assignment problem for the location of economic activities, and in 1959 Dantzig and J. H. Ramser studied the delivery of gasoline, the first vehicle routing problem. Richard Bellman, and independently Michael Held and Richard Karp, published the dynamic program over subsets for the TSP in 1962. G. Clarke and J. W. Wright proposed the savings method in 1964.

Matroids gave the first explanation of why some problems are easy. Richard Rado proved his theorem on independent transversals in 1942, and in 1957 he showed a greedy-type property of independence functions. David Gale (1968) and Jack Edmonds (1971) proved that greedy is optimal for all weights exactly on matroids. At the Calgary conference of 1969, published in 1970, Edmonds presented the matroid intersection theorem, polymatroids and the integrality of the intersection of two matroid polytopes. Eugene Lawler (1975) and Edmonds (1979) gave augmenting path algorithms. For submodular functions, George Nemhauser, Laurence Wolsey and Marshall Fisher proved the $1 - 1/e$ bound for greedy maximization in 1978, Martin Grötschel, László Lovász and Alexander Schrijver showed with the ellipsoid method that minimization is polynomial in 1981, and Lovász introduced his extension in 1983. Combinatorial minimization algorithms followed from Schrijver (2000) and from Iwata, Fleischer and Fujishige (2001).

The theory of NP-completeness changed the questions. Karp's list of 1972 included the knapsack problem, and the book of Michael Garey and David Johnson (1979) catalogued the hard problems. Oscar Ibarra and Chul Kim gave an FPTAS for knapsack in 1975. Sahni and Teofilo Gonzalez showed in 1976 that the general TSP and the quadratic assignment problem have no constant approximation ratio unless P = NP. In the same year Nicos Christofides, and independently Anatoliy Serdyukov, gave the $3/2$ ratio for metric TSP, which Anna Karlin, Nathan Klein and Shayan Oveis Gharan improved slightly in 2021. For facility location, the first constant ratio came from David Shmoys, Éva Tardos and Karen Aardal in 1997, and the best known ratio, 1.488, from Shi Li in 2013.

Practical methods grew next to the theory. Brian Kernighan and Shen Lin published their partitioning heuristic in 1970 and the Lin-Kernighan TSP heuristic in 1973. Charles Fiduccia and Robert Mattheyses made a pass linear in 1982, and their method became the refinement step of multilevel tools such as METIS of George Karypis and Vipin Kumar. Held and Karp introduced the 1-tree bound in 1970 and 1971, and Arthur Geoffrion described the strength of Lagrangian bounds in 1974. Paul Shaw introduced large neighborhood search in 1998, and Stefan Ropke and David Pisinger made it adaptive in 2006. Exact codes grew too: Anstreicher, Brixius, Goux and Linderoth solved the quadratic assignment instance nug30 in 2002, and Applegate, Bixby, Chvátal and Cook reported in 2006 the optimal tour of an instance with 85,900 cities.

## Further reading

- Bernhard Korte and Jens Vygen, *Combinatorial Optimization: Theory and Algorithms*, sixth edition (2018). A complete graduate text with proofs, covering every topic of the chapter.
- Alexander Schrijver, *Combinatorial Optimization: Polyhedra and Efficiency* (2003). The reference work on the polyhedral side, with the history of each problem and the full theory of matroids and submodular functions.
- Christos H. Papadimitriou and Kenneth Steiglitz, *Combinatorial Optimization: Algorithms and Complexity* (1982, Dover reprint 1998). A shorter classic linking linear programming, flows, NP-completeness and local search.
- Hans Kellerer, Ulrich Pferschy and David Pisinger, *Knapsack Problems* (2004). Everything about knapsack: dynamic programming, branch and bound, approximation schemes and variants.
- David L. Applegate, Robert E. Bixby, Vašek Chvátal and William J. Cook, *The Traveling Salesman Problem: A Computational Study* (2006). How exact TSP codes work, from cutting planes to the Concorde experiments.
- Paolo Toth and Daniele Vigo (editors), *The Vehicle Routing Problem* (2002). A survey of models, exact methods and heuristics for vehicle routing.
