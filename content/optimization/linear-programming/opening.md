A [linear program](/linear-programming-problem/) asks for the best value of a linear function of real variables, subject to linear equations and inequalities. A factory chooses how much of each product to make, where each unit earns a fixed amount and uses fixed amounts of machine time and material, and each resource has a limit. A transport company chooses how much to ship along each road. A flow in a network, a shortest path, the value of a zero-sum game and the bound inside many approximation algorithms are all linear programs too. The allowed points form a [polyhedron](/polyhedron/), a region with flat sides and corners, and if a best value exists, it is found at a corner.

This chapter is about that one problem: its forms, its theory and its algorithms. The theory is short and complete. Each program is infeasible, unbounded or has an optimum at a vertex, and each optimum comes with a second program, the dual, whose solution proves that nothing better exists. The algorithms are of three kinds. The [simplex method](/simplex-method/) walks along the edges of the polyhedron; it is fast in practice and exponential in the worst case. The [ellipsoid method](/ellipsoid-method/) and the interior-point methods, such as [Karmarkar's algorithm](/karmarkar-algorithm/), run in time polynomial in the bit length of the input. When the number of variables is fixed, [Seidel's algorithm](/seidel-linear-programming/) and [Megiddo's algorithm](/megiddo-fixed-dimension-lp/) take time linear in the number of constraints.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Linear programs and their forms.** The [problem](/linear-programming-problem/), its [feasibility](/linear-feasibility/) version, and the two [standard forms](/lp-standard-form/). [Slack variables](/slack-variables/) turn inequalities into equations, and the [slack form](/lp-slack-form/) is the shape that pivots rewrite.
2. **Vertices and basic feasible solutions.** A [basic feasible solution](/basic-feasible-solution/) is a vertex written in algebra. The [fundamental theorem](/fundamental-theorem-of-lp/) puts an optimum at one of them.
3. **Duality.** The [dual program](/dual-linear-program/), [weak duality](/weak-duality/), [Farkas' lemma](/farkas-lemma/) through [Fourier-Motzkin elimination](/fourier-motzkin-elimination/), [strong duality](/strong-duality/) and [complementary slackness](/complementary-slackness/).
4. **Network problems as linear programs.** [Maximum flow](/max-flow-linear-program/), [minimum-cost flow](/min-cost-flow-lp-formulation/) and [shortest paths](/shortest-paths-lp-formulation/), whose min-max theorems are cases of duality.
5. **The simplex method.** The [pivot](/pivot-operation/), the [ratio test](/ratio-test/), [reduced costs](/reduced-cost/), [degeneracy](/lp-degeneracy/), and the pivot rules of [Dantzig](/dantzig-pivot-rule/) and [Bland](/bland-rule/).
6. **The simplex method in practice.** How to find a first basis with the [two-phase method](/two-phase-simplex-method/) or the [big-M method](/big-m-method/), and the [revised](/revised-simplex-method/) and [dual](/dual-simplex-method/) simplex methods that solvers run.
7. **How many pivots.** The [Klee-Minty cube](/klee-minty-cube/), the [Hirsch conjecture](/hirsch-conjecture/) and the [smoothed analysis](/smoothed-simplex-analysis/) of the simplex method.
8. **Prices, sensitivity and large programs.** [Shadow prices](/shadow-price/), [sensitivity analysis](/lp-sensitivity-analysis/), [parametric programming](/parametric-linear-programming/), and [column generation](/column-generation/) for programs with huge numbers of columns.
9. **Polynomial-time algorithms.** The [separation oracle](/separation-oracle/), the ellipsoid method and the theorem that [linear programming is in P](/lp-in-p/), then Karmarkar's algorithm and the [affine scaling method](/affine-scaling-method/).
10. **Linear programs in fixed dimension.** Seidel's randomized algorithm and Megiddo's deterministic one.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these thirteen entries in order: [polyhedron](/polyhedron/), [linear programming](/linear-programming-problem/), [standard form](/lp-standard-form/), [slack variables](/slack-variables/), [basic feasible solution](/basic-feasible-solution/), [the fundamental theorem](/fundamental-theorem-of-lp/), [the dual program](/dual-linear-program/), [weak duality](/weak-duality/), [strong duality](/strong-duality/), [pivot operation](/pivot-operation/), [reduced costs](/reduced-cost/), [the simplex method](/simplex-method/) and [the two-phase method](/two-phase-simplex-method/). Many of these entries share one running example, $\max 3x_1 + 2x_2$ over a pentagon, so the numbers on one page check the numbers on the next. On a first pass you can skip the proof of strong duality from Farkas' lemma and read the short proof from the simplex method instead. Leave the groups on pivot counts, polynomial algorithms and fixed dimension for a second pass.

## Before you start

You need the basics of linear algebra from Volume 11: [linear independence](/linear-independence/), [Gauss-Jordan elimination](/gauss-jordan-elimination/) and, for the revised simplex method, the [LU decomposition](/lu-decomposition/). You need [convex sets](/convex-set/) from Volume 13. The network entries assume the [maximum flow problem](/max-flow-problem/), the [minimum-cost flow problem](/min-cost-flow-problem/) and [shortest paths](/single-pair-shortest-path/) from Volume 7. The advanced entries draw on [positive definite matrices](/positive-definite-matrix/) and [least squares](/least-squares-problem/) from Volume 12, [smoothed analysis](/smoothed-analysis/) from Volume 1, [backwards analysis](/backwards-analysis/) from Volume 2, and [prune and search](/prune-and-search/) from Volume 13. Integer programs, which add the condition that variables be integers, are the subject of the next chapter.
