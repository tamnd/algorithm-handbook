## History

Lagrange introduced the multiplier rule for equality constraints in the eighteenth century, in mechanics. The geometry of convex sets came much later: separation and support properties of convex bodies appear in the work of Hermann Minkowski, published in his collected papers in 1911. Werner Fenchel introduced conjugate convex functions of several variables in 1949 and proved his duality theorem in the same paper. R. Tyrrell Rockafellar extended it to compositions with a linear map in 1966, and his book *Convex Analysis* (1970) became the standard account of the subject.

Inequality constraints entered with the optimality conditions. William Karush stated them in a master's thesis at Chicago in 1939, Fritz John gave a version in 1948, and Harold Kuhn and Albert Tucker published them in 1951, which is why they are now called the KKT conditions. Morton Slater gave his condition for strong duality in a Cowles Commission paper of 1950. Harry Markowitz's portfolio selection of 1952 made the quadratic program a tool of finance, and Frank and Wolfe proved in 1956 that a quadratic program bounded below attains its minimum.

The methods came in two lines. Barrier functions go back to Frisch in 1955 and were developed by Fiacco and McCormick, whose book appeared in 1968. They fell out of use in the 1970s, because the Newton systems become ill-conditioned, until Karmarkar's algorithm of 1984 brought interior methods back. Then Gill, Murray, Saunders, Tomlin and Wright showed in 1986 that it is closely related to a barrier method. Nesterov and Nemirovski's theory of self-concordant barriers (1994) extended the polynomial bounds from linear programs to second-order cone and semidefinite programs. Alongside, Cheney and Goldstein (1959) and Kelley (1960) proposed cutting planes for convex functions, and the ellipsoid method of Shor and of Yudin and Nemirovski, in 1976 and 1977, gave Khachiyan the first polynomial algorithm for linear programming in 1979.

The multiplier methods start with Arrow, Hurwicz and Uzawa in 1958 and with Everett's use of multipliers for allocation problems in 1963. Hestenes and Powell proposed the method of multipliers independently in 1969, and Rockafellar showed in 1976 that it is the proximal point method, which Martinet had introduced in 1970, applied to the dual. Glowinski and Marrocco (1975) and Gabay and Mercier (1976) introduced ADMM for problems from mechanics. Lemaréchal and Wolfe proposed the first bundle methods in 1975. Two later events widened the use of the field: the 0.878-approximation for max-cut of Goemans and Williamson (1995), which made semidefinite relaxation a tool of combinatorial optimization, and the survey of ADMM by Boyd, Parikh, Chu, Peleato and Eckstein (2011), which brought it to statistics and machine learning.

## Further reading

- Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, Cambridge University Press, 2004. The standard textbook, with modelling, duality and interior-point methods, and the source of most of the examples in this chapter.
- R. Tyrrell Rockafellar, *Convex Analysis*, Princeton University Press, 1970. The classical reference for conjugates, subdifferentials and Fenchel duality, rigorous and complete.
- Jean-Baptiste Hiriart-Urruty and Claude Lemaréchal, *Fundamentals of Convex Analysis*, Springer, 2001. A gentler account of the same theory, with good pictures and a view toward algorithms such as the bundle method.
- Yurii Nesterov and Arkadii Nemirovskii, *Interior-Point Polynomial Algorithms in Convex Programming*, SIAM, 1994. The theory of self-concordant barriers and of path-following methods for conic problems.
- Dimitri P. Bertsekas, *Constrained Optimization and Lagrange Multiplier Methods*, Athena Scientific, 1996, first published in 1982. Penalty, multiplier and augmented Lagrangian methods, with their convergence theory.
- Stephen Boyd, Neal Parikh, Eric Chu, Borja Peleato and Jonathan Eckstein, Distributed optimization and statistical learning via the alternating direction method of multipliers, *Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning*, 2011. A readable survey of dual decomposition and ADMM, with many applications.
