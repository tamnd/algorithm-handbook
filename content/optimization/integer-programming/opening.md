An integer program is a linear program with one more rule: some of the variables must take whole-number values. That rule is what lets a model say yes or no. A variable $z \in \{0, 1\}$ opens a warehouse or leaves it closed, puts a crew on a flight or not, packs an item into a knapsack or leaves it out. With such variables, scheduling, routing, network design and production planning all become [mixed-integer programs](/mixed-integer-programming/), and most discrete decision problems of industry are written and solved this way.

The same rule makes the problem hard. Linear programming is in P, but [0-1 integer programming](/binary-integer-programming/) is one of Karp's 21 NP-complete problems. Rounding the LP optimum is no way out. Take $\max x_2$ subject to $3x_1 + 2x_2 \le 6$, $-3x_1 + 2x_2 \le 0$ and $x \ge 0$. The LP optimum is $(1, \tfrac32)$. Rounding up gives $(1, 2)$, which is infeasible. Rounding down gives $(1, 1)$, which is optimal here, but with many variables the rounded point can be infeasible or far from the optimum, and no rounding rule is safe in general.

The chapter is about the ways around this. Almost every method starts from the [LP relaxation](/lp-relaxation/), the linear program left when integrality is dropped, whose value bounds the integer optimum. Then it tightens the relaxation with cutting planes, splits the problem by branching, or does both. The last group shows when the hardness goes away: when the number of variables, or the number of constraints, is fixed.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **The problem and its relaxation.** Mixed-integer programs, the 0-1 case and the [integer feasibility problem](/integer-feasibility/). The LP relaxation gives a bound, and the [integrality gap](/integrality-gap/) says how far that bound can be from the truth.
2. **Modelling with binary variables.** The [big-M formulation](/big-m-formulation/) and [indicator constraints](/indicator-constraint/): how to write logic as linear inequalities, and why the size of $M$ matters.
3. **Integral polyhedra.** The [integer hull](/integer-hull/), [total unimodularity](/total-unimodularity/) and the [Hoffman-Kruskal theorem](/hoffman-kruskal-theorem/). They explain why flows and bipartite matching can be solved as linear programs.
4. **Chvátal-Gomory cuts and the closure.** The [cutting-plane method](/cutting-plane-method-integer-programming/), the [Chvátal-Gomory cut](/chvatal-gomory-cut/), and the [theorem](/chvatal-gomory-finite-convergence/) that finitely many rounds of such cuts reach the integer hull.
5. **Cuts from the simplex tableau and mixed-integer cuts.** [Gomory's algorithm](/gomory-cutting-plane-algorithm/), the [Gomory mixed-integer cut](/gomory-mixed-integer-cut/) and the [mixed-integer rounding inequality](/mixed-integer-rounding-inequality/), with [subadditive duality](/subadditive-duality/) as the theory behind them.
6. **Cuts from structure and disjunctions.** [Cover](/cover-inequality/) and [flow cover](/flow-cover-inequality/) inequalities, which use the form of one row, and [disjunctive cuts](/disjunctive-cut/) and [lift-and-project](/lift-and-project/), which use the two sides of a branching.
7. **Branch and cut and its parts.** [Branch and cut](/branch-and-cut/), the framework of every modern solver, with [presolve](/presolve/) and the branching rules from [strong branching](/strong-branching/) to [reliability branching](/reliability-branching/).
8. **Primal heuristics.** Ways to find good solutions early: [diving](/diving-heuristic/), the [feasibility pump](/feasibility-pump/), [RINS](/relaxation-induced-neighborhood-search/) and [local branching](/local-branching/).
9. **Decomposition.** [Benders decomposition](/benders-decomposition/) and [branch and price](/branch-and-price/), for programs with block structure.
10. **Fixed dimension and pseudo-polynomial algorithms.** [Lenstra's algorithm](/lenstra-algorithm/) solves integer programs with a fixed number of variables in polynomial time. [Papadimitriou's theorem](/papadimitriou-pseudopolynomial-theorem/) gives a pseudo-polynomial algorithm when the number of constraints is fixed.
