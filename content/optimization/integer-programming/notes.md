## History

Integer programming grew out of linear programming in the 1950s. In 1954 George Dantzig, Ray Fulkerson and Selmer Johnson solved a 49-city [traveling salesman problem](/traveling-salesman-problem/) with a linear program, adding subtour and other inequalities only when the LP solution broke them. Ralph Gomory turned the idea into an algorithm in 1958: his cuts come from the rows of the simplex tableau, and with a lexicographic rule the method ends after finitely many steps. He gave the mixed-integer cut and an all-integer algorithm in 1960, and a full proof of finiteness in 1963. In 1960 Ailsa Land and Alison Doig published the other main idea, [branch and bound](/land-doig-algorithm/); R. J. Dakin's form of 1965 branches on one variable at a time, as solvers still do. Egon Balas gave his additive algorithm for 0-1 programs in 1965.

The theory of integral polyhedra came at the same time. Alan Hoffman and Joseph Kruskal proved their theorem on [totally unimodular](/total-unimodularity/) matrices in 1956, in the volume *Linear Inequalities and Related Systems* edited by Harold Kuhn and Albert Tucker, where Isidor Heller and Charles Tompkins gave their sufficient condition. Alain Ghouila-Houri characterized TU matrices by row partitions in 1962, and Paul Seymour's decomposition of 1980 led to a polynomial test. Václav Chvátal showed in 1973 that rounds of rounding cuts reach the integer hull of a polytope, which linked Gomory's cuts to combinatorics, and Alexander Schrijver extended the result to rational polyhedra in 1980. Jack Edmonds and Rick Giles introduced [total dual integrality](/totally-dual-integrality/) in 1977.

Complexity theory placed the problem. Richard Karp listed 0-1 integer programming among his 21 NP-complete problems in 1972. Christos Papadimitriou showed in 1981 that a fixed number of constraints allows a pseudo-polynomial algorithm. Hendrik Lenstra used lattice basis reduction in 1983 to solve integer programs with a fixed number of variables in polynomial time, Ravi Kannan lowered the dependence on the dimension to $n^{O(n)}$, and András Frank and Éva Tardos showed in 1987 how to make the numbers of the input small. Friedrich Eisenbrand and Robert Weismantel proved their proximity bound with the Steinitz lemma in 2018.

Practical solvers took longer. Jacques Benders published his decomposition in 1962. Harlan Crowder, Ellis Johnson and Manfred Padberg solved large 0-1 programs with cover inequalities in 1983, and Padberg, Tony Van Roy and Laurence Wolsey introduced flow covers in 1985. Padberg and Giovanni Rinaldi named branch and cut in 1991, in their work on the traveling salesman problem. Balas, Sebastián Ceria and Gérard Cornuéjols gave lift-and-project cuts in 1993, and in 1996, with Natraj, showed that Gomory cuts work well inside branch and cut. Branch and price was surveyed by Cynthia Barnhart and coauthors in 1998. The 2000s added primal heuristics such as local branching (Matteo Fischetti and Andrea Lodi, 2003), the feasibility pump (Fischetti, Fred Glover and Lodi, 2005) and RINS (Emilie Danna, Edward Rothberg and Claude Le Pape, 2005), and reliability branching (Tobias Achterberg, Thorsten Koch and Alexander Martin, 2005), the default rule of the SCIP solver.

## Further reading

- Michele Conforti, Gérard Cornuéjols and Giacomo Zambelli, *Integer Programming* (2014). A modern graduate text: formulations, polyhedral theory, every family of cuts in this chapter, and lattices with Lenstra's algorithm.
- Laurence A. Wolsey, *Integer Programming* (1998). A short and practical introduction to formulations, relaxations, branch and cut and decomposition.
- Alexander Schrijver, *Theory of Linear and Integer Programming* (1986). The reference for total unimodularity, total dual integrality, the Chvátal closure and the complexity results, with full proofs.
- George L. Nemhauser and Laurence A. Wolsey, *Integer and Combinatorial Optimization* (1988). The source for valid inequalities, mixed-integer rounding and superadditive duality.
- Michael Jünger and others (editors), *50 Years of Integer Programming 1958–2008* (2010). Classic papers with comments by their authors, and surveys of the main research lines.
- Tobias Achterberg, *Constraint Integer Programming*, PhD thesis, Technische Universität Berlin (2007). A detailed account of how the SCIP solver branches, cuts, presolves and searches for solutions.
