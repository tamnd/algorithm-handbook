## History

Cauchy proposed the gradient method in a short note of 1847: move along the negative gradient, and choose the step that minimizes the function along that line. Curry studied the method for nonlinear functions in 1944. Akaike explained the zigzag of steepest descent with exact steps in 1959, and the bound $((\kappa - 1)/(\kappa + 1))^2$ per step on quadratics follows from an inequality of Kantorovich. The practical rules for inexact steps came in the 1960s: Armijo's condition of 1966, with $c = \frac12$ and halving, and the conditions of Wolfe in 1969. Goldstein (1964) and Levitin and Polyak (1966) added a projection to handle convex constraints. Polyak gave the linear rate under the gradient dominance condition (1963), the heavy ball method (1964) and the step size that uses the optimal value (1969).

The search for faster methods without second derivatives started with a report of Davidon in 1959, which Fletcher and Powell turned into the DFP method in 1963. Broyden described a family of updates in 1965, and in 1970 Broyden, Fletcher, Goldfarb and Shanno each published the update now called BFGS. Fletcher and Reeves extended the conjugate gradient method of Hestenes and Stiefel (1952) to nonlinear functions in 1964, and Polak and Ribière, and independently Polyak, gave the variant of 1969. Nocedal (1980) and Liu and Nocedal (1989) introduced limited memory BFGS.

Nemirovski and Yudin founded the complexity theory of optimization in their book of 1983, with lower bounds for first-order methods and the method that became known as mirror descent; Beck and Teboulle (2003) gave mirror descent its modern form through Bregman divergences. In the same year Nesterov published the accelerated gradient method, whose $O(1/k^2)$ rate matches the lower bound. Moreau (1965) introduced the proximal operator, and the forward-backward splitting of Lions and Mercier (1979) and Passty (1979) led, through the work of Daubechies, Defrise and De Mol (2004) on iterative soft thresholding, to FISTA of Beck and Teboulle (2009).

Stochastic approximation began with Robbins and Monro (1951) and Kiefer and Wolfowitz (1952). Polyak and Juditsky (1992) showed that averaging the iterates gives the optimal asymptotic rate, and Nemirovski, Juditsky, Lan and Shapiro (2009) gave the robust averaged method with nonasymptotic rates. For finite sums, SAG of Le Roux, Schmidt and Bach (2012) was the first method with a linear rate at the cost of one component gradient per step; SVRG of Johnson and Zhang (2013) and SAGA of Defazio, Bach and Lacoste-Julien (2014) followed. Nesterov (2012) proved the first global rates for randomized coordinate descent.

The training of neural networks produced its own methods. Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams (1986) used momentum with backpropagation, and Riedmiller and Braun (1993) proposed Rprop. Amari (1998) introduced the natural gradient. Duchi, Hazan and Singer (2011) published AdaGrad with a regret bound; RMSProp appeared in a lecture of Tieleman and Hinton in 2012, Adadelta in a paper of Zeiler the same year, and Adam in a paper of Kingma and Ba posted in 2014. Reddi, Kale and Kumar (2018) showed that the convergence proof of Adam is wrong. Karimi, Nutini and Schmidt (2016) revived the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition as a simple tool for linear rates without convexity.

## Further reading

- Yurii Nesterov, *Lectures on Convex Optimization*, second edition, Springer, 2018. The standard account of smooth convex optimization, lower bounds and accelerated methods, by the author of the accelerated method.
- Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright, *Numerical Optimization*, second edition, Springer, 2006. Line searches, the Wolfe conditions, nonlinear conjugate gradient, BFGS and L-BFGS, with implementation details.
- Amir Beck, *First-Order Methods in Optimization*, SIAM, 2017. Projected, proximal and mirror methods, FISTA and coordinate descent, with complete proofs.
- Neal Parikh and Stephen Boyd, *Proximal Algorithms*, Foundations and Trends in Optimization 1(3), 2014. A short survey of proximal operators and the methods built on them.
- Léon Bottou, Frank E. Curtis and Jorge Nocedal, *Optimization Methods for Large-Scale Machine Learning*, SIAM Review 60(2), 2018. Stochastic gradient methods, noise reduction and second-order ideas for training models.
- A. S. Nemirovsky and D. B. Yudin, *Problem Complexity and Method Efficiency in Optimization*, Wiley, 1983. The origin of the complexity theory of optimization and of mirror descent.
