## History

The idea of an efficient trade-off came from economics. Francis Ysidro Edgeworth described it in 1881 and Vilfredo Pareto in 1906. Harold Kuhn and Albert Tucker, in their paper on nonlinear programming of 1951, and Tjalling Koopmans, in his work on activity analysis in the same year, brought the notion into optimization. Harry Markowitz's portfolio selection of 1952 is the classic two-objective model. Wolfram Stadler traced this early history in a survey of 1979.

The scalarizations followed in the next three decades. Abraham Charnes, William Cooper and Robert Ferguson introduced goal programming in 1955. Arthur Geoffrion defined proper efficiency in 1968 and proved what weighted sums can and cannot reach. Yacov Haimes, Leon Lasdon and David Wismer gave the epsilon-constraint method in 1971, V. Joseph Bowman the Tchebycheff theorem in 1976, and Andrzej Wierzbicki the reference point approach and its achievement functions in 1980. Ralph Steuer and Eng-Ung Choo made the Tchebycheff method interactive in 1983. For linear programs, Heinz Isermann showed in 1974 that every efficient point is optimal for positive weights, P. L. Yu and Milan Zeleny gave a multi-objective simplex method in 1975, and Harold Benson moved the computation to the objective space in 1998. Indraneel Das and John Dennis gave normal-boundary intersection in 1998.

Algorithms on vectors and on graphs grew in parallel. H. T. Kung, Fabrizio Luccio and Franco Preparata found the front of a finite set by divide and conquer in 1975, and Jon Bentley and his coauthors counted the expected size of the front in 1978. Pierre Hansen showed in 1980 that bicriteria shortest paths can have exponentially many Pareto optimal paths, and gave an approximation scheme. Ernesto Martins extended the label-setting method of Dijkstra to several costs in 1984, and Horst Hamacher and Günter Ruhe studied spanning trees with two weights in 1994. Christos Papadimitriou and Mihalis Yannakakis proved in 2000 that an approximate Pareto set of polynomial size always exists.

Evolutionary methods began with J. David Schaffer's VEGA in 1985. David Goldberg proposed in his book of 1989 to rank a population by Pareto dominance and to keep diversity by sharing, and three methods took up the proposal: MOGA of Carlos Fonseca and Peter Fleming in 1993, and NSGA of N. Srinivas and Kalyanmoy Deb and NPGA of Jeffrey Horn, Nicholas Nafpliotis and Goldberg in 1994. Elitism came next, through the archive of SPEA by Eckart Zitzler and Lothar Thiele in 1999, the grid archive of PAES by Joshua Knowles and David Corne, and the survival of the best fronts in NSGA-II, published by Deb and his coauthors in 2002. Zitzler, Marco Laumanns and Thiele gave SPEA2 in 2001.

The measurement of results became a topic of its own. Zitzler and Thiele used the hypervolume in 1998, and Zitzler, Thiele, Laumanns, Fonseca and Viviane Grunert da Fonseca showed in 2003 which unary indicators can detect dominance between sets. Karl Bringmann and Tobias Friedrich proved in 2010 that the hypervolume is #P-hard to compute. Many objectives brought the decomposition of MOEA/D by Qingfu Zhang and Hui Li in 2007 and the reference lines of NSGA-III by Deb and Himanshu Jain in 2014.

## Further reading

- Matthias Ehrgott, *Multicriteria Optimization*, second edition, Springer (2005). The standard text on efficiency, scalarization, and linear and combinatorial problems with several objectives.
- Kaisa Miettinen, *Nonlinear Multiobjective Optimization*, Kluwer (1999). The methods for continuous problems, grouped by when the preferences of the decision maker enter.
- Kalyanmoy Deb, *Multi-Objective Optimization Using Evolutionary Algorithms*, Wiley (2001). The early evolutionary methods and NSGA-II, explained by the author of NSGA-II.
- Carlos A. Coello Coello, Gary B. Lamont and David A. Van Veldhuizen, *Evolutionary Algorithms for Solving Multi-Objective Problems*, second edition, Springer (2007). A broad survey of methods, test problems and applications.
- Eckart Zitzler, Lothar Thiele, Marco Laumanns, Carlos M. Fonseca and Viviane Grunert da Fonseca, *Performance Assessment of Multiobjective Optimizers: An Analysis and Review*, IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation 7(2), 2003. The paper to read before comparing two optimizers by an indicator.
- Andreia P. Guerreiro, Carlos M. Fonseca and Luís Paquete, *The Hypervolume Indicator: Computational Problems and Algorithms*, ACM Computing Surveys 54(6), 2021. The algorithms for the hypervolume and its contributions.
