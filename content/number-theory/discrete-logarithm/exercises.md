1. ◦ The integer $5$ is a primitive root modulo $47$. Solve $5^x \equiv 35 \pmod{47}$ by [baby-step giant-step](/baby-step-giant-step/) with $m = 7$: list the baby steps, give $5^{-7} \bmod 47$, and say at which giant step the search stops.

2. ◦ The integer $3$ is a primitive root modulo $31$. Find all solutions of $x^3 \equiv 8 \pmod{31}$, and decide whether $x^3 \equiv 3 \pmod{31}$ has any. Use the reduction of the [discrete root](/discrete-root/) problem to a linear congruence.

3. ◦ Find the cube root of $10$ modulo $101$. Why is there exactly one, and why does no discrete logarithm need to be computed?

4. ◦ In the additive group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with generator $g$, the "discrete logarithm" of $h$ is the $x$ with $xg \equiv h \pmod n$. Show how to compute it in time polynomial in $\log n$. Why does this not contradict the lower bound in the [generic group model](/generic-group-model/)?

5. ◦ The prime $p = 65537$ has $p - 1 = 2^{16}$. Roughly how many multiplications modulo $p$ does the [Pohlig-Hellman algorithm](/pohlig-hellman/) need to compute a logarithm to a primitive root? What does this say about using $p$ for Diffie-Hellman?

6. ◦ A group of prime order $q \approx 2^{112}$ is attacked with [Pollard's rho method](/pollard-rho-discrete-logarithm/). About how many steps are expected? With [distinguished points](/distinguished-points/) of probability $\theta = 2^{-32}$, about how many points end up in the central table?

7. ◦ The exponent $x$ is known to lie in an interval of width $w = 2^{40}$ in a group of order about $2^{256}$. Compare the cost and memory of baby-step giant-step on the interval with those of [Pollard's kangaroo method](/pollard-kangaroo/).

8. ◦ Let $q = 2^{160}$. By [Shoup's bound](/shoup-generic-lower-bound/), a generic algorithm that makes $m$ group operations succeeds with probability $O(m^2/q)$. Bound the probability for $m = 2^{60}$, and give the order of $m$ needed for success probability $1/2$.

9. • Let $p$ be an odd prime and $g$ a primitive root. Show that $h^{(p - 1)/2} \equiv (-1)^x \pmod p$ when $h = g^x$, so the parity of $x$ is always easy to find. Check it for $h = 35$ in Exercise 1. Why do protocols work in a subgroup of prime order?

10. • The integer $5$ is a primitive root modulo $97$, and $96 = 2^5 \cdot 3$. Solve $5^x \equiv 23 \pmod{97}$ by Pohlig-Hellman: find $x \bmod 3$, find the five binary digits of $x \bmod 32$ one at a time, and combine them.

11. • An [index calculus](/index-calculus/) computation modulo $p = 101$ with $g = 2$ and the [factor base](/factor-base/) $\{2, 3, 5, 7\}$ finds the relations $2^{10} \equiv 2 \cdot 7$, $2^{17} \equiv 3 \cdot 5^2$, $2^{33} \equiv 5 \cdot 7$ and $2^{38} \equiv 3^2$. Solve for $\log_2 3$, $\log_2 5$ and $\log_2 7$ modulo $100$, and explain what goes wrong if you use only the last relation for $\log_2 3$. Then compute $\log_2 37$, given $37 \cdot 2^6 \equiv 45 \pmod{101}$.

12. • In Pollard's rho method with $n = 46$, $g = 5$ and $h = 35$ modulo $47$, a collision $5^3 \cdot 35^5 \equiv 5^{43} \cdot 35 \pmod{47}$ is found. Write the linear congruence for $x$, list all of its solutions modulo $46$, and find the right one.

13. • You must compute $t$ discrete logarithms to the same base in a group of order $n$. Show that baby-step giant-step with $m$ baby steps, stored once, solves all $t$ in about $m + tn/m$ operations, and find the best $m$ and the total cost.

14. • Suppose that you know $x \bmod m$ for some $m$ dividing the order $n$, for example from Pohlig-Hellman on the small primes of $n$. Show that what remains is an [interval discrete logarithm](/interval-discrete-logarithm-problem/) of width about $n/m$, and give its cost.

15. • Count the monic polynomials of degree $10$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ whose irreducible factors all have degree at most $2$. What fraction of all monic polynomials of degree $10$ is this? This is the smoothness probability that drives index calculus in $\mathbb{F}_{2^n}$.

16. • Show that if $x$ is known to be even, baby-step giant-step for $g^x = h$ can run in the subgroup generated by $g^2$, and give the saving. Generalize to $x \equiv r \pmod d$.

17. • Let $g$ have prime order $q$, and suppose an algorithm returns $\log_g h$ only for a $1/1000$ fraction of the $h$ in the group. Show how to compute the logarithm of every $h$ with about $1000$ calls on average, using random $r$ and the element $h g^r$.

18. ★ In index calculus modulo $p$, take the factor base of the primes up to $B = L_p[1/2, c]$, assume that a random residue is $B$-smooth with probability $u^{-u(1 + o(1))}$ with $u = \ln p / \ln B$, test smoothness by trial division at cost $B$ per test, and solve the sparse system in $B^{2 + o(1)}$ operations. Show that the total is $L_p[1/2, 2c + 1/(2c)]$, minimized at $c = 1/2$ with cost $L_p[1/2, 2]$. What constant results if smoothness can be tested at cost $L_p[1/2, o(1)]$?

19. ★ Let $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ be prime and $E : y^2 = x^3 + x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Prove that $E$ has exactly $p + 1$ points, using that $-1$ is not a square modulo $p$. Deduce that for every prime $r > 2$ dividing $p + 1$ the embedding degree is $2$, so the [MOV reduction](/menezes-okamoto-vanstone-reduction/) maps the logarithm into $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$.

20. ★ Let $p$ be an odd prime and $g$ a primitive root modulo $p^2$. Show that $(1 + ap)^x \equiv 1 + axp \pmod{p^2}$, and use it to find $x \bmod p$ from $h = g^x \bmod p^2$ with no search: compare $h^{p-1}$ with $g^{p-1}$. Together with a logarithm modulo $p$, this solves the problem modulo $p^2$. Carry it out for $p = 7$, $g = 3$, $h = 17$.

21. ★ Let $G$ be cyclic of order $n$ with largest prime factor $l$. Show that a generic algorithm for logarithms in $G$ with $m$ operations gives a generic algorithm for logarithms in a group of order $l$ with $O(m + \log n)$ operations, and conclude that every generic algorithm for $G$ needs $\Omega(\sqrt l)$ operations. Pohlig-Hellman shows that this is tight.
