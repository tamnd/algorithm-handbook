## History

Bin packing was one of the first problems analyzed by its worst-case ratio. Jeffrey Ullman proved a bound of 1.7 for first fit in a Princeton technical report of 1971. David Johnson's thesis of 1973 analyzed next fit and first fit decreasing, and the paper of Johnson, Alan Demers, Ullman, Michael Garey and Ronald Graham in 1974 gave the weight-function proofs and the worst-case lists for the four classic rules. Andrew Yao proved in 1980 that no online rule has asymptotic ratio below $3/2$. Wenceslas Fernandez de la Vega and George Lueker gave the first asymptotic scheme in 1981. The exact constants came much later: György Dósa proved the bound $\frac{11}{9}\text{OPT} + \frac69$ for first fit decreasing in 2007, and Dósa and Jiří Sgall proved $\lfloor 1.7\,\text{OPT} \rfloor$ for first fit and best fit in 2013 and 2014.

Set cover followed at once. Johnson proved the ratio $H_d$ for the unit-cost greedy in 1974, Sherman Stein (1974) and László Lovász (1975) found the same bound for hypergraphs, and Vašek Chvátal extended it to arbitrary costs in 1979. Uriel Feige showed in 1998 that $(1 - \varepsilon)\ln n$ is out of reach unless NP has slightly superpolynomial algorithms, and Irit Dinur and David Steurer proved the same under P $\ne$ NP in 2014. For maximum coverage, Gérard Cornuéjols, Marshall Fisher and George Nemhauser analyzed greedy for a location problem in 1977, Nemhauser, Laurence Wolsey and Fisher proved the bound $1 - (1 - 1/k)^k$ for every monotone submodular function in 1978, and Feige showed in 1998 that $1 - 1/e$ is the best possible.

Garey and Johnson's book of 1979 credits the maximal matching algorithm to Fănică Gavril and to Mihalis Yannakakis. Nemhauser and Leslie Trotter proved in 1975 that the LP has a half-integral optimum. Reuven Bar-Yehuda and Shimon Even gave the linear-time primal-dual algorithm for weighted vertex cover and for set cover with ratio $f$ in 1981, and Dorit Hochbaum reached the same ratios by LP rounding in 1982. Bar-Yehuda and Even restated their idea as the local ratio theorem in 1985, and in 1995 Ajit Agrawal, Philip Klein and R. Ravi, and independently Michel Goemans and David Williamson, used the primal-dual method for the Steiner forest problem. On the hardness side, Dinur and Shmuel Safra proved in 2005 that ratio $1.3606$ for vertex cover is NP-hard, Subhash Khot and Oded Regev showed in 2008 that $2 - \varepsilon$ is hard under the Unique Games Conjecture, and Khot, Dor Minzer and Safra proved $\sqrt 2 - \varepsilon$ NP-hard in 2018.

Sartaj Sahni and Teofilo Gonzalez gave the greedy $1/2$ for maximum cut in 1976; nothing better was known until the semidefinite program of Goemans and Williamson in 1995. Yair Caro (1979) and Victor Wei (1981) found the lower bound $\sum_v 1/(\deg v + 1)$ on the independence number, and Magnús Halldórsson and Jaikumar Radhakrishnan proved the ratio $(\Delta + 2)/3$ of the minimum-degree greedy in 1997. For $k$-center, Wen-Lian Hsu and Nemhauser showed in 1979 that ratio $2 - \varepsilon$ is NP-hard, and in 1985 Gonzalez's farthest-first traversal and the threshold method of Hochbaum and David Shmoys both reached 2. Prabhakar Raghavan and Clark Thompson introduced randomized rounding for packing and routing problems in 1987, and Aravind Srinivasan sharpened the bounds for covering and packing integer programs in 1999.

## Further reading

- David P. Williamson and David B. Shmoys, *The Design of Approximation Algorithms* (2011). The main textbook of the field; its first chapter treats set cover by most of the methods of this chapter, side by side.
- Vijay V. Vazirani, *Approximation Algorithms* (2001). Part I covers the combinatorial algorithms and Part II the LP methods.
- Dorit S. Hochbaum, editor, *Approximation Algorithms for NP-Hard Problems* (1997). Survey chapters, among them Coffman, Garey and Johnson on bin packing.
- Michael R. Garey and David S. Johnson, *Computers and Intractability: A Guide to the Theory of NP-Completeness* (1979). The reference for the NP-hardness of the problems in this chapter.
- Reuven Bar-Yehuda, Keren Bendel, Ari Freund and Dror Rawitz, *Local ratio: a unified framework for approximation algorithms*, ACM Computing Surveys 36(4), 2004. A survey of the local ratio technique, with many problems worked out.
