## History

The first bounds came soon after NP-completeness. Sartaj Sahni and Teofilo Gonzalez proved in 1976 that the general TSP has no constant ratio unless P = NP. In the same year Michael Garey, David Johnson and Larry Stockmeyer proved 3-colouring NP-complete, which gives the bound $4/3$ for the chromatic number, and Garey and Johnson pushed it towards 2 with any additive constant. Wen-Lian Hsu and George Nemhauser showed in 1979 that $2 - \varepsilon$ is hard for $k$-center. Jan Karel Lenstra and Alexander Rinnooy Kan proved the bound $4/3$ for unit jobs with precedence constraints in 1978, and Lenstra, David Shmoys and Éva Tardos the bound $3/2$ for unrelated machines in 1990. All of these read a gap directly off an NP-complete problem with small optima.

A theory of reductions came next. Christos Papadimitriou and Mihalis Yannakakis defined the class MAX SNP and the L-reduction in 1991 and showed that MAX 3SAT, max cut and bounded-degree vertex cover are complete for it, so that one of them has a PTAS only if all do. Marshall Bern and Paul Plassmann used the same kind of reduction for Steiner tree in 1989. Later work defined PTAS-reductions and AP-reductions, and Sanjeev Khanna, Rajeev Motwani, Madhu Sudan and Umesh Vazirani proved in 1998 that MAX 3SAT is complete for all of APX.

The PCP theorem connected these classes to proofs. Uriel Feige, Shafi Goldwasser, László Lovász, Shmuel Safra and Mario Szegedy linked interactive proofs to clique in 1991, and Sanjeev Arora, Carsten Lund, Rajeev Motwani, Madhu Sudan and Mario Szegedy proved the PCP theorem in 1992, so that none of the MAX SNP-complete problems has a PTAS. Lund and Yannakakis derived bounds for set cover and colouring from two-prover proofs in 1994, and Ran Raz's parallel repetition theorem of 1995 made such proofs as strong as needed. Feige proved the threshold $\ln n$ for set cover and $1 - 1/e$ for maximum coverage in 1998. Johan Håstad gave the bound $n^{1 - \varepsilon}$ for clique in 1999 and, in "Some optimal inapproximability results" (2001), the tight constants for MAX E3SAT and Max 3LIN with the long code and Fourier analysis. Irit Dinur gave a combinatorial proof of the PCP theorem by gap amplification in 2007, David Zuckerman removed the randomness from the clique and colouring bounds in the same year, and Dinur and David Steurer proved the set cover threshold under P $\ne$ NP in 2014.

Subhash Khot stated the Unique Games Conjecture in 2002. Khot, Guy Kindler, Elchanan Mossel and Ryan O'Donnell showed in 2004 that it makes the Goemans-Williamson ratio optimal for max cut, given the Majority Is Stablest theorem that Mossel, O'Donnell and Krzysztof Oleszkiewicz proved in 2005. Khot and Oded Regev obtained $2 - \varepsilon$ for vertex cover, and Prasad Raghavendra showed in 2008 that the basic SDP is optimal for every CSP. In the other direction, Arora, Boaz Barak and Steurer found a subexponential algorithm for unique games in 2010, and Khot, Dor Minzer and Safra proved the 2-to-2 Games Theorem in 2018, a weaker form of the conjecture. Since 2016 the Gap Exponential Time Hypothesis of Dinur and of Pasin Manurangsi and Raghavendra has given tight bounds for parameterized problems.

## Further reading

- Sanjeev Arora and Boaz Barak, *Computational Complexity: A Modern Approach* (2009). Its chapters on PCPs and hardness of approximation give the PCP theorem by Dinur's proof, Håstad's 3-bit PCP and parallel repetition.
- David P. Williamson and David B. Shmoys, *The Design of Approximation Algorithms* (2011). Chapter 16 proves the main reductions of this chapter, from gap problems and L-reductions to label cover and the Unique Games Conjecture.
- Ryan O'Donnell, *Analysis of Boolean Functions* (2014). The Fourier analysis behind linearity testing, the long code, dictatorship tests and Majority Is Stablest, with Håstad's and Raghavendra's results as applications.
- Giorgio Ausiello, Pierluigi Crescenzi, Giorgio Gambosi, Viggo Kann, Alberto Marchetti-Spaccamela and Marco Protasi, *Complexity and Approximation* (1999). The classes NPO, APX and PTAS and the approximation-preserving reductions, with a compendium of known bounds.
- Dorit S. Hochbaum, editor, *Approximation Algorithms for NP-Hard Problems* (1997). Its chapter by Arora and Lund surveys hardness results from the first years of the PCP theorem.
