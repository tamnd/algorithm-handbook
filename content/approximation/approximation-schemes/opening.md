Some NP-hard problems can be solved as closely as you like. For the [knapsack problem](/knapsack-problem/) there is an algorithm that takes an accuracy $\varepsilon$ and returns a set of items worth at least $(1 - \varepsilon)$ times the best, in $O(n^3/\varepsilon)$ time. With $\varepsilon = 0.01$ that is a 1% loss at a cost of about $100 n^3$ steps, while every known exact method takes exponential time in the worst case. A scheduler that balances jobs over machines, or a drill that visits points on a circuit board, can do the same within $1 + \varepsilon$ of the best makespan or the shortest tour, though at a higher price in $1/\varepsilon$. Such a family of algorithms, one for each $\varepsilon$, is an approximation scheme. This chapter shows how schemes are built and where they stop.

Most schemes use one of a few ideas. Round the numbers of the input so that an exact dynamic program becomes small. Split the items into a few large ones, which can be tried in every way, and many small ones, which are added greedily. Cut the plane or a planar graph into pieces, solve each piece exactly, and choose the cut that loses least. Each idea costs a factor $1 + \varepsilon$, and the price is paid in running time.

The chapter also holds the basic definitions of the whole volume. The [approximation ratio](/approximation-ratio/) is a prerequisite of many entries in the other chapters, and the classes [PTAS](/ptas/), [FPTAS](/fptas/), [EPTAS](/eptas/), [asymptotic PTAS](/asymptotic-ptas/) and [APX](/apx/) are used wherever a scheme or a constant ratio appears. Readers of the other chapters come here for them. The convention is that of the entries: a minimization algorithm has ratio $\rho \ge 1$ when $\mathrm{ALG} \le \rho \cdot \mathrm{OPT}$ on every input, and a maximization algorithm has ratio $\alpha \le 1$ when $\mathrm{ALG} \ge \alpha \cdot \mathrm{OPT}$.

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Ratios and classes of schemes.** The [approximation ratio](/approximation-ratio/), with its absolute and asymptotic forms; the [PTAS](/ptas/), the [FPTAS](/fptas/), whose time is polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$ as well, and the [EPTAS](/eptas/), whose time is $f(1/\varepsilon) \cdot \mathrm{poly}(n)$; the [asymptotic PTAS](/asymptotic-ptas/), which may add a constant; and the class [APX](/apx/) of problems with a constant ratio. The hardness side of APX is on the page [APX seen from hardness](/apx-class/).
2. **Rounding the numbers.** The [profit-indexed dynamic program](/profit-indexed-dynamic-programming/), [rounding and scaling](/rounding-and-scaling/), and the [knapsack FPTAS](/knapsack-fptas/) of Ibarra and Kim; the [trimming technique](/trimming-technique/) and the [subset sum FPTAS](/subset-sum-fptas/); the theorem that [strongly NP-hard problems have no FPTAS](/no-fptas-for-strongly-np-hard-problems/); and the [multiple knapsack problem](/multiple-knapsack-problem/) with the [Chekuri-Khanna PTAS](/chekuri-khanna-ptas/).
3. **Scheduling and bin packing.** [Dual approximation](/dual-approximation/), the [Hochbaum-Shmoys PTAS](/hochbaum-shmoys-makespan-ptas/) for makespan, the [Fernandez de la Vega-Lueker APTAS](/fernandez-de-la-vega-lueker-aptas/) for bin packing and the [Epstein-Levin scheme](/epstein-levin-eptas-bin-packing/), which moves few items as new ones arrive. The scheduling chapter has its own page on [the Hochbaum-Shmoys scheme](/hochbaum-shmoys-ptas/), and the covering chapter one on the [bin packing APTAS](/bin-packing-aptas/).
4. **Shifting and planar graphs.** The [shifting technique](/shifting-technique/) of Hochbaum and Maass, [Baker's technique](/baker-technique/) and [Baker's PTAS](/baker-planar-ptas/) for planar graphs, and the [planar Steiner tree PTAS](/planar-steiner-tree-ptas/).
5. **Geometric schemes.** The [guillotine cut technique](/guillotine-cut-technique/) and [Mitchell's PTAS](/mitchell-guillotine-ptas/), [portal-based dissection](/portal-based-dissection/), and the [Euclidean k-median problem](/euclidean-k-median/) with the [Arora-Raghavan-Rao PTAS](/arora-euclidean-k-median-ptas/). The TSP chapter gives the two TSP schemes in full: [Arora's](/arora-euclidean-tsp-ptas/) and [Mitchell's](/mitchell-euclidean-tsp-ptas/).
6. **Dense instances.** The [PTAS for max cut on dense graphs](/dense-max-cut-ptas/), by sampling.

## A first reading

Read the [approximation ratio](/approximation-ratio/), [PTAS](/ptas/) and [FPTAS](/fptas/) first, since everything else uses them. Then follow the knapsack thread: the [profit-indexed dynamic program](/profit-indexed-dynamic-programming/), [rounding and scaling](/rounding-and-scaling/) and the [knapsack FPTAS](/knapsack-fptas/), then the [trimming technique](/trimming-technique/) and the [subset sum FPTAS](/subset-sum-fptas/). The theorem on [strongly NP-hard problems](/no-fptas-for-strongly-np-hard-problems/) and [APX](/apx/) show the limits. Finish with [dual approximation](/dual-approximation/), the [Hochbaum-Shmoys PTAS](/hochbaum-shmoys-makespan-ptas/), the [shifting technique](/shifting-technique/) and [Baker's technique](/baker-technique/).

Skip at first the research entries: portal-based dissection, the $k$-median scheme, dense max cut, the Epstein-Levin scheme and planar Steiner tree. The Chekuri-Khanna PTAS and the guillotine entries are long and can wait for a second pass.

## Before you start

From Volume 1 you need [polynomial time](/polynomial-time/), [expected value](/expected-value/) and [linearity of expectation](/linearity-of-expectation/). From Volume 2 you need [optimal substructure](/optimal-substructure/) and [tabulation](/tabulation/), the two halves of dynamic programming. From Volume 15 you need the [knapsack problem](/knapsack-problem/), its [dynamic program](/knapsack-dynamic-programming/) and the [LP relaxation](/lp-relaxation/). The planar groups use [planar graphs](/planar-graph/), [breadth-first levels](/bfs-layers/) and [treewidth](/treewidth/) from Volume 7, and the geometric group uses the [quadtree](/quadtree/) of Volume 3. From this volume, read [bin packing](/bin-packing/), [makespan minimization](/makespan-minimization/) and the [Euclidean TSP](/euclidean-tsp/) before the entries that solve them.

The entries also assume NP-hardness, pseudo-polynomial time and strong NP-hardness, which any textbook on computational complexity defines. The EPTAS entry uses fixed-parameter tractability.
