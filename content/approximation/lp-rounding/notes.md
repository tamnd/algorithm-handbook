## History

The notes of Chapter 18.1 tell the early story: greedy set cover, the first primal-dual and local ratio algorithms, and the randomized rounding of Prabhakar Raghavan and Clark Thompson in 1987. LP rounding as a method took shape around the same years. George Nemhauser and Leslie Trotter showed in 1974 and 1975 that the vertex cover LP has a half-integral optimum whose integral part can be kept. Dorit Hochbaum used this in 1982 for ratio 2 for weighted vertex cover, and rounded the set cover LP at $1/f$ for ratio $f$. David Johnson's random assignment of 1974 satisfies a $1 - 2^{-k}$ fraction of clauses of length $k$. Michel Goemans and David Williamson combined it with LP rounding in 1994 for ratio $3/4$ for MAX-SAT, a ratio that Mihalis Yannakakis had reached first by another route.

László Lovász in 1975 and Vašek Chvátal in 1979 bounded greedy set cover by $H_n$ times the LP value, by an argument now called dual fitting. Kamal Jain, Mohammad Mahdian, Evangelos Markakis, Amin Saberi and Vijay Vazirani used it with a factor-revealing LP for greedy facility location in 2003. The primal-dual schema found its main use in network design. Ajit Agrawal, Philip Klein and R. Ravi, and independently Goemans and Williamson, published 2-approximations for Steiner forest in 1995, by growing the duals of all active components at once. Jain and Vazirani carried the idea to metric facility location and got ratio 3 (conference 1999, journal 2001).

Kamal Jain proved in 1998 that every vertex of the cut LP for survivable network design has an edge of value at least $1/2$ (journal version 2001). Fixing such edges and solving again gave ratio 2 for any connectivity requirements, where the primal-dual ratios had grown with the largest requirement. Mohit Singh and Lap Chi Lau added iterative relaxation in 2007 and found a minimum cost spanning tree that exceeds each degree bound by at most one. Lau, Ravi and Singh collected the method in a book in 2011.

For scheduling, Jan Karel Lenstra, David Shmoys and Éva Tardos showed in 1990 that a vertex of the LP for unrelated machines has a pseudo-forest support, which gives ratio 2 for makespan. Shmoys and Tardos extended this in 1993 to the generalized assignment problem, with cost at most the LP and load at most $2T$. The configuration LP goes back to the cutting stock work of Paul Gilmore and Ralph Gomory in 1961; Narendra Karmarkar and Richard Karp rounded it in 1982 to within $O(\log^2 \text{OPT})$ bins of the optimum, and Rebecca Hoberg and Thomas Rothvoss brought this down to $O(\log \text{OPT})$ in 2017. Jyh-Han Lin and Jeffrey Vitter introduced filtering in 1992, and Shmoys, Tardos and Karen Aardal used it in 1997 for the first constant ratio, 3.16, for metric facility location.

Dependent rounding grew out of the need to keep sums fixed. Alexander Ageev and Maxim Sviridenko introduced pipage rounding in 2004. Rajiv Gandhi, Samir Khuller, Srinivasan Parthasarathy and Aravind Srinivasan published dependent rounding on bipartite graphs in 2006, after Srinivasan's level-set rounding of 2001. Chandra Chekuri, Jan Vondrák and Rico Zenklusen gave swap rounding for matroids in 2010, with negative correlation and simpler steps than pipage rounding.

## Further reading

- David P. Williamson and David B. Shmoys, *The Design of Approximation Algorithms* (Cambridge University Press, 2011), treats deterministic and randomized rounding, the primal-dual method and iterative rounding, and returns to each technique on harder problems later in the book.
- Vijay V. Vazirani, *Approximation Algorithms* (Springer, 2001), gives LP-based algorithms in its second part, with clear chapters on dual fitting, Steiner forest and facility location.
- Lap Chi Lau, R. Ravi and Mohit Singh, *Iterative Methods in Combinatorial Optimization* (Cambridge University Press, 2011), develops iterative rounding and relaxation from bipartite matching up to network design.
- Michel X. Goemans and David P. Williamson, "The primal-dual method for approximation algorithms and its application to network design problems", Chapter 4 of Dorit S. Hochbaum (ed.), *Approximation Algorithms for NP-Hard Problems* (PWS, 1997), is the standard survey of the primal-dual schema.
