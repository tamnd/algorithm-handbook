## History

The logic came first. Kripke gave modal logic its semantics on structures of worlds and transitions in 1963, and Pnueli proposed in 1977 that temporal logic should state what a running program does. In the same year Lamport named safety and liveness properties, and Alpern and Schneider (1985) defined both on infinite words and showed that every property is the intersection of one of each. Model checking itself began in 1981 and 1982, when Clarke and Emerson, and independently Queille and Sifakis, checked branching-time formulas against finite state graphs. Clarke, Emerson and Sistla (1986) gave the labelling algorithm that runs in time linear in the structure and the formula. Sistla and Clarke (1985) proved that LTL model checking is PSPACE-complete. Clarke, Emerson and Sifakis received the Turing Award for this work in 2007.

Linear time got its own algorithm through automata. Büchi (1962) had introduced automata on infinite words to decide the monadic second-order theory of the natural numbers. Wolper, Vardi and Sistla (1983) translated temporal formulas into such automata, and Vardi and Wolper (1986) reduced LTL model checking to the emptiness of a product automaton. Courcoubetis, Vardi, Wolper and Yannakakis (1990) found the nested depth-first search that checks emptiness on the fly, and Holzmann built it into SPIN together with bitstate hashing. Partial-order reduction grew from Valmari's stubborn sets (1990), Godefroid's persistent and sleep sets, and Peled's ample sets (1994). Godefroid also built stateless search into VeriSoft (1997), and with Flanagan he gave dynamic partial-order reduction in 2005.

Symbolic methods let the checkers handle very large state spaces. Burch, Clarke, McMillan, Dill and Hwang (1990) computed fixed points with BDDs and checked systems with more than $10^{20}$ states, and McMillan's SMV (1993) made the method a tool for hardware. Biere, Cimatti, Clarke and Zhu (1999) replaced BDDs with SAT in bounded model checking. Sheeran, Singh and Stålmarck (2000) added induction, McMillan (2003) made the method complete with interpolants, and Bradley (2011) built IC3, which Eén, Mishchenko and Brayton reimplemented as PDR in the same year. Abstraction grew alongside: Graf and Saïdi gave predicate abstraction in 1997, Clarke, Grumberg, Jha, Lu and Veith the CEGAR loop in 2000, and Henzinger, Jhala, Majumdar and Sutre lazy abstraction in BLAST in 2002.

The fixed points and equivalences have older roots. Knaster and Tarski proved their fixed-point theorem for sets in 1928, and Tarski proved it for complete lattices in 1955. Park (1981) introduced bisimulation, and Milner made it the central equivalence of his calculus CCS. Kanellakis and Smolka (1990) and Paige and Tarjan (1987) computed it by partition refinement. Kozen defined the modal mu-calculus in 1983, Emerson and Lei (1986) studied the cost of nested fixed points, and Zielonka (1998) gave the recursive solver for the parity games to which mu-calculus model checking is equivalent. Beyond finite graphs, Karp and Miller (1969) decided coverability for vector addition systems, and Mayr (1981) and Kosaraju (1982) decided reachability. Alur and Dill (1990) gave timed automata their region graph, Bouajjani, Esparza and Maler (1997) the saturation procedures for pushdown systems, and Hansson and Jonsson (1994) the logic PCTL.

## Further reading

- Edmund M. Clarke, Orna Grumberg and Doron A. Peled, *Model Checking*, MIT Press, 1999. The first textbook, by one of the founders, covering CTL and LTL checking, BDDs and partial-order reduction.
- Christel Baier and Joost-Pieter Katoen, *Principles of Model Checking*, MIT Press, 2008. The standard modern textbook, with full proofs, many examples and long chapters on timed and probabilistic systems.
- Edmund M. Clarke, Thomas A. Henzinger, Helmut Veith and Roderick Bloem (editors), *Handbook of Model Checking*, Springer, 2018. Surveys of every branch of the field by the people who built it, from SAT-based methods to software and games.
- Moshe Y. Vardi and Pierre Wolper, "Reasoning about infinite computations", *Information and Computation* 115(1), 1994. The automata-theoretic approach to linear time, in its full journal form.
- Erich Grädel, Wolfgang Thomas and Thomas Wilke (editors), *Automata, Logics, and Infinite Games*, Springer, 2002. A graduate guide to Büchi and parity automata, parity games and the mu-calculus.
