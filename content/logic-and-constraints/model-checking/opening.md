Peterson's mutual exclusion algorithm for two processes fits in a dozen lines: each process raises its flag, gives the turn to the other, and waits. Its state is the two program counters, the two flags and the turn, 200 vectors in all, and a breadth-first search from the initial state reaches 34 of them. None has both processes in the critical section, so the algorithm is correct. Now swap the two assignments, so that each process gives away the turn before it raises its flag. The search reaches 50 states and finds one with both processes critical, and the parent pointers give a run of 8 steps that leads there. A test could run the program for hours without hitting that interleaving. The search visits every interleaving, so it cannot miss it.

That is model checking: a finite model of a system, a property in temporal logic, and an algorithm that decides whether every run of the model has the property, and returns a counterexample when one does not. The algorithms are graph searches and fixed-point computations, the hard part is the size of the state space, and most of the chapter is about ways to fight it: on-the-fly search, hashing, reductions by independence and symmetry, BDDs, SAT solvers and abstraction. Clarke, Emerson and Sifakis received the Turing Award for the method in 2007.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Systems and properties.** [Kripke structures](/kripke-structure/), [labelled transition systems](/labelled-transition-system/) and [Petri nets](/petri-net/) as models; [safety](/safety-property/) and [liveness](/liveness-property/) as the two kinds of property.
2. **Temporal logics.** [CTL](/computation-tree-logic/), [LTL](/linear-temporal-logic/) and [CTL*](/ctl-star/), [fairness](/fairness/), and [probabilistic CTL](/probabilistic-ctl/).
3. **Reachability and CTL model checking.** [Invariant checking](/invariant-checking/) by search, the [labelling algorithm](/ctl-labelling-algorithm/) that checks CTL in linear time, and [counterexamples](/counterexample-generation/).
4. **LTL and automata.** [LTL model checking](/ltl-model-checking/) through [Büchi automata](/ltl-to-buchi-translation/) and [nested depth-first search](/nested-depth-first-search/), and why the problem is PSPACE-complete.
5. **Fixed points and equivalences.** [Fixed points](/fixed-point/) and the [Knaster-Tarski theorem](/knaster-tarski-theorem/), [bisimulation](/bisimulation/), [simulation](/simulation-relation/) and their relatives.
6. **Explicit-state search and its reductions.** [Explicit-state checking](/explicit-state-model-checking/), the [state explosion](/state-explosion-problem/), [bitstate hashing](/bitstate-hashing/), [partial-order reduction](/partial-order-reduction/), [symmetry](/symmetry-reduction/) and [compositional](/compositional-verification/) methods.
7. **Symbolic and SAT-based model checking.** [BDD-based](/symbolic-model-checking/) fixed points, [bounded model checking](/bounded-model-checking/), [k-induction](/k-induction/), [interpolation](/interpolation-based-model-checking/) and [IC3](/ic3-pdr/).
8. **Abstraction.** [Predicate abstraction](/predicate-abstraction/), [CEGAR](/cegar/) and [lazy abstraction](/lazy-abstraction/).
9. **The mu-calculus and parity games.** The [modal mu-calculus](/modal-mu-calculus/), [parity games](/parity-game/) and [Zielonka's algorithm](/zielonka-algorithm/).
10. **Probabilities, clocks, stacks and counters.** [PCTL model checking](/pctl-model-checking/), [timed automata](/timed-automata/), [pushdown systems](/pushdown-system/) and [Petri net coverability](/coverability/).
