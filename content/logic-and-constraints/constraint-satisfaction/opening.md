A sudoku puzzle is a constraint satisfaction problem. It has 81 variables, one per cell, each with the domain $\{1, \ldots, 9\}$, and 27 constraints, one for each row, column and box, that say the nine cells of a unit take different values. The given digits shrink some domains to one value. Then each constraint removes that value from the other cells of its units, which may leave a cell with one value, which removes more values in turn. Many easy puzzles are solved by this propagation alone. Hard ones need a guess, more propagation, and a step back when some cell is left with no value. Propagation, search, and stepping back are what this chapter is about.

The same model fits timetables, rosters, configuration of products, frequency assignment and puzzles of every kind. A [constraint satisfaction problem](/constraint-satisfaction-problem/) is NP-complete in general, since graph colouring is one, so the methods aim to make the search small rather than polynomial. Some problems are easy all the same, because their constraint graph is a tree, or because their constraints come from a tractable language. The chapter covers both the general tools and these easy cases.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Problems and models.** The [CSP](/constraint-satisfaction-problem/) itself, the [constraint graph](/constraint-graph/), [constraint programming](/constraint-programming/) as a way of working, and the dual graph for constraints of higher arity.
2. **Local consistency.** [Node consistency](/node-consistency/), [arc consistency](/arc-consistency/), [constraint propagation](/constraint-propagation/) as a fixpoint, [AC-3](/ac-3/) and [AC-4](/ac-4/), bounds and singleton arc consistency, [path consistency](/path-consistency/) and PC-2, [k-consistency](/k-consistency/), and [global constraints](/global-constraint/).
3. **Search and heuristics.** [Forward checking](/forward-checking/), [maintaining arc consistency](/maintaining-arc-consistency/), the [minimum remaining values](/minimum-remaining-values/) and degree heuristics, least constraining value, and [min-conflicts](/min-conflicts/) local search.
4. **Smarter backtracking.** [Graph-based backjumping](/graph-based-backjumping/), [conflict-directed backjumping](/conflict-directed-backjumping/), nogood learning and dynamic backtracking.
5. **All-different and symmetry.** The [all-different constraint](/all-different-constraint/), [Régin's filter](/regin-all-different-filtering/), and symmetry breaking.
6. **Structure.** [Freuder's theorem](/freuder-theorem/), [tree-structured CSPs](/tree-structured-csp/), [cutset conditioning](/cutset-conditioning/) and [bucket elimination](/bucket-elimination/).
7. **Costs, real numbers and time.** Maximum CSP, soft constraints, numeric CSPs, interval propagation and the [simple temporal problem](/simple-temporal-problem/).
8. **Complexity of constraint languages.** Boolean CSPs, polymorphisms, [Schaefer's theorem](/schaefer-dichotomy-theorem/), counting CSPs and the [CSP dichotomy theorem](/csp-dichotomy-theorem/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these thirteen entries in order: [the CSP](/constraint-satisfaction-problem/), [the constraint graph](/constraint-graph/), [arc consistency](/arc-consistency/), [constraint propagation](/constraint-propagation/), [AC-3](/ac-3/), [forward checking](/forward-checking/), [minimum remaining values](/minimum-remaining-values/), [maintaining arc consistency](/maintaining-arc-consistency/), [min-conflicts](/min-conflicts/), [conflict-directed backjumping](/conflict-directed-backjumping/), [the all-different constraint](/all-different-constraint/), [tree-structured CSPs](/tree-structured-csp/) and [cutset conditioning](/cutset-conditioning/). They give the model, the main propagation algorithm, the standard search with its heuristics, one form of backjumping, the most used global constraint and the first structural result. Skip path consistency and the higher consistency levels, the numeric and soft problems of group 7, and all of group 8 on a first pass. The last group needs some algebra and is research-level in its final entries.

## Before you start

Search in this chapter builds on [chronological backtracking](/chronological-backtracking/) and [variable ordering heuristics](/variable-ordering-heuristics/) from Volume 2. The models use [graph colouring](/graph-coloring/) from Volume 7 as their standard example. Régin's filter needs [bipartite matching](/bipartite-matching/) and [strongly connected components](/strongly-connected-components/), also from Volume 7, and the simple temporal problem needs [shortest paths](/shortest-path/) and [negative cycles](/negative-cycle/). Bucket elimination uses [treewidth](/treewidth/), and numeric CSPs use [interval arithmetic](/interval-arithmetic/) from Volume 12. The complexity results of the last group assume [NP-completeness](/np-complete/) from Volume 38. Chapter 16.1 on [SAT](/sat/) is useful but not required: a SAT formula is a CSP with Boolean domains.
