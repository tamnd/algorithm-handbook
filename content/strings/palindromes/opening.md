A [palindrome](/palindrome/) is a string that equals its own reversal, such as `racecar` or `abba`. The definition takes one line, but palindromes inside a longer string have a rich structure, and the problems about them reward care. In DNA, the sites where many restriction enzymes cut read the same on both strands, which makes them palindromes in a changed sense. In programming contests, palindromes are among the most common string tasks. In the combinatorics of words, they connect with Sturmian words and with the theory of periods.

Two facts carry most of the chapter. First, every palindrome inside a string has a center, at a symbol or between two symbols, and the palindromes at one center are nested: the longest one determines all the others. So $2n + 1$ numbers describe every palindromic substring of a string of length $n$, and [Manacher's algorithm](/manacher/) computes them in linear time. Second, a string of length $n$ has at most $n$ distinct nonempty palindromes, because each new symbol adds at most one that was not there before. So one node for each distinct palindrome fits in linear space, and the [palindromic tree](/palindromic-tree/) is that structure.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in five groups, read in order.

1. **Palindromes and how many there are.** The definition, the [reverse-complement palindrome](/reverse-complement-palindrome/) of DNA, [palindrome recognition](/palindrome-recognition/) with two pointers in $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ comparisons, and the [palindrome count bound](/palindrome-count-bound/).
2. **Finding all palindromes.** The [longest palindromic substring](/longest-palindromic-substring/), Manacher's algorithm, [counting palindromic substrings](/count-palindromic-substrings/) by occurrence and as distinct strings, [palindrome substring queries](/palindrome-substring-queries/) in constant time each, and [gapped palindromes](/gapped-palindrome/), whose two arms are separated by a gap.
3. **Making a string a palindrome.** The [shortest palindrome](/shortest-palindrome/) by adding symbols in front, the [palindromic closure](/palindromic-closure/) by adding them at the end, and the [palindrome edit distance](/palindrome-edit-distance/).
4. **Cutting a string into palindromes.** The [partitioning](/palindrome-partitioning/) problem in its three forms, listing all partitions by [backtracking](/palindrome-partition-backtracking/), and the [fewest pieces](/minimum-palindrome-partition/) by a [quadratic dynamic program](/palindrome-partition-dp/).
5. **The palindromic tree.** The eertree, its [series links](/series-links/), which bring the fewest pieces down to $O(n \log n)$ time, and [rich strings](/rich-string/), which reach the count bound.
