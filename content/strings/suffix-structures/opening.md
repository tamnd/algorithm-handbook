The matchers of the first two chapters spend time on the pattern and then read the whole text. When the text is fixed and the patterns come later, the trade turns around: spend time once on the text, and then answer each query in time that depends on the pattern, not on the text. A genome is searched for millions of short reads; a corpus of source code is searched for any string a user types; a compressor looks for long repeats. All of these rest on one fact. Every substring of a text is a prefix of one of its [suffixes](/suffix/), so a structure that holds all suffixes in order holds all substrings.

The chapter covers the four structures built on this fact. The [suffix tree](/suffix-tree/) is the oldest and the most general: a compacted [trie](/trie/) of the suffixes with at most $2n + 1$ nodes. The [suffix array](/suffix-array/) keeps only the sorted list of start positions and takes $n$ integers; with the [LCP array](/lcp-array/) beside it, it does nearly everything the tree does. The [suffix automaton](/suffix-automaton/) is the smallest automaton that recognizes the substrings. The [FM-index](/fm-index/) stores the same information in space close to the size of the compressed text. Most of the chapter is about building these structures in linear time and about the questions each one answers in a single pass.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Suffixes and indexes.** The suffix, the [sentinel](/sentinel-character/) that keeps one suffix from being a prefix of another, the [full-text index](/full-text-index/) as an abstract type, and the [suffix trie](/suffix-trie/), which has $\Theta(n^2)$ nodes in the worst case.
2. **The suffix array.** The array itself, [pattern search](/suffix-array-search/) by two binary searches, [keyword-in-context](/keyword-in-context-search/) listings, and the [circular suffix array](/circular-suffix-array/) of rotations.
3. **Building the suffix array.** [Prefix doubling](/prefix-doubling/) in the forms of [Karp-Miller-Rosenberg](/karp-miller-rosenberg-algorithm/), [Manber-Myers](/manber-myers-algorithm/) and [Larsson-Sadakane](/larsson-sadakane-algorithm/), all $O(n \log n)$, and the linear-time [DC3](/dc3-skew-algorithm/) and [SA-IS](/sa-is/).
4. **The LCP array and its uses.** [Kasai's algorithm](/kasai-algorithm/), the [reduction to range minima](/lcp-rmq-reduction/), [longest common extension](/longest-common-extension-query/) in $O(1)$ time, and four problems solved by one scan: [distinct substrings](/count-distinct-substrings/), the [longest repeated substring](/longest-repeated-substring/), the [longest common substring](/longest-common-substring/) and [shortest unique substrings](/shortest-unique-substring/).
5. **The suffix tree.** Its [size bound](/suffix-tree-size-theorem/), the [implicit tree](/implicit-suffix-tree/), [suffix links](/suffix-link/), and the four linear-time constructions of [Ukkonen](/ukkonen-algorithm/), [McCreight](/mccreight-algorithm/), [Weiner](/weiner-algorithm/) and [Farach](/farach-algorithm/).
6. **Suffix trees at work.** The [generalized suffix tree](/generalized-suffix-tree/) of several strings, [matching statistics](/matching-statistics/), [maximal repeats](/maximal-repeats/), and the [LCP interval tree](/lcp-interval-tree/) and [enhanced suffix array](/enhanced-suffix-array/), which run tree algorithms on arrays.
7. **The suffix automaton.** The automaton, its [size theorem](/suffix-automaton-size-theorem/), and the [generalized automaton](/generalized-suffix-automaton/) of a set of strings.
8. **Compressed indexes and document retrieval.** The FM-index, the [r-index](/r-index/) for repetitive text, the [compressed suffix array](/compressed-suffix-array/) and [compressed suffix tree](/compressed-suffix-tree/), and the queries [document listing](/document-listing/) and [top-k retrieval](/top-k-document-retrieval/).
