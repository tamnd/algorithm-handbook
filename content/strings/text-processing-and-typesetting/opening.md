The rest of this volume treats a string as a sequence of symbols from an alphabet. Real text is harder. The word "café" can be four code points or five, and both forms must compare equal. The flag of France is two code points that a reader sees as one character. A line of Hebrew with an English word in it is stored in one order and shown in another. A paragraph must be cut into lines of equal width with even spaces, and a file that two people have edited must be merged without losing either set of changes. This chapter collects the algorithms that do this work: the Unicode algorithms that every text system runs, the line and page breaking of TeX, and the diff and merge of version control.

Two kinds of result run through the chapter. The Unicode algorithms are specifications. Their entries follow Unicode 16.0.0 and its annexes, and an implementation is correct when it matches the standard and passes its conformance tests, not when it meets a cost bound. The typesetting and diff algorithms are optimization methods, and here the entries say exactly what is optimized. [Myers' diff algorithm](/myers-diff/) finds a shortest edit script, but patience and histogram diff do not try to. Knuth-Plass line breaking is optimal for its model of demerits, and greedy breaking is not.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Code points and encodings.** A [code point](/unicode-code-point/) is an integer from 0 to 10FFFF. [UTF-8](/utf-8/) writes each scalar value in one to four bytes, and [UTF-16](/utf-16/) in one or two 16-bit units. [Punycode](/punycode/) writes Unicode in the letters, digits and hyphens of domain names, and [Keiser-Lemire validation](/simd-utf-8-validation/) checks UTF-8 with a few vector instructions per block.
2. **Equal strings, different code points.** [Canonical equivalence](/canonical-equivalence/) says when two strings are the same text, and the [normalization forms](/unicode-normalization-forms/) NFD, NFC, NFKD and NFKC pick one string from each class. The [normalization algorithm](/unicode-normalization-algorithm/) decomposes, sorts the marks by combining class and composes again, with [Hangul syllables](/hangul-syllable-composition/) done by arithmetic. [Case folding](/unicode-case-folding/) and [confusable detection](/unicode-confusable-detection/) compare strings up to case and up to look.
3. **Characters, words and sorting order.** The [extended grapheme cluster](/extended-grapheme-cluster/) is the character a reader sees. [Text segmentation](/unicode-text-segmentation/) finds grapheme, word and sentence boundaries with the ordered rules of UAX 29, and the [collation algorithm](/unicode-collation-algorithm/) sorts strings by levels of weights.
4. **From characters to glyphs and lines.** [Kerning](/kerning/) and [text shaping](/text-shaping/) turn characters into positioned glyphs. The [bidirectional algorithm](/unicode-bidirectional-algorithm/) gives the display order of mixed text, and the [line breaking algorithm](/unicode-line-breaking-algorithm/) says where a line may end, but not where it should.
5. **Breaking paragraphs into lines.** The [box, glue and penalty model](/box-glue-penalty-model/) and the [badness](/typesetting-badness/) of a line define the problem. [Greedy line breaking](/greedy-line-breaking/) fills one line at a time. The [least weight subsequence](/least-weight-subsequence/) problem chooses all breaks at once, and the [Hirschberg-Larmore](/hirschberg-larmore-algorithm/) and [Wilber](/wilber-algorithm/) algorithms solve it in $O(n \log n)$ and $O(n)$ time when the weight satisfies the quadrangle inequality.
6. **Hyphenation.** The [hyphenation problem](/hyphenation-problem/), [Liang's patterns](/liang-hyphenation/) as used by TeX, the [packed trie](/packed-trie/) that stores them, and the [generation of patterns](/hyphenation-pattern-generation/) from a word list.
7. **Pages, tables and formulas.** [Widows and orphans](/widows-and-orphans/), the [page breaking problem](/page-breaking-problem/), the [best-fit page builder](/best-fit-page-breaking/) of TeX, [optimal pagination](/optimal-pagination/) when figures keep their order, and the [NP-completeness](/pagination-np-completeness/) of pagination when they do not. The [table layout problem](/table-layout-problem/) is NP-complete as well, and [TeX math layout](/tex-math-layout/) builds a formula from the inside out.
8. **Differences between files.** [Heckel's diff](/heckel-diff/), [patience diff](/patience-diff/) and [histogram diff](/histogram-diff/) anchor on rare lines, [Tichy's block moves](/tichy-block-moves/) describe moved text with the fewest copies, and [Chawathe's method](/chawathe-tree-diff/) finds an edit script between two trees.
9. **Merging.** [Three-way merge](/three-way-merge/) combines two versions with their ancestor, [diff3](/diff3/) is the standard algorithm, conflicts and all, and [semistructured merge](/semistructured-merge/) merges the declarations of programs by name.

## A first reading

Read [Unicode code point](/unicode-code-point/), [UTF-8](/utf-8/), [canonical equivalence](/canonical-equivalence/), the [normalization forms](/unicode-normalization-forms/) and the [extended grapheme cluster](/extended-grapheme-cluster/) first; they explain what a string of real text is. Then read the [box, glue and penalty model](/box-glue-penalty-model/), [badness](/typesetting-badness/), [greedy line breaking](/greedy-line-breaking/) and [Liang's hyphenation](/liang-hyphenation/) for typesetting, and [patience diff](/patience-diff/), [three-way merge](/three-way-merge/) and [diff3](/diff3/) for version control. The three parts of the chapter are nearly independent, so you can read them in any order.

The collation, bidirectional and confusable algorithms, the fast least weight subsequence methods, pattern generation, the pagination and table results, the tree diff and semistructured merge can wait for a second reading.

## Before you start

The optimal line breaking of TeX itself is [Knuth-Plass line breaking](/knuth-plass-line-breaking/) in Volume 2, with [optimal text wrapping](/optimal-text-wrapping/), [printing neatly](/printing-neatly/), the [Monge property](/monge-property/) and [SMAWK](/smawk-algorithm/) beside it; the line breaking part of this chapter leans on all of them. The diff entries build on the [longest common subsequence](/longest-common-subsequence/), the [shortest edit script](/shortest-edit-script/), Myers' diff and [Hunt-Szymanski](/hunt-szymanski/) of Volume 6, and on the [edit distance](/edit-distance/) of Chapter 8.4. Hyphenation uses the [trie](/trie/) of Volume 3, UTF-8 is a [prefix code](/prefix-code/) in the sense of Volume 24, and the hardness results reduce from [partition](/partition-problem/) and [3-partition](/three-partition/) of Volume 39. Tree diff relates to the [tree edit distance](/tree-edit-distance/) of Volume 7.
