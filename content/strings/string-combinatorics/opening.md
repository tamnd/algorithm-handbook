The other chapters of this volume search texts. This one studies words for their own sake: what a word can repeat, how it can be cut, how many words of a kind there are, and which patterns no long word can avoid. The subject is called combinatorics on words, and it is older than the algorithms. Thue showed in 1906 that an infinite word over three letters can avoid two equal adjacent blocks; de Bruijn counted in 1946 the shortest cyclic sequences that contain every word of a given length. The answers turned into tools. The [border](/border/) and the [period](/string-period/) drive Knuth-Morris-Pratt and the two-way matcher, the [Lyndon factorization](/lyndon-factorization/) gives a canonical cut of every word, and the [runs](/maximal-repetition/) of a text measure how repetitive it is.

Most entries are short definitions and theorems with a proof; a few are algorithms, and those run in linear time. The facts here are used all over the volume, so the chapter is also a reference to come back to.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Alphabets, borders and periods.** The [alphabet](/alphabet/), borders and periods, [periodicity detection](/periodicity-detection/), the [Fine and Wilf theorem](/fine-and-wilf-theorem/) on two periods, and the [critical factorization](/critical-factorization/) theorem, which relates local and global periods.
2. **Rotations and primitive words.** [Conjugate](/word-conjugacy/) words, [primitive words](/primitive-word/) and the [primitive root](/primitive-root-of-a-word/), and the [least rotation](/minimal-rotation/) by [Booth's algorithm](/booth-algorithm/) and by [Shiloach's](/shiloach-least-rotation/).
3. **Necklaces and Lyndon words.** [Necklaces](/necklace/), [bracelets](/bracelet/) and [Lyndon words](/lyndon-word/), counted by [Witt's formula](/witt-formula/) and listed by the [FKM algorithm](/fkm-algorithm/) and its [recursive form](/cattell-necklace-generation/).
4. **Lyndon factorization.** The [Chen-Fox-Lyndon theorem](/lyndon-schutzenberger-theorem/), [Duval's algorithm](/duval-algorithm/), the [Lyndon array](/lyndon-array/) and the [longest Lyndon substring](/longest-lyndon-substring/).
5. **de Bruijn sequences and superstrings.** The [de Bruijn graph](/de-bruijn-graph/), [de Bruijn sequences](/de-bruijn-sequence/) and the [theorem](/de-bruijn-existence-theorem/) that they exist, then the NP-hard [shortest common superstring](/shortest-common-superstring/) and [greedy merging](/greedy-superstring-merge/).
6. **Repetitions.** [Squares](/square/), [cubes](/cube/), [overlaps](/overlap/) and runs; the [Main-Lorentz algorithm](/main-lorentz-algorithm/), the [runs theorem](/runs-theorem/) and the [linear-time computation](/lyndon-based-runs-computation/) of all runs; and [counting distinct squares](/counting-distinct-squares/).
7. **Avoiding repetitions.** [Morphisms](/string-morphism/), the [Thue-Morse word](/thue-morse-word/), [overlap-free](/overlap-free-word/) and [square-free](/square-free-word/) words, [Thue's theorem](/thue-theorem/), the [repetition threshold](/repetition-threshold/) and [Dejean's theorem](/dejean-theorem/), and [Zimin words](/zimin-word/), which decide [avoidability](/avoidability/) of patterns.
8. **Letter counts.** The [Parikh vector](/parikh-vector/), [Abelian equivalence](/abelian-equivalence/), [Abelian squares](/abelian-square/), [Keränen's theorem](/keranen-theorem/) and [Abelian complexity](/abelian-complexity/).
9. **Infinite words and complexity.** [Morphic words](/morphic-word/), [subword complexity](/subword-complexity/), the [Morse-Hedlund theorem](/morse-hedlund-theorem/), [Sturmian words](/sturmian-word/) and their [characterizations](/sturmian-characterization/), the [Fibonacci word](/fibonacci-word/) and [Christoffel words](/christoffel-word/).
10. **Absent words and attractors.** [Minimal absent words](/minimal-absent-word/), the [problem](/minimal-absent-words/) of listing them and its [linear-time algorithm](/minimal-absent-words-algorithm/), and the [string attractor](/string-attractor/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these thirteen entries in order: [alphabet](/alphabet/), [border](/border/), [string period](/string-period/), [the Fine and Wilf theorem](/fine-and-wilf-theorem/), [word conjugacy](/word-conjugacy/), [primitive word](/primitive-word/), [minimal rotation](/minimal-rotation/), [necklace](/necklace/), [Lyndon word](/lyndon-word/), [Lyndon factorization](/lyndon-factorization/), [Duval's algorithm](/duval-algorithm/), [de Bruijn sequence](/de-bruijn-sequence/) and [square](/square/). Add the [Thue-Morse word](/thue-morse-word/) and [square-free words](/square-free-word/) to see the oldest results of the subject. The rest of groups 6 to 10 is closer to research and can wait until a problem needs it: the runs are the place to start for repetitions in texts, the Sturmian words for infinite words, and the minimal absent words for sequence analysis.

## Before you start

Words form a free [monoid](/monoid/) under concatenation, from Volume 3, and conjugacy is an [equivalence relation](/equivalence-relation/), also from Volume 3. The [lexicographic order](/lexicographic-order/) of Volume 14 is behind every Lyndon word and least rotation. The [greatest common divisor](/greatest-common-divisor/) and [Möbius inversion](/mobius-inversion-formula/) from Volume 10 appear in the Fine and Wilf theorem and in Witt's formula, and [Burnside's lemma](/burnside-lemma/) from Volume 14 counts necklaces and bracelets. de Bruijn sequences are [Eulerian circuits](/eulerian-circuit/) in a [directed graph](/directed-graph/), from Volume 7, and [NP-hardness](/np-hard/) from Volume 38 explains why superstrings are hard. From this volume, the [prefix function](/prefix-function/) and the [Z-algorithm](/z-algorithm/) of Chapter 8.1 compute borders and periods, and the [suffix array](/suffix-array/), [longest common extension](/longest-common-extension-query/) queries and the [suffix automaton](/suffix-automaton/) of Chapter 8.3 are used for runs and absent words.
