Exact matching fails as soon as the text holds a typing error, a sequencing error or a different spelling of a name. Approximate matching asks for the places where a pattern occurs with a few errors, and for that it first needs a measure of how far apart two strings are. The standard measure is the [edit distance](/edit-distance/): the least number of single-character insertions, deletions and substitutions that turn one string into the other. A spelling checker suggests the dictionary words at distance 1 or 2 from a misspelt word; a read aligner places a short DNA read in a genome despite a few wrong bases; a record linkage job finds that two entries name the same person.

The chapter starts from one table. The [Wagner-Fischer algorithm](/wagner-fischer-algorithm/) fills an $(m+1) \times (n+1)$ table of distances between prefixes, and [Sellers' algorithm](/sellers-algorithm/) changes one row of it to search a text. Almost every faster method computes the same table, or the part of it that matters, in a smarter way: by cutting it off, by packing a column into a machine word, by jumping along its diagonals, or by never computing it at all on text that a filter has ruled out.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Measuring the difference.** [Edit operations](/edit-operations/), the edit distance and the Wagner-Fischer table, then the [weighted](/weighted-edit-distance/), [Damerau-Levenshtein](/damerau-levenshtein-distance/), [Jaro-Winkler](/jaro-winkler-distance/) and [block](/block-edit-distance/) variants, and the [conditional lower bound](/edit-distance-hardness/) that rules out a strongly subquadratic algorithm under the Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis.
2. **Matching by sound.** [Phonetic matching](/phonetic-string-matching/) with [Soundex](/soundex/) and [Metaphone](/metaphone/), where names match when their codes are equal.
3. **Searching a text with errors.** [Approximate string matching](/approximate-string-matching/), its two main forms, the [k-differences](/k-differences-problem/) and [k-mismatch](/k-mismatch-problem/) problems, Sellers' algorithm, and [Ukkonen's cut-off](/ukkonen-cutoff-algorithm/), which takes $O(kn)$ expected time.
4. **Bit-parallel search.** The [bitap](/bitap-algorithm/) method for mismatches, the [Wu-Manber](/wu-manber-approximate-matching/) algorithm of the `agrep` tool, and [Myers' algorithm](/myers-bit-parallel-algorithm/), which runs in $O(\lceil m/w \rceil n)$ time.
5. **Diagonals and extensions.** The [Landau-Vishkin](/landau-vishkin-algorithm/) and [Galil-Park](/galil-park-algorithm/) algorithms in $O(kn)$ time, and [Cole-Hariharan](/cole-hariharan-algorithm/), which is linear when the pattern is long compared with $k^4$.
6. **Mismatches by convolution.** [Hamming string matching](/hamming-string-matching/), the [Fischer-Paterson](/fischer-paterson-algorithm/) reduction to convolutions, and the [Amir-Lewenstein-Porat](/amir-lewenstein-porat-algorithm/) algorithm.
7. **Filters.** [Pigeonhole filtering](/pigeonhole-filtering/), the [q-gram lemma](/q-gram-lemma/) and [q-gram filtering](/q-gram-filtering/), and the [Chang-Lawler](/chang-lawler-algorithm/) algorithm, which reads only part of a random text.
8. **Dictionaries and collections.** [Approximate dictionary lookup](/approximate-dictionary-lookup/), the [Levenshtein automaton](/levenshtein-automaton/), and the [string similarity join](/string-similarity-join/) of two collections.
