## History

Several of the families are older than the threat. Robert McEliece proposed encryption with a hidden Goppa code in 1978, the year Berlekamp, McEliece and van Tilborg proved that decoding a general linear code is NP-complete, and Harald Niederreiter gave the dual form with syndromes in 1986. The attacks are as old: Eugene Prange's information set decoding dates from 1962, and Lee and Brickell (1988) and Stern (1989) refined it. Shamir broke the Merkle-Hellman knapsack in 1982, Lagarias and Odlyzko then broke low-density knapsacks with lattice reduction, and Patarin broke the C* scheme of Matsumoto and Imai in 1995 and proposed HFE in its place.

Lattice cryptography grew from two results of 1996. Ajtai showed that the short integer solution problem is hard on average if lattice problems are hard in the worst case, by a classical reduction. In the same year Hoffstein, Pipher and Silverman presented NTRU, a fast ring-based scheme with no such proof. Regev defined learning with errors in 2005 and proved it as hard as worst-case lattice problems for quantum algorithms, work that won the Gödel Prize in 2018. Peikert (2009) and Brakerski, Langlois, Peikert, Regev and Stehlé (2013) gave classical reductions with weaker parameters. Lyubashevsky, Peikert and Regev (2010) moved LWE into rings, and Langlois and Stehlé (2015) into modules, the setting of Kyber and Dilithium. For signatures, Gentry, Peikert and Vaikuntanathan (2008) fixed the leak through which Nguyen and Regev (2006) had broken GGH and NTRUSign, and Lyubashevsky introduced Fiat-Shamir with aborts in 2009 and 2012.

Shor's algorithm, first presented at FOCS in 1994, gave the field its purpose. NIST called for proposals in December 2016 and received 82 by the deadline of November 30, 2017; 69 entered round 1. Round 2 (January 2019) had 26 candidates, and round 3 (July 2020) seven finalists and eight alternates. On July 5, 2022 NIST selected Kyber, Dilithium, Falcon and SPHINCS+, and sent BIKE, Classic McEliece, HQC and SIKE to a fourth round. ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA became FIPS 203, 204 and 205 on August 13, 2024, and NIST selected HQC in March 2025. A separate call for additional signatures received 50 submissions by June 2023; nine of them reached round 3 in May 2026.

The process was also a record of breaks. Rainbow, a layered form of unbalanced oil and vinegar by Ding and Schmidt (2005), was a finalist when Beullens recovered a level-1 key in about 53 hours on a laptop in 2022. The isogeny line runs from Couveignes (1997) and Rostovtsev and Stolbunov (2006) through Jao and De Feo's SIDH (2011) to the KEM SIKE. Castryck and Decru posted their attack on SIDH on July 30, 2022, weeks after SIKE entered round 4; it appeared at EUROCRYPT 2023 with the follow-up attacks of Maino, Martindale, Panny, Pope and Wesolowski and of Robert. CSIDH (2018), based on a commutative group action, is not affected.

Deployment ran alongside. Google tested NewHope in Chrome in 2016 as CECPQ1. OpenSSH made the hybrid sntrup761x25519-sha512 its default in version 9.0 (2022) and mlkem768x25519-sha256 in version 10.0 (2025). Signal added PQXDH in September 2023, Apple added PQ3 to iMessage in 2024, and Chrome moved to X25519MLKEM768 in version 131.

## Further reading

- Daniel J. Bernstein, Johannes Buchmann and Erik Dahmen (editors), *Post-Quantum Cryptography*, Springer, 2009. Survey chapters on each family by its main researchers.
- Chris Peikert, *A Decade of Lattice Cryptography*, Foundations and Trends in Theoretical Computer Science 10(4), 2016. The standard survey of SIS, LWE, their ring forms, trapdoors and the reductions.
- Oded Regev, *On Lattices, Learning with Errors, Random Linear Codes, and Cryptography*, Journal of the ACM 56(6), 2009. The paper that defined LWE, its encryption scheme and its quantum reduction.
- NIST IR 8413, *Status Report on the Third Round of the NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardization Process*, 2022. Why NIST chose Kyber, Dilithium, Falcon and SPHINCS+, and its view of every other candidate.
- Luca De Feo, *Mathematics of Isogeny Based Cryptography*, lecture notes, 2017. Elliptic curves, isogeny graphs and the schemes built on them.
- Andrew M. Odlyzko, *The Rise and Fall of Knapsack Cryptosystems*, in Cryptology and Computational Number Theory, AMS, 1990. How lattice reduction broke a whole family.
