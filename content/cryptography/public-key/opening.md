Take $N = 47 \cdot 59 = 2773$ and $e = 17$, and publish the pair $(N, e)$. Anyone can encrypt the message $920$ as $920^{17} \bmod 2773 = 948$. To undo it you need $d = 17^{-1} \bmod 2668 = 157$, and $2668 = 46 \cdot 58$ is easy to compute only for someone who knows the factors. With a modulus of 3072 binary digits instead of 12, nobody can find them, and that asymmetry is [RSA](/rsa-cryptosystem/). The toy key falls to trial division at once; it is here only to make the numbers checkable.

The textbook scheme is not yet encryption anyone should use. The same message always gives the same ciphertext, so an eavesdropper who guesses $920$ can check the guess. Multiplying the ciphertext by $2^{17}$ gives a valid ciphertext of $1840$, so an attacker can change a message without reading it. Most of the chapter is about closing these gaps: definitions that say exactly what a ciphertext must hide, from [IND-CPA](/ind-cpa-security/) to [IND-CCA2](/ind-cca-security/), and schemes that meet them under a named assumption, either in the standard model or in the [random oracle model](/random-oracle-model/). Bleichenbacher showed in 1998 that a server which only says "bad padding" leaks enough to decrypt [PKCS #1 v1.5](/rsa-pkcs1-v1-5-encryption/) ciphertexts, so the chosen-ciphertext notions describe real attackers. Every scheme here based on factoring or discrete logarithms falls to [Shor's algorithm](/shor-algorithm/) on a large quantum computer; the replacements belong to [post-quantum cryptography](/post-quantum-cryptography/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Trapdoors and assumptions.** [Public-key cryptography](/public-key-cryptography/), the syntax of [public-key encryption](/public-key-encryption/), [hardness assumptions](/computational-hardness-assumption/), [one-way](/one-way-function/) and [trapdoor](/trapdoor-one-way-function/) functions, [hard-core predicates](/hard-core-predicate/), and the broken [Merkle-Hellman knapsack](/merkle-hellman-knapsack-cryptosystem/).
2. **RSA and factoring.** [RSA](/rsa-cryptosystem/), the [RSA problem](/rsa-problem/), [Rabin](/rabin-cryptosystem/) and [Wiener's attack](/wiener-attack/) on small private exponents.
3. **What a ciphertext must hide.** [IND-CPA](/ind-cpa-security/), [probabilistic encryption](/probabilistic-encryption/), the [chosen-ciphertext attack](/chosen-ciphertext-attack/), [IND-CCA](/ind-cca-security/) and [non-malleability](/non-malleability/).
4. **Padding RSA.** [PKCS #1 v1.5](/rsa-pkcs1-v1-5-encryption/), the [padding oracle attack](/padding-oracle-attack/) and [OAEP](/rsa-oaep/).
5. **Encryption from discrete logarithms.** [CDH](/computational-diffie-hellman/), [DDH](/decisional-diffie-hellman/), [ElGamal](/elgamal-encryption/), [elliptic-curve cryptography](/elliptic-curve-cryptography/) and [Cramer-Shoup](/cramer-shoup-cryptosystem/).
6. **KEMs and generic transforms.** The [key encapsulation mechanism](/key-encapsulation-mechanism/), [hybrid encryption](/hybrid-encryption/), [ECIES](/ecies/), the [Fujisaki-Okamoto transform](/fujisaki-okamoto-transform/) and the [Naor-Yung paradigm](/naor-yung-paradigm/).
7. **Residuosity and homomorphic encryption.** The [quadratic residuosity problem](/quadratic-residuosity-problem/), [Goldwasser-Micali](/goldwasser-micali-cryptosystem/), [Blum-Goldwasser](/blum-goldwasser-cryptosystem/), [DCR](/decisional-composite-residuosity/), [Paillier](/paillier-cryptosystem/), [Okamoto-Uchiyama](/okamoto-uchiyama-cryptosystem/), [Naccache-Stern](/naccache-stern-cryptosystem/), [homomorphic encryption](/homomorphic-encryption/) and [Gentry's scheme](/gentry-fully-homomorphic-encryption/).
8. **Pairings and encryption to many readers.** The [bilinear pairing](/bilinear-pairing/), [Miller's algorithm](/miller-algorithm/), [BDH](/bilinear-diffie-hellman/), [identity-based encryption](/identity-based-encryption/), [Boneh-Franklin](/boneh-franklin-ibe/), [attribute-based](/attribute-based-encryption/) and [functional encryption](/functional-encryption/), and [broadcast encryption](/broadcast-encryption/).
9. **Attacks on implementations.** The [timing attack](/timing-attack/), [RSA blinding](/rsa-blinding/) and the [CRT fault attack](/rsa-fault-attack/).

## A first reading

A first pass can take thirteen entries. Start with [public-key encryption](/public-key-encryption/) for the syntax, then work the toy examples of [RSA](/rsa-cryptosystem/) by hand. Read [IND-CPA](/ind-cpa-security/), the [chosen-ciphertext attack](/chosen-ciphertext-attack/) and [IND-CCA](/ind-cca-security/) in that order; they are the vocabulary of every later claim. Then see the vocabulary at work on RSA: [PKCS #1 v1.5](/rsa-pkcs1-v1-5-encryption/) and why it fails, and [OAEP](/rsa-oaep/) and what its proof assumes. Move to discrete logarithms with [DDH](/decisional-diffie-hellman/) and [ElGamal](/elgamal-encryption/). Finish with the way systems use all of this: the [KEM](/key-encapsulation-mechanism/), [hybrid encryption](/hybrid-encryption/), [ECIES](/ecies/), and [RSA blinding](/rsa-blinding/) as the first defence of an implementation.

Skip on a first pass the research entries: hard-core predicates, non-malleability, Naor-Yung, Miller's algorithm, BDH, attribute-based and functional encryption, Gentry's scheme, Naccache-Stern and Okamoto-Uchiyama. The Merkle-Hellman knapsack is worth a later look as a lesson in how a plausible trapdoor fails.

## Before you start

The chapter uses number theory from another volume. You need [modular exponentiation](/modular-exponentiation/) by [repeated squaring](/binary-exponentiation/), inverses by the [extended Euclidean algorithm](/extended-euclidean-algorithm/), [Euler's totient](/euler-totient/) and the [Carmichael function](/carmichael-function/), and the [Chinese remainder theorem](/chinese-remainder-theorem/). The residuosity schemes use [quadratic residues](/quadratic-residue/), the [Legendre](/legendre-symbol/) and [Jacobi](/jacobi-symbol/) symbols and [modular square roots](/modular-square-root/). The discrete-log schemes need [cyclic groups](/cyclic-group/) and the [discrete logarithm problem](/discrete-logarithm-problem/); the RSA entries need [integer factorization](/integer-factorization/) and [Miller-Rabin](/miller-rabin/). Wiener's attack uses [continued fraction convergents](/continued-fraction-convergents/), and the knapsack attack uses [LLL](/lll-algorithm/).

The security definitions build on [semantic security](/semantic-security/) and the [chosen-plaintext attack](/chosen-plaintext-attack/) from the symmetric-cipher chapter, on [computational indistinguishability](/computational-indistinguishability/) and the [hybrid argument](/hybrid-argument/), and on [polynomial time](/polynomial-time/). Hybrid encryption uses [authenticated encryption](/authenticated-encryption/), several proofs model a [hash function](/cryptographic-hash-function/) as a random oracle, and ECIES runs on [ECDH](/ecdh/).
