1. ◦ Build a toy RSA key from $p = 47$, $q = 59$ and $e = 17$. Give $N$, $\varphi(N)$, $\lambda(N)$ and $d = e^{-1} \bmod \varphi(N)$. Encrypt $m = 920$, then decrypt with the CRT: give $d_p = d \bmod (p - 1)$, $d_q = d \bmod (q - 1)$, $q^{-1} \bmod p$, $m_1 = c^{d_p} \bmod p$ and $m_2 = c^{d_q} \bmod q$, and recombine.

2. ◦ An RSA modulus $N = 2773$ is the product of two primes, and $\varphi(N) = 2668$ leaks. Recover $p$ and $q$ from these two numbers alone.

3. ◦ A sender uses textbook RSA with $e = 3$ and a modulus $N$ of 2048 binary digits, and sends the ciphertext $c = 74088$. Find the message without the private key, and say which property of the message made this possible.

4. ◦ In the IND-CPA game against textbook RSA with public key $(N, e) = (2773, 17)$, the adversary submits $m_0 = 920$ and $m_1 = 921$ and receives $c^* = 948$. Which message was encrypted, and how many encryptions does the adversary compute to decide? What advantage does this strategy have?

5. ◦ Rabin encryption with $N = 7 \cdot 19 = 133$ sends $m = 10$ as $c = 100$. Find the four square roots of 100 modulo 133 with the CRT, using $r^{(p+1)/4}$ for each prime. Then show how someone who holds the roots 10 and 66, but not the factors, factors $N$.

6. ◦ ElGamal runs in the subgroup of order 11 of $\mathbb{Z}_{23}^*$ generated by $g = 2$, with private key $x = 6$. Give $h$, encrypt $m = 3$ with $r = 4$, and decrypt. Then change the second component of the ciphertext so that it decrypts to $6$, without knowing $x$ or $m$.

7. ◦ Paillier with $p = 5$, $q = 7$ and $g = N + 1$ has $N = 35$, $N^2 = 1225$, $\lambda = 12$ and $\mu = 3$. Encrypt $m_1 = 4$ with $r = 2$ and $m_2 = 9$ with $r = 3$, multiply the two ciphertexts modulo $N^2$, and decrypt the product.

8. ◦ RSA-OAEP with SHA-256 carries at most $k - 2h - 2$ octets, where $k$ is the length of $N$ in octets and $h = 32$. Give the maximum message length and the ciphertext length for moduli of 3072 and 4096 binary digits.

9. • The same message $m$ goes to three receivers with $e = 3$ and moduli $N_1 = 55$, $N_2 = 391$ and $N_3 = 1189$. The ciphertexts are $3$, $189$ and $370$. Combine them with the CRT and recover $m$.

10. • Two users share the modulus $N = 2773$ with public exponents $e_1 = 3$ and $e_2 = 5$. The same message is sent to both, as $c_1 = 1720$ and $c_2 = 1854$. Recover the message without any private key.

11. • The key $(N, e, d) = (2773, 17, 157)$ leaks. Factor $N$ with the randomized method: write $ed - 1 = 2^t s$ with $s$ odd, compute $a^s, a^{2s}, \ldots \bmod N$ for $a = 2$, and find a square root of 1 that is not $\pm 1$.

12. • A device signs with RSA-CRT under $N = 2773$ and $e = 17$. For the message representative $m = 100$ it outputs the faulty signature $s' = 1841$. Factor $N$ from public data, and say in which half the fault happened. What is the correct signature?

13. • Wiener's attack: the public key is $N = 1022117$, $e = 816077$. Expand $e/N$ as a continued fraction, test the convergents $k/d$ in order, and recover $d$, $\varphi(N)$, $p$ and $q$. Check that $d < N^{1/4}/3$.

14. • ElGamal runs in all of $\mathbb{Z}_{23}^*$ with generator $g = 5$ and public key $h = 17$. Decide from the ciphertext $(c_1, c_2) = (10, 2)$ alone whether the message is a square modulo 23. Then decide which of the triples $(10, 4, 18)$ and $(10, 4, 20)$ cannot be of the form $(g^a, g^b, g^{ab})$.

15. • Goldwasser-Micali with $N = 11 \cdot 19 = 209$ and the pseudosquare $y = 2$. Encrypt the bits $1, 1, 0$ with $r = 3, 5, 7$ as $y^m r^2 \bmod N$, decrypt by the Legendre symbol modulo 11, and decrypt the product of the first two ciphertexts. Then give one decryption query, different from the challenge $c^* = 18$, that reveals the bit inside $c^*$.

16. • A Merkle-Hellman key has private sequence $a = (2, 3, 7, 14, 30, 57, 120, 251)$, modulus $m = 491$ and multiplier $w = 41$. Compute the public sequence, and decrypt the ciphertext 881 with the private key. The scheme is broken; this is only an exercise in the trapdoor.

17. • On the curve $y^2 = x^3 + 2x + 2$ over $\mathbb{F}_{17}$ with $G = (5, 1)$ of order 19, a receiver has $d = 3$ and an ECIES sender picks $k = 5$. Compute $Q = dG$, $R = kG$ and the shared point $Z = kQ$, and check that $dR = Z$. Which point does the receiver compute from $-R$, and why must the KDF input include $R$?

18. • Let $e : \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_1 \to \mathbb{G}_T$ be a symmetric pairing and $P$ a generator of $\mathbb{G}_1$. Give a one-line test that decides DDH in $\mathbb{G}_1$ for a triple $(aP, bP, cP)$, and explain why Boneh-Franklin therefore rests on BDH and not on DDH in $\mathbb{G}_1$.

19. • Blind an RSA decryption with $N = 2773$, $e = 17$, $d = 157$, ciphertext $c = 948$ and blinding value $r = 7$. Give $r^e \bmod N$, the blinded input, the result of the private exponentiation, $r^{-1} \bmod N$ and the final plaintext.

20. • In the IND-CCA2 game against Paillier with $N = 35$, the adversary submits $m_0 = 4$ and $m_1 = 9$ and receives $c^* = 88$. Give one decryption query that is not $c^*$, the answer the oracle returns, and the bit $b$.

21. ★ A server decrypts textbook RSA and reveals only the parity of the plaintext. Show that the parity of the decryption of $c \cdot 2^e$ tells whether $m < N/2$, and turn this into an algorithm that recovers $m$ with $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil$ queries. Run it on $N = 2773$, $e = 17$, $c = 948$: give the first three answers and the final $m$. How many queries does a modulus of 2048 binary digits need?

22. ★ Prove that an algorithm which returns some square root of $x^2 \bmod N$, for a uniform $x \in \mathbb{Z}_N^*$, factors $N = pq$ with probability at least $1/2$ per query. How many queries make the failure probability at most $2^{-40}$? Apply the method to $N = 133$ with $x = 10$ when the algorithm returns 66.

23. ★ Prove that Paillier decryption with $g = N + 1$ is correct: show $(1 + N)^m \equiv 1 + mN \pmod{N^2}$ and $r^{N\lambda} \equiv 1 \pmod{N^2}$, and conclude that $L(c^\lambda \bmod N^2) \cdot \mu \equiv m \pmod N$. Check each step on $c = 88$ with $N = 35$.

24. ★ Textbook RSA with $e = 3$ encrypts two related messages $m$ and $m + 1$ under the same key. Find rational functions of $c_1 = m^3$ and $c_2 = (m + 1)^3$ modulo $N$ whose quotient is $m$. Recover $m$ from $N = 2773$, $c_1 = 1720$ and $c_2 = 1518$.

25. ★ An algorithm inverts the RSA function $x \mapsto x^e \bmod N$ on 1% of the inputs. Use random self-reducibility to invert every input in expected 100 calls, and prove that each call sees a uniform input. Run one step on $N = 2773$, $e = 17$, $y = 948$ with $r = 2$, assuming the algorithm answers 1840 on the input it receives.
