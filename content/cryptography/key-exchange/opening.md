A browser opens a TLS 1.3 connection to a server it has never talked to. Within one round trip the two machines share a 32-byte secret that nobody else on the network knows. The client sends a fresh X25519 public key, the server sends its own and signs the transcript, each side multiplies the other's point by its own secret scalar, and a key schedule turns the result into separate keys for each direction. An eavesdropper who records everything faces a Diffie-Hellman problem on an elliptic curve. An attacker who changes messages fails the signature check. A thief who steals the server's signing key next year still cannot read today's traffic, because the ephemeral scalars are gone. This chapter is about the protocols that make such a key and about the attacks that shaped them.

Every security claim in the chapter is made against a stated adversary. A passive adversary only reads the messages. An active one also changes, drops, replays and injects them, and may corrupt long-term keys or reveal session keys. Plain [Diffie-Hellman](/diffie-hellman/) resists the first and falls to the second. Most of the chapter is about closing that gap: with signatures, with static keys in the key formula, with a trusted server, with predistributed secrets, or with a shared password. The entries name the assumption, such as computational or decisional Diffie-Hellman, and the model of the proof: the standard model, the random oracle model, or the ideal cipher model.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **The problems.** The [session key](/session-key/), and the [key agreement](/key-agreement-problem/), [key distribution](/key-distribution-problem/) and [key transport](/key-transport-problem/) problems.
2. **Diffie-Hellman, its curves and its forerunners.** Diffie-Hellman, the [man-in-the-middle attack](/man-in-the-middle-attack/), [forward secrecy](/perfect-forward-secrecy/), [ECDH](/ecdh/), [X25519](/x25519/) and [X448](/x448/), with [Merkle's puzzles](/merkle-puzzles/) and [Shamir's three-pass protocol](/shamir-three-pass-protocol/).
3. **Attacks and security goals.** The [small subgroup attack](/small-subgroup-attack/), [authenticated key exchange](/authenticated-key-exchange/) and its models, [key confirmation](/key-confirmation/), the [unknown key-share attack](/unknown-key-share-attack/) and [key compromise impersonation](/key-compromise-impersonation/).
4. **Authenticated Diffie-Hellman.** [Station-to-station](/station-to-station-protocol/), [SIGMA](/sigma-key-exchange/), [IKE](/ike-protocol/), [MQV](/mqv/) and [HMQV](/hmqv/).
5. **Key derivation, handshakes and ratchets.** [HKDF](/hkdf/), the [Noise framework](/noise-protocol-framework/), the [key ratchet](/key-ratchet/), [X3DH](/x3dh/), the [double ratchet](/double-ratchet/) and [hybrid key exchange](/hybrid-key-exchange/).
6. **Key distribution with a trusted server.** The protocols of [Needham and Schroeder](/needham-schroeder-protocol/), [Denning and Sacco](/denning-sacco-protocol/), [Otway and Rees](/otway-rees-protocol/), [Yahalom](/yahalom-protocol/) and the [Wide-Mouthed Frog](/wide-mouthed-frog-protocol/).
7. **Key predistribution.** The [predistribution problem](/key-predistribution-problem/) and [Blom's scheme](/blom-scheme/).
8. **Password-authenticated key exchange.** The [PAKE problem](/password-authenticated-key-exchange/), [EKE](/encrypted-key-exchange/), [SRP](/secure-remote-password/), [SPAKE2](/spake2/), [J-PAKE](/j-pake/), [CPace](/cpace/) and [OPAQUE](/opaque/).
9. **Group key agreement.** The [group problem](/group-key-agreement-problem/), the ring protocol of [Ingemarsson, Tang and Wong](/ingemarsson-tang-wong/), [GDH](/group-diffie-hellman/), [Burmester-Desmedt](/burmester-desmedt/) and [tree-based group Diffie-Hellman](/tree-based-group-diffie-hellman/).

## A first reading

A first pass needs about a dozen entries. Start with the [session key](/session-key/) and [key agreement](/key-agreement-problem/), then [Diffie-Hellman](/diffie-hellman/) and the [man-in-the-middle attack](/man-in-the-middle-attack/), which together show why authentication is the real problem. Read [forward secrecy](/perfect-forward-secrecy/) next, then [ECDH](/ecdh/) and [X25519](/x25519/) for the form that runs in practice. [Authenticated key exchange](/authenticated-key-exchange/) gives the security models, and the [unknown key-share attack](/unknown-key-share-attack/) explains why identities must enter the signed or MACed data. [SIGMA](/sigma-key-exchange/) is the design behind IKEv2 and the TLS 1.3 handshake, and [HKDF](/hkdf/) is how modern protocols turn a shared secret into keys. Finish with [X3DH](/x3dh/) and the [double ratchet](/double-ratchet/) for messaging, and with the [PAKE problem](/password-authenticated-key-exchange/) for passwords. The first seven are marked *Intro* or *Core*; the others are *Advanced*, except SIGMA, which is *Research*.

Skip the server-based protocols of group 6 on a first pass unless you study protocol analysis; they are history and test cases, not designs to deploy. MQV and HMQV, the individual PAKE protocols and the group protocols can wait. The code on every page is for study only: many examples use toy groups, nothing is constant time, and real systems take X25519, HKDF and the handshakes from a vetted library.

## Before you start

The chapter uses number theory from another volume: [modular exponentiation](/modular-exponentiation/) by [repeated squaring](/binary-exponentiation/), [cyclic groups](/cyclic-group/), the [discrete logarithm problem](/discrete-logarithm-problem/), the [Chinese remainder theorem](/chinese-remainder-theorem/), [Pohlig-Hellman](/pohlig-hellman/) and [safe primes](/safe-prime/). From the other chapters of this volume it needs [elliptic-curve cryptography](/elliptic-curve-cryptography/), the [computational](/computational-diffie-hellman/) and [decisional](/decisional-diffie-hellman/) Diffie-Hellman assumptions, [digital signatures](/digital-signature/), [HMAC](/hmac/), the [pseudorandom function](/pseudorandom-function/), the [random oracle model](/random-oracle-model/), [public-key encryption](/public-key-encryption/) and the [key encapsulation mechanism](/key-encapsulation-mechanism/). The server-based protocols assume the [key distribution center](/key-distribution-center/), and the PAKE entries assume [password hashing](/password-hashing/). Blom's scheme uses the [Vandermonde matrix](/vandermonde-matrix/), and the tree protocol uses the [binary tree](/binary-tree/).
