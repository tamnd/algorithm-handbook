## History

Key exchange as a public problem began in the mid-1970s. In 1974 Ralph Merkle, a student at Berkeley, proposed his [puzzles](/merkle-puzzles/) in a course project that the instructor did not accept; the paper appeared in the Communications of the ACM in 1978. Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman published "New directions in cryptography" in 1976, with the exchange now named after them. At GCHQ, Malcolm Williamson had found the same method in the mid-1970s, but the work stayed secret until 1997. Shamir's [three-pass protocol](/shamir-three-pass-protocol/), from about 1980, showed that a commutative cipher also gives a key transport with no shared key.

The server-based protocols came from the same years. Roger Needham and Michael Schroeder published their [two protocols](/needham-schroeder-protocol/) in 1978, and Denning and Sacco found the replay on the symmetric one in 1981. The protocols of [Otway and Rees](/otway-rees-protocol/) (1987), [Yahalom](/yahalom-protocol/) and the [Wide-Mouthed Frog](/wide-mouthed-frog-protocol/) became test cases when Burrows, Abadi and Needham introduced their logic of authentication in 1989. In 1995 Gavin Lowe found the attack on the public-key Needham-Schroeder protocol, and in 1996 he found it again with the FDR model checker. Group protocols and predistribution started early too: [Ingemarsson, Tang and Wong](/ingemarsson-tang-wong/) gave a conference key protocol in 1982, and Rolf Blom presented his [optimal scheme](/blom-scheme/) at EUROCRYPT 1984.

The modern view of authenticated key exchange formed between 1989 and 2005. Günther named perfect forward secrecy in 1989. Diffie, van Oorschot and Wiener defined it in 1992 and gave the [station-to-station protocol](/station-to-station-protocol/), and Bellovin and Merritt gave [EKE](/encrypted-key-exchange/), the first password protocol, in the same year. Bellare and Rogaway gave the first formal model in 1993. Then came [Burmester-Desmedt](/burmester-desmedt/) in 1994, [MQV](/mqv/) in 1995, [GDH](/group-diffie-hellman/) in 1996 and [SRP](/secure-remote-password/) in 1998. Bellare, Pointcheval and Rogaway modelled password protocols in 2000. Canetti and Krawczyk published their model in 2001 and in 2002 proved the core of [SIGMA](/sigma-key-exchange/) secure in it. Krawczyk's [HMQV](/hmqv/) of 2005 is a variant of MQV with a proof in the random oracle model.

The protocols in daily use today are younger. Bernstein published Curve25519 in 2006, and RFC 7748 standardized [X25519](/x25519/) and [X448](/x448/) in 2016. Krawczyk's [HKDF](/hkdf/) appeared in 2010. TextSecure ran a triple Diffie-Hellman handshake from 2013, and Signal published the [X3DH](/x3dh/) and [double ratchet](/double-ratchet/) specifications in 2016. TLS 1.3 followed in 2018, with ephemeral Diffie-Hellman in every public-key handshake. In 2020 the CFRG chose [CPace](/cpace/) and [OPAQUE](/opaque/) as its recommended PAKEs. OpenSSH 9.0 added a [hybrid](/hybrid-key-exchange/) exchange with a post-quantum KEM in 2022, browsers and servers followed in 2023, and NIST published ML-KEM as FIPS 203 in August 2024.

## Further reading

- Alfred J. Menezes, Paul C. van Oorschot and Scott A. Vanstone, *Handbook of Applied Cryptography* (CRC Press, 1996). Chapter 12 covers key transport, key agreement and the server-based protocols, with precise definitions and careful notes on who did what.
- Colin Boyd, Anish Mathuria and Douglas Stebila, *Protocols for Authentication and Key Establishment*, second edition (Springer, 2020). A catalogue of protocols and attacks, with the security models of the last thirty years in one place.
- Whitfield Diffie and Martin E. Hellman, *New Directions in Cryptography*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 22(6) (1976). The paper that introduced public key distribution and set out the questions of the field.
- Ran Canetti and Hugo Krawczyk, *Analysis of Key-Exchange Protocols and Their Use for Building Secure Channels*, EUROCRYPT 2001. Defines SK-security and proves that it gives secure channels.
- Hugo Krawczyk, *SIGMA: The SIGn-and-MAc Approach to Authenticated Diffie-Hellman and Its Use in the IKE Protocols*, CRYPTO 2003. Explains with care why each part of the protocol is there, through the attacks on the simpler variants.
- Hugo Krawczyk, *Cryptographic Extraction and Key Derivation: The HKDF Scheme*, CRYPTO 2010. The design and analysis of the extract-then-expand pattern behind most key schedules.
