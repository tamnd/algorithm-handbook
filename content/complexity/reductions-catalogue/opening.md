A university must place its exams in three time slots so that no student has two exams in one slot. Draw a vertex for each exam and an edge between two exams that share a student. The question is now whether the graph has a [3-coloring](/three-coloring/). A reduction runs the other way, too. Given a 3-CNF formula with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses, the standard construction builds a graph with $3 + 2n + 6m$ vertices and $3 + 3n + 12m$ edges that is 3-colorable exactly when the formula is satisfiable. For a formula with three variables and three clauses that is a graph with 27 vertices and 48 edges. So an exam scheduler that always ran fast would also decide 3-SAT fast, and that is not possible unless P = NP.

A reduction of this kind is a program that rewrites instances of one problem as instances of another and keeps the answer. This chapter is a catalogue of such programs and of the rules for using them. Chapter 39.2 defines NP-completeness and proves the first complete problems. Here the reductions themselves are the subject: what kinds there are, how they compose, how a proof is written, and the standard reductions from SAT to graphs, numbers and covering problems. Every reduction in the chapter is checked in both directions, and most entries run the construction on a small formula and count the results.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Reductions and how they compose.** A [polynomial-time reduction](/polynomial-time-reduction/), or Karp reduction, maps each instance to one instance. By [transitivity](/transitivity-of-reductions/), a chain of a constant number of reductions is again a reduction. A [Turing reduction](/turing-reduction/), or Cook reduction, may ask an oracle many questions.
2. **How to write a hardness proof.** The parts of an [NP-completeness proof](/np-completeness-proof/), the [gadget](/gadget-reduction/) that carries each part of an instance, the [common patterns of NP-hardness reductions](/np-hardness-reductions/), such as restriction and local replacement, and the full [tree of Karp's reductions](/karp-21-np-complete/).
3. **Circuits and variants of satisfiability.** [Circuit satisfiability](/circuit-sat/) is NP-complete, while the [circuit value problem](/circuit-value-problem/) is P-complete under logspace reductions. [Not-all-equal SAT](/not-all-equal-sat/) and [one-in-three SAT](/one-in-three-sat/) stay NP-complete even without negations.
4. **Graph problems.** [Clique](/clique-problem/), [3-coloring](/three-coloring/), [Hamiltonian cycle](/hamiltonian-cycle-np-completeness/) and the [traveling salesman decision problem](/traveling-salesman-decision/).
5. **Numbers and exact covers.** [Zero-one equations](/zero-one-equations/), [knapsack](/knapsack-decision/) and the strongly NP-complete [3-partition](/three-partition/).
6. **Search, solutions and counting.** [Search problems](/search-problem/), the [reduction from search to decision](/search-to-decision-reduction/) and [self-reducibility](/self-reducibility/), then [Levin reductions](/levin-reduction/), which also map solutions, and [parsimonious reductions](/parsimonious-reduction/), which keep their number.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these eleven entries in order: [polynomial-time reduction](/polynomial-time-reduction/), [transitivity of reductions](/transitivity-of-reductions/), [Turing reduction](/turing-reduction/), [NP-completeness proof](/np-completeness-proof/), [gadget reduction](/gadget-reduction/), [circuit satisfiability](/circuit-sat/), [clique](/clique-problem/), [3-coloring](/three-coloring/), [Hamiltonian cycle](/hamiltonian-cycle-np-completeness/), [knapsack](/knapsack-decision/) and [search to decision](/search-to-decision-reduction/). Work through at least one reduction by hand: build the instance for a small formula, find the solution that a satisfying assignment gives, and read the assignment back from a solution.

The full Karp tree, the circuit value problem, 3-partition and the reductions that keep solutions or their number can wait. Read them when you need a source problem for a proof of your own, or when you reach counting problems.
