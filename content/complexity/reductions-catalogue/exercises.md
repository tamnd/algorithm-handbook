1. ◦ A problem $X$ is in NP. Which of these facts prove that $X$ is NP-hard, which prove that $X$ is in P, and which prove neither? (a) A Karp reduction from $X$ to SAT. (b) A Karp reduction from 3-SAT to $X$. (c) A Karp reduction from $X$ to 2-SAT. (d) A Cook reduction from SAT to $X$. (e) A Karp reduction from $X$ to a problem that is NP-complete and also in P.

2. ◦ A reduction $f$ from $A$ to $B$ runs in time $O(n^2)$, and a reduction $g$ from $B$ to $C$ runs in time $O(n^3)$. (a) Bound the time of $g \circ f$ and the length of its output, as in [transitivity of reductions](/transitivity-of-reductions/). (b) A chain of four reductions each runs in time $O(n^2)$. Bound the time of the composite. (c) Why does the argument fail for a chain whose length grows with $n$?

3. ◦ Apply the reduction from 3-SAT to [clique](/clique-problem/), one vertex per literal occurrence and edges between consistent literals in different clauses, to $(x_1 \lor x_2 \lor x_3) \land (\lnot x_1 \lor \lnot x_2 \lor x_3)$ with $k = 2$. (a) How many vertices and edges does the graph have? (b) How many satisfying assignments does the formula have, and how many cliques of size 2 does the graph have? (c) Is the reduction parsimonious?

4. ◦ Turn the subset sum instance with numbers $3, 5, 8, 9$ and target 17 into an instance of [knapsack](/knapsack-decision/) by setting each weight and each value to the number, and the capacity and the value bound to the target. (a) Which item sets reach value 17 within capacity 17? (b) With target 15 instead, what is the best value within capacity 15, and what is the answer?

5. ◦ A circuit has inputs $x_1 = 1$, $x_2 = 0$, $x_3 = 1$ and the gates $g_1 = x_1 \land \lnot x_2$, $g_2 = x_2 \lor x_3$, $g_3 = g_1 \land g_2$, $g_4 = \lnot g_3$ and the output $g_5 = g_4 \lor x_2$. (a) Evaluate it gate by gate. (b) Build the dual-rail monotone circuit of the [circuit value problem](/circuit-value-problem/) entry, with a pair of gates for each AND or OR gate and the inputs $x_i$ and $\lnot x_i$. How many gates does it have, and what happens to the NOT gates?

6. ◦ Check that $B = 30$ and the numbers $8, 9, 13, 8, 10, 12$ form a valid instance of [3-partition](/three-partition/), with every number strictly between $B/4$ and $B/2$. Find all ways to split them into two triples with sum 30.

7. ◦ List the assignments of $x_1, \ldots, x_4$ that satisfy $\mathrm{NAE}(x_1, x_2, x_3) \land \mathrm{NAE}(\lnot x_1, x_2, x_4)$, in the sense of [not-all-equal SAT](/not-all-equal-sat/). Pair each with its complement. Why is the number of solutions of every NAE formula even?

8. ◦ The reduction from Hamiltonian cycle to the [traveling salesman decision problem](/traveling-salesman-decision/) gives distance 1 to edges and 2 to non-edges. Find the cost of an optimal tour for (a) the 4-cycle $1, 2, 3, 4$ with the chord $\{1, 3\}$; (b) the star with center 1 and leaves $2, 3, 4$. What bound does the reduction use, and what answer does each instance get?

9. • EXACT-CLIQUE asks whether the largest clique of $G$ has exactly $k$ vertices. (a) Give a Cook reduction from EXACT-CLIQUE to the clique problem with two questions. (b) EXACT-CLIQUE is complete for the class $\mathsf{D^P}$ of the [Boolean hierarchy](/boolean-hierarchy/), which contains coNP. Show that a Karp reduction from EXACT-CLIQUE to clique would give $\mathsf{NP} = \mathsf{coNP}$.

10. • Find a subset of $3, 5, 8, 9$ with sum 17 using only a decision procedure for subset sum, by the method of [search to decision](/search-to-decision-reduction/): go through the numbers in order and ask whether the target is reachable without the current number. List the questions and answers, and count them. Bound the number of questions for $n$ numbers.

11. • Write the CNF that reduces 3-coloring to SAT: three variables per vertex, one clause saying that the vertex has a color, three saying that it has at most one, and three per edge saying that the ends differ. (a) Count the clauses for a graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges. (b) For the 5-cycle, how many satisfying assignments are there, and how many proper 3-colorings? (c) For the complete graph on four vertices, how many? (d) Is the reduction parsimonious from colorings to assignments? What breaks if you drop the "at most one" clauses?

12. • In the reduction from [zero-one equations](/zero-one-equations/) to subset sum, each column of $A$ becomes a number with one digit per row, and the target has all digits 1. (a) Show that base 2 does not work: take the three numbers $1 = 001_2$, $1 = 001_2$ and $5 = 101_2$ with target $7 = 111_2$, and check that subset sum says yes while $Ax = \mathbf{1}$ has no solution. (b) Explain why base $n + 1$, for $n$ columns, prevents every carry.

13. • A [Levin reduction](/levin-reduction/) maps instances, maps solutions forward and maps solutions back. For the reduction from 3-SAT to independent set, with a triangle per clause and edges between complementary literals, give the forward map from a satisfying assignment to an independent set of size $m$, and the backward map from an independent set of size $m$ to a satisfying assignment. Show that both run in polynomial time and that the backward map always returns a satisfying assignment.

14. • The majority of three bits is $(x \land y) \lor (x \land z) \lor (y \land z)$, with three AND gates and two OR gates of fan-in 2. (a) Write the Tseitin clauses of [circuit satisfiability](/circuit-sat/) and the clause for the output, and count the variables and the clauses. (b) How many satisfying assignments does the CNF have? Compare with the number of inputs on which the majority is 1.

15. • Use the NP-completeness of [3-coloring](/three-coloring/) to show that no polynomial-time algorithm approximates the chromatic number of a graph within a ratio less than $4/3$, unless P = NP. Why does the argument not rule out the ratio $4/3$ itself?

16. • Show that 3-coloring of graphs with maximum degree at most 3 is in P. Use Brooks' theorem: a connected graph other than a complete graph or an odd cycle has a coloring with at most $\Delta$ colors. Say which components need special care. Contrast with the result that 3-coloring of planar graphs with maximum degree 4 is NP-complete.

17. • Let $H$ be the [halting problem](/halting-problem/). Show that $H$ reduces to its complement $\overline{H}$ by a Turing reduction, but not by a computable many-one reduction. Use the fact that a many-one reduction of $\overline{H}$ to $H$ would make $\overline{H}$ recursively enumerable. Compare with the open question whether SAT and its complement are Karp-equivalent.

18. • Show that $2\text{-SAT} \le_m^p 3\text{-SAT}$ by a simple map. Then show that a Karp reduction from 3-SAT to 2-SAT would give P = NP. Where does the clause-splitting gadget, which turns a clause of width $k$ into $k - 2$ clauses of width 3, stop working if you try to reach width 2?

19. ★ For a 3-clause $(a \lor b \lor c)$, take a new variable $d$ and the ten clauses $(a)$, $(b)$, $(c)$, $(d)$, $(\lnot a \lor \lnot b)$, $(\lnot a \lor \lnot c)$, $(\lnot b \lor \lnot c)$, $(a \lor \lnot d)$, $(b \lor \lnot d)$, $(c \lor \lnot d)$ (Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer 1976). (a) Show that if the clause is satisfied, some value of $d$ satisfies exactly 7 of the ten, and none satisfies more; if it is not satisfied, at most 6 are. (b) Deduce that MAX-2-SAT, which asks whether at least $K$ clauses of a 2-CNF can be satisfied at once, is NP-complete, although 2-SAT is in P.

20. ★ Show that every 3-CNF formula in which each clause has exactly three distinct variables and each variable occurs in exactly three clauses is satisfiable (Tovey 1984). Build the bipartite graph between clauses and variables and apply Hall's theorem to match each clause with a variable of its own. Tovey also showed that allowing four occurrences of each variable keeps 3-SAT NP-complete; why does the matching argument fail there?

21. ★ A set $S$ is sparse when it has at most $p(n)$ strings of length at most $n$. Show that if some sparse set is NP-hard under polynomial-time [Turing reductions](/turing-reduction/), then $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$, and so the polynomial hierarchy collapses to $\Sigma_2^p$ by the [Karp-Lipton theorem](/karp-lipton-theorem/). Use as advice the list of all strings of $S$ up to the largest length the reduction can ask about.

22. ★ A padding function for SAT is a polynomial-time map $\mathrm{pad}(\varphi, y)$ from a formula and a string to a formula, with $\mathrm{pad}(\varphi, y) \in \mathrm{SAT} \iff \varphi \in \mathrm{SAT}$, which is one-to-one and whose inverse, recovering $\varphi$ and $y$, is computable in polynomial time. Build one by adding new variables and clauses that encode the bits of $y$. Berman and Hartmanis (1977) showed that two NP-complete sets with padding functions are polynomial-time isomorphic; state what that means for SAT and clique.
