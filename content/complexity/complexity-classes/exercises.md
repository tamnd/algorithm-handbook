1. ◦ For each statement, say whether it is proved, open or false, and name the result that settles it when there is one. (a) $\mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{PSPACE}$. (b) $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{EXP}$. (c) $\mathsf{NP} \ne \mathsf{PSPACE}$. (d) $\mathsf{L} \ne \mathsf{PSPACE}$. (e) $\mathsf{NL} = \mathsf{coNL}$. (f) $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{NP}$. (g) $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{EXP}$. (h) $\mathsf{PSPACE} = \mathsf{EXPSPACE}$.

2. ◦ An [RP](/rp/) algorithm accepts each yes-instance with probability at least $1/2$. (a) How many independent runs bring the error below $1/1000$, if you accept when some run accepts? (b) A [BPP](/bpp/) algorithm errs with probability exactly $1/3$ on some input, independently in each run. Find the smallest odd $k$ for which the majority vote of $k$ runs errs with probability at most $1/10$, and the smallest for at most $1/100$. Write the error as a binomial sum and evaluate it with a short program.

3. ◦ A directed graph has $N = 2^{20}$ vertices. (a) How many bits does breadth-first search use for its marks alone? (b) [Savitch's theorem](/savitch-theorem/) decides reachability by a recursion on midpoints. How deep is the recursion, and how many bits does one frame need if it holds two endpoints, a midpoint and a level, each in 20 bits? Give the total. (c) How many bits does the nondeterministic algorithm of [NL](/nondeterministic-logspace/) need?

4. ◦ For each pair, say whether the [time hierarchy theorem](/time-hierarchy-theorem/) or the [space hierarchy theorem](/space-hierarchy-theorem/), as stated in the entries, proves that the second class is strictly larger. (a) $\mathsf{DTIME}(n)$ and $\mathsf{DTIME}(n^2)$. (b) $\mathsf{DTIME}(n^2)$ and $\mathsf{DTIME}(n^2 \log n)$. (c) $\mathsf{DTIME}(n^2)$ and $\mathsf{DTIME}(n^2 \log^2 n)$. (d) $\mathsf{DTIME}(2^n)$ and $\mathsf{DTIME}(n 2^n)$. (e) $\mathsf{DSPACE}(n)$ and $\mathsf{DSPACE}(n \log n)$. (f) $\mathsf{DSPACE}(\log n)$ and $\mathsf{DSPACE}(\log^2 n)$.

5. ◦ Write each problem with quantifiers over strings of polynomial length and a polynomial-time test, and give the lowest level of the [polynomial hierarchy](/polynomial-hierarchy/) that the form shows. (a) The graph $G$ has a clique of size $k$. (b) The graph $G$ has no clique of size $k$. (c) The Boolean circuit $C$ has an equivalent circuit with at most $s$ gates. (d) Every 3-coloring of the vertices of $G$ that uses each color at least once leaves some edge with equal colors at its ends.

6. ◦ Which of these classes are known to be closed under complement: P, NP, L, NL, PSPACE, RP, BPP, ZPP? For each one, give the reason or say that the question is open.

7. ◦ Explain why every instance of each search problem has a solution, so that the problem is in [TFNP](/tfnp/). (a) Given an integer $N \ge 2$ in binary, find its prime factorization. (b) Given a graph with integer edge weights, find a cut that no single move of a vertex to the other side improves. (c) Given a two-player game by its two payoff tables, find a mixed [Nash equilibrium](/nash-equilibrium/). (d) Given a Boolean circuit $C$ with $n$ inputs and $n - 1$ outputs, find two inputs $x \ne y$ with $C(x) = C(y)$.

8. ◦ The [Valiant-Vazirani theorem](/valiant-vazirani-theorem/) gives a formula with exactly one solution with probability at least $1/(8n)$ when $\varphi$ is satisfiable. Take $n = 20$ variables and suppose the probability is exactly $1/160$ in each independent run. How many runs make the probability that no run gives a uniquely satisfiable formula at most $1/2$? At most $1/100$?

9. • The 2-CNF formula $(x_1 \lor x_2) \land (\lnot x_1 \lor x_3) \land (\lnot x_2 \lor x_3) \land (\lnot x_3 \lor x_4) \land (\lnot x_3 \lor \lnot x_4)$ has the implication graph with an edge $\lnot a \to b$ and an edge $\lnot b \to a$ for each clause $(a \lor b)$. (a) Draw the graph and find paths $x_3 \to \lnot x_3$ and $\lnot x_3 \to x_3$. (b) Explain why such a pair of paths for one variable proves that the formula is unsatisfiable, and why 2-SAT is therefore in $\mathsf{coNL}$, and so in NL. Use the [Immerman-Szelepcsényi theorem](/immerman-szelepcsenyi-theorem/).

10. • Show by padding that if $\mathsf{L} = \mathsf{NL}$, then $\mathsf{DSPACE}(n) = \mathsf{NSPACE}(n)$. For $A \in \mathsf{NSPACE}(n)$, consider the strings $x \# 1^{2^{|x|}}$ with $x \in A$, and explain why the logspace machine for the padded set gives a linear-space machine for $A$.

11. • Show that if $\mathsf{NP} = \mathsf{coNP}$, then the whole [polynomial hierarchy](/polynomial-hierarchy/) equals NP. Prove $\Sigma_2^p = \mathsf{NP}$ first, by replacing the inner universal quantifier with an NP certificate, and then use induction on the level.

12. • Show that [NL](/nondeterministic-logspace/) is closed under Kleene star: if $A \in \mathsf{NL}$, then the set of strings that split into zero or more pieces, each in $A$, is in NL. The machine may not write the pieces down. Say what it stores while it guesses the cut points.

13. • A random walk on the path with vertices $0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ starts at $0$ and moves to a uniformly random neighbor at each step. (a) Solve the linear equations for the expected number of steps to reach $4$ from each vertex. (b) Compare the answer with the bound $2|E|(|V| - 1)$ on the expected hitting time in a connected graph. (c) Use [Markov's inequality](/markov-inequality/) to show that a walk of $2T$ steps, where $T$ is that bound, reaches $4$ with probability at least $1/2$. Why does this put undirected reachability in [RL](/randomized-logspace/)?

14. • A graph on vertices $1, \ldots, 5$ has the edge weights $w(1,2) = 3$, $w(1,3) = 1$, $w(2,3) = 2$, $w(2,4) = 4$, $w(3,4) = 1$, $w(3,5) = 5$, $w(4,5) = 2$. Local search for max cut starts with all vertices on one side and moves the vertex that increases the cut weight the most, breaking ties by the smaller label, until no move helps. (a) Run it and give each move and the cut weight after it. (b) Find all local optima by a short program, and give one that is not a maximum cut. This is the standard complete problem of [PLS](/pls/).

15. • An END-OF-THE-LINE instance on the vertices $0, \ldots, 7$ has successor and predecessor tables $S = (3, 4, 2, 5, 4, 5, 2, 7)$ and $P = (0, 1, 6, 0, 4, 3, 6, 7)$, listed for vertices $0$ to $7$. There is an edge $u \to v$ when $u \ne v$, $S(u) = v$ and $P(v) = u$. (a) List the edges. (b) Vertex $0$ is a source. Find every other vertex with in-degree and out-degree that differ, and say which one the walk from $0$ reaches. (c) Why is it enough for [PPAD](/ppad/) that some other end exists, even if the walk is long?

16. • Run the sum-check protocol over the field of 7 elements for $g(x_1, x_2, x_3) = 2x_1x_2 + x_2x_3 + x_1 + 3$ and the claimed value $H = \sum_{x \in \{0,1\}^3} g(x)$. (a) Compute $H$ over the integers and modulo 7. (b) With the random values $r_1 = 3$, $r_2 = 5$, $r_3 = 2$, give the honest prover's polynomials $g_1$, $g_2$, $g_3$ and each check of the verifier. The protocol is the core of [IP = PSPACE](/ip-equals-pspace/).

17. • Show that $\mathsf{PCP}(0, O(\log n)) = \mathsf{P}$ and $\mathsf{PCP}(O(\log n), 0) = \mathsf{P}$, in the notation of [probabilistically checkable proofs](/probabilistically-checkable-proof/). Then show that $\mathsf{PCP}(0, \mathrm{poly}(n)) = \mathsf{NP}$.

18. • Let $f$ be a permutation of $\{0,1\}^n$, for each $n$, that is computable in polynomial time. Show that the language $\{(y, i) : \text{bit } i \text{ of } f^{-1}(y) \text{ is } 1\}$ is in $\mathsf{UP} \cap \mathsf{coUP}$, in the sense of [unambiguous polynomial time](/unambiguous-polynomial-time/). What does this say about one-way permutations if $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{UP} \cap \mathsf{coUP}$?

19. ★ Show that $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{DSPACE}(n)$ and $\mathsf{NP} \ne \mathsf{DSPACE}(n)$ (Book 1974). Use the [space hierarchy theorem](/space-hierarchy-theorem/) and padding to show that $\mathsf{DSPACE}(n)$ is not closed under polynomial-time Karp reductions. Explain why the proof does not say which class has a problem that the other lacks.

20. ★ Show that for every constant $k$ some language in $\Sigma_2^p \cap \Pi_2^p$ has no circuits of size $O(n^k)$ (Kannan 1982). First show that $\Sigma_4^p$ contains such a language, by a counting argument for the circuits and a quantified definition of the lexicographically first hard function. Then split into cases by whether SAT has polynomial-size circuits, and use the [Karp-Lipton theorem](/karp-lipton-theorem/).

21. ★ Use the [Valiant-Vazirani theorem](/valiant-vazirani-theorem/) to show that $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{RP}^{\oplus \mathsf{P}}$: a randomized polynomial-time machine with one-sided error that may ask an oracle for the parity of the number of solutions of a formula decides SAT. This is the first step of the proof of [Toda's theorem](/toda-theorem/).

22. ★ Show that if $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{BPP}$, then $\mathsf{NP} = \mathsf{RP}$ (Ko 1982). Use the BPP algorithm for SAT, amplified, to search for a satisfying assignment by fixing one variable at a time, and accept only after checking the assignment it finds.
