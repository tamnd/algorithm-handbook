Take a directed graph with a million vertices and two of them, $s$ and $t$. Breadth-first search decides whether $t$ can be reached from $s$ in time linear in the size of the graph, but it keeps a mark on every vertex. A different method guesses the path one vertex at a time and keeps only the current vertex and a step counter, about forty bits. A third method, Savitch's recursion on a midpoint, uses no guessing and about $(\log n)^2$ bits. Now ask whether the vertices can be colored with three colors so that no edge joins two vertices of one color. No method is known that does this in polynomial time, but a coloring, once given, is checked in one pass over the edges. Ask finally who wins a game in which two players take turns choosing the values of variables of a formula. The best known methods use polynomial space and exponential time.

These three questions belong to three classes: reachability is complete for [NL](/nondeterministic-logspace/), 3-coloring for [NP](/np/) and the formula game for [PSPACE](/pspace/). A [complexity class](/complexity-class/) is the set of problems that one model of computation decides within one bound on one resource. This chapter defines the classes that the rest of the volume uses, proves the relations between them that are known, and states which ones are open. Most are open. The chain $\mathsf{L} \subseteq \mathsf{NL} \subseteq \mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{PSPACE} \subseteq \mathsf{EXP}$ is proved, and the hierarchy theorems show that $\mathsf{NL} \ne \mathsf{PSPACE}$ and $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{EXP}$. Whether any single step of the chain is strict is not known. P versus NP, P versus PSPACE, L versus P and BPP versus P are all open.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Classes, P and NP.** A [complexity class](/complexity-class/) fixes a model, a resource and a bound. [Computational tractability](/computational-tractability/) explains why [P](/p/) stands for the efficient problems. [NP](/np/), [coNP](/co-np/) and the [P versus NP problem](/p-versus-np-problem/) follow, and a [good characterization](/good-characterization/) is a problem in $\mathsf{NP} \cap \mathsf{coNP}$.
2. **Space and exponential time.** [L](/logspace/), [NL](/nondeterministic-logspace/), [PSPACE](/pspace/) with its complete problem [QSAT](/qsat/), [EXP](/exptime/) and [NEXP](/nexptime/), joined in the [chain of inclusions](/class-inclusions-chain/).
3. **Hierarchies and simulations.** The [time hierarchy theorem](/time-hierarchy-theorem/) and the [space hierarchy theorem](/space-hierarchy-theorem/) prove separations. [Savitch's theorem](/savitch-theorem/) squares space to remove nondeterminism, and the [Immerman-Szelepcsényi theorem](/immerman-szelepcsenyi-theorem/) shows that $\mathsf{NL} = \mathsf{coNL}$.
4. **Randomness.** [BPP](/bpp/), [RP](/rp/), [coRP](/co-rp/), [ZPP](/zpp/), [PP](/pp/) and the quantum class [BQP](/bqp/).
5. **Small space and parallel time.** [RL](/randomized-logspace/), [SL](/symmetric-logspace/), [SC](/sc-class/) and [NC](/nc/).
6. **Above and beside NP.** The [polynomial hierarchy](/polynomial-hierarchy/), the [Boolean hierarchy](/boolean-hierarchy/), [UP](/unambiguous-polynomial-time/), [NP-intermediate](/np-intermediate/) problems by [Ladner's theorem](/ladner-theorem/), and the [Sipser-Gács theorem](/sipser-gacs-theorem/), which puts BPP in the second level of the hierarchy.
7. **Circuits.** [Circuit complexity](/circuit-complexity/), [AC0](/ac0/), [TC0](/tc0/), [P/poly](/p-poly/), [Adleman's theorem](/adleman-theorem/) and the [Karp-Lipton theorem](/karp-lipton-theorem/).
8. **Search problems.** [FNP](/fnp/), [TFNP](/tfnp/), [PLS](/pls/) and [PPAD](/ppad/).
9. **Interactive and checkable proofs.** [IP](/interactive-proofs/), [AM](/arthur-merlin/), [MA](/merlin-arthur/), [MIP](/multi-prover-interactive-proofs/) and [QMA](/qma/), with the theorems [IP = PSPACE](/ip-equals-pspace/), [MIP = NEXP](/mip-equals-nexp/) and the [PCP theorem](/pcp-theorem/) about [probabilistically checkable proofs](/probabilistically-checkable-proof/).
10. **Counting and the barriers.** The [Valiant-Vazirani theorem](/valiant-vazirani-theorem/) and [Toda's theorem](/toda-theorem/) on counting, then [relativization](/relativization/), the [Baker-Gill-Solovay theorem](/baker-gill-solovay-theorem/), the [natural proofs barrier](/natural-proofs-barrier/) and the [algebrization barrier](/algebrization-barrier/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fourteen entries in order: [complexity class](/complexity-class/), [P](/p/), [NP](/np/), [coNP](/co-np/), [the P versus NP problem](/p-versus-np-problem/), [L](/logspace/), [NL](/nondeterministic-logspace/), [PSPACE](/pspace/), [QSAT](/qsat/), [the chain of inclusions](/class-inclusions-chain/), [the time hierarchy theorem](/time-hierarchy-theorem/), [Savitch's theorem](/savitch-theorem/), [BPP](/bpp/) and [the polynomial hierarchy](/polynomial-hierarchy/). Each class entry ends with its relations to the others, so draw the inclusions as you go. Mark each one as proved or open.

The circuit lower bounds, the interactive proofs and the barriers can wait. They are research topics, and their proofs use most of the earlier groups. Read the theorem statements first and the proofs when you need them.
