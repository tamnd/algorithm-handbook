## History

The oldest thread is geometric. Gajentaan and Overmars observed in 1995 that many problems of computational geometry, among them deciding whether $n$ points in the plane have three on a line, are at least as hard as 3SUM, and they named this class 3SUM-hard. On the exponential side, Impagliazzo and Paturi formulated the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and Impagliazzo, Paturi and Zane proved the sparsification lemma in 2001. The strong form, SETH, came from the same work; the name and the version with randomized algorithms are due to Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi (2009).

The bridge from exponential to polynomial time was built by Williams, who showed at ICALP 2004 that a split-and-list reduction turns CNF-SAT into orthogonal vectors, so that a strongly subquadratic algorithm for orthogonal vectors would refute SETH. In 2010, Pătraşcu introduced Convolution 3SUM and the multiphase problem to prove polynomial lower bounds for dynamic data structures, and Vassilevska Williams and Williams proved at FOCS 2010 that all-pairs shortest paths, the min-plus product and negative triangle detection are equivalent under subcubic reductions.

The field grew fast in the years after 2013. Roditty and Vassilevska Williams related the diameter of sparse graphs to orthogonal vectors. Bringmann proved the SETH bound for the Fréchet distance in 2014, Backurs and Indyk the bound for edit distance at STOC 2015, and Abboud, Backurs and Vassilevska Williams and, independently, Bringmann and Künnemann the bounds for LCS at FOCS 2015. Henzinger, Krinninger, Nanongkai and Saranurak stated the OMv conjecture at STOC 2015, which unified most dynamic lower bounds. New hypotheses followed: the set cover conjecture of Cygan and coauthors (2012), the min-plus convolution hypothesis of Cygan, Mucha, Węgrzycki and Włodarczyk (2017), and the weighted clique and hyperclique hypotheses of Lincoln, Vassilevska Williams and Williams (2018). Carmosino and coauthors showed in 2016 that, under a nondeterministic form of SETH, 3SUM and APSP cannot be SETH-hard by deterministic reductions, so the hypotheses are at least partly independent.

In the other direction, Williams' algorithmic method turned faster satisfiability algorithms into circuit lower bounds. He proved in 2011 that $\mathsf{NEXP}$ has no polynomial-size $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuits, and Murray and Williams extended the result to $\mathsf{NQP}$ in 2018.

In October 2026, preprints by Alman and Vassilevska Williams and by Chan, Itakura, Ma and Yu claimed truly subquadratic algorithms for 3SUM and a truly subcubic algorithm for APSP. They are not yet refereed. If they are correct, two of the three main hypotheses of the field are false, and the reductions built on them become statements about how problems relate rather than evidence of hardness. The entries of the chapter record these claims as preprints.

## Further reading

- Virginia Vassilevska Williams, "On some fine-grained questions in algorithms and complexity", *Proceedings of the International Congress of Mathematicians* (ICM 2018). The standard survey of the hypotheses, the main reductions and the equivalence classes.
- Virginia Vassilevska Williams and R. Ryan Williams, "Subcubic equivalences between path, matrix, and triangle problems", *Journal of the ACM* 65(5), 2018. The paper that defined subcubic reductions and proved the APSP class equivalent to negative triangle.
- Monika Henzinger, Sebastian Krinninger, Danupon Nanongkai and Thatchaphol Saranurak, "Unifying and strengthening hardness for dynamic problems via the online matrix-vector multiplication conjecture", STOC 2015. The source of the OMv conjecture and of a long table of tight dynamic lower bounds.
- Marek Cygan, Fedor V. Fomin, Łukasz Kowalik, Daniel Lokshtanov, Dániel Marx, Marcin Pilipczuk, Michał Pilipczuk and Saket Saurabh, *Parameterized Algorithms* (Springer, 2015). Chapter 14 gives lower bounds based on ETH and SETH with full proofs, including sparsification.
- R. Ryan Williams, "Nonuniform ACC circuit lower bounds", *Journal of the ACM* 61(1), 2014. The paper of the algorithmic method, readable once the nondeterministic time hierarchy is known.
