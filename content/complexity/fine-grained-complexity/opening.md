The edit distance of two strings of length $n$ is computed by a table with $n^2$ cells. For two strings of length $10^5$ that is $10^{10}$ cells, and fifty years of work have removed only logarithmic factors from that count. Backurs and Indyk showed in 2015 why that may be the best possible: an algorithm with time $O(n^{2-\delta})$ for some $\delta > 0$ would give a faster algorithm for satisfiability than the Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis allows. The same pattern holds for longest common subsequence, the Fréchet distance, the diameter of sparse graphs, and many problems of geometry and dynamic data structures.

Fine-grained complexity studies such statements. Polynomial time is too coarse a measure for them: edit distance is in P, and the question is whether its exponent 2 can be lowered. NP-hardness says nothing here, and unconditional lower bounds of this size are out of reach, as is a proof of $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{NP}$, which is open. So the field picks a few problems that are believed hard, states a precise hypothesis for each, and connects other problems to them by fine-grained reductions, which keep track of the exponents. The main hypotheses are SETH for $k$-SAT, the orthogonal vectors hypothesis, the 3SUM conjecture for time $n^2$, the APSP conjecture for time $n^3$, and the OMv conjecture for dynamic problems. Every lower bound of the chapter for a problem in P is conditional on one of these or a related hypothesis, and each statement names which.

The hypotheses are not equally safe. In October 2026, preprints by Alman and Vassilevska Williams and by Chan, Itakura, Ma and Yu, not yet refereed, gave truly subquadratic algorithms for 3SUM and a truly subcubic algorithm for APSP. If they are correct, those two conjectures are false. The reductions built on them remain theorems, but they no longer give lower bounds. The authors state that SETH, the orthogonal vectors hypothesis and OMv are not affected. The entries of the chapter say, case by case, what the preprints change.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Conditional lower bounds.** The [quadratic barrier](/quadratic-barrier/), what a [conditional lower bound](/conditional-lower-bound/) is, the [fine-grained reduction](/fine-grained-reduction/), the list of [quadratic-time hardness](/quadratic-time-hardness/) results, and [equivalence classes](/fine-grained-equivalence-class/) of problems.
2. **SETH and orthogonal vectors.** The [Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis](/strong-exponential-time-hypothesis/), with ETH and sparsification, [orthogonal vectors](/orthogonal-vectors/), the proof that [SETH implies the OV lower bound](/seth-implies-ov-lower-bound/), and the bounds for [edit distance](/edit-distance-seth-lower-bound/) and [LCS](/lcs-seth-lower-bound/).
3. **3SUM.** The problem [3SUM](/3sum/) and [$k$-SUM](/k-sum/), the [3SUM conjecture](/3sum-conjecture/), [3SUM-hardness](/3sum-hardness/), [Convolution 3SUM](/convolution-3sum/) and [min-plus convolution](/min-plus-convolution/).
4. **APSP, triangles and cliques.** The [APSP conjecture](/apsp-conjecture/), [negative triangle](/negative-triangle/) and the [proof of their equivalence](/apsp-negative-triangle-equivalence/), the weighted clique hypotheses, the [triangle detection hypothesis](/triangle-detection-hypothesis/) and the [combinatorial BMM conjecture](/combinatorial-bmm-conjecture/).
5. **Dynamic problems.** [Online matrix-vector multiplication](/online-matrix-vector-multiplication/), the [OMv conjecture](/omv-conjecture/) and the [dynamic lower bounds](/dynamic-lower-bounds/) that follow from it.
6. **Hypercliques and the algorithmic method.** The [hyperclique hypothesis](/hyperclique-hypothesis/), and the [algorithmic method](/algorithmic-method/), which turns faster satisfiability algorithms into circuit lower bounds.
