## History

Parameterized complexity took its present form in the early 1990s in the work of Downey and Fellows. Their papers "Fixed-parameter tractability and completeness I: basic results" (SIAM Journal on Computing, 1995) and its sequel in Theoretical Computer Science defined fixed-parameter tractability, parameterized reductions and the W-hierarchy, and proved that independent set and clique are $\mathsf{W}[1]$-complete and dominating set is $\mathsf{W}[2]$-complete. Some of the motivation came from graph minor theory. Robertson and Seymour had shown that testing for a fixed minor takes time $f(H) \cdot n^3$, so many problems were FPT long before the class had a name, often by proofs that give no explicit algorithm.

The algorithmic toolbox grew around vertex cover. The kernel with at most $k^2$ edges is credited to Buss. The linear program of Nemhauser and Trotter, from 1974 and 1975, was turned into a kernel with $2k$ vertices by Chen, Kanj and Jia in 2001, and crown reductions gave a combinatorial kernel with $3k$ vertices. Search trees improved step by step until Chen, Kanj and Xia reached $O(1.2738^k + kn)$ in 2010. Alon, Yuster and Zwick introduced color coding in 1995 for $k$-path and subgraph problems. Reed, Smith and Vetta solved odd cycle transversal in 2004 with a new idea, iterative compression, and Marx introduced important separators in 2006. Courcelle's theorem of 1990, with the linear-time treewidth algorithm of Bodlaender (1996), made monadic second-order logic a quick test for fixed-parameter tractability on graphs of bounded treewidth.

The hardness side became sharper in the 2000s. Chen, Huang, Kanj and Xia showed in 2006 that clique has no $f(k) \cdot n^{o(k)}$ algorithm under the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and lower bounds of this form became as common as $\mathsf{W}[1]$-hardness. The books of Flum and Grohe and of Niedermeier, both from 2006, gave the field its first textbook treatments of the logic side and the algorithm side.

The question of which problems have kernels of polynomial size was opened by Bodlaender, Downey, Fellows and Hermelin in 2009. With the theorem of Fortnow and Santhanam, they showed that $k$-path has no polynomial kernel unless $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{coNP/poly}$. Bodlaender, Thomassé and Yeo added polynomial parameter transformations, Bodlaender, Jansen and Kratsch generalized compositions to cross-compositions in 2011, and Dell and van Melkebeek proved in 2014 that vertex cover has no kernel of $O(k^{2-\varepsilon})$ bits. Lokshtanov, Panolan, Ramanujan and Saurabh defined lossy kernels in 2017.

On the structural side, the grid minor theorem of Robertson and Seymour led to bidimensionality, with subexponential algorithms on planar graphs, and to the irrelevant vertex technique for disjoint paths. Chekuri and Chuzhoy proved a polynomial bound for the grid minor theorem in 2014. Cygan and coauthors introduced cut and count in 2011, which made dynamic programs for connectivity problems on tree decompositions single exponential. The book of Cygan, Fomin, Kowalik, Lokshtanov, Marx, Pilipczuk, Pilipczuk and Saurabh (2015) collects these methods, and the PACE challenge, which took vertex cover as its problem in 2019, tests them in practice.

## Further reading

- Marek Cygan, Fedor V. Fomin, Łukasz Kowalik, Daniel Lokshtanov, Dániel Marx, Marcin Pilipczuk, Michał Pilipczuk and Saket Saurabh, *Parameterized Algorithms* (Springer, 2015). The standard textbook on algorithms, from search trees and kernels to treewidth, cuts, algebraic methods and lower bounds.
- Rodney G. Downey and Michael R. Fellows, *Fundamentals of Parameterized Complexity* (Springer, 2013). The founders' account of the theory, with the W-hierarchy and its completeness results in full.
- Jörg Flum and Martin Grohe, *Parameterized Complexity Theory* (Springer, 2006). The best source for the logic side: weighted satisfiability, the W- and A-hierarchies, model checking and meta-theorems.
- Rolf Niedermeier, *Invitation to Fixed-Parameter Algorithms* (Oxford University Press, 2006). A short introduction to search trees, kernels and iterative compression, with many worked problems.
- Fedor V. Fomin and Dieter Kratsch, *Exact Exponential Algorithms* (Springer, 2010). The companion topic of exponential algorithms in $n$, including measure and conquer.
