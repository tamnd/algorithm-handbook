10. Let $L$ be in NP, with some $y \in L$ and some $z \notin L$. For $A$ in NP, which is P, decide $x \in A$ in polynomial time, and output $y$ if yes and $z$ if no. This is a Karp reduction from $A$ to $L$. A reduction to $\emptyset$ has no yes-instance to map to, and one to $\{0,1\}^*$ has no no-instance, so only $\emptyset$ reduces to $\emptyset$ and only $\{0,1\}^*$ reduces to $\{0,1\}^*$. SAT is neither.

17. Every triple of a maximum matching meets some triple that greedy keeps, or greedy would have kept it too. A kept triple has three elements, and the triples of a matching are disjoint, so it meets at most three of them. For the instance, scan $(1, b, z)$ first, then $(1, a, x)$, $(2, b, y)$ and $(3, c, z)$. Greedy keeps only the first triple, and the last three form a matching of size 3.

21. A yes-certificate is the divisor $d$. A no-certificate is the prime factorization $N = p_1 \cdots p_r$ with every $p_i > k$, together with a primality proof for each $p_i$. There are at most $\lg N$ factors, so it has polynomial length. If FACTOR were NP-complete, every NP problem would be in coNP, and NP = coNP would follow.

23. A perfect matching takes all of $T_i^t$ or all of $T_i^f$ in each ring, and this choice is an assignment $a$. Each clause pair $s_1[j], s_2[j]$ needs a free literal element of a true literal of clause $j$, which gives $t_j(a)$ choices, or none if $a$ falsifies the clause. That uses $m$ of the $mn$ free literal elements. Each garbage pair then takes one of the other $m(n-1)$, in any order, which gives $(m(n-1))!$ ways. For the formula of the exercise, $n = 3$ and $m = 2$. It has 6 satisfying assignments, the sum of the products is 12, and there are $4! \cdot 12 = 288$ perfect matchings. A script counted 288 by search.

24. The $m - 1$ unit jobs must run in fixed slots, and the total length equals the horizon $m(B + 1) - 1$. So the machine is never idle, and each of the $m$ gaps of length $B$ is filled by jobs whose lengths sum to $B$. Conversely, any split into groups of sum $B$ fills the gaps. The direction from a schedule to a 3-partition uses the bounds: a group with sum $B$ of numbers strictly between $B/4$ and $B/2$ has exactly three numbers. The numbers stay at most $m(B + 1)$, which is polynomial in the size and largest number of the source instance. 3-partition is strongly NP-complete, so the target is too. The reduction from partition keeps numbers as large as those of partition, which is only weakly hard.

25. Search the assignments of the formula variable by variable. At each level keep a list of partial formulas, simplified so that none is longer than the input, and compute the image $f(\psi)$ of each under the reduction $f$ from SAT to $T$. Drop $\psi$ if $f(\psi) \notin 1^*$, since then $\psi$ is unsatisfiable. Keep only one $\psi$ for each image, since two formulas with the same image are both satisfiable or both not. The images have length at most a polynomial $p(n)$, so at most $p(n) + 1$ formulas survive at each level, and the search takes polynomial time.
