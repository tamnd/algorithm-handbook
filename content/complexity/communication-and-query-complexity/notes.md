## History

Yao defined the two-party model in his STOC 1979 paper "Some complexity questions related to distributive computing", with the protocol tree, the rectangle property and the first lower bounds. Mehlhorn and Schmidt proved the rank bound in 1982, in a paper that compares Las Vegas protocols with deterministic ones, and Aho, Ullman and Yannakakis compared the fooling set and rank methods and related deterministic to nondeterministic cost in 1983. Lovász and Saks stated the log-rank conjecture at FOCS 1988. It is still open; Kushilevitz found a function whose cost is about $(\log \operatorname{rank})^{1.63}$, Göös, Pitassi and Watson raised it to about 2, and Lovett and then Sudakov and Tomon brought the upper bound down to $O(\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}})$.

Randomized protocols came next. Babai, Frankl and Simon (1986) built complexity classes of communication problems and proved the $\Omega(\sqrt n)$ bound for disjointness under a product distribution. Paturi and Simon (1986) described unbounded-error protocols by sign rank, Chor and Goldreich (1988) bounded inner product by discrepancy, and Newman (1991) showed that public coins save at most $O(\log n)$ bits. Kalyanasundaram and Schnitger proved $R(\mathrm{DISJ}_n) = \Omega(n)$, and Razborov (1992) gave the shorter proof by corruption. Chandra, Furst and Lipton introduced the number-on-forehead model in 1983, and Babai, Nisan and Szegedy (1992) proved its first strong bounds. Yao defined quantum communication in 1993.

Information complexity grew from the direct sum question. Chakrabarti, Shi, Wirth and Yao (2001) used information cost in the simultaneous model, and Bar-Yossef, Jayram, Kumar and Sivakumar (2004) reproved the disjointness bound with it. Barak, Braverman, Chen and Rao (2010) compressed protocols to their information cost, and Braverman and Rao (2011) proved that information equals amortized communication. Karchmer and Wigderson connected communication to formula depth at STOC 1988 and used it for the first super-logarithmic bound on monotone depth.

On the query side, Nisan introduced block sensitivity in 1989, in a paper on CREW PRAMs, and Nisan and Szegedy (1992) related it to the degree and asked whether sensitivity is polynomially related too. Saks and Wigderson (1986) found the exponent $0.7537$ for randomized evaluation of NAND trees, and Rubinstein (1995) gave a quadratic gap between sensitivity and block sensitivity. Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani (1997) proved that Grover search is optimal with the hybrid argument, Beals, Buhrman, Cleve, Mosca and de Wolf introduced the polynomial method at FOCS 1998, and Ambainis introduced the adversary method in 2000. Huang proved the sensitivity conjecture in 2019, and Aaronson, Ben-David, Kothari, Rao and Tal (2021) used his theorem to prove $D^{dt}(f) = O(Q(f)^4)$.

## Further reading

- Eyal Kushilevitz and Noam Nisan, *Communication Complexity* (Cambridge University Press, 1997). The classic text on the two-party model, randomized protocols, multiparty models and applications.
- Anup Rao and Amir Yehudayoff, *Communication Complexity and Applications* (Cambridge University Press, 2020). A modern text with information complexity, lifting and many applications to data structures and circuits.
- Harry Buhrman and Ronald de Wolf, "Complexity measures and decision tree complexity: a survey", *Theoretical Computer Science* 288 (2002). The standard reference for the query measures and the relations among them, classical and quantum.
- Stasys Jukna, *Boolean Function Complexity: Advances and Frontiers* (Springer, 2012). Covers formula lower bounds, the Karchmer-Wigderson game and multiparty communication in the context of circuits.
- Sanjeev Arora and Boaz Barak, *Computational Complexity: A Modern Approach* (Cambridge University Press, 2009). Its chapters on communication complexity and decision trees are a short first introduction.
