Most numerical computations end in a linear system $Ax = b$. Newton's method for a nonlinear system solves one in every step, an implicit method for a differential equation solves one in every time step, and a least squares fit solves one in the sense of minimizing $\|Ax - b\|_2$. Solving by hand is [Gaussian elimination](/gaussian-elimination/); solving on a computer adds three concerns that elimination in exact arithmetic does not have. Rounding errors can grow during elimination unless rows are exchanged. Some matrices are so close to singular that no method can give an accurate answer in double precision: the [Hilbert matrix](/hilbert-matrix/) of order 12 has a condition number above $10^{16}$. And the matrices that arise from physics and engineering are often large and sparse, with millions of rows but only a few nonzeros in each, so that a dense method costing $2n^3/3$ operations is out of the question.

The chapter follows these concerns in turn. It first builds the dense direct methods and their error analysis, then least squares, then the methods that keep a sparse matrix sparse, and finally the iterative methods that never factor the matrix at all.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Matrices and how to measure them.** [Matrix norms](/matrix-norms/), [positive definite](/positive-definite-matrix/) and [diagonally dominant](/diagonally-dominant-matrix/) matrices, the [spectral radius](/spectral-radius/) and the [residual](/residual/).
2. **Triangular systems and factorizations.** [Back substitution](/back-substitution/), the [LUP decomposition](/lup-decomposition/), [LUP-SOLVE](/lup-solve/) and the [Cholesky decomposition](/cholesky-decomposition/).
3. **Pivoting and accuracy.** The [growth factor](/growth-factor/), [partial](/partial-pivoting/) and [complete pivoting](/complete-pivoting/), [iterative refinement](/iterative-refinement/) and [condition estimation](/hagers-condition-estimator/).
4. **Block elimination and updates.** The [Schur complement](/schur-complement/), the [Sherman-Morrison formula](/sherman-morrison-formula/), the [Woodbury identity](/woodbury-identity/), and the [Sylvester](/sylvester-equation/) and [Lyapunov](/lyapunov-equation/) equations.
5. **Least squares and orthogonal factorizations.** The [least squares problem](/least-squares-problem/), the [normal equations](/normal-equations/), [Householder reflections](/householder-reflections/), [Givens rotations](/givens-rotations/), the [QR decomposition](/qr-decomposition/), [Gram-Schmidt](/gram-schmidt-process/), the [pseudoinverse](/pseudoinverse/) and [Tikhonov regularization](/tikhonov-regularization/).
6. **Sparse, banded and structured matrices.** [Sparse matrices](/sparse-matrix/), [banded matrices](/banded-matrix/), the [Thomas algorithm](/thomas-algorithm/), [cyclic reduction](/cyclic-reduction/) and the [Levinson-Durbin recursion](/levinson-durbin-recursion/).
7. **Orderings and sparse factorization.** [Fill-in](/fill-in/), [reverse Cuthill-McKee](/reverse-cuthill-mckee/), [minimum degree](/minimum-degree-ordering/), [nested dissection](/nested-dissection/), the [elimination tree](/elimination-tree/) and the [multifrontal method](/multifrontal-method/).
8. **Stationary iterations.** [Matrix splitting](/matrix-splitting/), the [Jacobi](/jacobi-method/) and [Gauss-Seidel](/gauss-seidel-method/) methods, [SOR](/successive-over-relaxation/), [Richardson iteration](/richardson-iteration/) and the [Kaczmarz method](/kaczmarz-method/).
9. **Krylov methods and preconditioning.** [Krylov subspaces](/krylov-subspace/), [conjugate gradients](/conjugate-gradient-method/), [preconditioning](/preconditioning/), [incomplete Cholesky](/incomplete-cholesky-factorization/), [MINRES](/minres/), [GMRES](/gmres/) and [BiCGSTAB](/bicgstab/).
10. **Multilevel methods.** [Multigrid](/multigrid-method/), [algebraic multigrid](/algebraic-multigrid/) and [domain decomposition](/domain-decomposition/).
