To solve an equation numerically, write it as $f(x) = 0$ and search for a point where $f$ vanishes. Most equations have no formula for their solutions, and even the cubic, which has one, is often better solved by iteration. A root finder evaluates $f$ at points of its choosing and uses the values to pick the next point. Two questions then decide everything: does the sequence of points reach a root, and how many evaluations does it need for a given accuracy. [Bisection](/bisection-method/) answers the first question with a guarantee and the second with about one bit per evaluation. [Newton's method](/newton-raphson-method/) doubles the number of correct digits in each step near a simple root and may wander off or cycle when started far from one. For $x^2 - 2$ on $[1, 2]$, bisection needs 52 halvings to reach full double precision; Newton's method from $x_0 = 1$ needs five steps.

Most of the chapter is about getting both properties at once, and about what changes when the equation is a polynomial or a system. The [order of convergence](/order-of-convergence/) compares methods, and [floating-point](/floating-point-arithmetic/) limits set how far any of them can go: no method finds a root more accurately than the values of $f$ near it can be computed.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **The problem and its measures.** The [root-finding problem](/root-finding-problem/), [order of convergence](/order-of-convergence/), [multiplicity](/multiplicity-of-roots/), [quadratic convergence](/quadratic-convergence/) and [stopping criteria](/stopping-criteria/).
2. **Brackets and bisection.** The [bracketing](/bracketing-method/) invariant and the [bisection method](/bisection-method/).
3. **Equations with formulas.** The stable [quadratic formula](/quadratic-formula/), the [fundamental theorem of algebra](/fundamental-theorem-of-algebra/), [Cardano's formula](/cardano-formula/), [Ferrari's method](/ferrari-method/) and the [Abel-Ruffini theorem](/abel-ruffini-theorem/).
4. **Iterations from a starting point.** [Fixed-point iteration](/fixed-point-iteration/), Newton's method, the [secant method](/secant-method/), [regula falsi](/regula-falsi/), [Newton's square root](/newton-square-root/) and the [Banach fixed-point theorem](/banach-fixed-point-theorem/).
5. **Safe and fast methods for one equation.** The [safeguarded Newton method](/newton-bisection-hybrid/), the [Illinois](/illinois-method/), [Ridders](/ridders-method/) and [Dekker](/dekker-method/) methods, [Brent's method](/brent-method/) and [Chandrupatla's method](/chandrupatla-method/).
6. **Higher order without a bracket.** [Halley's method](/halley-method/), [Householder's methods](/householder-methods/), [Muller's method](/muller-method/) and [Steffensen's method](/steffensen-method/).
7. **Polynomial roots and their sensitivity.** [Polynomial root finding](/polynomial-root-finding/), [root conditioning](/root-conditioning/), [Wilkinson's polynomial](/wilkinson-polynomial/), root bounds and [real root counting](/real-root-counting/).
8. **Systems of equations.** [Newton's method for systems](/newton-method-for-systems/), [Broyden's method](/broyden-method/), the [Kantorovich theorem](/kantorovich-theorem/) and [homotopy continuation](/homotopy-continuation/).
9. **Polynomial root finders.** [Deflation](/polynomial-deflation/), [Laguerre's method](/laguerre-method/), [Bairstow's method](/bairstow-method/), the [Durand-Kerner](/durand-kerner-method/) and [Aberth-Ehrlich](/aberth-ehrlich-method/) methods, [Jenkins-Traub](/jenkins-traub-algorithm/) and the [companion matrix method](/companion-matrix-method/).
10. **Certified roots and global behaviour.** The [interval Newton method](/interval-newton-method/), the [Schur-Cohn test](/schur-cohn-test/), the [Lehmer-Schur method](/lehmer-schur-method/) and the [Newton fractal](/newton-fractal/).
