A floating-point number is a fixed number of significant digits together with a scale, as in scientific notation. A double-precision float keeps 53 binary digits, about 16 decimal ones, and a scale that reaches from about $10^{-308}$ to $10^{308}$. After each operation the exact result is rounded to the nearest number of that form. One rounding changes a value by at most one part in $2^{53}$, and that is usually far below anything that matters. But the errors add up, and some steps magnify them. In double precision `0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3` is false, $10^{16} + 1$ equals $10^{16}$, and the textbook formula for the small root of $x^2 - 10^8 x + 1$ is wrong by 25 percent.

This chapter explains the arithmetic and how to reason about its errors. Every later chapter of the volume uses it: the error bound of Gaussian elimination, the accuracy that Newton's method can reach and the stopping tests of iterative solvers all rest on the facts collected here.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Floating-point numbers.** The number system, the [IEEE 754](/ieee-754/) formats and their bit encodings, the [special values](/special-floating-point-values/) and the [exception flags](/floating-point-exceptions/), and two other formats in use today: bfloat16 and decimal floating point.
2. **Spacing and rounding.** The [ulp](/ulp/), [machine epsilon](/machine-epsilon/) and the unit roundoff $u$, [relative error](/relative-error/), [subnormal numbers](/subnormal-numbers/), the five [rounding modes](/rounding-modes/), and [double rounding](/double-rounding/).
3. **Error analysis.** The [standard model](/floating-point-model/) of one operation, [catastrophic cancellation](/catastrophic-cancellation/), the [condition number](/condition-number/) of a problem, and [backward error analysis](/backward-error-analysis/), which together decide whether an algorithm is [numerically stable](/numerical-stability/).
4. **Error-free transformations.** [TwoSum](/two-sum/), [Fast2Sum](/fast-two-sum/) and [TwoProduct](/two-product/) compute the exact rounding error of one operation, and [double-double arithmetic](/double-double-arithmetic/) builds about 106 bits of precision from them.
5. **Summation and dot products.** [Pairwise](/pairwise-summation/), [Kahan](/kahan-summation/) and [Neumaier](/neumaier-summation/) summation, the [accurate dot product](/accurate-dot-product/), and exact and reproducible sums.
6. **Binary and decimal.** Reading a decimal string into the [correctly rounded float](/decimal-to-floating-point/), and printing a float as the [shortest string](/shortest-round-trip-representation/) that reads back to it, with [Dragon4](/dragon4/), [Grisu](/grisu/) and [Ryu](/ryu/).
7. **Division and square roots in hardware.** [SRT division](/srt-division/), [Goldschmidt division](/goldschmidt-division/), and the [fast inverse square root](/fast-inverse-square-root/).
8. **Elementary functions.** What it takes to round $\exp$ or $\sin$ correctly (the [table-maker's dilemma](/table-maker-dilemma/)), and how to reduce a large argument modulo $\pi/2$ with [Cody-Waite](/cody-waite-range-reduction/) or [Payne-Hanek](/payne-hanek-argument-reduction/) reduction.
9. **Other formats and roundings.** Block floating point, posits, logarithmic number systems and [stochastic rounding](/stochastic-rounding/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: [floating-point arithmetic](/floating-point-arithmetic/), [IEEE 754](/ieee-754/), [ulp](/ulp/), [machine epsilon](/machine-epsilon/), [round to nearest, ties to even](/round-to-nearest-even/), the [standard model](/floating-point-model/), [catastrophic cancellation](/catastrophic-cancellation/), the [condition number](/condition-number/), [backward error analysis](/backward-error-analysis/), [numerical stability](/numerical-stability/), [floating-point summation](/floating-point-summation/) and [Kahan summation](/kahan-summation/). They are the entries that the rest of the volume cites most. Groups 6, 7 and 8 can wait: they matter to those who write compilers, runtimes, math libraries and hardware, and the rest of the volume does not depend on them. The proofs in [rounding error analysis](/rounding-error-analysis/) are worth one careful reading, since the same few steps prove most error bounds in Chapters 12.3 and 12.4.

## Before you start

You need [positional number systems](/positional-number-system/) and the idea of a [radix](/radix/) from Volume 11, and [radix conversion](/radix-conversion/) and [big integers](/big-integer/) for the conversion entries. The fast inverse square root uses one step of [Newton's method](/newton-raphson-method/) from Chapter 12.2. The error analysis uses only algebra and the triangle inequality, but its examples include sums, [dot products](/dot-product/), [Horner's rule](/horner-rule/) and [Gaussian elimination](/gaussian-elimination/).
