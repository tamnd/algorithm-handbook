## History

Random numbers for computers began with the Monte Carlo work of the late 1940s. John von Neumann suggested the [middle-square method](/middle-square-method/) about 1946 and presented it at a Monte Carlo symposium in 1949, published in 1951, where he wrote that anyone who considers arithmetical methods of producing random digits is in a state of sin. At the Harvard symposium of 1949 D. H. Lehmer proposed the multiplicative [congruential method](/linear-congruential-generator/). Knuth credits the mixed method to W. E. Thomson (1958) and A. Rotenberg (1960). Greenberger had the power-of-two case in 1961, and Hull and Dobell proved the general full-period conditions of the [Hull-Dobell theorem](/hull-dobell-theorem/) in SIAM Review in 1962.

The trouble with congruential generators came to light in the late 1960s. Coveyou and MacPherson introduced the [spectral test](/spectral-test/) in 1967, and in 1968 Marsaglia published "Random numbers fall mainly in the planes", which bounded the number of planes for every linear congruential generator. [RANDU](/randu/), IBM's generator in the Scientific Subroutine Package of System/360, became the standard bad example. Lewis, Goodman and Miller proposed the multiplier 16807 in 1969, Schrage gave a portable way to compute the product in 1979, and Park and Miller named it the [minimal standard](/park-miller-minimal-standard/) in 1988. Park, Miller and Stockmeyer preferred 48271 in 1993.

Generators over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ grew from the shift registers of the 1950s, which Golomb's book of 1967 surveyed. Tausworthe used them for random numbers in 1965 and Lewis and Payne introduced the GFSR, a [linear feedback shift register](/linear-feedback-shift-register/) generator in 1973. Matsumoto and Kurita twisted it in 1992 and 1994, and Matsumoto and Nishimura published the [Mersenne Twister](/mersenne-twister/) MT19937 in 1998. Marsaglia introduced [xorshift](/xorshift/) in 2003, Panneton, L'Ecuyer and Matsumoto introduced [WELL](/well-rng/) in 2006, and Blackman and Vigna introduced xoroshiro128+ in 2016 and [xoshiro](/xoshiro-xoroshiro/) in 2018. Along another line, L'Ecuyer [combined generators](/lecuyer-combined-generator/) in 1988 and published [MRG32k3a](/mrg32k3a/) in 1999, and Lüscher's [RANLUX](/ranlux/) of 1994 repaired the subtract-with-borrow generator of Marsaglia and Zaman, a [lagged Fibonacci generator](/lagged-fibonacci-generator/) with a borrow. In the 2010s came generators built for speed and parallel use: Salmon, Moraes, Dror and Shaw presented [Philox](/philox/) and [Threefry](/threefry/) in 2011, Steele, Lea and Flood introduced [SplitMix](/splitmix64/) in 2014, and O'Neill introduced [PCG](/pcg-random/) in the same year. NumPy adopted PCG64 in 2019, the year Lemire published his [method for bounded integers](/lemire-bounded-integers/).

Cryptography asked for a stronger kind of generator. [Blum and Micali](/blum-micali/) presented their construction at FOCS in 1982, at the same conference Yao showed that unpredictability is equivalent to passing every polynomial-time statistical test, and Blum, Blum and Shub presented their quadratic residue generator, [Blum Blum Shub](/blum-blum-shub/), at CRYPTO 1982, with the full paper in 1986. In 1999 Håstad, Impagliazzo, Levin and Luby published a pseudorandom generator built from any one-way function. NIST published SP 800-90, by Barker and Kelsey, in June 2006. Shumow and Ferguson pointed out the possible back door in [Dual_EC_DRBG](/dual-ec-drbg/) at the CRYPTO 2007 rump session, NIST recommended against it in 2013, and SP 800-90A Rev. 1 removed it in 2015.

Low-discrepancy sequences have a separate history. Van der Corput gave his one-dimensional sequence in 1935, [Halton](/halton-sequence/) extended it to many bases in 1960, and Sobol' introduced [his sequence](/sobol-sequence/) in 1967. Antonov and Saleev gave the Gray code construction in 1979, and Joe and Kuo published direction numbers in 2003 and 2008.

## Further reading

- Donald E. Knuth, *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 2, Seminumerical Algorithms*, third edition, Addison-Wesley, 1997. The standard account of congruential generators, the spectral test and the statistical tests.
- Pierre L'Ecuyer, "Uniform random number generation", Annals of Operations Research 53, 1994, pages 77 to 120. A survey of linear generators and their lattice and equidistribution structure.
- Harald Niederreiter, *Random Number Generation and Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods*, SIAM, CBMS-NSF Regional Conference Series in Applied Mathematics 63, 1992. The theory of pseudorandom and low-discrepancy sequences in one book.
- Pierre L'Ecuyer and Richard Simard, "TestU01, a C library for empirical testing of random number generators", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 33(4), 2007. The test batteries most modern generators are judged by.
- Oded Goldreich, *Foundations of Cryptography, Volume 1, Basic Tools*, Cambridge University Press, 2001. Pseudorandom generators from one-way functions, with full proofs.
