A computer that follows rules cannot flip a coin, yet simulation, randomized algorithms, games and cryptography all consume random numbers in bulk. A [pseudorandom number generator](/pseudorandom-number-generator/) answers with a deterministic rule: it keeps a small state, changes it by a fixed map at each call, and computes an output from the state. The outputs only imitate independent uniform draws, and two questions decide whether the imitation is good enough. The first is mathematical: how long the [period](/generator-period/) is, and how evenly consecutive outputs fill space. The second is empirical: whether any test that a program could run tells the outputs from true randomness.

The answers differ by use. A Monte Carlo simulation needs speed, a long period and no pattern that its own computation could pick up. The [linear congruential generator](/linear-congruential-generator/) is fast and has an exact theory, but its triples lie on parallel planes, and for [RANDU](/randu/) there are only 15 of them. A cryptographic key needs unpredictability against an adversary, which no statistical generator gives: 624 outputs of the [Mersenne Twister](/mersenne-twister/) reveal its whole state. And every generator is only as good as its [seed](/seed/). From 2006 to 2008 the Debian package of OpenSSL drew its seeds from little more than the process identifier, so every key it made came from a small known set.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Generators, seeds and periods.** The [period of a generator](/generator-period/), the generator as a state machine, the seed, the [middle-square method](/middle-square-method/) as a warning, and [statistical tests for randomness](/statistical-tests-for-randomness/).
2. **Linear congruential generators.** The LCG, the [Park-Miller minimal standard](/park-miller-minimal-standard/), RANDU, the [Hull-Dobell theorem](/hull-dobell-theorem/) on full period, [Marsaglia's theorem](/marsaglia-hyperplane-theorem/) on hyperplanes and the [spectral test](/spectral-test/).
3. **Linear generators over GF(2).** The [linear feedback shift register](/linear-feedback-shift-register/), [xorshift](/xorshift/), the Mersenne Twister, [equidistribution](/equidistribution/), the [WELL generators](/well-rng/) and [xoshiro and xoroshiro](/xoshiro-xoroshiro/).
4. **Longer recurrences and parallel streams.** [Multiple recursive generators](/multiple-recursive-generator/), [L'Ecuyer's combined generators](/lecuyer-combined-generator/), [jump-ahead](/jump-ahead/), [MRG32k3a](/mrg32k3a/), [multiply-with-carry](/multiply-with-carry/), the [lagged Fibonacci generator](/lagged-fibonacci-generator/) and [RANLUX](/ranlux/).
5. **Scrambled outputs and counter-based generators.** [PCG](/pcg-random/), [SplitMix64](/splitmix64/), [Philox](/philox/) and [Threefry](/threefry/).
6. **Random integers in a range.** [Modulo bias](/modulo-bias/), [uniform random integer generation](/uniform-random-integer-generation/) and [Lemire's method](/lemire-bounded-integers/).
7. **Physical randomness and secure generators.** The [entropy source](/entropy-source/), the [true random number generator](/true-random-number-generator/), [unbiased bits from a biased coin](/unbiased-bits-from-biased-coin/), the [cryptographically secure generator](/cryptographically-secure-prng/), [Blum-Micali](/blum-micali/) and [Blum Blum Shub](/blum-blum-shub/).
8. **The NIST deterministic generators.** [Hash_DRBG](/hash-drbg/), [HMAC_DRBG](/hmac-drbg/), [CTR_DRBG](/ctr-drbg/) and the withdrawn [Dual_EC_DRBG](/dual-ec-drbg/).
9. **Low-discrepancy sequences.** The [Halton](/halton-sequence/) and [Sobol](/sobol-sequence/) sequences for quasi-Monte Carlo integration.

## A first reading

Read these entries first, in this order: the [pseudorandom number generator](/pseudorandom-number-generator/), the [period](/generator-period/), the [seed](/seed/), the [linear congruential generator](/linear-congruential-generator/), the [Hull-Dobell theorem](/hull-dobell-theorem/), [RANDU](/randu/), [Marsaglia's theorem](/marsaglia-hyperplane-theorem/), the [linear feedback shift register](/linear-feedback-shift-register/), [xorshift](/xorshift/), the [Mersenne Twister](/mersenne-twister/), [statistical tests for randomness](/statistical-tests-for-randomness/), [PCG](/pcg-random/), [modulo bias](/modulo-bias/), [Lemire's method](/lemire-bounded-integers/) and the [cryptographically secure generator](/cryptographically-secure-prng/). With them you can say what a library generator does, why its period and its tests matter, how to draw an unbiased integer from it, and when it must not be used. The combined and lagged generators of group 4, the NIST generators and the proofs behind Blum-Micali and Blum Blum Shub can wait.
