An [eigenvalue](/eigenvalue/) of a square matrix $A$ is a number $\lambda$ with $Av = \lambda v$ for some nonzero vector $v$. The eigenvalues are the roots of the [characteristic polynomial](/characteristic-polynomial/), but that is not how they are computed. The roots of a polynomial can be far more sensitive to its coefficients than the eigenvalues are to the entries of the matrix: the diagonal matrix with entries $1, 2, \dots, 20$ has perfectly conditioned eigenvalues, while its characteristic polynomial is [Wilkinson's polynomial](/wilkinson-polynomial/), whose roots scatter into the complex plane under a change in the eighth digit of one coefficient. And by the [Abel-Ruffini theorem](/abel-ruffini-theorem/) there is no formula in radicals for $n \ge 5$, so every eigenvalue method is an iteration.

Numerical methods work on the matrix itself. They apply [similarity transformations](/similarity-transformation/) $S^{-1}AS$, which keep the eigenvalues, and choose $S$ unitary so that rounding errors are not magnified. The standard dense method reduces $A$ to [Hessenberg form](/hessenberg-reduction/) and runs the [QR algorithm](/qr-algorithm/) until the matrix is triangular; it finds all eigenvalues of a general matrix of order $n$ in about $10n^3$ flops, and those of a symmetric one in about $4n^3/3$. For large sparse matrices the chapter turns to methods that only multiply by $A$ and find a few eigenvalues.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Eigenvalues, eigenvectors and similarity.** [Eigenvectors](/eigenvector/), [diagonalization](/diagonalization/), the [eigenvalue problem](/eigenvalue-problem/), the [Schur decomposition](/schur-decomposition/) and the [spectral theorem](/spectral-theorem/).
2. **Bounds and variational principles.** The [Gershgorin theorem](/gershgorin-circle-theorem/), the [Rayleigh quotient](/rayleigh-quotient/), the [Courant-Fischer theorem](/courant-fischer-theorem/), [Cauchy interlacing](/cauchy-interlacing-theorem/), [Weyl's inequality](/weyls-inequality/) and the [Perron-Frobenius theorem](/perron-frobenius-theorem/).
3. **Vector iterations.** [Power iteration](/power-iteration/), [inverse iteration](/inverse-iteration/), the [Rayleigh quotient iteration](/rayleigh-quotient-iteration/), [deflation](/deflation/) and [subspace iteration](/subspace-iteration/).
4. **The nonsymmetric problem and the QR algorithm.** The [nonsymmetric eigenproblem](/nonsymmetric-eigenproblem/), Hessenberg reduction, the QR algorithm, the [Francis double-shift step](/francis-double-shift-qr/) and [balancing](/matrix-balancing/).
5. **The symmetric problem.** The [symmetric eigenproblem](/symmetric-eigenproblem/), [tridiagonalization](/tridiagonalization/), the [tridiagonal eigenproblem](/tridiagonal-eigenproblem/), the [Wilkinson shift](/wilkinson-shift/) and the [Jacobi algorithm](/jacobi-eigenvalue-algorithm/).
6. **Solvers for the tridiagonal problem.** [Sturm counts](/sturm-sequence-eigenvalue-count/), [bisection](/bisection-eigenvalue-algorithm/), the [secular equation](/secular-equation/), [divide and conquer](/divide-and-conquer-eigensolver/) and [MRRR](/mrrr-algorithm/).
7. **Singular values.** The [singular value decomposition](/singular-value-decomposition/), the [Golub-Kahan algorithm](/golub-kahan-svd-algorithm/) and [dqds](/dqds-algorithm/).
8. **Sensitivity of eigenvalues and subspaces.** The [eigenvalue condition number](/eigenvalue-condition-number/), the [Bauer-Fike](/bauer-fike-theorem/), [Hoffman-Wielandt](/hoffman-wielandt-theorem/) and [Davis-Kahan](/davis-kahan-theorem/) theorems, and the [pseudospectrum](/pseudospectrum/).
9. **Large sparse problems.** The [Rayleigh-Ritz method](/rayleigh-ritz-method/), the [Arnoldi iteration](/arnoldi-iteration/), the [Lanczos algorithm](/lanczos-algorithm/) and [implicit restarts](/implicitly-restarted-arnoldi/).
10. **Generalized problems.** The [generalized eigenvalue problem](/generalized-eigenvalue-problem/) and the [QZ algorithm](/qz-algorithm/).

## A first reading

Read these entries first, in this order: [eigenvalue](/eigenvalue/), [eigenvector](/eigenvector/), [similarity transformation](/similarity-transformation/), the [spectral theorem](/spectral-theorem/), the [Gershgorin theorem](/gershgorin-circle-theorem/), the [Rayleigh quotient](/rayleigh-quotient/), [power iteration](/power-iteration/), [inverse iteration](/inverse-iteration/), the [Schur decomposition](/schur-decomposition/), [Hessenberg reduction](/hessenberg-reduction/), the [QR algorithm](/qr-algorithm/), the [symmetric eigenproblem](/symmetric-eigenproblem/), the [singular value decomposition](/singular-value-decomposition/), the [eigenvalue condition number](/eigenvalue-condition-number/) and the [Lanczos algorithm](/lanczos-algorithm/). With them you can say where the eigenvalues of a matrix lie, compute one of them by a vector iteration, understand what a library routine does to find all of them, and judge how far to trust the result. The tridiagonal solvers of group 6, dqds and the QZ algorithm are refinements that can wait.
