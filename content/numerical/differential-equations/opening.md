A differential equation gives the rate of change of a quantity, and the task is to recover the quantity. Planet orbits, chemical reactions, heat flowing through a wall and air flowing over a wing are all written this way. Only a few such equations have solutions in closed form, so programs compute approximate solutions on a grid of times, or of points in space and time, and the questions are how large the error is and how much work it costs.

The simplest method shows both sides of the problem. [Euler's method](/euler-method/) follows the slope at the current point for one step of length $h$. On $y' = -50(y - \cos t)$, $y(0) = 0$, whose solution settles onto $\cos t$ after a short transient, Euler with $h = 0.03$ has an error of $1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ at $t = 1.2$. With $h = 0.04$ the error is $1.0$, and with $h = 0.05$ it is $1.7 \times 10^4$. The solution is smooth, so accuracy does not explain the jump; stability does. [Backward Euler](/backward-euler-method/), which uses the slope at the end of the step, keeps an error below $3 \times 10^{-3}$ even with $h = 0.4$. Problems like this are called [stiff](/stiff-ode-problem/), and much of the chapter is about choosing a method whose stability fits the problem.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups. The first seven are about ordinary differential equations, the last three about partial ones.

1. **Initial value problems and one-step basics.** The [initial value problem](/ode-initial-value-problem/), the [Lipschitz condition](/lipschitz-condition/), the [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](/picard-lindelof-theorem/) and [Gronwall's inequality](/gronwall-inequality/); then [local truncation error](/local-truncation-error/), [global error](/global-error/), [order](/order-of-accuracy/), and the methods of Euler, Heun and Taylor.
2. **Linear stability and stiffness.** The [test equation](/dahlquist-test-equation/) $y' = \lambda y$, [stability regions](/stability-region/), [A-stability](/a-stability/), [L-stability](/l-stability/) and stiff problems.
3. **Runge-Kutta methods and step-size control.** The [Butcher tableau](/butcher-tableau/), [Runge-Kutta methods](/runge-kutta-methods/), [RK4](/classical-runge-kutta-4/), the [order conditions](/butcher-order-conditions/), [adaptive step size](/adaptive-step-size-control/), [embedded pairs](/embedded-runge-kutta-pairs/) such as [Dormand-Prince](/dormand-prince-method/), [dense output](/dense-output/) and [Bulirsch-Stoer](/bulirsch-stoer-method/) extrapolation.
4. **Linear multistep methods.** [Multistep methods](/linear-multistep-methods/), [zero-stability](/zero-stability/), the [Adams-Bashforth](/adams-bashforth-methods/) and [Adams-Moulton](/adams-moulton-methods/) methods, the [backward differentiation formulas](/backward-differentiation-formulas/) and the [equivalence theorem](/dahlquist-equivalence-theorem/) and [barrier](/dahlquist-barrier/) of Dahlquist.
5. **Boundary value problems.** [Boundary value problems](/bvp-boundary-value-problem/), [shooting](/shooting-method/), [collocation](/collocation-method/) and [Sturm-Liouville problems](/sturm-liouville-problem/).
6. **Implicit methods for stiff problems and DAEs.** [Implicit Runge-Kutta methods](/implicit-runge-kutta-methods/), [Gauss](/gauss-legendre-runge-kutta/) and [Radau IIA](/radau-iia-methods/) methods, [Rosenbrock methods](/rosenbrock-methods/) and [differential-algebraic equations](/differential-algebraic-equations/).
7. **Structure, noise, delay and parallel time.** [Hamiltonian systems](/hamiltonian-systems/) and [symplectic integrators](/symplectic-integrators/), [stochastic equations](/stochastic-differential-equation/) and the [Euler-Maruyama method](/euler-maruyama-method/), [delay equations](/delay-differential-equations/) and the [parareal algorithm](/parareal-algorithm/).
8. **Model PDEs and finite differences.** The [classification of PDEs](/pde-classification/), the [heat](/heat-equation/), [advection](/advection-equation/), [wave](/wave-equation/) and [Poisson](/poisson-equation/) equations, [finite differences](/finite-difference-method/), the [CFL condition](/courant-friedrichs-lewy-condition/), [Crank-Nicolson](/crank-nicolson-method/), [von Neumann analysis](/von-neumann-stability-analysis/) and the [Lax equivalence theorem](/lax-equivalence-theorem/).
9. **Semi-discretization, spectral and finite element methods.** The [method of lines](/method-of-lines/), [operator splitting](/operator-splitting/), [exponential integrators](/exponential-integrators/), [spectral methods](/spectral-methods/) and the [Galerkin](/galerkin-method/) and [finite element](/finite-element-method/) methods.
10. **Conservation laws, fronts and fluids.** [Upwinding](/upwind-scheme/), [characteristics](/method-of-characteristics/), the [Riemann problem](/riemann-problem/), [finite volumes](/finite-volume-method/), [Godunov's method](/godunov-method/), [TVD schemes](/total-variation-diminishing/), [WENO](/weno-scheme/), the [level set method](/level-set-method/) and the [Navier-Stokes equations](/navier-stokes-equations/).

## A first reading

Read these entries first, in this order: the [initial value problem](/ode-initial-value-problem/), [Euler's method](/euler-method/), [local truncation error](/local-truncation-error/), [global error](/global-error/), the [test equation](/dahlquist-test-equation/), [stability regions](/stability-region/), [stiff problems](/stiff-ode-problem/), [backward Euler](/backward-euler-method/), [Runge-Kutta methods](/runge-kutta-methods/), [RK4](/classical-runge-kutta-4/), [adaptive step size](/adaptive-step-size-control/), [multistep methods](/linear-multistep-methods/), the [heat equation](/heat-equation/), the [FTCS scheme](/ftcs-method/) and the [method of lines](/method-of-lines/). With them you can solve most initial value problems with a library code and know which kind of code to ask for, and you can see why an explicit scheme for a PDE has a step limit. The rest of the chapter refines these ideas for stiff, structured and hyperbolic problems.

## Before you start

You need calculus with Taylor's theorem and a little linear algebra. Implicit methods solve nonlinear equations by [Newton's method for systems](/newton-method-for-systems/) from Chapter 12.2, and PDE schemes solve large linear systems with the [Thomas algorithm](/thomas-algorithm/), [sparse matrices](/sparse-matrix/) and [multigrid](/multigrid-method/) from Chapter 12.3. Multistep methods and collocation are built on [polynomial interpolation](/polynomial-interpolation/) and [finite differences](/finite-differences/) from Chapter 12.5, and Gauss methods and extrapolation use [Gauss-Legendre quadrature](/gauss-legendre-quadrature/) and [Richardson extrapolation](/richardson-extrapolation/) from Chapter 12.6. Von Neumann analysis uses the [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/) of Volume 11. The fast marching method is a version of [Dijkstra's algorithm](/dijkstra/) from Volume 7 with a [priority queue](/priority-queue/) from Volume 3.
