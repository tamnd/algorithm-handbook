## History

Euler described his polygon method in 1768, in the first volume of *Institutiones calculi integralis*. Cauchy proved in his lectures of the 1820s that the polygons converge, which also proved existence, and Lipschitz replaced Cauchy's bounded derivative by the condition that now carries his name in 1868. Picard introduced successive approximations in 1890 and Lindelöf the Lipschitz version in 1894. J. C. Adams designed his multistep formulas around 1855 for the capillary equation; they were published in 1883 in a book by Bashforth and Adams on drops of fluid. Runge (1895), Heun (1900) and Kutta (1901) built the one-step methods that evaluate $f$ several times per step, and Kutta found the classical fourth-order method. Moulton used the implicit Adams formula as a corrector for ballistics in the First World War and published it in 1926.

Stiffness was named by Curtiss and Hirschfelder in 1952, in a paper on chemical kinetics that also introduced the backward differentiation formulas. Dahlquist's 1956 paper proved that a linear multistep method converges exactly when it is consistent and zero-stable, and bounded the order of zero-stable methods. In 1963 he defined A-stability and proved that no A-stable multistep method has order above two. The same year Butcher organized the order conditions of Runge-Kutta methods by rooted trees, and in 1964 he introduced implicit methods of order $2s$ built on Gauss points. Ehle (1969) proved that the Gauss and Radau IIA methods are A-stable and introduced L-stability. Gear's code DIFSUB (1971) set the design of variable-step, variable-order BDF solvers that LSODE, DASSL and CVODE followed.

Automatic step control came with embedded pairs. Fehlberg published pairs of several orders in NASA reports in 1968 and 1969, and the RKF45 code of Shampine and Watts, printed in the 1977 book of Forsythe, Malcolm and Moler, made the 4(5) pair standard. Dormand and Prince (1980) chose coefficients to make the fifth-order result accurate and propagated it; their pair became DOPRI5 and later MATLAB's `ode45`. Long-time integration of Hamiltonian systems led to symplectic methods: de Vogelaere described symplectic Euler in a 1956 report, Verlet used the leapfrog scheme for molecular dynamics in 1967, and Ruth (1983) and Feng Kang (1985) began their systematic study. For stochastic equations Maruyama proved mean-square convergence of the Euler scheme in 1955, and Milstein added the correction term in 1974.

Partial differential equations have their own line. Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy showed in 1928 that difference solutions converge only under a condition linking the mesh widths. Crank and Nicolson (1947) gave an implicit scheme for heat conduction, von Neumann's Fourier analysis of stability came into use in the same years, and Lax's equivalence theorem appeared with Richtmyer in 1956. Peaceman and Rachford introduced ADI for oil reservoir simulation in 1955. Courant proposed piecewise linear trial functions in 1943, engineers at Boeing and Berkeley built the finite element method in 1956, and Clough named it in 1960. For conservation laws, Lax (1954), Godunov (1959) and Lax and Wendroff (1960) set out conservative schemes; Godunov also proved that linear monotone schemes are at most first order. Van Leer's MUSCL (1979), Harten's TVD theory (1983) and the WENO schemes of Liu, Osher and Chan (1994) and Jiang and Shu (1996) are the nonlinear answer to that barrier.

## Further reading

- Ernst Hairer, Syvert P. Nørsett and Gerhard Wanner, *Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I: Nonstiff Problems*, second edition, Springer, 1993. Nonstiff methods, order theory and step control, with history.
- Ernst Hairer and Gerhard Wanner, *Solving Ordinary Differential Equations II: Stiff and Differential-Algebraic Problems*, second edition, Springer, 1996. Stiff methods and DAEs.
- Arieh Iserles, *A First Course in the Numerical Analysis of Differential Equations*, second edition, Cambridge University Press, 2009. A short course covering both ODEs and PDEs.
- Randall J. LeVeque, *Finite Difference Methods for Ordinary and Partial Differential Equations*, SIAM, 2007. Stability, stiffness and the model PDEs.
- Randall J. LeVeque, *Finite Volume Methods for Hyperbolic Problems*, Cambridge University Press, 2002. Riemann problems, Godunov's method, limiters and TVD theory.
- Ernst Hairer, Christian Lubich and Gerhard Wanner, *Geometric Numerical Integration*, second edition, Springer, 2006. Symplectic and symmetric methods and their long-time behaviour.
