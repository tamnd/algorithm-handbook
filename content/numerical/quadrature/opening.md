Most integrals that come up in practice have no closed form, and many integrands are known only through a program that returns $f(x)$ for a given $x$. Quadrature computes $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ from such values. Every method in the chapter ends with the same kind of formula, a weighted sum $\sum_i w_i f(x_i)$, and the methods differ in where they put the nodes $x_i$, how they choose the weights $w_i$, and how they decide that the answer is good enough. The choice matters a great deal. On $\int_0^1 e^x\,dx$, five values give the [trapezoidal rule](/trapezoidal-rule/) an error of about $9 \times 10^{-3}$ and [Simpson's rule](/simpsons-rule/) an error of $4 \times 10^{-5}$, while three well-placed values give [Gauss-Legendre quadrature](/gauss-legendre-quadrature/) an error of $8 \times 10^{-7}$.

Integrals appear wherever a quantity is accumulated: areas and lengths, expected values in statistics, prices of financial options, and the steps of solvers for [initial value problems](/ode-initial-value-problem/). The integrand can be smooth, or singular at an end, or oscillate thousands of times, or live in hundreds of dimensions, and each case has its own best method. No method that only samples $f$ can certify its answer, since $f$ may do anything between the samples; the chapter is also about how far an error estimate can be trusted.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Rules and their accuracy.** The [numerical integration](/numerical-integration/) problem, the [quadrature rule](/quadrature-rule/), the [degree of precision](/degree-of-precision/), [interpolatory quadrature](/interpolatory-quadrature/) and the [quadrature error](/quadrature-error/).
2. **Rules on equally spaced points.** The [midpoint rule](/midpoint-rule/), the trapezoidal and Simpson rules, the [Newton-Cotes formulas](/newton-cotes-formulas/), [composite rules](/composite-quadrature/) and the [Peano kernel theorem](/peano-kernel-theorem/).
3. **Extrapolation and adaptivity.** The [Euler-Maclaurin formula](/euler-maclaurin-formula/), [Richardson extrapolation](/richardson-extrapolation/), [Romberg integration](/romberg-integration/) and [adaptive quadrature](/adaptive-quadrature/).
4. **Gaussian quadrature.** The [Gauss quadrature theorem](/gauss-quadrature-theorem/), [Gaussian quadrature](/gaussian-quadrature/) for any weight, the Gauss-Legendre rule, the problem of [computing nodes and weights](/gauss-nodes-and-weights/) and the [Golub-Welsch algorithm](/golub-welsch-algorithm/).
5. **Gauss rules for weights, endpoints and error estimates.** The [Gauss-Jacobi](/gauss-jacobi-quadrature/), [Gauss-Laguerre](/gauss-laguerre-quadrature/) and [Gauss-Hermite](/gauss-hermite-quadrature/) rules, the [Radau](/gauss-radau-quadrature/) and [Lobatto](/gauss-lobatto-quadrature/) variants, and [Gauss-Kronrod quadrature](/gauss-kronrod-quadrature/).
6. **Rules on Chebyshev points.** [Clenshaw-Curtis quadrature](/clenshaw-curtis-quadrature/) and [Fejér's rules](/fejer-quadrature/).
7. **Singular ends and infinite intervals.** [Improper integration](/improper-integration/), [tanh-sinh quadrature](/tanh-sinh-quadrature/) and [sinc quadrature](/sinc-quadrature/).
8. **Oscillatory integrals.** [Oscillatory integration](/oscillatory-integration/), [Filon quadrature](/filon-quadrature/) and [Levin's method](/levin-method/).
9. **Integration in several dimensions.** [Multidimensional integration](/multidimensional-integration/), the [cubature rule](/cubature-rule/), [product quadrature](/product-quadrature/) and [sparse grids](/sparse-grid-quadrature/).
10. **Random and quasi-random points.** [Monte Carlo integration](/monte-carlo-integration/), [discrepancy](/discrepancy/), the [Koksma-Hlawka inequality](/koksma-hlawka-inequality/) and [quasi-Monte Carlo integration](/quasi-monte-carlo-integration/).

## A first reading

Read these entries first, in this order: [numerical integration](/numerical-integration/), the [quadrature rule](/quadrature-rule/), the [degree of precision](/degree-of-precision/), the [trapezoidal rule](/trapezoidal-rule/), [Simpson's rule](/simpsons-rule/), [composite rules](/composite-quadrature/), the [quadrature error](/quadrature-error/), [Romberg integration](/romberg-integration/), [adaptive quadrature](/adaptive-quadrature/), [Gaussian quadrature](/gaussian-quadrature/), [Gauss-Legendre quadrature](/gauss-legendre-quadrature/), [Gauss-Kronrod quadrature](/gauss-kronrod-quadrature/), [Clenshaw-Curtis quadrature](/clenshaw-curtis-quadrature/), [improper integration](/improper-integration/) and [Monte Carlo integration](/monte-carlo-integration/). With them you can integrate a smooth function on an interval to a requested tolerance, understand what a library integrator does, and know when to switch to sampling. The Peano kernel theorem, the Gauss rules for special weights, the oscillatory methods and the theory of discrepancy can wait until a problem calls for them.

## Before you start

You need [floating-point arithmetic](/floating-point-arithmetic/) and [Kahan summation](/kahan-summation/) from Chapter 12.1, and [asymptotic notation](/asymptotic-notation/) from Volume 1; the Monte Carlo entries use [variance](/variance/) and the [law of large numbers](/law-of-large-numbers/) from the same volume. Most rules integrate an interpolating polynomial, so the [polynomial interpolation](/polynomial-interpolation/), [Lagrange interpolation](/lagrange-interpolation/), [Chebyshev polynomials](/chebyshev-polynomials/) and [orthogonal polynomials](/orthogonal-polynomials/) entries of Chapter 12.5 come first, together with the [Runge phenomenon](/runge-phenomenon/), which explains why equally spaced rules of high degree fail. The Golub-Welsch algorithm solves a [tridiagonal eigenproblem](/tridiagonal-eigenproblem/) from Chapter 12.4, the Chebyshev rules use the [fast Fourier transform](/fast-fourier-transform/) of Volume 11, adaptive quadrature keeps its panels in a [priority queue](/priority-queue/) from Volume 3, and quasi-Monte Carlo uses the [Halton](/halton-sequence/) and [Sobol](/sobol-sequence/) sequences of Chapter 12.8. Calculus with Taylor's theorem and the mean value theorem for integrals is assumed throughout.
