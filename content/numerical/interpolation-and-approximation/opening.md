Computers evaluate functions through simpler ones. A library computes $\sin x$ from a polynomial chosen to be within one rounding error on a short interval; a plotting program draws a smooth curve through a few measured points; a solver for differential equations needs values of its solution between the steps it took. All three are the same task: replace a function, known through a formula or through samples, by a member of a family that is cheap to evaluate. If the replacement must agree with the samples exactly, the task is [interpolation](/interpolation-problem/). If it must only be close in some norm, it is [approximation](/function-approximation/).

The obvious family is polynomials, and the obvious choice of points is an even spacing. That combination fails. Interpolating Runge's function $1/(1 + 25x^2)$ at equally spaced points on $[-1, 1]$ gives a maximum error of $0.43$ with degree 5 and $1.05 \times 10^5$ with degree 40, while at [Chebyshev nodes](/chebyshev-nodes/) the error falls from $0.56$ to $2.9 \times 10^{-4}$ over the same degrees. Much of the chapter explains this contrast: where to put the nodes, how to write the polynomial so that it can be evaluated stably, and when to give up high degree for [splines](/cubic-spline-interpolation/) or rational functions.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Interpolation and approximation problems.** The two problems, [approximation theory](/approximation-theory/), [least-squares approximation](/least-squares-approximation/) and [curve fitting](/curve-fitting/).
2. **Polynomial interpolation.** [Polynomial interpolation](/polynomial-interpolation/), the [Vandermonde matrix](/vandermonde-matrix/), [uniqueness](/interpolating-polynomial-uniqueness/), the [Lagrange](/lagrange-interpolation/) and [Newton](/newton-interpolation/) forms, [divided](/divided-differences/) and [finite differences](/finite-differences/), [Neville's algorithm](/neville-algorithm/), the [barycentric formula](/barycentric-interpolation/) and [Hermite interpolation](/hermite-interpolation/).
3. **Error and Chebyshev nodes.** The [error theorem](/polynomial-interpolation-error-theorem/), [Chebyshev polynomials](/chebyshev-polynomials/) and Chebyshev nodes.

5. **Orthogonal polynomials and series.** [Orthogonal polynomials](/orthogonal-polynomials/), [Legendre polynomials](/legendre-polynomials/), [Chebyshev series](/chebyshev-series/), [Chebyshev interpolation](/chebyshev-interpolation/), [economization](/chebyshev-economization/) and [trigonometric interpolation](/trigonometric-interpolation/).
6. **Piecewise interpolation and splines.** [Nearest-neighbour](/nearest-neighbor-interpolation/) and [piecewise linear](/piecewise-linear-interpolation/) interpolation, [cubic Hermite splines](/cubic-hermite-spline/), [monotone interpolation](/fritsch-carlson-monotone-interpolation/), cubic splines, [smoothing splines](/smoothing-spline/) and [spline evaluation](/spline-evaluation/).
7. **Grids and many variables.** [Bilinear](/bilinear-interpolation/) and [bicubic](/bicubic-interpolation/) interpolation, [multivariate interpolation](/multivariate-interpolation/), [tensor products](/tensor-product-interpolation/) and [sparse grids](/smolyak-sparse-grids/).

## A first reading

Read these entries first, in this order: the [interpolation problem](/interpolation-problem/), [polynomial interpolation](/polynomial-interpolation/), [Lagrange interpolation](/lagrange-interpolation/), [divided differences](/divided-differences/), the [error theorem](/polynomial-interpolation-error-theorem/), the [Runge phenomenon](/runge-phenomenon/), [Chebyshev polynomials](/chebyshev-polynomials/), [Chebyshev nodes](/chebyshev-nodes/), [barycentric interpolation](/barycentric-interpolation/), [piecewise linear interpolation](/piecewise-linear-interpolation/), [cubic splines](/cubic-spline-interpolation/), [least-squares approximation](/least-squares-approximation/) and [minimax approximation](/minimax-approximation/). The several-variable groups and the rational methods build on these and can be read as needed.
