The earlier chapters of this volume search for a key that is either present or absent. Similarity search asks a softer question: which stored objects are close to the query. A spelling checker wants the dictionary words a few edits away from a typed word. A search engine wants the pages that are near copies of a page it has just crawled. A recommender wants the items whose vectors point in nearly the same direction as a user's vector, and a system built on text embeddings wants the hundred passages nearest to a question among a billion. There is no order to search in and no exact match to find, only a distance.

The chapter starts from that distance. It defines [metric spaces](/metric-space/) and the similarities used in practice, states the [nearest neighbor](/nearest-neighbor-search/) and range problems, and then follows the two ways of doing better than a scan. Exact indexes prune with the [triangle inequality](/triangle-inequality/) and work well when the data has few real dimensions. Approximate methods give up a small factor in distance and gain sublinear query time in any dimension, first with [locality-sensitive hashing](/locality-sensitive-hashing/) and today mostly with graphs such as [HNSW](/hnsw/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Distances and similarities.** [Cosine similarity](/cosine-similarity/) for vectors, [Jaccard similarity](/jaccard-similarity/) for sets, [shingling](/shingling/) to turn documents into sets, and [intrinsic dimensionality](/intrinsic-dimensionality/), which predicts how hard the search will be.
2. **The search problems.** The [similarity index](/similarity-index/) as an abstract type, [k-nearest neighbor search](/k-nearest-neighbor-search/), the [range query](/similarity-range-query/), [brute-force search](/brute-force-nearest-neighbor-search/) as the baseline, and three relatives: [reverse nearest neighbors](/reverse-nearest-neighbor-search/), [fixed-radius near neighbors](/fixed-radius-near-neighbors/) and [maximum inner product search](/maximum-inner-product-search/).
3. **Exact indexes for metric spaces.** [Metric space indexing](/metric-space-indexing/) and [pivots](/pivot-based-indexing/), then the [BK-tree](/bk-tree/), the [vantage-point tree](/vantage-point-tree/) and its variants, the [ball tree](/ball-tree/), the [M-tree](/m-tree/), the [cover tree](/cover-tree/) and the [random projection tree](/rp-tree/).
4. **Approximate search and locality-sensitive hashing.** The [c-approximate nearest neighbor](/c-approximate-nearest-neighbor/), the [hash families](/locality-sensitive-hash-family/) and their [amplification](/lsh-amplification/), and the schemes for Hamming distance, angles and Euclidean distance, with [multi-probe LSH](/multi-probe-lsh/), the [LSH forest](/lsh-forest/) and [asymmetric LSH](/asymmetric-lsh/).
5. **Near duplicates and similarity joins.** [MinHash](/minhash/) and [SimHash](/simhash/) sketches, then [prefix filtering](/prefix-filtering/) and the exact joins [All-Pairs](/allpairs/) and [PPJoin](/ppjoin/).
6. **Graph indexes.** The [k-nearest neighbor graph](/k-nearest-neighbor-graph/) and [NN-Descent](/nn-descent/), the [navigable small world](/navigable-small-world/) graph, HNSW, [DiskANN](/diskann/) and [hubness](/hubness/).
7. **Partitions and compressed vectors.** The [inverted file index](/inverted-file-index/) and [product quantization](/product-quantization/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: [metric space](/metric-space/), [triangle inequality](/triangle-inequality/), [cosine similarity](/cosine-similarity/), [Jaccard similarity](/jaccard-similarity/), [nearest neighbor search](/nearest-neighbor-search/), [brute-force nearest neighbor search](/brute-force-nearest-neighbor-search/), the [vantage-point tree](/vantage-point-tree/), the [c-approximate nearest neighbor](/c-approximate-nearest-neighbor/), [locality-sensitive hashing](/locality-sensitive-hashing/), [random hyperplane LSH](/random-hyperplane-lsh/), [MinHash](/minhash/) and [HNSW](/hnsw/). Work the example of each by hand; the numbers are small on purpose. Keep two questions in mind throughout: how many distances a query computes, and whether the answer is exact. The research entries of the last groups, the proofs of the LSH bounds and the cover tree analysis can wait for a second pass.
