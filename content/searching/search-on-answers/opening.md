Binary search does not need an array. It needs a question with a yes or no answer that flips once: false for every value below some threshold and true for every value from the threshold on. Many optimization problems hide such a question. "What is the smallest time in which the jobs can finish?" is hard to answer head on, but "can the jobs finish within $T$?" is often a short greedy pass, and its answer changes only once as $T$ grows. Binary search over $T$ then finds the optimum with a logarithmic number of passes. This is searching on the answer, and it turns many hard-looking problems into a loop around an easy test.

The chapter starts with this idea and the problems it solves. It then turns to a second kind of search over values: finding the top of a unimodal function, where the test is a comparison of two function values. It goes on to ratios, where a search on the answer turns a quotient into a sum, and ends with the methods that answer many searches at once or search over an answer that is not known to be an integer.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in five groups, read in order.

1. **Searching over answers.** A [monotone predicate](/monotone-predicate/) is the condition that makes binary search valid. The [reduction from optimization to decision](/optimization-to-decision-reduction/) asks "is $t$ achievable?" in place of "what is the best $t$?". [Binary search on the answer](/binary-search-on-answer/) puts the two together for integer answers, and [binary search on real numbers](/binary-search-on-real-numbers/) does the same on a continuous range.
2. **Problems solved by a search on the answer.** [Maximin placement](/maximin-placement/) and [linear partition](/minimax-partition/) each have a greedy test, and the [k-th smallest pair distance](/kth-smallest-pair-distance/) has a counting test with two pointers.
3. **Unimodal functions.** A [unimodal function](/unimodal-function/) rises to one peak and then falls. [Unimodal optimization](/unimodal-optimization/) finds the peak with function evaluations only. [Ternary search](/ternary-search/) is the simple method, and [Fibonacci search](/fibonacci-search-unimodal/) and [golden-section search](/golden-section-search/) reuse one evaluation in each step, which makes them close to optimal.
4. **Optimizing a ratio.** [Fractional programming](/fractional-programming/) minimizes a quotient $N(x)/D(x)$. The [minimum ratio problem](/minimum-ratio-problem/) is its combinatorial form, and [Dinkelbach's algorithm](/dinkelbach-algorithm/) replaces the binary search by a Newton-like iteration that often needs only a few rounds.
5. **Advanced searches on the answer.** [Parallel binary search](/parallel-binary-search/) runs many searches together over one shared sweep of updates. [Parametric search](/parametric-search/) finds an optimum that is not known to lie on any grid by simulating a parallel algorithm. The [post office problem](/post-office-problem/) uses a search on a penalty, not on the answer itself.
