22. The unit $a$ is bad when $r$ is odd, which means $v_1 = v_2 = 0$, or when $r$ is even and $a^{r/2} \equiv -1$ modulo both primes. Modulo $p_i$, the power $a^{r/2}$ is a square root of 1, and it is $-1$ exactly when the order modulo $p_i$ does not divide $r/2$, that is, when $v_i$ equals the exponent $\max(v_1, v_2)$ of 2 in $r$. Both hold exactly when $v_1 = v_2 \ge 1$. If $p - 1 = 2^s m$ with $m$ odd, then $v = 0$ with probability $2^{-s}$ and $v = j$ with probability $2^{j-1-s}$ for $1 \le j \le s$. For 5 and 13, $s = 2$ and $\Pr[v_1 = v_2] = \frac1{16} + \frac1{16} + \frac14 = \frac38$, so 30 of the 48 units are good, a fraction of $5/8$.

25. Write $\mathbb{E}[T] = \sum_{t \ge 0}\Pr[T > t]$. The terms for $t = 0$ and $t = 1$ are at most 1. For $t \ge 2$, use the union bound: $1 - \prod_{p \mid r}(1 - p^{-t}) \le \sum_p p^{-t}$, and $\sum_{t \ge 2} p^{-t} = \frac{1}{p(p-1)}$. The prime sum is $0.77316$, so the bound is $2.7732$. For the supremum, $\prod_{p \mid r}(1 - p^{-t}) \ge \prod_p (1 - p^{-t}) = 1/\zeta(t)$ for $t \ge 2$, with equality in the limit along the products of the first primes. So the supremum is $2 + \sum_{t \ge 2}(1 - 1/\zeta(t)) \approx 2.7052$.
