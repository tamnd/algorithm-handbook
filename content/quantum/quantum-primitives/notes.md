## History

Born gave the probability rule in 1926, von Neumann the density matrix in 1927 and the Hilbert-space form of the theory in his book of 1932. The idea of computing with it came from two directions. Landauer argued in 1961 that erasing a bit costs energy, Bennett showed in 1973 that any computation can be done reversibly, and Toffoli gave his universal reversible gate in 1980. Feynman asked in 1982 whether a quantum system could be simulated efficiently by a classical one, and suggested a computer made of quantum parts. Deutsch defined the quantum Turing machine in 1985 and gave the first problem that it solves with fewer queries; in 1989 he described quantum circuits, and Yao showed in 1993 that the two models have the same power. Schumacher coined the word qubit in 1995.

The query separations followed quickly. Deutsch and Jozsa gave their promise problem in 1992. Bernstein and Vazirani in 1993 defined the class BQP and built a superpolynomial oracle separation from recursive Fourier sampling. Simon's exponential separation of 1994 led Shor to factoring and discrete logarithms in the same year, and Coppersmith wrote out the circuit for the Fourier transform modulo $2^n$. Kitaev gave phase estimation in 1995, Griffiths and Niu showed in 1996 that the transform before a measurement needs only one-qubit gates and classical control, and Cleve, Ekert, Macchiavello and Mosca in 1998 recast the known algorithms as phase estimation, with the one-query form of Deutsch-Jozsa.

Entanglement was a question in the foundations of physics before it was a resource. Bell proved his theorem in 1964, Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt gave the testable form in 1969, and Tsirelson found the quantum bound $2\sqrt2$ in 1980. Freedman and Clauser in 1972 and Aspect and coworkers in 1982 measured violations, the loophole-free tests came in 2015, and Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger shared the Nobel Prize in 2022. The no-cloning theorem was proved by Wootters and Zurek and by Dieks in 1982. Bennett and Wiesner gave superdense coding in 1992, and Bennett, Brassard, Crépeau, Jozsa, Peres and Wootters gave teleportation in 1993. The theory of channels goes back to Stinespring (1955), Choi (1975) and Kraus (1983).

Error correction looked impossible, since measuring disturbs the state and errors are continuous. Shor's nine-qubit code of 1995 showed otherwise, and Steane's seven-qubit code and the CSS construction of Calderbank, Shor and Steane followed in 1996. Gottesman's thesis of 1997 gave the stabilizer formalism, and his paper of 1998 the Gottesman-Knill theorem. Shor showed in 1996 how to compute on encoded data, and Aharonov and Ben-Or, Kitaev, and Knill, Laflamme and Zurek proved the threshold theorem in the late 1990s; Aliferis, Gottesman and Preskill gave a rigorous threshold of $2.73 \times 10^{-5}$ for a concatenated code in 2006. The Solovay-Kitaev theorem, from an unpublished note of Solovay in 1995 and from Kitaev's work of 1997, showed that a finite gate set loses only a polylogarithmic factor; Dawson and Nielsen wrote out the algorithm in 2006.

## Further reading

- Michael A. Nielsen and Isaac L. Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*, 10th anniversary edition, Cambridge University Press, 2010. The standard text, from gates and phase estimation to channels, distances and fault tolerance.
- Ronald de Wolf, *Quantum Computing: Lecture Notes*, arXiv:1907.09415, updated regularly. A short course from the computer-science side, with the query model, the oracle algorithms and exercises.
- John Watrous, *The Theory of Quantum Information*, Cambridge University Press, 2018. Density matrices, channels, Kraus and Choi representations, fidelity and entropy, with complete proofs.
- A. Yu. Kitaev, A. H. Shen and M. N. Vyalyi, *Classical and Quantum Computation*, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 47, American Mathematical Society, 2002. The circuit model, universal gate sets and the Solovay-Kitaev theorem, phase estimation and the abelian stabilizer problem.
- Richard Cleve, Artur Ekert, Chiara Macchiavello and Michele Mosca, "Quantum algorithms revisited", *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 454 (1998), 339–354. Deutsch-Jozsa, phase estimation and order finding as one pattern of kickback and interference.
- Nicolas Brunner, Daniel Cavalcanti, Stefano Pironio, Valerio Scarani and Stephanie Wehner, "Bell nonlocality", *Reviews of Modern Physics* 86 (2014), 419–478. Bell inequalities, CHSH and the Tsirelson bound, nonlocal games and the experiments.
