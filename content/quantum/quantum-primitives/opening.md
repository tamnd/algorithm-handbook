A classical computer with $n$ bits is in one of $2^n$ states. A quantum computer with $n$ [qubits](/qubit/) is in a [state](/quantum-state/) given by $2^n$ complex amplitudes, one for each bit string, whose squared magnitudes add to 1. Gates change the amplitudes by unitary matrices, and a [measurement](/quantum-measurement/) at the end returns one bit string $x$ with probability $|\alpha_x|^2$, the [Born rule](/born-rule/). That rule is the catch. The amplitudes are never read out; what a program gets is one sample. An algorithm has to arrange its gates so that the amplitudes of wrong answers cancel and the amplitude of the right answer grows, which is [interference](/quantum-interference/).

This chapter gives the parts that every later chapter of the volume is built from: the states, the gates, the circuits, the oracle model, the first algorithms that beat classical ones, the [quantum Fourier transform](/quantum-fourier-transform/) and [phase estimation](/quantum-phase-estimation/), and the tools for noise: [density matrices](/density-matrix/), [channels](/quantum-channel/) and [error correction](/quantum-error-correction/). One convention runs through all of it. Qubit 0 is the least significant bit and the rightmost symbol: in $|b_{n-1} \cdots b_1 b_0\rangle$ the string stands for $x = \sum_j b_j 2^j$, and in a Pauli string such as $XZ$ the $Z$ acts on qubit 0. A circuit that applies $A$ and then $B$ has the matrix $BA$.

The early algorithms count queries, not gates. A [quantum oracle](/quantum-oracle/) for $f$ is a unitary that maps $|x\rangle|y\rangle$ to $|x\rangle|y \oplus f(x)\rangle$, and the cost is the number of times a circuit calls it. In that model the [Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm](/deutsch-jozsa-algorithm/) uses one query and never errs, the [Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm](/bernstein-vazirani-algorithm/) uses one query where every bounded-error classical algorithm needs $n$, and [Simon's algorithm](/simon-algorithm/) uses $O(n)$ queries where classical ones need $\Omega(2^{n/2})$. These are proved separations, but only relative to an oracle. They do not show that quantum computers solve any explicit problem faster than every classical algorithm, and the classes BQP and BPP are not known to differ. Each cost in the chapter names its model: queries or gates, exact or bounded error, noiseless or under a stated noise model.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Qubits, states and measurement.** The qubit, the [quantum state](/quantum-state/), the Born rule, measurement, the [tensor product](/tensor-product/), [superposition](/quantum-superposition/), the [Bloch sphere](/bloch-sphere/) and [entanglement](/entanglement/).
2. **Gates and circuits.** [Unitary operators](/unitary-operator/), [quantum gates](/quantum-gate/), the [Pauli](/pauli-gates/), [Hadamard](/hadamard-gate/), [phase and T](/phase-and-t-gates/), [CNOT](/cnot-gate/) and [Toffoli](/toffoli-gate/) gates, interference, the [circuit model](/quantum-circuit-model/), [reversible computation](/reversible-computation/), [ancilla qubits](/ancilla-qubits/), [uncomputation](/uncomputation/) and the [quantum Turing machine](/quantum-turing-machine/).

4. **Oracles and the first separations.** The quantum oracle, [quantum parallelism](/quantum-parallelism/), [phase kickback](/phase-kickback/), the [Deutsch-Jozsa](/deutsch-jozsa-problem/), [Bernstein-Vazirani](/bernstein-vazirani-problem/) and [Simon](/simon-problem/) problems with their algorithms, and [quantum RAM](/quantum-random-access-memory/).

## A first reading

Take these fourteen entries in order: qubit, quantum state, Born rule, tensor product, Hadamard gate, CNOT gate, entanglement, quantum interference, quantum oracle, phase kickback, Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm, Simon's algorithm, quantum Fourier transform and quantum phase estimation. They give the state space, the two gates that make entanglement, the reason a quantum algorithm can win, the query model, the simplest exact separation, the first exponential one, and the two subroutines that the rest of the volume calls most.

Leave groups 6 to 8 for a second pass. Density matrices are needed as soon as noise or half of an entangled pair enters, and error correction is needed to believe that long circuits can run at all, but none of the algorithms of groups 4 and 5 uses them.

## Before you start

The chapter is linear algebra over $\mathbb{C}$. States are unit vectors with the inner product of the [dot product](/dot-product/) (Volume 13), several qubits combine by the [Kronecker product](/kronecker-product/) (Volume 11), and gates are judged by their [eigenvalues](/eigenvalue/) (Volume 12); density matrices are [positive semidefinite](/positive-definite-matrix/) with trace 1. Measurement outcomes form a [probability distribution](/probability-distribution/) (Volume 1), and trace distance generalizes [total variation distance](/total-variation-distance/) (Volume 17). The circuit model extends the [Boolean circuit](/boolean-circuit/) and the quantum Turing machine extends the [probabilistic Turing machine](/probabilistic-turing-machine/) (both Volume 1). The Fourier entries lean on the [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/) and the [Walsh-Hadamard transform](/walsh-hadamard-transform/) (Volume 11); Simon's algorithm ends by solving a [linear system modulo 2](/linear-system-modulo-2/) (Volume 11), and its classical lower bound is the [birthday paradox](/birthday-paradox/) (Volume 1). Entropy starts from [Shannon entropy](/shannon-entropy/), and the Steane code from the [Hamming code](/hamming-code/) (both Volume 24).
