1. The outcomes $00, 01, 10, 11$ have probabilities $\frac16, \frac16, 0, \frac23$. Qubit 0 reads 1 on the outcomes $01$ and $11$, with probability $\frac56$, and leaves $\frac{1}{\sqrt5}(i|0\rangle - 2|1\rangle) \otimes |1\rangle$. Write the amplitudes as a $2 \times 2$ matrix with rows indexed by qubit 1 and columns by qubit 0; a two-qubit pure state is a product exactly when its determinant is 0. Here the determinant is $-\frac13$, so the state is entangled.

3. $HSH|0\rangle = \frac12\left((1 + i)|0\rangle + (1 - i)|1\rangle\right) = e^{i\pi/4}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle - i|1\rangle)$. Each outcome has probability $\frac12$, and the Bloch vector is $(0, -1, 0)$.

5. After the query the input register holds $\frac12\sum_x (-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle$, and after the Hadamards the amplitude of $y$ is $\frac14 \sum_x (-1)^{f(x) \oplus y \cdot x}$. For $f = x_0 \oplus x_1$ the run returns $11$, and for $f = x_1$ it returns $10$. For the function that is 1 only at $11$ every amplitude is $\pm\frac12$, so each of the four outcomes has probability $\frac14$; the formula $(1 - 2w)^2$ with $w = \frac14$ gives the same value for $00$.

7. The amplitude of $y$ is $\frac14 e^{2\pi i y/8}\left(1 + (-1)^y\right)$, so the outcomes $0, 2, 4, 6$ each have probability $\frac14$ and the odd outcomes never occur. The 5-qubit circuit has $5$ Hadamards, $10$ controlled phases and $2$ swaps, 17 gates in all.

9. $D(|0\rangle, |+\rangle) = \sqrt{1 - |\langle 0|+\rangle|^2} = 1/\sqrt2$, so the best guess is right with probability $(1 + 1/\sqrt2)/2 \approx 0.854$. The best unambiguous measurement succeeds with probability $1 - |\langle 0|+\rangle| = 1 - 1/\sqrt2 \approx 0.293$.

11. Use $x_1 \vee x_2 \vee x_3 = \neg(\bar x_1 \wedge \bar x_2 \wedge \bar x_3)$. Apply $X$ to the three inputs, a Toffoli from $\bar x_1, \bar x_2$ into the ancilla, a Toffoli from the ancilla and $\bar x_3$ into $y$, and $X$ on $y$. Then repeat the first Toffoli to clear the ancilla and undo the three $X$ gates: 3 Toffoli gates and 7 $X$ gates.

12. $\rho_0 = \frac13\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with eigenvalues $(3 \pm \sqrt5)/6 \approx 0.873$ and $0.127$, and the entropy is about $0.550$ bits. The amplitude matrix $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is real and symmetric. Its eigenvectors give the Schmidt basis on both qubits, and its eigenvalues $(1 \pm \sqrt5)/(2\sqrt3)$ give the Schmidt coefficients up to sign.

13. The outcomes are the $y$ with $y \cdot s = 0$: $000$, $001$, $110$ and $111$, each with probability $\frac14$. Two runs determine $s$ when both are nonzero and different, with probability $\frac34 \cdot \frac24 = \frac38$. The wait for a nonzero $y$ has mean $\frac43$, and the wait for one outside the span of the first has mean 2, so the expected number of runs is $\frac{10}{3} = 2 + 1 + \frac13$.

14. $P(m) = \sin^2(\pi T\delta)/(T^2 \sin^2(\pi\delta))$ with $T = 4$ and $\delta = \varphi - m/4$. This gives $P(0) = \frac{1}{16}$, $P(1) = \frac{3(2 + \sqrt3)}{16} \approx 0.700$, $P(2) = \frac{3}{16}$ and $P(3) = \frac{3(2 - \sqrt3)}{16} \approx 0.050$. The outcomes 1 and 2 lie within $\frac14$ of $\frac13$ and have total probability about $0.887 \ge 8/\pi^2 \approx 0.811$. The rule gives $t = 5 + \lceil \log_2 52 \rceil = 11$.

15. Against $|0\rangle$, the state $|+\rangle$ makes a run read 0 with probability $(1 + \frac12)/2 = \frac34$, and $|0\rangle$ itself always reads 0. The test errs only when the state is $|+\rangle$ and all $k$ runs read 0, with probability $(3/4)^k$. Since $(3/4)^{16} \approx 0.0100$ and $(3/4)^{17} \approx 0.0075$, it takes 17 runs.

16. After $H$: $IIX$, $IZI$, $ZII$. After the first CNOT: $IXX$, $IZZ$, $ZII$. After the second: $XXX$, $IZZ$, $ZZI$, the GHZ state. The measured operator $IIX$ anticommutes with $IZZ$ and commutes with the other two, so each outcome $\pm 1$ has probability $\frac12$. Replace $IZZ$ by $\pm IIX$: the generators $XXX$, $ZZI$, $\pm IIX$ describe $|\Phi^+\rangle$ on qubits 2 and 1 and $|\pm\rangle$ on qubit 0.

17. $\sum_k K_k^\dagger K_k = (1 - \frac{3p}{4} + 3 \cdot \frac{p}{4}) I = I$. Since $\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho = 1$, $\mathcal D_q(\mathcal D_p(\rho)) = (1 - q)(1 - p)\rho + \left(1 - (1 - p)(1 - q)\right) I/2$. The output on $|0\rangle$ is $\mathrm{diag}(1 - p/2, p/2)$; its fidelity with $|0\rangle$ is $\sqrt{1 - p/2}$ and its trace distance from $|0\rangle\langle 0|$ is $p/2$.

21. The qubits carry $4, 3, 2, 1$ controlled phases with $k$ from 2 up, and $2 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 7$ of the 10 have $k \le 3$. The dropped gates are two $\mathrm{CR}_4$ and one $\mathrm{CR}_5$, so the error is at most $4\sin(\pi/16) + 2\sin(\pi/32) \approx 0.976$; the exact operator-norm error is about $0.943$. In general each qubit drops at most $\sum_{k > m} 2\sin(\pi/2^k) < 2\pi/2^m$, so the error is below $2\pi n/2^m$, and $m = \lceil \log_2(2\pi n/\varepsilon) \rceil$ suffices.

22. Shared randomness is a mixture of deterministic strategies, so it is enough to bound those. With answers $a_r$, $b_s$, $c_t$ the four conditions are $a_0 \oplus b_0 \oplus c_0 = 0$ and $a_0 \oplus b_1 \oplus c_1 = a_1 \oplus b_0 \oplus c_1 = a_1 \oplus b_1 \oplus c_0 = 1$. Each variable appears twice on the left, so the four left sides XOR to 0, while the right sides XOR to 1; at most three hold. For the quantum strategy, map the outcome $\pm 1$ to the bit $a$ with $(-1)^a$, and check that the GHZ state is a $+1$ eigenvector of $XXX$ and a $-1$ eigenvector of $XYY$, $YXY$ and $YYX$.

23. Write the inverse QFT as a circuit and look at its first qubit: it holds $(|0\rangle + e^{2\pi i\, 2^{t-1}\varphi}|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$, gets only a Hadamard, and gives the least significant bit of $m$. Every later controlled phase is controlled by a qubit that is measured right after, so it can be replaced by a phase controlled by the classical bit (Griffiths and Niu). The phase on the next qubit is then exactly $\mathrm{diag}(1, e^{-2\pi i\omega_r})$, and the joint distribution of the bits does not change. For $\varphi = 5/8$ the bits are $1, 0, 1$ with certainty; for $\varphi = 0.3$ and $t = 4$ the outcome $m = 5$ has probability about $0.876$, as in the circuit.

24. A Bell measurement is a CNOT, a Hadamard on the control and a measurement in the computational basis; the singlet gives $11$. The swap of the two qubits of a pair has eigenvalue $-1$ on $|\Psi^-\rangle$ and $+1$ on the other three Bell states. The swap of the registers is the product of the pairwise swaps, so $(-1)^{\#\text{singlets}}$ is a measurement of the register swap. Its expectation on $|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle$ is $|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^2$, and $P(\text{odd}) = (1 - |\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^2)/2$.

25. The identity part of $\rho_p$ contributes nothing, so $T = p\,\mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 1)$. With unit vectors, $S = \vec a_0^{\mathsf T} T(\vec b_0 + \vec b_1) + \vec a_1^{\mathsf T} T(\vec b_0 - \vec b_1)$, and $\vec b_0 \pm \vec b_1$ are orthogonal with lengths $2\cos\theta$ and $2\sin\theta$. The maximum is $2\sqrt{t_1^2 + t_2^2}$ over the two largest singular values of $T$, here $2\sqrt2\,p$. Entanglement for $p > 1/3$ follows from the partial transpose, whose smallest eigenvalue is $(1 - 3p)/4$.

26. The two states are pure with overlap $c^k$, $c = |\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|$, so their trace distance is $\sqrt{1 - c^{2k}}$ and the Helstrom bound $(1 + D)/2$ gives the formula. For $|0\rangle$ and $|+\rangle$, $c^2 = \frac12$, and the error $(1 - \sqrt{1 - 2^{-k}})/2$ is $0.0159$ for $k = 4$ and $0.0079$ for $k = 5$: five copies. Each one-copy measurement errs with probability $0.146$, and a majority vote errs with probability $0.0111$ for 7 copies and $0.0051$ for 9: nine copies. A scheme that measures one copy at a time and chooses each measurement from the earlier outcomes can reach the collective bound for two pure states.
