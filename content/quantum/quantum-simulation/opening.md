To predict how the spins of a magnet move after a field is switched on, or how a metal cluster in an enzyme helps to fix nitrogen, a program has to follow the state of a quantum system. The state of $n$ two-level particles has $2^n$ complex amplitudes: for $n = 50$ that is about $10^{15}$ numbers, some 18 petabytes at 16 bytes each, and every step of the evolution touches all of them. Feynman proposed in 1982 to build the simulator from quantum parts instead. A quantum computer keeps the state in $n$ qubits and applies the evolution $e^{-iHt}$ as a circuit, so the hard part becomes how many gates that circuit needs for a given time and error.

That question is the [Hamiltonian simulation](/hamiltonian-simulation/) problem, and most of the chapter answers it. The Hamiltonian comes in a stated access model: a list of local terms, a sum of Pauli strings with coefficient sum $\lambda$, a sparse matrix behind oracles, or a block encoding with normalization $\alpha$. Product formulas apply the terms one after another and have error bounds such as $t^2\|[A, B]\|/(2r)$ for $r$ steps of the first order formula, in the spectral norm. Sums of unitaries bring the cost in the error down to a logarithmic factor, and polynomials of $H$ reach $O(\alpha t + \log(1/\epsilon))$ queries to a block encoding of $H/\alpha$. The no-fast-forwarding theorem shows that a number of queries linear in $t$ is needed for general sparse Hamiltonians; that is a lower bound in the query model, not on gates for a given Hamiltonian.

Each result names its model, and the pages keep the kinds of claim apart. Trotter errors are bounds with stated norms and orders, often far above the error measured on small cases. qDRIFT is random and bounds its error in the diamond norm. Gate counts for molecules such as FeMoco are estimates under a cost model. VQE and imaginary-time evolution are heuristics whose results are measured, not proved. Deciding the ground energy of a 2-local Hamiltonian to inverse-polynomial precision is QMA-complete, so the proved methods for ground states need a guiding state with overlap $\gamma$ with the ground state, and for preparation also a spectral gap.

The chapter writes multi-qubit operators as Pauli strings such as $XZI$. Qubit 0 is the least significant bit of a basis state and the rightmost symbol in a ket or a string, so $XI$ acts with $X$ on qubit 1 and $|01\rangle$ has qubit 0 set.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **The simulation problem.** [Feynman's idea](/feynman-quantum-simulation/), [analog](/analog-quantum-simulation/) and [digital](/digital-quantum-simulation/) simulation, the Hamiltonian simulation problem with its access models, and the [Pauli string decomposition](/pauli-string-decomposition/).
2. **Product formulas.** The [Lie-Trotter formula](/lie-trotter-product-formula/), Lloyd's [universal quantum simulator](/universal-quantum-simulator/), [Trotter-Suzuki formulas](/trotter-suzuki-product-formulas/) of first and second order and [higher orders](/higher-order-trotter-suzuki/), [commutator scaling](/commutator-scaling/), and the random [qDRIFT](/qdrift/).
3. **Sums of unitaries and block encodings.** The [linear combination of unitaries](/linear-combination-of-unitaries/), [truncated Taylor series](/truncated-taylor-series-simulation/), [interaction picture](/interaction-picture-simulation/) and [multi-product formulas](/multi-product-formulas/), and the [block encoding](/block-encoding/).
4. **Polynomials of a Hamiltonian.** [Quantum signal processing](/quantum-signal-processing/), [qubitization](/qubitization/), the [quantum eigenvalue transformation](/quantum-eigenvalue-transformation/), [sparse Hamiltonian simulation](/sparse-hamiltonian-simulation/) and [optimal Hamiltonian simulation](/optimal-hamiltonian-simulation/).
5. **Other dynamics and limits.** [Time-dependent](/time-dependent-hamiltonian-simulation/) and [open systems](/open-quantum-system-simulation/), [density matrix exponentiation](/density-matrix-exponentiation/), the [no-fast-forwarding theorem](/no-fast-forwarding-theorem/) and the [Lieb-Robinson bound](/lieb-robinson-bound/).
6. **Fermions as qubits.** [Second quantization](/second-quantization/), [fermion-to-qubit mappings](/fermion-to-qubit-mapping/), and the [Jordan-Wigner](/jordan-wigner-transformation/) and [Bravyi-Kitaev](/bravyi-kitaev-transformation/) transformations.
7. **Ground and thermal states.** The [variational method](/variational-method/), the [variational quantum eigensolver](/variational-quantum-eigensolver/), [ground state energy estimation](/ground-state-energy-estimation/) and [preparation](/ground-state-preparation/), [imaginary-time evolution](/quantum-imaginary-time-evolution/), [Gibbs state preparation](/gibbs-state-preparation/) and [quantum Metropolis sampling](/quantum-metropolis-sampling/).
8. **Applications.** [Many-body](/quantum-many-body-simulation/), [chemistry](/quantum-chemistry-simulation/), [field theory](/quantum-field-theory-simulation/) and [scattering](/quantum-scattering-simulation/) simulation, and [Hamiltonian learning](/hamiltonian-learning/).

## A first reading

Take these thirteen entries in order: Feynman's idea, Hamiltonian simulation, Pauli string decomposition, the Lie-Trotter formula, Trotter-Suzuki formulas, qDRIFT, linear combination of unitaries, block encoding, quantum signal processing, qubitization, the no-fast-forwarding theorem, the variational quantum eigensolver and ground state energy estimation. They give the problem, the two main families of methods, the lower bound, and the step from dynamics to energies.

Leave for later the higher-order formulas, commutator scaling, the interaction picture and multi-product formulas, which refine methods you have already met, and the eigenvalue transformation, which generalizes qubitization. Read the fermion group when you reach chemistry, and the field theory and scattering entries last.
