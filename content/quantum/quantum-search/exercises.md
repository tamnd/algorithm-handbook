1. ◦ Take $N = 16$ items with one marked item. Start from the uniform state, with every amplitude $1/4$, and apply the Grover iteration four times, each time as the sign flip of the marked amplitude followed by the inversion about the mean $a_x \mapsto 2\bar a - a_x$ of the [diffusion operator](/grover-diffusion-operator/). Give the marked amplitude and one unmarked amplitude after each iteration as fractions, and the success probabilities. Check them against $\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)$ with $\sin\theta = 1/4$, and say how many iterations [Grover's algorithm](/grover-search/) makes.

2. ◦ Write qubit 0 as the least significant bit and the rightmost symbol of a ket, so that $|10\rangle$ is the item $x = 2$. For $n = 2$, write the $4 \times 4$ matrix of $D = 2|s\rangle\langle s| - I$, and show that $D = H^{\otimes 2}(2|00\rangle\langle 00| - I)H^{\otimes 2}$ without multiplying matrices. Then mark $x = 2$, apply one Grover iteration to $|s\rangle$, and say which qubit of the result is 1.

3. ◦ Let $N = 2^{20}$. Find the number of iterations $k = \lfloor \pi/(4\theta) \rfloor$ of [Grover's algorithm](/grover-search/) and its success probability for $M = 1$ and for $M = 16$. Then find the success probability when the count for $M = 1$ is used with $M = 16$ marked items, and when the count for $M = 16$ is used with one marked item.

4. ◦ For $N = 2^{20}$ and one marked item, an algorithm must succeed with probability at least $1/2$. Compare three numbers: the bound $T \ge \frac12(\sqrt{pN} - 1)$ of Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani, the bound $\lfloor \sin(\pi/8)\sqrt N \rfloor$ on the expected number of queries of Boyer, Brassard, Høyer and Tapp (both on [Grover optimality](/grover-optimality/)), and the least $k$ with $\sin^2((2k+1)\theta) \ge 1/2$. In which model are the two lower bounds proved?

5. ◦ In one round of [BBHT search](/bbht-search/) with $m = 2$, the number of iterations $j$ is chosen uniformly from $\{0, 1\}$. For $N = 16$ and $M = 1$, compute the success probability of the round as an exact fraction, and check it against $\frac12 - \frac{\sin(4m\theta)}{4m\sin 2\theta}$. Lemma 2 of Boyer, Brassard, Høyer and Tapp promises at least $1/4$ only for $m \ge 1/\sin 2\theta$; is $m = 2$ large enough?

6. ◦ [Quantum counting](/quantum-counting/) with $N = 32$ and $P = 32$ makes 31 queries. The measurement gives $y = 4$. What is the estimate $\tilde M = N\sin^2(\pi y/P)$, and which other outcome gives the same estimate? If in fact $M = 5$, compute the error bound $2\pi\sqrt{M(N-M)}/P + \pi^2N/P^2$, and list the outcomes $y \le 16$ whose estimates lie within it.

7. ◦ A table of $N = 8$ values is $T = (6, 2, 7, 4, 9, 1, 8, 3)$, indexed from 0, and the current threshold of [minimum finding](/minimum-finding/) is $T[2] = 7$. How many items does the next search mark? Find the probability that a measurement gives a marked item after 0, 1 and 2 Grover iterations. What does this say about the choice of the number of iterations in the early rounds?

8. ◦ For $N = 2^{20}$, compare the number of queries of binary search, $\log_3 N$ (the leading term of the [Høyer-Neerbek-Shi algorithm](/hoyer-neerbek-shi-ordered-search/)), $4\log_{605} N$ (Childs, Landahl and Parrilo) and the lower bound $\frac{1}{\pi}(\ln N - 1)$ for exact [ordered search](/quantum-ordered-search/). How many queries must an exact algorithm make at least? To what ratio does $\lg N$ divided by the lower bound tend?

9. ◦ Run the counts of the [Brassard-Høyer-Tapp algorithm](/brassard-hoyer-tapp-collision/) for $N = 2^{32}$ and a four-to-one function: the table size $k = (N/r)^{1/3}$, the number $t = (r-1)k$ of inputs outside the table whose value is in it, the number of Grover iterations $\lfloor \pi/(4\theta) \rfloor$ with $\sin\theta = \sqrt{t/N}$, and the queries of one round, $k + \lfloor \pi/(4\theta) \rfloor + 1$. Compare with $\sqrt{N/r}$.

10. • An algorithm $A$ without measurements succeeds with probability $a = 0.05$. Find the number of rounds $m = \lfloor \pi/(4\theta_a) \rfloor$ of [amplitude amplification](/amplitude-amplification/), the success probability after them, and the number of calls of $A$, of $A^{-1}$ and of the phase flip $S_\chi$. How many independent classical repeats of $A$ give the same success probability? How much more may $A^{-1}$ cost than $A$ before the gain disappears?

11. • Take $N = 16$. For $M = 1$, find the least level $n$ of Grover's $\pi/3$ recursion of [fixed-point search](/fixed-point-quantum-search/) with failure $(1 - M/N)^{3^n} \le 0.01$, and its number of queries $(3^n - 1)/2$, counting one selective phase as one query. Compare with Grover's algorithm for a known $M$. Then compare the level-2 circuit, 4 queries, with one and with two plain Grover iterations for $M = 1, 2, 4, 8$. What does the fixed point buy, and what does it cost?

12. • A set of $N = 2^{30}$ inputs has about $M = 10^4$ marked ones, and you want $M$ within $\pm 500$. Find the least power of two $P$ for which the error bound of [quantum counting](/quantum-counting/) is at most 500, and the number of queries. Compare with the order $N/(\varepsilon^2 M)$ of classical samples for the relative error $\varepsilon = 0.05$. How would you raise the confidence from $8/\pi^2$ to $0.99$?

13. • Run [Grover adaptive search](/grover-adaptive-search/) on $N$ distinct values with patience $P = 1$. Show that every round uses $r = 0$ iterations, so the method is classical sampling that stops at the first failure. Show that it returns the minimum with probability $(1 + 1/N)^{N-1}/N$, which is $125/256$ for $N = 4$ and about $e/N$ for large $N$.

14. • In [minimum finding](/minimum-finding/), assume that the first threshold is a uniform random item, that each new threshold is uniform among the items below the current one, and that the $N$ values are distinct. Show that the item of rank $r$ (rank 1 is the minimum) is ever a threshold with probability $1/r$, so that the expected number of thresholds is $H_N$; compute $H_8$. If a search from a threshold of rank $r$ costs at most $c\sqrt{N/(r-1)}$ expected queries, show that the expected total is at most a constant times $c\sqrt N$, and give the constant.

15. • The [Brassard-Høyer-Tapp algorithm](/brassard-hoyer-tapp-collision/) reads a table of $k$ values and then searches for one of the $(r-1)k$ inputs outside the table whose value is in it. Show that its cost $k + \sqrt{N/((r-1)k)}$ is smallest, up to constants, at $k \approx (N/r)^{1/3}$. Then replace the Grover search by a classical search, which needs about $N/((r-1)k)$ evaluations, and show that the best $k$ gives the birthday bound $\Theta(\sqrt{N/r})$ ([birthday paradox](/birthday-paradox/)).

16. • Apply the cost $O(S + \frac{1}{\sqrt\varepsilon}(\frac{U}{\sqrt\delta} + C))$ of [quantum walk search](/quantum-walk-search/) to two chains. First, the complete graph with loops, where the state is one item and $C$ is one query: recover Grover's bound. Second, the walk on $r$-subsets of the input for [k-distinctness](/k-distinctness/), with $S = r$, $U = O(1)$, $C = 0$ and $\delta \approx 1/r$: find $\varepsilon$ for one $k$-collision, choose $r$, and show the bound $O(N^{k/(k+1)})$ of [Ambainis's walk](/ambainis-element-distinctness/).

17. • Show that the exponent $1 - 2^{k-2}/(2^k - 1)$ of [Belovs's algorithm](/belovs-k-distinctness/) equals $\frac34 - \frac{1}{4(2^k - 1)}$. Compute it for $k = 2, 3, 4, 5$, compare with $k/(k+1)$, and show that it is below $3/4$ for every $k$ while $k/(k+1)$ tends to 1.

18. • Given $x_1, \ldots, x_N$, let $x'$ have length $(k-1)N$ and hold each $x_i$ exactly $k - 1$ times. Show that $x'$ has $k$ equal values if and only if $x$ has two equal values, and that one query to $x'$ costs one query to $x$. What lower bound does the quantum bound $\Omega(N^{2/3})$ of Aaronson and Shi for [element distinctness](/element-distinctness/) give for [k-distinctness](/k-distinctness/), and how does it compare with the upper bounds of exercise 17?

19. • In the locality model of [spatial search](/spatial-search/), consider a $d$-dimensional grid with wrap-around and side $L$, so $N = L^d$, and an algorithm that starts at one vertex. Combine the bounds $\Omega(\sqrt N)$ and $\Omega(D)$, where $D$ is the largest distance from the start. For which $d$ does each one dominate? For the $1000 \times 1000$ grid, compare $D$, $\sqrt N$, the best known $O(\sqrt{N \log N})$ without its constant, and $N\ln N$ for a classical random walk.

20. ★ Show that the plain Grover iteration finds a marked item with certainty after some number $k \ge 1$ of iterations only when $M/N = 1/4$ or $M = N$. Use Niven's theorem: if $q$ is rational and $\cos(q\pi)$ is rational, then $\cos(q\pi) \in \{0, \pm\frac12, \pm 1\}$. How does [amplitude amplification](/amplitude-amplification/) reach certainty for other ratios?

21. ★ After $T$ queries, every amplitude of a quantum algorithm is a polynomial of degree at most $T$ in the input bits $x_1, \ldots, x_N$ (the polynomial method of Beals, Buhrman, Cleve, Mosca and de Wolf). Use this to show that an algorithm that decides with certainty whether some $x_i = 1$ needs at least $N/2$ queries. Then look at the amplitude of a single rejecting basis state to improve the bound to $N$. Why does this not contradict the $O(\sqrt N)$ queries of [unstructured search](/unstructured-search/) with bounded error?

22. ★ Let $P \ge 4$ be a power of two. Show that [quantum counting](/quantum-counting/) returns $\tilde M = M$ with certainty exactly when $M \in \{0, N/2, N\}$. For which of these is $P = 2$ enough?

23. ★ In the [Magniez-Santha-Szegedy algorithm](/magniez-santha-szegedy-triangle/) the cost is $\tilde O(r^2 + \frac{n}{r}(c + \sqrt r \cdot r))$ for walk sets of size $r$, where $c = \sqrt n\, r^{\alpha}$ is the cost of the check and $r^\alpha$ the cost of graph collision on $r$ vertices. For $\frac12 \le \alpha \le \frac34$, find the best $r$ and the exponent of $n$. Check $\alpha = 2/3$, which gives $13/10$, and $\alpha = 1/2$. Show that the plain check with $\alpha = 1$ gives $n^{3/2}$ for every $r$.

24. ★ Element distinctness with one colliding pair: pick a random set $K$ of $k$ indices, read their values, and run Grover search over the other indices for one whose value is in $K$. Find the success probability of this procedure, and use [amplitude amplification](/amplitude-amplification/) on it to get $O(N^{3/4})$ queries in all, the bound of Buhrman and others. Explain what [Ambainis's walk](/ambainis-element-distinctness/) saves to reach $N^{2/3}$.
