2. $D$ has $-\frac12$ on the diagonal and $\frac12$ everywhere else. $H^{\otimes 2}$ is its own inverse and maps $|00\rangle$ to $|s\rangle$, so $H^{\otimes 2}(2|00\rangle\langle 00| - I)H^{\otimes 2} = 2|s\rangle\langle s| - I$. The oracle turns $|s\rangle$ into $\frac12(1, 1, -1, 1)$ in the order $x = 0, 1, 2, 3$; the mean is $\frac14$, and $D$ gives $(0, 0, 1, 0)$, the state $|10\rangle$ with certainty. 7. The search marks the 5 items below 7, so $\sin^2\theta = 5/8$. The probabilities are $5/8$ with no iteration, $\sin^2 3\theta = 5/32$ with one and $\sin^2 5\theta = 125/128$ with two. When more than a quarter of the items are marked, one iteration is worse than none, so the count must be small and random in the early rounds; that is why the loop starts with a range $\{0, \ldots, \lceil m \rceil - 1\}$ that holds only 0.

8. Binary search needs 20 queries, $\log_3 N \approx 12.6$, $4\log_{605} N \approx 8.66$, and the lower bound is $(13.86 - 1)/\pi \approx 4.09$, so an exact algorithm makes at least 5 queries. The ratio $\lg N / (\frac1\pi \ln N)$ tends to $\pi/\ln 2 = \pi\lg e \approx 4.53$, the most an exact quantum algorithm can save.

9. $k = (2^{30})^{1/3} = 1024$ and $t = 3 \cdot 1024 = 3072$. Then $\sqrt{N/t} \approx 1182.4$ and $\lfloor \pi/(4\theta) \rfloor = 928$, with success $1 - 8 \cdot 10^{-8}$. One round makes $1024 + 928 + 1 = 1953$ queries, against $\sqrt{N/r} = 2^{15} = 32768$.

13. With $P = 1$ the run stops at the first failure, so $k$ never grows past 1 and $r$ is always 0: each round measures the uniform state. Let $p_j$ be the probability of ending at the minimum from the item of rank $j$. Then $p_1 = 1$ and $p_j = \frac1N\sum_{i<j} p_i$, so $p_{j+1} = p_j(1 + \frac1N)$ and $p_j = \frac1N(1 + \frac1N)^{j-2}$ for $j \ge 2$. Average over the uniform start.

16. On the complete graph with loops, one step mixes completely, so $\delta = 1$; with $S = U = 0$, $C = 1$ and $\varepsilon = M/N$ the bound is $O(\sqrt{N/M})$. For $k$-distinctness a random $r$-set holds a fixed $k$-collision with probability about $(r/N)^k$. The cost $r + (N/r)^{k/2}\sqrt r$ is balanced when $r^{(k+1)/2} = N^{k/2}$.

20. Certainty after $k$ iterations needs $(2k+1)\theta = \pi/2 + j\pi$, so $\theta$ is a rational multiple of $\pi$, and $\cos 2\theta = 1 - 2M/N$ is rational. By Niven's theorem $1 - 2M/N \in \{0, \pm\frac12, \pm 1\}$. Check each case: $M/N = 1/2$ gives $\theta = \pi/4$ and $M/N = 3/4$ gives $\theta = \pi/3$, and neither makes $(2k+1)\theta$ an odd multiple of $\pi/2$. For other ratios, the exact version of amplitude amplification lowers the success of one call to $a' = \sin^2(\pi/(4\tilde m + 2))$ with an extra rotated qubit, so that $\tilde m$ rounds reach the angle $\pi/2$ exactly.

21. The acceptance probability is a sum of squared moduli of amplitudes, a real polynomial of degree at most $2T$; reduce it with $x_i^2 = x_i$ to a multilinear one. It equals $1 - \prod_i(1 - x_i)$ on every input, and multilinear representations are unique, so $2T \ge N$. For the second part, an input $x \ne 0$ is rejected with probability 0, so every rejecting amplitude vanishes on all $x \ne 0$, and one of them is nonzero at $x = 0$. A multilinear polynomial with these values is $c\prod_i(1 - x_i)$, of degree $N$.

22. $|s\rangle$ is a sum of the eigenvectors with phases $\theta/\pi$ and $1 - \theta/\pi$. If $P\theta/\pi$ is an integer, phase estimation returns $y = P\theta/\pi$ or $P - y$ with certainty, and both give $\tilde M = M$. If it is not, no $y$ gives exactly $M$. So $M/N = \sin^2(\pi y/P)$ with $y$ an integer, and Niven's theorem applied to $\cos(2\pi y/P)$ leaves $0$ and $\pm 1$, since $\pm\frac12$ would need a 3 in the denominator of $y/P$. $M = 0$ and $M = N$ work with $P = 2$; $M = N/2$ needs $P \ge 4$.

24. If both indices of the pair are in $K$, the table shows the collision; if one is, the Grover search finds the other. So one run succeeds with probability about $2k/N$ and costs $k + O(\sqrt N)$ queries. Amplification needs $O(\sqrt{N/k})$ runs, for $O(\sqrt{Nk} + N/\sqrt k)$ queries, balanced at $k = \sqrt N$. Make the inner search exact, or reduce its error, so that amplification sees a fixed success probability. The walk changes one element of the set per step at $O(1)$ queries, instead of reading a new set of $k$ values in each round.
