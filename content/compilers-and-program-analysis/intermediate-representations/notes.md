## History

Compilers used intermediate languages from the start, and in 1958 the UNCOL proposal asked for one universal language, so that $s$ source languages and $t$ machines would need $s + t$ translators in place of $s \cdot t$. Dominators come from Prosser's 1959 paper on the analysis of flow diagrams. Lowry and Medlock used them to find loops in the Fortran H compiler in 1969, and Allen's 1970 paper on control flow analysis set out intervals, loops and the graph view that this chapter uses. Hecht and Ullman introduced the T1 and T2 transformations in 1972, and in 1974 Tarjan gave an almost-linear test for reducibility. Lengauer and Tarjan published their dominator algorithm in 1979, and Cooper, Harvey and Kennedy described their simpler iterative one in 2001.

SSA form grew out of work at IBM. Rosen, Wegman and Zadeck used it for global value numbers and redundant computations at POPL 1988, and Cytron, Ferrante, Rosen, Wegman and Zadeck placed phi nodes at iterated dominance frontiers at POPL 1989, with the journal version in TOPLAS in 1991. The same paper computed control dependence as reverse dominance frontiers, for the program dependence graph that Ferrante, Ottenstein and Warren had defined in 1987. Choi, Cytron and Ferrante introduced pruned SSA in 1991. Briggs, Cooper, Harvey and Simpson described semi-pruned SSA and the lost-copy and swap problems in 1998, and Sreedhar, Ju, Gillies and Santhanam gave conventional SSA and their three methods at SAS 1999. Aycock and Horspool built SSA by removing phi nodes in 2000, and Braun and five coauthors gave the on-demand construction at Compiler Construction 2013.

Graph IRs without blocks came with Click and Paleczny's sea of nodes in 1995, which became the IR of the HotSpot server compiler. Steensgaard defined loop nesting forests for irreducible graphs in a 1993 report, and Havlak and Ramalingam gave other forests later in the decade. Ananian introduced SSI form in 1999.

The functional side began with Landin's SECD machine of 1964, which made closures concrete. Reynolds in 1972 used continuations and defunctionalization to make a definitional interpreter first-order, and Plotkin proved in 1975 that the CPS translation simulates call-by-value evaluation. Steele built the Rabbit compiler on CPS in 1978. Hughes introduced supercombinators in 1982, Cardelli used flat closures in 1984, and Johnsson defined lambda lifting in 1985. Danvy and Filinski gave a one-pass CPS conversion without administrative redexes in 1990 and 1992, and Flanagan, Sabry, Duba and Felleisen defined A-normal form in 1993. Kelsey in 1995 and Appel in 1998 showed that SSA form is a restricted kind of functional program.

## Further reading

- Ron Cytron, Jeanne Ferrante, Barry K. Rosen, Mark N. Wegman and F. Kenneth Zadeck, "Efficiently computing static single assignment form and the control dependence graph", *ACM Transactions on Programming Languages and Systems* 13(4), 1991. The paper behind dominance frontiers, minimal SSA and control dependence as reverse frontiers.
- Fabrice Rastello and Florent Bouchez Tichadou (eds.), *SSA-based Compiler Design*, Springer, 2022. Construction, destruction, SSI form and SSA in register allocation, by many of the people who developed them.
- Keith D. Cooper and Linda Torczon, *Engineering a Compiler*, 3rd edition, Morgan Kaufmann, 2022. Three-address code, control flow graphs, dominance and SSA form at textbook pace, with the iterative dominator algorithm.
- Steven S. Muchnick, *Advanced Compiler Design and Implementation*, Morgan Kaufmann, 1997. The high, medium and low levels of IR and the control flow analyses used by optimizers.
- Andrew W. Appel, *Compiling with Continuations*, Cambridge University Press, 1992. CPS as the IR of a full ML compiler, with closure conversion and its costs.
- James Stanier and Des Watson, "Intermediate representations in imperative compilers: a survey", *ACM Computing Surveys* 45(3), 2013. A survey of linear, graph and functional IRs.
