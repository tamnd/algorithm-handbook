Take a loop that adds the odd numbers below $n$: start with `i = 0` and `s = 0`, and while `i < n`, add `i` to `s` when `i % 2` is not zero, then add one to `i`. A compiler does not optimize this text. It first writes the loop as [three-address code](/three-address-code/), with one operator per instruction and explicit jumps. It cuts the code into six [basic blocks](/basic-block/), B1 to B6, and joins them by seven edges into a [control flow graph](/control-flow-graph/). The edge from B5 back to B2 closes a [natural loop](/natural-loop/) of four blocks. Block B2 dominates the loop, and its [dominance frontier](/dominance-frontier/) contains B2 itself. That is why [SSA form](/static-single-assignment-form/) needs a [phi node](/phi-function/) at B2 for both `i` and `s`, and one more at B5 for `s`, three in all. Minimal SSA would place five, but two of them merge the temporaries of the loop tests, which are dead there. The machine has no phi nodes, so at the end each one becomes copies on the incoming edges. The edge from B3 to B5 is critical, and a copy for it needs a block of its own.

Each step is a small algorithm on a graph, and each must be correct for every program. The compiler works on a representation that it can analyze, and the shape of that representation decides which analyses are cheap. When $k$ definitions of a variable all reach $k + 1$ uses through one join, def-use chains need $k(k + 1)$ links, while SSA form with one phi node needs about $2k$. Minimal SSA can need $\Theta(n^2)$ phi nodes, although on real programs their number was measured to grow about linearly. The [Cooper-Harvey-Kennedy algorithm](/cooper-harvey-kennedy-dominance/) has a worse worst case than the near-linear Lengauer-Tarjan algorithm, and was measured to be faster on real control flow graphs.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter has six groups, read in order.

1. **Programs as code and graphs.** The [intermediate representation](/intermediate-representation/) in general, three-address code, basic blocks, the control flow graph, the [call graph](/call-graph/) and [def-use chains](/def-use-chains/).

## A first reading

Take these twelve entries in order: three-address code, basic block and control flow graph; the Cooper-Harvey-Kennedy algorithm, the dominance frontier and natural loops; SSA form, the phi node and the Cytron construction; out-of-SSA translation; continuation-passing style and closure conversion. They give the graph that every later analysis walks, the dominance facts that place phi nodes and find loops, one full road into SSA form and out of it, and the two basic tools of functional compilers.

On a second pass, read post-dominance and control dependence together, then pruned SSA and the Braun construction, and the lost-copy and swap problems before Sreedhar's translation. The correspondence of SSA and CPS ties the two halves of the chapter together and reads best last.
