## History

Global dataflow analysis grew up in optimizing compilers around 1970. Frances Allen (1970) split the flow graph into intervals, and Allen and Cocke (1976) solved the equations over them by substitution, as did Graham and Wegman (1976). Gary Kildall's paper at the first POPL (1973) gave the general iterative algorithm over a lattice of facts, with constant propagation as a main example. Hecht and Ullman bounded the number of depth-first passes for bit-vector problems, and Kam and Ullman (1976) found the rapid condition under which the general algorithm meets the same bound. Their 1977 paper set out monotone frameworks and showed that constant propagation is the standard framework that is not distributive. Ryder and Paull surveyed the elimination algorithms in 1986. Wegbreit (1975) tracked branches and values together, and Wegman and Zadeck turned that into sparse conditional constant propagation on SSA form (POPL 1985, journal version 1991).

Patrick and Radhia Cousot analyzed intervals with widening and narrowing in 1976, and their POPL 1977 paper gave the theory behind all of this: a program's meaning as a fixed point and analyses as approximations of it. Their POPL 1979 paper set out the systematic design of analyses with Galois connections and introduced the reduced product. Cousot and Halbwachs (1978) introduced convex polyhedra, and Miné's octagons (2001, with a journal version in 2006) gave a relational domain of cubic cost; Bagnara, Hill and Zaffanella (2008) gave the tight closure for integer octagons. Bourdoncle (1993) chose widening points and iteration orders through weak topological orderings. Strictness analysis began with Mycroft (1980) and was extended to higher-order functions by Burn, Hankin and Abramsky (1986).

Sharir and Pnueli (1981) set out the two classic approaches across procedures, call strings and the functional approach with summaries. Callahan, Cooper, Kennedy and Torczon (1986) carried constants across calls in a working compiler with jump functions. Reps, Horwitz and Sagiv (POPL 1995) reduced the distributive subset problems to graph reachability with IFDS, and Sagiv, Reps and Horwitz extended it to environments with IDE in the same year. Taint analysis for Android apps in FlowDroid (2014) runs on an IFDS solver. Datalog became a common way to write analyses with Whaley and Lam's BDD-based pointer analysis (PLDI 2004) and the Doop framework of Bravenboer and Smaragdakis (OOPSLA 2009).

Mark Weiser defined program slicing in his 1979 thesis and published the dataflow algorithm in 1981 and 1984. Ottenstein and Ottenstein (1984) computed slices as reachability in the program dependence graph, which Ferrante, Ottenstein and Warren (1987) set out in full. Horwitz, Reps and Binkley (PLDI 1988, TOPLAS 1990) built the system dependence graph and the two-pass slicer for calls. Korel and Laski (1988) and Agrawal and Horgan (PLDI 1990) defined dynamic slices.

Shape analysis started with Jones and Muchnick (1979) and Chase, Wegman and Zadeck (1990). Sagiv, Reps and Wilhelm used three-valued logic for it in POPL 1999, with a longer journal version in 2002, and the TVLA system implemented the approach. Calcagno, Distefano, O'Hearn and Yang (POPL 2009) introduced bi-abduction, which made shape analysis compositional and became the core of the Infer analyzer.

## Further reading

- Flemming Nielson, Hanne Riis Nielson and Chris Hankin, *Principles of Program Analysis*, Springer, 1999. Dataflow analysis, constraint-based analysis, abstract interpretation and type and effect systems in one consistent notation.
- Xavier Rival and Kwangkeun Yi, *Introduction to Static Analysis: An Abstract Interpretation Perspective*, MIT Press, 2020. A textbook that builds a static analyzer step by step, with domains, widening and soundness proofs.
- Patrick Cousot, *Principles of Abstract Interpretation*, MIT Press, 2021. The theory from its founder, in full mathematical detail.
- Alfred V. Aho, Monica S. Lam, Ravi Sethi and Jeffrey D. Ullman, *Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools*, second edition, Pearson Addison-Wesley, 2007. Chapter 9 gives the classic dataflow problems, frameworks, depth-first ordering and the iteration bound.
- Thomas Reps, "Program analysis via graph reachability", *Information and Software Technology* 40(11-12), 1998. A survey of IFDS, interprocedural slicing and related analyses as reachability problems.
