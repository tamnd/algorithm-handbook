## History

The proof side of the chapter starts with Robert Floyd, who in 1967 attached assertions to the edges of flowcharts, and with C. A. R. Hoare, who turned them into a logic of triples in 1969. Edsger Dijkstra's weakest preconditions (1975) made the generation of verification conditions mechanical. Symbolic execution came in the same years: the SELECT system of Robert Boyer, Bernard Elspas and Karl Levitt and the work of Lori Clarke appeared in 1975 and 1976, and James King's paper of 1976 gave the method its name. John McCarthy and James Painter proved a compiler for arithmetic expressions correct in 1967, the first step towards [verified compilation](/verified-compilation/).

Testing became a search problem later. Barton Miller, Lars Fredriksen and Bryan So fed random input to Unix utilities in 1990 and crashed or hung between a quarter and a third of them. Andreas Zeller introduced delta debugging in 1999, and with Ralf Hildebrandt gave the ddmin algorithm in 2002. Concolic testing came in 2005, with DART by Patrice Godefroid, Nils Klarlund and Koushik Sen and with CUTE by Sen, Darko Marinov and Gul Agha; SAGE, by Godefroid, Michael Levin and David Molnar, added generational search in 2008. Michał Zalewski released AFL in 2013, and coverage-guided fuzzing became the common way to test programs that parse input. Vu Le, Mehrdad Afshari and Zhendong Su used equivalence modulo inputs to test compilers in 2014.

Invariants and ranking functions were found by algorithms from the early 2000s. Cormac Flanagan and Rustan Leino described Houdini in 2001. Michael Colón, Sriram Sankaranarayanan and Henny Sipma used Farkas' lemma to generate linear invariants in 2003. Andreas Podelski and Andrey Rybalchenko gave a complete method for linear ranking functions and the theory of transition invariants in 2004, and the Terminator tool of Byron Cook, Podelski and Rybalchenko (2006) proved termination of device drivers. George Necula and Peter Lee proposed proof-carrying code in 1996; Amir Pnueli, Michael Siegel and Eli Singerman proposed translation validation in 1998; Xavier Leroy's CompCert compiler, verified in Coq, followed from 2006. Joseph Goguen and José Meseguer defined noninterference in 1982, Dennis Volpano, Cynthia Irvine and Geoffrey Smith gave a type system for it in 1996, and Gilles Barthe, Pedro D'Argenio and Tamara Rezk reduced it to a safety check by self-composition in 2004.

Synthesis is older than most of this. Alonzo Church posed the problem of building a circuit from a logical specification in 1957, and J. Richard Büchi and Lawrence Landweber solved it for monadic second-order specifications in 1969. Amir Pnueli and Roni Rosner showed in 1989 that synthesis from LTL is doubly exponential. On the side of sequential programs, Cordell Green extracted programs from resolution proofs in 1969, and Zohar Manna and Richard Waldinger built deductive synthesis in the 1970s. The modern methods came after 2005: sketching and CEGIS from Armando Solar-Lezama and his colleagues, Flash Fill from Sumit Gulwani (2011), the SyGuS format and competition (2013), and type-directed synthesis from Peter-Michael Osera and Steve Zdancewic (2015) and from Nadia Polikarpova and her colleagues (2016). GenProg, by Claire Le Goues, Westley Weimer and their colleagues, started the study of automated program repair around 2009.

## Further reading

- Aaron R. Bradley and Zohar Manna, *The Calculus of Computation*, Springer (2007). Verification conditions, decision procedures and invariant generation, with exercises.
- Cristian Cadar and Koushik Sen, *Symbolic Execution for Software Testing: Three Decades Later*, Communications of the ACM 56(2), 2013. A short survey of symbolic and concolic execution.
- Valentin J. M. Manès and others, *The Art, Science, and Engineering of Fuzzing: A Survey*, IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering 47(11), 2021.
- Andrei Sabelfeld and Andrew C. Myers, *Language-Based Information-Flow Security*, IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications 21(1), 2003. Noninterference and the type systems that enforce it.
- Xavier Leroy, *Formal Verification of a Realistic Compiler*, Communications of the ACM 52(7), 2009. The CompCert project in its author's words.
- Sumit Gulwani, Oleksandr Polozov and Rishabh Singh, *Program Synthesis*, Foundations and Trends in Programming Languages 4(1–2), 2017. Enumerative, deductive, constraint-based and example-based synthesis in one place.
