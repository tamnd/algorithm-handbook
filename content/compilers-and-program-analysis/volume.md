A compiler reads the loop `for i in 0..n: y[i] = y[i] + a * x[i]`. It checks the types of `a`, `x` and `y`, cuts the loop into basic blocks joined by a control flow graph, and puts it in SSA form, with a phi-function where the two definitions of `i` meet. A dataflow analysis finds that `a` does not change inside the loop. A dependence test shows that no iteration reads what another writes, so four iterations can run at once in SIMD registers, but only if `x` and `y` do not overlap, which a pointer analysis must prove or a run-time check must test. Register allocation and scheduling come last. Each step is an algorithm that must be right on every program, though almost nothing about what programs do can be decided.

This volume is about those algorithms. Its eight chapters hold 272 entries. The first six follow a compiler from types to machine code: type systems and inference, intermediate representations, dataflow analysis and abstract interpretation, optimizations, loop transformations, and code generation with JIT compilation. The last two cover analyses that prove facts about programs: pointer and alias analysis, and verification, symbolic execution and synthesis.

## Why compilers get a volume of its own

A compiler uses the graphs of Volume 7 and the logic of Volume 16, but it must work on all programs at once. A few ideas come back in every chapter:

- **Analyses are sound in one direction.** Every nontrivial property of what a program computes is undecidable (Rice's theorem), and the [full employment theorem](/full-employment-theorem/) says the same of optimizers. A may-analysis, such as [live variables](/live-variable-analysis/), over-approximates; a must-analysis, such as [available expressions](/available-expressions/), under-approximates. How precise the answer is gets measured.
- **Analyses are fixed points on lattices.** The [iterative algorithm](/worklist-dataflow-algorithm/) stops for monotone functions on a lattice of finite height and computes the [maximal fixed point](/maximal-fixed-point-solution/). This equals the [meet over all paths](/meet-over-all-paths-solution/) only in distributive frameworks, by the [coincidence theorem](/mop-mfp-coincidence-theorem/). On domains of infinite height a [widening](/widening-operator/) forces the iteration to stop, at a cost in precision.
- **Exact for a model, hard in general, or measured.** Tree tiling by dynamic programming is optimal for its cost model, while coloring an arbitrary interference graph is NP-complete. Linear scan, list scheduling and most spilling rules are heuristics, and the speedups of JIT compilers, inlining and vectorization are measurements on named benchmarks.
- **A theorem holds for its calculus.** [Type soundness](/type-soundness/) is proved for a stated calculus and semantics, and it rules out only what that semantics calls stuck.

Volume 1, Chapter 1.4, gives one entry each to [Hoare logic](/hoare-logic/), [weakest preconditions](/weakest-precondition/), [symbolic execution](/symbolic-execution/), [abstract interpretation](/abstract-interpretation/) and [separation logic](/separation-logic/). This volume turns them into algorithms: abstract domains and interprocedural solvers in Chapter 34.3, and verification condition generators, concolic testers and invariant synthesizers in Chapter 34.8.

## The chapters

**Chapter 34.1, Type systems and inference**, starts from the [simply typed lambda calculus](/simply-typed-lambda-calculus/), whose soundness follows from progress and preservation. [Algorithm W](/algorithm-w/) returns the [principal type](/principal-type-theorem/) of a [Hindley-Milner](/hindley-milner-type-system/) term; ML typability is [DEXPTIME-complete](/ml-typability-dexptime-completeness/), yet inference is fast on real programs. Inference is undecidable for [System F](/system-f-inference-undecidability/) and with [polymorphic recursion](/polymorphic-recursion-undecidability/), so such languages ask for annotations, and [bidirectional checking](/bidirectional-type-checking/) says where. The chapter goes on to [subtyping](/subtyping/), [type classes](/type-classes/), [gradual typing](/gradual-typing/), [linear types](/linear-types/), [borrow checking](/borrow-checking/) and [liquid types](/liquid-types/), checked by an SMT solver.

**Chapter 34.2, Intermediate representations**, builds [three-address code](/three-address-code/), the [control flow graph](/control-flow-graph/), [dominance frontiers](/dominance-frontier/), [natural loops](/natural-loop/) and [control dependence](/control-dependence/). The [Cooper-Harvey-Kennedy algorithm](/cooper-harvey-kennedy-dominance/) has a worse worst case than the [Lengauer-Tarjan algorithm](/lengauer-tarjan-algorithm/) of Volume 7, but was measured faster on real control flow graphs. [SSA form](/static-single-assignment-form/) is built by [Cytron's method](/cytron-ssa-construction/), at iterated dominance frontiers, or by [Braun's](/braun-ssa-construction/), without dominance; minimal SSA can need $\Theta(n^2)$ phi-functions, though their number was measured to grow about linearly. Leaving SSA must avoid the [lost-copy and swap problems](/lost-copy-and-swap-problems/). The functional side covers [CPS](/continuation-passing-style/), [closure conversion](/closure-conversion/) and the [correspondence of SSA and CPS](/ssa-cps-correspondence/).

**Chapter 34.3, Dataflow analysis and abstract interpretation**, poses the classic analyses in a [monotone framework](/monotone-dataflow-framework/); for rapid frameworks the [Kam-Ullman bound](/kam-ullman-iteration-bound/) gives at most $d + 2$ passes in reverse postorder. [Constant propagation](/constant-propagation-problem/) is monotone but not distributive. Abstract interpretation runs from the [collecting semantics](/collecting-semantics/) to [abstract domains](/abstract-domain/): [intervals](/interval-domain/), [octagons](/octagon-domain/) with $O(v^3)$ closure, and [polyhedra](/polyhedra-domain/), exponential in the worst case, with widening and [narrowing](/narrowing-operator/). Across procedures, [IFDS](/ifds-framework/) solves distributive subset problems exactly in $O(ED^3)$ time. The chapter ends with [slicing](/program-slicing/), [shape analysis](/shape-analysis/) and [Datalog-based analysis](/datalog-program-analysis/).

**Chapter 34.4, Optimizations**, covers [dead code elimination](/dead-code-elimination/) and [value numbering](/global-value-numbering/), where equality of values is undecidable and [Alpern-Wegman-Zadeck](/alpern-wegman-zadeck-value-numbering/) finds the coarsest congruence. [Lazy code motion](/lazy-code-motion/) solves [partial redundancy elimination](/partial-redundancy-elimination/) optimally, in evaluations and lifetimes, among safe placements. [Inlining](/function-inlining/) and [profile-guided optimization](/profile-guided-optimization/) are judged by measured speed, and the best code layout is NP-hard, so [Pettis-Hansen](/pettis-hansen-code-positioning/) is greedy. [Partial evaluation](/partial-evaluation/) gives the [Futamura projections](/futamura-projections/). A [superoptimizer](/superoptimization/) rejects candidates by tests but accepts one only after a full check, and [equality saturation](/equality-saturation/) keeps every rewrite in an e-graph.

**Chapter 34.5, Loop transformations**, starts from [data dependence](/data-dependence/). A transformation is [legal](/loop-transformation-legality/) when every dependence distance stays lexicographically positive. The [GCD](/gcd-test/) and [Banerjee](/banerjee-test/) tests are conservative: "independent" is always right, "maybe" can be wrong. The [practical tests](/practical-dependence-tests/) are exact on single-index subscripts, and the [omega test](/omega-test/) of Volume 16 is exact for integer linear constraints, with no polynomial bound. Then come [interchange](/loop-interchange/), [tiling](/loop-tiling/), [unimodular transformations](/unimodular-transformations/) and [vectorization](/vectorization/). The [polyhedral model](/polyhedral-model/) is exact only for static control parts with affine bounds and subscripts; [Feautrier's scheduling](/feautrier-scheduling/) and [Pluto](/pluto-algorithm/) choose legal schedules by objectives that stand in for speed.

**Chapter 34.6, Code generation and JIT compilation**, selects instructions: [maximal munch](/maximal-munch/) is locally best, [BURS](/burs/) is optimal on trees for its cost model, and optimal code for a DAG is [NP-complete](/dag-code-generation-np-completeness/). Register allocation as coloring is [NP-complete](/register-allocation-np-completeness/) for every fixed $k \ge 3$, and [Chaitin's](/chaitin-register-allocation/) and [iterated coalescing](/iterated-register-coalescing/) are heuristics. Strict SSA programs have [chordal interference graphs](/ssa-chordal-interference-theorem/) that color optimally, but spilling and coalescing stay NP-hard. [Linear scan](/linear-scan-register-allocation/) is a fast heuristic. [Instruction scheduling](/instruction-scheduling-problem/) is NP-complete, and [modulo scheduling](/iterative-modulo-scheduling/) is a heuristic. JIT compilers add [inline caches](/inline-caching/), [deoptimization](/deoptimization/), [tracing](/tracing-jit-compilation/) and [copy-and-patch](/copy-and-patch-compilation/), all with measured speedups.

**Chapter 34.7, Pointer and alias analysis**, asks which memory a reference may touch. Precise may-alias analysis is [undecidable](/may-alias-undecidability/), and even its flow-insensitive form is [NP-hard](/flow-insensitive-alias-np-hardness/). [Andersen's analysis](/andersen-analysis/) runs in $O(n^3)$ time, [Steensgaard's](/steensgaard-analysis/) in almost linear time with less precision, and [one-level flow](/das-one-level-flow/) sits between them by measurement. [Cycle detection](/hybrid-cycle-detection/) speeds Andersen's analysis in practice without lowering its worst case. [k-CFA](/k-cfa/) is [EXPTIME-complete](/k-cfa-exptime-completeness/) for every $k \ge 1$, while [0-CFA](/zero-cfa/) is cubic. [Escape analysis](/escape-analysis/) moves objects to the stack.

**Chapter 34.8, Verification, symbolic execution and synthesis**, follows [path conditions](/path-condition/) through the [symbolic execution tree](/symbolic-execution-tree/) into [path explosion](/path-explosion-problem/). [Concolic testing](/concolic-testing/) and [fuzzing](/coverage-guided-fuzzing/) find bugs but prove nothing beyond the paths they run. [Verification condition generation](/verification-condition-generation/) proves partial correctness from given invariants; [invariant synthesis](/loop-invariant-synthesis/) is undecidable in general. Linear [ranking functions](/ranking-function-synthesis/) over the rationals are found in polynomial time. [Verified compilation](/verified-compilation/) proves the compiler, while [translation validation](/translation-validation/) checks one output and is incomplete, since equivalence is undecidable. Synthesis runs from [enumeration](/bottom-up-enumerative-synthesis/), complete up to its size bound, to [reactive synthesis](/reactive-synthesis/), which is [2EXPTIME-complete](/pnueli-rosner-theorem/) for LTL.

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: the [type system](/type-system/), simply typed lambda calculus, the Hindley-Milner type system, algorithm W, control flow graph, static single assignment form, the Cytron SSA construction, [dataflow analysis](/dataflow-analysis/), live variable analysis, monotone dataflow framework, the MOP-MFP coincidence theorem, abstract domain, widening operator, partial redundancy elimination, data dependence, loop transformation legality, the [instruction selection problem](/instruction-selection-problem/), Chaitin register allocation, Andersen's analysis and verification condition generation.

If you already know the basics, start with a chapter's opening page. Read Chapter 34.2 first, since the others work on its representations, then Chapter 34.3, since optimization, pointer analysis and verification compute fixed points. Chapter 34.1 stands on its own.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.
