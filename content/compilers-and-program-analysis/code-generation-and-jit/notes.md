## History

The first results of the chapter are about expressions. Ershov counted in 1958 the registers that an arithmetic expression needs, and Sethi and Ullman proved in 1970 that their labels give the fewest registers and the fewest instructions for a tree. Aho and Johnson turned this into dynamic programming over a machine description in 1976. In the same year Bruno and Sethi showed that code for a DAG is hard even on a machine with one register, and Aho, Johnson and Ullman studied common subexpressions in 1977. Cattell built a code generator generator around the largest pattern first, in his 1978 thesis and in 1980, and Pelegrí-Llopart and Graham gave the BURS theory in 1988. The table-driven matchers BURG and iburg followed in 1992.

Chaitin and his colleagues at IBM wrote register allocation as graph coloring in 1981, and Chaitin added spilling by cost and degree in 1982. Briggs, Cooper, Kennedy and Torczon made the coloring optimistic in 1989, and Briggs, Cooper and Torczon gave the conservative coalescing test in 1994. George and Appel combined simplification and coalescing in 1996. For fast compilers, Poletto and Sarkar described linear scan in 1999, after a version in the tcc system of Poletto, Engler and Kaashoek in 1997; Traub, Holloway and Smith, and later Wimmer with Mössenböck and with Franz, refined it. In 2005 three groups, Brisk and his colleagues, Hack, Grund and Goos, and Bouchez, Darte, Guillon and Rastello, found independently that strict SSA programs have chordal interference graphs. Bouchez, Darte and Rastello then showed that spilling and coalescing stay hard under SSA.

Hennessy and Gross reordered code for pipeline delays in 1983, and Gibbons and Muchnick gave a list scheduler for a pipelined machine in 1986. Fisher gave trace scheduling in 1981 for wide machines, and the superblock of Hwu and his colleagues in 1993 removed the side entrances of traces, and with them much of the repair code at joins. Rau and Glaeser described modulo scheduling in 1981, Lam made software pipelining practical in 1988, and Rau gave iterative modulo scheduling in 1994.

Compiling at run time is older than its name. Thompson compiled regular expressions into machine code in 1968, Bell described threaded code in 1973 and Dewar its indirect form in 1975. Deutsch and Schiffman compiled Smalltalk-80 methods on demand and cached the receiver class at each send in 1984. The SELF group added maps in 1989, polymorphic inline caches in 1991, deoptimization in 1992 and type feedback in 1994, and the Jalapeño virtual machine of 2000 chose its tiers by a cost model. Dynamo in 2000 and TraceMonkey in 2009 compiled traces, Bolz and his colleagues traced the interpreter itself in PyPy in 2009, and Würthinger and his colleagues built self-optimizing AST interpreters in 2012. Xu and Kjolstad gave copy-and-patch compilation in 2021. Aycock's survey of 2003 tells the earlier story.

## Further reading

- Andrew W. Appel, *Modern Compiler Implementation in ML*, Cambridge University Press, 1998. Instruction selection by tiling, and graph coloring with iterated coalescing, explained with complete code.
- Keith D. Cooper and Linda Torczon, *Engineering a Compiler*, third edition, Morgan Kaufmann, 2022. Instruction selection, scheduling and register allocation as they are built in practice.
- Alfred V. Aho, Monica S. Lam, Ravi Sethi and Jeffrey D. Ullman, *Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools*, second edition, Addison-Wesley, 2007. Run-time environments, code generation for trees, and the chapters on instruction-level parallelism and software pipelining.
- Fabrice Rastello and Florent Bouchez Tichadou (editors), *SSA-based Compiler Design*, Springer, 2022. Register allocation on SSA form and the complexity of spilling and coalescing.
- John Aycock, "A brief history of just-in-time", *ACM Computing Surveys* 35(2), 2003. The history of run-time compilation from the early systems to Java.
