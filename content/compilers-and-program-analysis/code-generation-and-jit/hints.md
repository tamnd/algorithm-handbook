1. The leaves $a$, $c$, $e$ and $f$ are left children and get 1; $b$, $d$ and $g$ get 0. Then $a + b$, $c - d$ and $f + g$ get 1, both products get 2, and the root gets 3. With $k = 2$, GEN computes the right product first, stores it and reads it back as a memory operand: `R0 ← e; R1 ← f; R1 ← R1 + g; R0 ← R0 * R1; t0 ← R0; R0 ← a; R0 ← R0 + b; R1 ← c; R1 ← R1 - d; R0 ← R0 * R1; R0 ← R0 + t0`, 11 instructions and 1 store. With $k = 3$ there are 10 instructions and no store, and with $k = 1$ there are 13 instructions and 3 stores.

2. Each send has a different class from the one before, so the inline cache misses all 30 times. With $k = 3$ the cache misses on the first A, B and C and hits on the other 27 sends; each round of A, B, C costs $1 + 2 + 3$ tests, so the 9 later rounds cost 54. With $k = 2$ the third send, C, misses while the cache is full, and the site is megamorphic from then on: the last 27 sends use the global lookup cache.

4. The `op` stencils become $14 \cdot 9 = 126$, and `copy`, `br`, `ret` and `phi` get 3 variants each. The total is $1 + 3 + 126 + 3 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 143$. The `op` count grows by $9/4 = (3/2)^2$: the square of the ratio of the new number of kinds to the old, not the square of the number of kinds.

6. $k = \lceil 300/6 \rceil = 50$. For $n = 51$ the rule costs $50 \cdot 8 + 300 + 2 = 702$ and the best cost is $\min(408, 402) = 402$, a ratio of about 1.75. For $n = 1000$ it costs $400 + 300 + 950 \cdot 2 = 2600$ against 2300, a ratio of about 1.13.

9. At MEM, LDO is the largest match. At the inner ADD, ADDM and MADD both have size 2 and ADDM comes first, so munch emits LDO, ADDM, MUL and loads of $a$ and $b$: $3 + 3 + 3 + 6 = 15$. The optimum is LDO, MADD and three loads, $3 + 2 + 9 = 14$. Count the tilings bottom-up by multiplying the counts of the holes of each matching pattern: there are 12.

12. With $k = 2$: $a \to r_0$, $b \to r_1$; at $c$ the interval with the largest end is $a$, so $a$ is spilled and $c$ takes $r_0$; $d$ takes $r_1$ after $b$ ends; at $e$ the largest end is $d$, so $d$ is spilled; then $f \to r_0$ and $g \to r_1$. Positions 5 and 6 lie in $a$, $c$, $d$ and $e$, so any choice must drop two intervals, and two spills are the least. With $k = 3$ only $a$ is spilled, which is again the least.

14. In the merged graph the neighbours of $uv$ have degrees $a$: 3, $b$: 3, $c$: 2, $d$: 4. For $k = 4$, only $d$ has degree at least 4, so the Briggs test passes. George merging $v$ into $u$ fails at $d$, a neighbour of $v$ of degree 4 that is not adjacent to $u$; merging $u$ into $v$ passes, since $a$ has degree $3 < 4$. For $k = 3$, $a$, $b$ and $d$ have degree at least 3, and both George directions fail, at $d$ and at $a$.

17. The heights are $a$: 7, $b$: 7, $c$: 4, $d$: 3, $e$: 5, $f$: 2, $g$: 1. In program order the issue cycles are 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10, so the block takes 11 cycles. List scheduling issues $a$, $b$, $e$ at cycles 0, 1, 2, then $c$ at 4, $d$ at 5, $f$ at 6 and $g$ at 7, 8 cycles. It is optimal: one of the loads $a$ and $b$ issues at cycle 1 or later, so $c$ issues at 4 or later, $d$ at 5 or later and $g$ at 7 or later, and every schedule takes at least 8 cycles.

19. With threshold 5 the trace records $i = 5$, on the common path. Iterations 6 to 49 run on the trace: the 11 multiples of 4 from 8 to 48 take the side exit, each followed by one interpreted iteration, and the other 33 complete in the trace. With threshold 4 the trace records $i = 4$, on the rare path, so it completes only the 11 rare iterations and exits 34 times. Both runs give $s = (1225 - 312) + 13 \cdot 100 = 2213$, as the interpreter alone does.

20. A node has label $r \ge 2$ only if both children have label $r - 1$ or one child has label $r$, and the second choice has more leaves. For $r = 2$ the right child needs label 1, which a right leaf does not have, so it is an inner node with two leaves. For $r \ge 3$ both children have label at least 2 and are inner nodes, so $L(r) = 2L(r-1)$.

21. Use induction on $h$ with $k$ fixed. For $h \le k$ no store is needed. For $h > k$ both children have label $h - 1 \ge k$, so GEN computes the right child with all registers, stores it, computes the left child and finishes with a memory operand: $S(h) = 2S(h-1) + 1$. The instructions are one operation for each of the $2^h - 1$ inner nodes, a load for each of the $2^{h-1}$ left leaves, and the stores.

22. The cycle has no vertex of degree below 2, so the first removal is a spill candidate $v$, which Chaitin's allocator spills. What is left is a path, which simplify removes completely. In optimistic coloring $v$ is at the bottom of the stack: each vertex of the path finds at most one colored neighbour when it is popped, so the path is properly 2-colored. A path has only one proper 2-coloring up to swapping colors, so the two neighbours of $v$ get the same color exactly when their distance on the path, $n - 2$, is even.

24. The break-even rule is $j = 25$; its worst case is $n = 26$, with cost $125 + 100 + 1 = 226$ against $\min(130, 126) = 126$, a ratio of $113/63 \approx 1.794$. For these numbers the worst case of every rule $j$ is $n = j + 1$: past it, each run adds $b$ to the cost of the rule and at least $b$ to the best cost, so the ratio falls, and a table of the smaller $n$ shows lower ratios. The best is $j = 24$, with $221/125 = 1.768$ at $n = 25$.
