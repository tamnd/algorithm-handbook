## History

Types entered logic before programming. Church defined the simply typed lambda calculus in 1940 to keep the lambda calculus free of the paradoxes of the untyped system. Curry had noticed in 1934 that the types of his combinators read as formulas of implicational logic, and Curry and Feys proved subject reduction in their 1958 book. Howard made the match between proofs and terms exact in a manuscript of 1969, published in 1980. Tait proved strong normalization of simple types in 1967, and Statman showed in 1979 that deciding whether a simple type has a closed term is PSPACE-complete.

Polymorphism came from two directions. Girard defined System F in 1972 to study second-order arithmetic, and Reynolds found the same calculus in 1974 as a model of programs that work at every type. Hindley proved in 1969 that typable terms of combinatory logic have principal types. Milner gave algorithm W for ML in 1978, with the slogan that well-typed expressions do not go wrong, and Damas and Milner proved in 1982 that W finds the principal type scheme of every typable term. Cardelli gave a semantics of record subtyping in 1984, Wand inferred record types with row variables and a constraint pass in 1987, and Rémy refined rows in 1989. Wadler and Blott introduced type classes in 1989.

The limits were found around 1990. Mairson, and independently Kfoury, Tiuryn and Urzyczyn, proved in 1990 that ML typability is complete for deterministic exponential time. Henglein, and again Kfoury, Tiuryn and Urzyczyn, showed in 1993 that inference with polymorphic recursion is equivalent to semi-unification, which they had proved undecidable. Wells proved in 1994 that typability in System F without annotations is undecidable, answering a question that Leivant had asked in 1983. In the same years Wright and Felleisen made soundness proofs routine with progress and preservation (1994), and Wright's value restriction of 1995 entered the 1997 revision of Standard ML. Pierce and Turner's local type inference of 2000 made bidirectional checking a common design.

Later work made types say more. Girard's linear logic of 1987 led to Wadler's linear types for in-place update in 1990. Lucassen and Gifford introduced effect systems in 1988, Talpin and Jouvelot inferred effects in 1992, and Tofte and Talpin built region inference on them in 1997. Martin-Löf's intuitionistic type theory of the 1970s and Coquand and Huet's calculus of constructions of 1988 are the bases of today's proof assistants. Freeman and Pfenning refined datatypes in 1991, Xi and Pfenning added integer indices in 1998, and Rondon, Kawaguchi and Jhala inferred refinements as liquid types in 2008. Siek and Taha formalized gradual typing in 2006. Rust's 2018 edition replaced lexical lifetimes by lifetimes computed from liveness (RFC 2094), and RustBelt proved a core of Rust sound in the same year.

## Further reading

- Benjamin C. Pierce, *Types and Programming Languages*, MIT Press, 2002. The standard textbook, with the simply typed lambda calculus, soundness proofs, subtyping, recursive types, System F and ML inference, and an implementation for each.
- Benjamin C. Pierce (editor), *Advanced Topics in Types and Programming Languages*, MIT Press, 2005. Chapters on substructural types, dependent types, effect and region systems, and Pottier and Rémy's account of ML inference by constraints.
- Luca Cardelli, "Type systems", in *The Computer Science and Engineering Handbook*, CRC Press, 1997. A short survey of the vocabulary: typed and untyped languages, trapped and untrapped errors, and the main type constructors.
- Morten Heine Sørensen and Paweł Urzyczyn, *Lectures on the Curry-Howard Isomorphism*, Elsevier, 2006. The logic side of the chapter, with normalization, inhabitation, System F and the undecidability results.
- Jana Dunfield and Neel Krishnaswami, "Bidirectional typing", *ACM Computing Surveys* 54(5), 2021. A survey of bidirectional checking, its design recipe and its use with polymorphism and subtyping.
