Two puzzles are usually told together. In Königsberg, can one walk across each of seven bridges exactly once? And on a dodecahedron, can one visit each of the twenty corners exactly once and return? The first asks for a route through every edge, the second for a route through every vertex. Euler answered the first in 1736 with a rule about degrees that is checked in a glance. The second has no such rule: deciding whether a [Hamiltonian cycle](/hamiltonian-cycle/) exists is NP-complete, and the best known exact algorithms take exponential time. This contrast between [Eulerian](/eulers-theorem-eulerian-graphs/) and Hamiltonian questions runs through the chapter.

The chapter starts with the words. A [walk](/walk/) may repeat vertices and edges, a [trail](/trail/) repeats no edge, a [simple path](/simple-path/) repeats no vertex, and a [simple cycle](/simple-cycle/) is a closed path. Then come the easy problems, solved in linear time by graph search: [detecting a cycle](/dfs-cycle-detection/) with depth-first search, building an [Eulerian circuit](/hierholzer-algorithm/), and finding the [longest path in a DAG](/dag-longest-path/). After them come the hard ones and their special cases: [Hamiltonian cycles](/hamiltonian-cycle-problem/) with the sufficient conditions of [Dirac](/dirac-theorem/) and [Ore](/ore-theorem/), the [longest path](/longest-path-problem/), and the [feedback arc](/feedback-arc-set-problem/) and [vertex set](/feedback-vertex-set-problem/) problems. Between the two sit problems that are polynomial for less obvious reasons. The [Chinese postman problem](/chinese-postman-problem/) uses matching, the [minimum path cover](/minimum-path-cover-problem/) of a DAG uses bipartite matching, and the [minimum mean cycle](/karp-minimum-mean-cycle/) uses dynamic programming.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Walks, paths and cycles.** [Walks](/walk/), [simple paths](/simple-path/), [trails](/trail/), [simple cycles](/simple-cycle/), [girth](/girth/) and [counting walks](/counting-fixed-length-paths/) with matrix powers.
2. **Detecting cycles.** [Cycle detection](/cycle-detection/) as a problem, [finding a cycle](/cycle-detection-in-graph/), the [DFS method](/dfs-cycle-detection/) and the [source lemma](/dag-source-lemma/) for DAGs.
3. **Eulerian trails and circuits.** [Eulerian paths](/eulerian-path/), [circuits](/eulerian-circuit/) and [graphs](/eulerian-graph/), [Euler's theorem](/eulers-theorem-eulerian-graphs/), the [problem](/eulerian-path-problem/), [Hierholzer's algorithm](/hierholzer-algorithm/) and [Fleury's algorithm](/fleury-algorithm/).
4. **Postman problems.** The [Chinese postman problem](/chinese-postman-problem/), the [Edmonds-Johnson algorithm](/edmonds-johnson-algorithm/) and the [rural postman problem](/rural-postman-problem/).
5. **Hamiltonian paths and cycles.** [Paths](/hamiltonian-path/) and [cycles](/hamiltonian-cycle/), the two [decision](/hamiltonian-cycle-problem/) [problems](/hamiltonian-path-problem/), and the theorems of [Dirac](/dirac-theorem/) and [Ore](/ore-theorem/).
6. **Longest paths and path covers.** The [longest path problem](/longest-path-problem/), [longest paths in DAGs](/dag-longest-path/), and [minimum path covers](/minimum-path-cover-problem/) by [bipartite matching](/path-cover-by-bipartite-matching/).
7. **Shortest cycles and cycle structure.** The [shortest cycle problem](/shortest-cycle-problem/), [Itai-Rodeh](/itai-rodeh-girth-algorithm/), [cycle bases](/cycle-basis/), and the [minimum mean cycle](/minimum-mean-cycle-problem/) with [Karp's algorithm](/karp-minimum-mean-cycle/).
8. **Breaking cycles.** The [maximum acyclic subgraph](/maximum-acyclic-subgraph/) and the [feedback arc set](/feedback-arc-set-problem/) and [feedback vertex set](/feedback-vertex-set-problem/) problems.
9. **Listing paths and cycles.** [Enumerating paths](/enumerating-simple-paths/) with [Read-Tarjan](/read-tarjan-path-enumeration/), and [enumerating cycles](/enumerating-elementary-cycles/) with [Johnson's algorithm](/johnson-elementary-cycles-algorithm/).
