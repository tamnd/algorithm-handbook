Many scheduling problems are coloring problems in disguise. Exams that share a student cannot sit in the same slot, radio transmitters that are close cannot share a frequency, and variables that are live at the same time cannot share a register. Draw a vertex for each exam, transmitter or variable and an edge for each conflict, and a schedule with $k$ slots is a coloring of the vertices with $k$ colors in which adjacent vertices differ. The fewest colors that work is the [chromatic number](/chromatic-number/).

Cliques are the other side of the same problem. A [clique](/clique/) is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices, so its members all need different colors, and the [clique number](/clique-number/) is a lower bound on the chromatic number. An [independent set](/independent-set/), a set with no edges inside, is what one color class looks like. Finding the largest clique, the largest independent set or the chromatic number is NP-hard, so the chapter has three kinds of results: fast heuristics with guarantees, such as [greedy coloring](/greedy-coloring/), which never uses more than $\Delta + 1$ colors; exact exponential algorithms such as [Bron-Kerbosch](/bron-kerbosch/); and classes of graphs where the hard problems become easy, of which [chordal](/chordal-graph/) and [perfect graphs](/perfect-graph/) are the main ones. Theorems bound the answers from outside: [Ramsey](/ramsey-theorem/), [Turán](/turan-theorem/), [Brooks](/brooks-theorem/) and [Vizing](/vizing-theorem/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Cliques and independent sets.** [Cliques](/clique/), [maximal cliques](/maximal-clique/), the [clique](/clique-number/) and [independence numbers](/independence-number/), and the [decision](/clique-decision/), [maximum clique](/maximum-clique/) and [maximum independent set](/maximum-independent-set/) problems.
2. **Colorings and the chromatic number.** [Graph coloring](/graph-coloring/), [$k$-coloring](/k-coloring/), the [odd cycle theorem](/bipartite-odd-cycle-theorem/), [bipartiteness testing](/bipartiteness-testing/) and [clique cover](/clique-cover/).
3. **Greedy coloring and vertex orders.** [Greedy coloring](/greedy-coloring/), the [$\Delta + 1$ bound](/delta-plus-one-bound/), [Welsh-Powell](/welsh-powell-algorithm/), [degeneracy](/degeneracy/) and the [smallest-last order](/degeneracy-ordering/), [DSATUR](/dsatur/), [RLF](/recursive-largest-first/) and [Brooks' theorem](/brooks-theorem/).
4. **Chordal and perfect graphs.** [Chordal graphs](/chordal-graph/), [perfect elimination orderings](/perfect-elimination-ordering/), [coloring them](/chordal-graph-coloring/), [circular arcs](/circular-arc-coloring/), [perfect graphs](/perfect-graph/) and the [weak](/perfect-graph-theorem/) and [strong](/strong-perfect-graph-theorem/) perfect graph theorems.
5. **Exact search.** [Maximal clique enumeration](/maximal-clique-enumeration/), [Bron-Kerbosch](/bron-kerbosch/), [Carraghan-Pardalos](/carraghan-pardalos-algorithm/), [Tomita](/tomita-maximum-clique/), [Lawler](/lawler-chromatic-number/), [Björklund](/bjorklund-chromatic-number/) and the [planted clique](/planted-clique/).
6. **Extremal bounds.** [Ramsey](/ramsey-theorem/), [Turán](/turan-theorem/), [Motzkin-Straus](/motzkin-straus-theorem/), [Mycielski graphs](/mycielski-graph/), the [Lovász theta function](/lovasz-theta-function/) and the [Hadwiger-Nelson problem](/hadwiger-nelson-problem/).
7. **Kempe chains and colorings with lists.** [Kempe chains](/kempe-chain/), the [five color theorem](/five-color-theorem/), [Hadwiger's conjecture](/hadwiger-conjecture/) and [list coloring](/list-coloring/).
8. **Edge coloring.** The [chromatic index](/chromatic-index/), [edge coloring](/edge-coloring/), and [Vizing's theorem](/vizing-theorem/) and [algorithm](/vizing-algorithm/).
