## History

Graph coloring grew out of the four color problem. Francis Guthrie asked in 1852 whether four colors suffice for every map, and Alfred Kempe published a proof in 1879 that swapped colors along two-colored chains. In 1890 Percy Heawood found a map on which the swaps interfere, and used the same chains to prove that five colors always suffice. George Birkhoff showed in 1912 that the number of colorings is a polynomial, hoping to attack the problem through it, and William Tutte extended the polynomial to two variables in 1954. The four color theorem itself was proved by Appel and Haken in 1976 with a large computer check.

The extremal side began with W. Mantel's 1907 result on triangle-free graphs and Frank Ramsey's 1930 theorem, rediscovered by Erdős and Szekeres in 1935. Pál Turán found the largest graphs without a clique of given size in 1941, the same year that R. L. Brooks characterized the graphs that need $\Delta + 1$ colors. Jan Mycielski gave triangle-free graphs of every chromatic number in 1955, and Vadim Vizing proved in 1964 that edges need at most $\Delta + 1$ colors. Hadwiger's conjecture of 1943, that a graph with chromatic number $k$ has a $K_k$ minor, remains open.

Claude Berge introduced perfect graphs in 1961, motivated by Shannon's question on zero-error communication. László Lovász proved the weak perfect graph conjecture in 1972, and in 1979 introduced the theta function, which showed that the Shannon capacity of the 5-cycle is $\sqrt{5}$ and made coloring perfect graphs polynomial. Chudnovsky, Robertson, Seymour and Thomas proved the strong conjecture in 2002, and the paper appeared in 2006. For chordal graphs, Fulkerson and Gross characterized them by elimination orderings in 1965, Gavril used the orderings to solve coloring and clique problems in 1972, and Rose, Tarjan and Lueker gave a linear-time recognition algorithm in 1976.

The algorithms came with the computers. Welsh and Powell published the largest-first order for exam timetables in 1967, Brélaz DSATUR and Leighton RLF in 1979, and Matula and Beck the smallest-last order in 1983. Karp listed clique and coloring among his NP-complete problems in 1972, and Holyer showed in 1981 that edge coloring with $\Delta$ colors is NP-complete too. For exact search, Bron and Kerbosch published their clique enumeration in 1973, Lawler the subset dynamic program for the chromatic number in 1976, and Björklund, Husfeldt and Koivisto the $O^*(2^n)$ algorithm in 2006.

## Further reading

- Tommy R. Jensen and Bjarne Toft, *Graph Coloring Problems* (1995). A survey of open problems in coloring, with their histories.
- Martin Charles Golumbic, *Algorithmic Graph Theory and Perfect Graphs* (second edition 2004). Chordal, interval and perfect graphs, with the algorithms on them.
- R. M. R. Lewis, *A Guide to Graph Colouring: Algorithms and Applications* (2016). Greedy, DSATUR, RLF and local search compared on real instances.
- Fedor V. Fomin and Dieter Kratsch, *Exact Exponential Algorithms* (2010). Subset dynamic programming and inclusion-exclusion for the chromatic number and independent sets.
- Reinhard Diestel, *Graph Theory*, Chapter 5. Coloring theorems from Brooks and Vizing to perfect graphs, with short proofs.
