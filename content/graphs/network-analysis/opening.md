The earlier chapters of this volume ask for one exact answer about a graph: a shortest path, a maximum flow, a coloring. Network analysis asks broader questions about large graphs that come from the world, such as friendships, web pages that link to each other, proteins that interact, or roads and power lines. Which vertices matter most? Which vertices belong together? How far apart are typical vertices, and what would a random graph with the same degrees look like? How does an idea or a disease spread? The answers are scores, partitions and statistics rather than single optimal objects, and the graphs are often so large that only linear or near-linear algorithms can be run on them.

The chapter has three kinds of entries. Measures define what to compute: the [clustering coefficient](/clustering-coefficient/), the [centralities](/centrality/), [modularity](/modularity/). Algorithms compute them at scale: [Brandes' algorithm](/brandes-algorithm/) for betweenness, [PageRank](/pagerank/) by power iteration, [triangle listing](/triangle-listing-by-degree/), [core decomposition](/batagelj-zaversnik-algorithm/) and the [Louvain method](/louvain-method/). Models generate random graphs to compare against: the [Erdős-Rényi model](/erdos-renyi-model/), the [Watts-Strogatz](/watts-strogatz-model/) and [Barabási-Albert](/barabasi-albert-model/) models and the [configuration model](/configuration-model/). A pattern in a real network means something only when it is rare in a suitable random model, so the three kinds of entry are used together.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Local structure and social ties.** The [degree distribution](/degree-distribution/), [clustering](/clustering-coefficient/), [common neighbours](/common-neighbors/), [reciprocity](/reciprocity/), [assortativity](/assortativity/), and the sociological ideas of [triadic closure](/triadic-closure/), [weak ties](/weak-ties/), [structural holes](/structural-holes/) and [structural balance](/structural-balance-theorem/).
2. **Centrality.** [Degree](/degree-centrality/), [closeness](/closeness-centrality/), [harmonic](/harmonic-centrality/), [betweenness](/betweenness-centrality/), [eigenvector](/eigenvector-centrality/) and [Katz](/katz-centrality/) centrality, and [Brandes' algorithm](/brandes-algorithm/).
3. **Link analysis.** [PageRank](/pagerank/), [personalized PageRank](/personalized-pagerank/) and [HITS](/hits-algorithm/).
4. **Similarity, link prediction and embeddings.** The [Adamic-Adar index](/adamic-adar-index/), [SimRank](/simrank/), [link prediction](/link-prediction/), [node embeddings](/node-embedding/), [DeepWalk](/deepwalk/) and [node2vec](/node2vec/).
5. **Triangles and cores.** [Triangle counting](/triangle-counting/) and the [compact-forward algorithm](/compact-forward-algorithm/), [k-cores](/k-core/) and the [Batagelj-Zaversnik algorithm](/batagelj-zaversnik-algorithm/).
6. **Distances and layout of large networks.** [Six degrees of separation](/six-degrees-of-separation/), the [neighbourhood function](/neighborhood-function/), the [effective diameter](/effective-diameter/), the [ANF algorithm](/anf-algorithm/) and [force-directed layout](/force-directed-layout/).
7. **Random graphs and small worlds.** The [Erdős-Rényi model](/erdos-renyi-model/), the [giant component](/erdos-renyi-giant-component-theorem/), the [connectivity threshold](/erdos-renyi-connectivity-threshold/), and the [Watts-Strogatz](/watts-strogatz-model/) and [Kleinberg](/kleinberg-small-world-model/) models.
8. **Degrees, null models and motifs.** [Preferential attachment](/preferential-attachment/), [scale-free networks](/scale-free-network/), the [Chung-Lu](/chung-lu-model/), [configuration](/configuration-model/), [Kronecker](/kronecker-graph-model/) and [exponential random graph](/exponential-random-graph-model/) models, the [rich club](/rich-club-coefficient/) and [network motifs](/network-motif/).
9. **Communities.** [Community detection](/community-detection/), [modularity](/modularity/), [Girvan-Newman](/girvan-newman-algorithm/), [Louvain](/louvain-method/), [Leiden](/leiden-algorithm/), [label propagation](/label-propagation-algorithm/), random-walk methods, [clique percolation](/clique-percolation-method/) and the [stochastic block model](/stochastic-block-model/).
10. **Percolation and spreading.** [Percolation](/percolation/), the [SIR model](/sir-epidemic-model/), the [independent cascade](/independent-cascade-model/) and [linear threshold](/linear-threshold-model/) models, and [influence maximization](/influence-maximization/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [degree distribution](/degree-distribution/), [clustering coefficient](/clustering-coefficient/), [centrality](/centrality/), [betweenness centrality](/betweenness-centrality/), [Brandes' algorithm](/brandes-algorithm/), [PageRank](/pagerank/), [triangle counting](/triangle-counting/), [k-core](/k-core/), [Erdős-Rényi model](/erdos-renyi-model/), [small-world network](/small-world-network/), [configuration model](/configuration-model/), [modularity](/modularity/) and the [Louvain method](/louvain-method/).

Several entries pair a problem with the algorithm that solves it. [Betweenness centrality computation](/betweenness-centrality-computation/) is solved by [Brandes' algorithm](/brandes-algorithm/), [k-core decomposition](/k-core-decomposition/) by [Batagelj-Zaversnik](/batagelj-zaversnik-algorithm/), and [triangle counting](/triangle-counting/) by [triangle listing by degree](/triangle-listing-by-degree/) and the [compact-forward algorithm](/compact-forward-algorithm/), which are two versions of one idea. [Network diameter estimation](/network-diameter-estimation/) is solved by [ANF](/anf-algorithm/), [network motif enumeration](/network-motif-enumeration/) by [ESU](/esu-algorithm/), and [influence maximization](/influence-maximization/) by the [greedy method](/greedy-influence-maximization/). [Giant component](/giant-component/) and the [giant component theorem](/erdos-renyi-giant-component-theorem/) cover the same phenomenon from two sides. [Force-directed layout](/force-directed-layout/) is a graph-drawing method and fits with [graph drawing](/graph-drawing/) in Chapter 7.11.

## Before you start

From Chapter 7.1 you need [degrees](/degree/), [neighbourhoods](/neighborhood/), [induced subgraphs](/induced-subgraph/), [directed graphs](/directed-graph/) and the [adjacency matrix](/adjacency-matrix/). From Chapters 7.2 and 7.3 you need [breadth-first search](/breadth-first-search/) and [Dijkstra's algorithm](/dijkstra/). The spectral entries use [eigenvectors](/eigenvector/), the [Perron-Frobenius theorem](/perron-frobenius-theorem/) and [power iteration](/power-iteration/). The random models use the [binomial distribution](/binomial-distribution/), [linearity of expectation](/linearity-of-expectation/) and the [Chernoff bound](/chernoff-bound/). Several entries rest on Markov chains and their stationary distributions, and the influence entries on submodular functions; these are not yet written, and any probability or optimization text covers them.
