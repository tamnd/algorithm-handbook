## History

Network analysis began in sociology. In the 1940s and 1950s Heider studied triads of sentiments and Harary turned them into signed graphs and proved the [structural balance theorem](/structural-balance-theorem/) in 1953. Milgram's letter-forwarding experiments of the 1960s gave the idea of [six degrees of separation](/six-degrees-of-separation/), and Granovetter's 1973 study of people who had changed jobs gave [weak ties](/weak-ties/) and the tendency toward [triadic closure](/triadic-closure/). Anthonisse in 1971 and Freeman in 1977 defined [betweenness](/betweenness-centrality/) by the fraction of shortest paths through a vertex, and Freeman also measured how centralized a whole network is.

The mathematics of random graphs grew in parallel. Erdős and Rényi proved the [connectivity threshold](/erdos-renyi-connectivity-threshold/) in 1959 and described the emergence of the [giant component](/erdos-renyi-giant-component-theorem/) in 1960, with the "double jump" at half as many edges as vertices; Gilbert studied the closely related model $G(n, p)$ in the same years. Price proposed in 1976 a growth model of citations with cumulative advantage, the first form of [preferential attachment](/preferential-attachment/).

Around 2000 large data sets turned the field into a part of computer science and physics. Watts and Strogatz showed in 1998 that a few random shortcuts give a clustered lattice short paths, and named [small-world networks](/small-world-network/). Barabási and Albert proposed their [growth model](/barabasi-albert-model/) in 1999 to explain heavy-tailed degrees on the web, and Kleinberg asked in 2000 how people [find short chains](/kleinberg-small-world-model/) using local information. On the web itself, Brin and Page introduced [PageRank](/pagerank/) and Kleinberg [hubs and authorities](/hits-algorithm/) in 1998.

Scale forced new algorithms. Brandes's [dependency accumulation](/brandes-algorithm/) of 2001 brought betweenness from cubic time to $O(nm)$. Batagelj and Zaveršnik gave [core numbers](/batagelj-zaversnik-algorithm/) in linear time in 2003, building on Seidman's cores and on Matula and Beck's smallest-last ordering of 1983. Schank and Wagner and then Latapy turned the old $O(m^{3/2})$ triangle bounds of Itai and Rodeh and of Chiba and Nishizeki into the practical [forward](/triangle-listing-by-degree/) and [compact-forward](/compact-forward-algorithm/) algorithms. Palmer, Gibbons and Faloutsos used Flajolet-Martin sketches in 2002 to estimate [neighbourhood functions](/anf-algorithm/) of graphs too large for memory.

Community detection followed the same path. Girvan and Newman [removed edges of high betweenness](/girvan-newman-algorithm/) in 2002, and Newman and Girvan defined [modularity](/modularity/) in 2004. Blondel and co-authors gave the [Louvain method](/louvain-method/) in 2008, and Traag, Waltman and van Eck repaired its disconnected communities with [Leiden](/leiden-algorithm/) in 2019. In 2003 Kempe, Kleinberg and Tardos posed [influence maximization](/influence-maximization/) and showed that the [greedy method](/greedy-influence-maximization/) is within $1 - 1/e$ of the best, and from 2014 [DeepWalk](/deepwalk/) and [node2vec](/node2vec/) brought word-embedding methods to graphs.

## Further reading

- M. E. J. Newman, *Networks: An Introduction* (2010), covers measures, models and algorithms in one book, at the level of this chapter.
- David Easley and Jon Kleinberg, *Networks, Crowds, and Markets* (2010), explains weak ties, balance, link analysis and cascades for readers from economics and sociology as well as computing.
- Albert-László Barabási, *Network Science* (2016), treats random graphs, scale-free networks and the survival of networks under failures, with many data sets.
- Stanley Wasserman and Katherine Faust, *Social Network Analysis: Methods and Applications* (1994), is the standard reference for the sociological measures.
- Santo Fortunato, "Community detection in graphs" (Physics Reports, 2010), surveys the community methods of this chapter and many more.
