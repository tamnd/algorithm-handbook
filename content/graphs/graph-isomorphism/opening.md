Two drawings of a graph can look nothing alike and still describe the same graph. Whether two graphs are the same up to renaming of their vertices is the [graph isomorphism problem](/graph-isomorphism-problem/). It arises whenever graphs are stored and compared: a chemist asks whether a new molecule is already in a database, a compiler matches a pattern of instructions, a program that enumerates graphs must avoid listing the same graph twice, and a symmetry of a problem can be used to prune a search. The problem has an odd standing in complexity theory. It is in NP, it is not known to be in P, and it is not believed to be NP-complete; since 2015 it is known to be solvable in quasipolynomial time.

The chapter starts with the definitions: [isomorphisms](/graph-isomorphism-definition/), [invariants](/isomorphism-invariant/) that prove two graphs different, and the [automorphism group](/graph-automorphism-group/) of a graph with its [orbits](/automorphism-orbit/). [Trees](/tree-isomorphism/) are handled in linear time by the [AHU algorithm](/ahu-algorithm/). The neighbouring problem of finding one graph inside another, [subgraph isomorphism](/subgraph-isomorphism/), is NP-complete, and [VF2](/vf2-algorithm/) is the usual tool for it. For isomorphism itself the main practical idea is [color refinement](/weisfeiler-leman/): color the vertices by their degrees, then by the colors of their neighbours, until the colors are stable. Combined with branching, refinement gives [canonical labelings](/canonical-labeling/) in programs such as [nauty](/nauty/) and [bliss](/bliss/), which handle graphs with millions of vertices. The chapter ends with special classes, [planar](/planar-graph-isomorphism/) and [bounded-degree](/bounded-degree-graph-isomorphism/) graphs, and [Babai's algorithm](/babai-quasipolynomial-algorithm/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Isomorphism and invariants.** The [definition](/graph-isomorphism-definition/), [invariants](/isomorphism-invariant/), [membership in NP](/graph-isomorphism-in-np/) and the [problem](/graph-isomorphism-problem/).
2. **Automorphisms and symmetry.** The [automorphism group](/graph-automorphism-group/), [orbits](/automorphism-orbit/), [asymmetric graphs](/asymmetric-graph/), the fact that [almost all graphs are asymmetric](/almost-all-graphs-asymmetric/), and [computing automorphisms](/graph-automorphism/).
3. **Trees.** [Tree isomorphism](/tree-isomorphism/) and the [AHU algorithm](/ahu-algorithm/).
4. **Subgraphs, common subgraphs and edit distance.** [Subgraph isomorphism](/subgraph-isomorphism/), [Ullmann's algorithm](/ullmann-algorithm/), [VF2](/vf2-algorithm/), the [maximum common subgraph](/maximum-common-subgraph/) and the [graph edit distance](/graph-edit-distance/).
5. **Color refinement and Weisfeiler-Leman.** [Equitable partitions](/equitable-partition/), [partition refinement](/partition-refinement/), [color refinement](/weisfeiler-leman/), its [$k$-dimensional form](/k-dimensional-weisfeiler-leman/) and the [Cai-Fürer-Immerman graphs](/cai-furer-immerman-construction/).
6. **Canonical labeling in practice.** [Canonical labeling](/canonical-labeling/), [individualization-refinement](/individualization-refinement/), [nauty](/nauty/) and [bliss](/bliss/).
7. **Special classes and the complexity of isomorphism.** [Planar graphs](/planar-graph-isomorphism/) and the [Hopcroft-Tarjan](/hopcroft-tarjan-isomorphism/) method, [bounded degree](/bounded-degree-graph-isomorphism/) and [Luks's algorithm](/luks-algorithm/), [GI-completeness](/gi-completeness/) and [Babai's algorithm](/babai-quasipolynomial-algorithm/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [graph isomorphism](/graph-isomorphism-definition/), [isomorphism invariant](/isomorphism-invariant/), [automorphism group](/graph-automorphism-group/), [automorphism orbit](/automorphism-orbit/), [tree isomorphism](/tree-isomorphism/), [AHU algorithm](/ahu-algorithm/), [subgraph isomorphism](/subgraph-isomorphism/), [VF2](/vf2-algorithm/), [equitable partition](/equitable-partition/), [Weisfeiler-Leman](/weisfeiler-leman/), [canonical labeling](/canonical-labeling/) and [individualization-refinement](/individualization-refinement/).

Some entries pair a problem with its algorithm. [Tree isomorphism](/tree-isomorphism/) is solved by [AHU](/ahu-algorithm/), [planar graph isomorphism](/planar-graph-isomorphism/) by [Hopcroft-Tarjan](/hopcroft-tarjan-isomorphism/), and [bounded-degree isomorphism](/bounded-degree-graph-isomorphism/) by [Luks](/luks-algorithm/). [Partition refinement](/partition-refinement/) and [Weisfeiler-Leman](/weisfeiler-leman/) compute the same coarsest equitable partition by different routes. [Nauty](/nauty/) and [bliss](/bliss/) are two implementations of [individualization-refinement](/individualization-refinement/), and [graph isomorphism in NP](/graph-isomorphism-in-np/) is one property of the [problem](/graph-isomorphism-problem/). The [graph edit distance](/graph-edit-distance/) extends the [edit distance](/edit-distance/) of strings.
