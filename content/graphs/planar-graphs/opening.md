A graph is planar when it can be drawn on paper with no two edges crossing. Road maps, the meshes of finite element methods, the adjacency of countries, printed circuit layers and the triangulations of computational geometry are planar, or nearly so. The condition sounds geometric, but it has strong combinatorial consequences. By [Euler's formula](/euler-formula/), a simple planar graph has at most $3n - 6$ edges, so it is sparse, and it always has a vertex of degree at most 5. It has a [separator](/planar-separator-theorem/) of $O(\sqrt{n})$ vertices, so divide and conquer works on it. And it has a [dual graph](/planar-dual-graph/), whose vertices are the faces, so that cuts in one graph are cycles in the other.

The chapter first fixes the objects: [planar graphs](/planar-graph/), their [faces](/planar-face/), the [edge bound](/planar-edge-bound/), and the two forbidden graphs $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$ of [Kuratowski's theorem](/kuratowski-theorem/). An algorithm stores an embedding as a [rotation system](/rotation-system/), the cyclic order of edges around each vertex, and everything else, from the faces to the dual, is computed from it. [Planarity testing](/planarity-testing/) decides in linear time whether a rotation system without crossings exists. The rest of the chapter uses the structure: [coloring](/planar-graph-coloring/) with four or five colors, [straight-line drawings](/planar-straight-line-drawing/) on small grids, [separators](/lipton-tarjan-separator/) and fast [shortest paths](/planar-shortest-paths/), and finally graphs on surfaces with handles and the [graph minor theorem](/graph-minor-theorem/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Planar graphs, faces and Euler's formula.** [Planar graphs](/planar-graph/), [faces](/planar-face/), the [Jordan curve theorem](/jordan-curve-theorem/), [Euler's formula](/euler-formula/), the [edge bound](/planar-edge-bound/), [maximal planar](/maximal-planar-graph/) and [outerplanar](/outerplanar-graph/) graphs.
2. **Minors and the forbidden graphs.** [Graph minors](/graph-minor/), [Kuratowski's theorem](/kuratowski-theorem/) and [Wagner's theorem](/wagner-theorem/).
3. **Embeddings as data.** [Rotation systems](/rotation-system/), [planar embeddings](/planar-embedding/), [face finding](/planar-face-finding/), the [doubly connected edge list](/doubly-connected-edge-list/) and the [dual graph](/planar-dual-graph/).
4. **Testing planarity.** The [problem](/planarity-testing/), [st-numberings](/st-numbering/) and [how to compute them](/lempel-even-cederbaum-st-numbering/), and the tests of [Hopcroft and Tarjan](/hopcroft-tarjan-planarity-test/) and [Boyer and Myrvold](/boyer-myrvold-planarity-test/).
5. **Coloring planar graphs.** [Planar graph coloring](/planar-graph-coloring/), the [five-color algorithm](/five-color-algorithm/) and the [four color theorem](/four-color-theorem/).
6. **Crossings and drawing in general.** The [crossing number](/crossing-number/), the [crossing lemma](/crossing-lemma/), the [Hanani-Tutte theorem](/hanani-tutte-theorem/), [graph drawing](/graph-drawing/) and the [planarization heuristic](/planarization-heuristic/).
7. **Straight-line drawings and representations.** [Fáry's theorem](/fary-theorem/), [straight-line drawing](/planar-straight-line-drawing/), the [canonical ordering](/canonical-ordering/), the [shift method](/de-fraysseix-pach-pollack/), [Schnyder woods](/schnyder-woods/) and [Schnyder's drawing](/schnyder-drawing/), [Tutte's spring theorem](/tutte-spring-theorem/), [circle packing](/circle-packing-theorem/) and [book embeddings](/book-embedding/).
8. **Separators and planar shortest paths.** The [separator theorem](/planar-separator-theorem/), [finding separators](/planar-separator-finding/), [Lipton-Tarjan](/lipton-tarjan-separator/), [r-divisions](/r-division/), [planar shortest paths](/planar-shortest-paths/) and [Frederickson's algorithm](/frederickson-planar-shortest-paths/).
9. **Beyond the plane.** [Graph genus](/graph-genus/), [computing it](/graph-genus-computation/), and the [graph minor theorem](/graph-minor-theorem/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [planar graph](/planar-graph/), [face](/planar-face/), [Euler's formula](/euler-formula/), [planar edge bound](/planar-edge-bound/), [graph minor](/graph-minor/), [Kuratowski's theorem](/kuratowski-theorem/), [rotation system](/rotation-system/), [finding faces](/planar-face-finding/), [planar dual graph](/planar-dual-graph/), [planarity testing](/planarity-testing/), [five-color algorithm](/five-color-algorithm/), [Fáry's theorem](/fary-theorem/) and [planar separator theorem](/planar-separator-theorem/).

Several entries overlap. [Finding planar separators](/planar-separator-finding/) states the problem that [Lipton-Tarjan](/lipton-tarjan-separator/) solves, [planarity testing](/planarity-testing/) the problem of the two named tests, and [planar straight-line drawing](/planar-straight-line-drawing/) the problem of the [shift method](/de-fraysseix-pach-pollack/) and [Schnyder's drawing](/schnyder-drawing/).
