A scratched compact disc still plays, a QR code with a corner torn off still scans, and a server whose memory flips one bit still reads the right word. Each of them stores more symbols than the data needs, made from the data by fixed rules, so that a decoder can find the damage and undo it. The [Hamming code](/hamming-code/) of 1950 adds three check bits to four data bits and corrects any one flipped bit. A [Reed-Solomon code](/reed-solomon-code/) adds $2t$ check bytes to a block and corrects any $t$ wrong bytes, wherever they are. In 1993 a [turbo code](/turbo-code/) reached a bit error rate of $10^{-5}$ well within 1 dB of the limit that Shannon had proved in 1948, and the [LDPC codes](/ldpc-code/) of Wi-Fi and 5G work close to that limit too.

The chapter keeps three questions apart. The first is combinatorial: a code of length $n$ with $q^k$ words and [minimum distance](/minimum-distance/) $d$, written $[n, k, d]_q$ when it is linear, has guarantees that hold for every error pattern of a given weight. The second is probabilistic: on a named channel, the [binary symmetric channel](/binary-symmetric-channel/), the [binary erasure channel](/binary-erasure-channel/) or the [AWGN channel](/additive-white-gaussian-noise-channel/), how often does a decoder fail, and how close does the rate $R = k/n$ come to [capacity](/channel-capacity/). The third is algorithmic: how fast can one decode, with hard decisions (one bit per position) or soft decisions (a reliability per position). Each entry says which of these it answers, and each bound says whether it holds for all codes, only for linear codes, or only as $n \to \infty$.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Distance and what it guarantees.** The [Hamming distance](/hamming-distance/), the minimum distance, and the two uses of a code: [error detection](/error-detection/) with retransmission and [forward error correction](/forward-error-correction/).
2. **Linear codes and syndromes.** The [linear code](/linear-code/), its [generator](/generator-matrix/) and [parity-check](/parity-check-matrix/) matrices, the [dual code](/dual-code/), the [syndrome](/syndrome/), the Hamming and [simplex](/simplex-code/) codes, and [syndrome decoding](/syndrome-decoding/).
3. **Channels, capacity and decoding rules.** The three channel models, the [repetition code](/repetition-code/) as a baseline, the [channel coding theorem](/channel-coding-theorem/), [maximum likelihood](/maximum-likelihood-decoding/) and [bitwise MAP](/symbol-by-symbol-map-decoding/) decoding, and [erasure codes](/erasure-codes/).
4. **Bounds and hardness.** Upper bounds from [Hamming](/hamming-bound/), [Singleton](/singleton-bound/), [Plotkin](/plotkin-bound/), [Griesmer](/griesmer-bound/), [Elias](/elias-bound/) and [Delsarte](/delsarte-lp-bound/), the [Gilbert-Varshamov](/gilbert-varshamov-bound/) lower bound, the [MacWilliams identity](/macwilliams-identity/), [list decoding](/list-decoding/), and the NP-hard [nearest codeword](/nearest-codeword-problem/) and [minimum distance](/minimum-distance-problem/) problems.
5. **Algebraic codes.** Reed-Solomon, [cyclic](/cyclic-code/), [BCH](/bch-code/), [Golay](/golay-code/) and [Goppa](/goppa-code/) codes, and the [Hadamard](/hadamard-code/) and [Reed-Muller](/reed-muller-code/) codes with [majority-logic](/majority-logic-decoding/) and [local](/locally-decodable-codes/) decoding.
6. **Decoding Reed-Solomon and BCH codes.** The [syndrome pipeline](/reed-solomon-decoding/), [PGZ](/peterson-gorenstein-zierler/), [Berlekamp-Massey](/berlekamp-massey/), [Chien search](/chien-search/), [Forney](/forney-algorithm/), [Berlekamp-Welch](/berlekamp-welch/) and [Guruswami-Sudan](/guruswami-sudan/).
7. **Bursts and codes built from codes.** [Burst errors](/burst-errors/), [interleaving](/interleaving/), [product codes](/product-codes/) and [concatenated codes](/concatenated-codes/).
8. **Convolutional and turbo codes.** The [convolutional code](/convolutional-code/), its [trellis](/trellis/), the [Viterbi algorithm](/maximum-likelihood-sequence-estimation/), the [BCJR algorithm](/bcjr-algorithm/) and [turbo decoding](/turbo-decoding/).
9. **LDPC codes and message passing.** The [Tanner graph](/tanner-graph/), [belief propagation](/belief-propagation-decoding/), [min-sum](/min-sum-decoding/), [bit flipping](/bit-flipping-decoding/) and [expander codes](/expander-codes/).
10. **Fountain, storage and polar codes.** The [peeling decoder](/peeling-decoding/), [fountain](/fountain-codes/), [LT](/lt-codes/) and [Raptor](/raptor-codes/) codes, [regenerating codes](/regenerating-codes/), and [polar codes](/polar-code/) with [successive cancellation](/successive-cancellation-decoding/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fifteen entries in order: Hamming distance, minimum distance, linear code, parity-check matrix, syndrome, Hamming code, syndrome decoding, binary symmetric channel, channel capacity, channel coding theorem, Hamming bound, Singleton bound, Reed-Solomon code, convolutional code and LDPC code. They give the language of codes, the one decoder that works for every linear code, the limit that every code is measured against, the two bounds that frame what is possible, and one example of each of the three families in wide use: algebraic codes, trellis codes and codes on sparse graphs.

Skip at first the research entries: the LP bound, Goppa codes, locally decodable codes, expander codes, regenerating codes, Guruswami-Sudan, and the three polar code entries. The two hardness entries and the standard array are worth reading once the rest is clear; the standard array is a picture of syndrome decoding rather than a practical decoder. The Peterson-Gorenstein-Zierler decoder is the easiest Reed-Solomon decoder to prove correct, but for large $t$ the Berlekamp-Massey algorithm replaces it.

## Before you start

You need linear algebra over a field: [linear independence](/linear-independence/), the [null space](/nullspace/) and [Gaussian elimination](/gaussian-elimination/). You need arithmetic in a finite field $\mathrm{GF}(q)$; [GF(2) polynomial arithmetic](/gf2-polynomial-arithmetic/) from the chapter on checksums covers the binary case, and the algebraic codes also use the [polynomial ring](/polynomial-ring/), [polynomial division](/polynomial-division/), [polynomial evaluation](/polynomial-evaluation/) and the [extended Euclidean algorithm for polynomials](/polynomial-extended-euclidean-algorithm/). The channel entries use [independent random variables](/independent-random-variables/), the [binomial distribution](/binomial-distribution/) and the [union bound](/union-bound/), and from the chapter on entropy coding the [binary entropy function](/binary-entropy-function/), [conditional entropy](/conditional-entropy/) and the [asymptotic equipartition property](/asymptotic-equipartition-property/). The decoders on graphs and trellises are [dynamic programs on a DAG](/dag-dynamic-programming/) and forms of the [forward-backward algorithm](/forward-backward-algorithm/) and [belief propagation](/belief-propagation/). The LP bound needs [linear programming](/linear-programming-problem/) and [LP duality](/lp-duality/), and the fast decoder of the Hadamard code uses the [Walsh-Hadamard transform](/walsh-hadamard-transform/).
