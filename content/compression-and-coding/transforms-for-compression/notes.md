## History

Quantization theory began with pulse code modulation. W. R. Bennett derived the $\Delta^2/12$ error of a fine uniform quantizer in 1948, and C. Chapin Cutler patented differential PCM, the first predictive coder, in 1952. Stuart Lloyd described the optimal scalar quantizer in a Bell Labs memo of 1957, which was published only in 1982, and Joel Max found the same conditions independently in 1960. Claude Shannon set the limit for all of these in 1959 with the rate-distortion function. Linde, Buzo and Gray turned Lloyd's method into a design algorithm for vector quantizers in 1980.

Transform coding came out of statistics. Harold Hotelling introduced principal components in 1933, and Kosambi, Karhunen and Loève gave the expansion of random processes in the eigenfunctions of their covariance in the 1940s. Huang and Schultheiss showed in 1963 that the eigenvector transform and the logarithmic bit allocation are optimal for Gaussian blocks at high rate. Because the KLT depends on the data, Ahmed, Natarajan and Rao proposed the discrete cosine transform in 1974 as a fixed substitute. Makhoul computed it with one FFT in 1980, and Byeong Gi Lee gave a recursive fast algorithm in 1984. The DCT became the core of the JPEG standard, ITU-T T.81 of 1992.

Filter banks grew in parallel in speech coding. Crochiere, Webber and Flanagan coded speech in subbands in 1976, the year Croisier, Esteban and Galand introduced quadrature mirror filters, which cancel aliasing but do not reconstruct exactly with FIR filters. Smith and Barnwell found exact FIR solutions with conjugate quadrature filters in 1984. Princen and Bradley introduced time-domain aliasing cancellation in 1986, the basis of the MDCT in AAC, Vorbis and Opus. Daubechies built compactly supported orthogonal wavelets in 1988, and Cohen, Daubechies and Feauveau the biorthogonal family of 1992 that contains the 9/7 filters. Sweldens described the lifting scheme in 1996.

Embedded image coding followed. Shapiro's EZW coder of 1993 sent wavelet coefficients by bit planes over zerotrees, and Said and Pearlman refined it into SPIHT in 1996. Taubman's EBCOT of 2000 coded independent code blocks and chose their truncation points after coding. It became the core of JPEG 2000, whose first part was published as ISO/IEC 15444-1 in December 2000.

The text transforms have a separate history. Golomb gave his run-length codes in 1966, and Gallager and Van Voorhis showed in 1975 that they are optimal for geometric run lengths. Ryabko described move-to-front as a book stack in 1980, and Bentley, Sleator, Tarjan and Wei analyzed it as a locally adaptive code in 1986; Elias studied interval and recency rank coding in 1987. David Wheeler found the block-sorting transform in 1983, and Burrows and Wheeler published it with move-to-front in a DEC report in 1994. Julian Seward released bzip2 in 1996. Gil and Scott, and independently Kufleitner, described the bijective variant in 2009.

## Further reading

- Michael Burrows and David J. Wheeler, "A block-sorting lossless data compression algorithm", SRC Research Report 124, Digital Equipment Corporation, 1994. The short original report: the transform, its inverse and move-to-front, with measurements.
- N. S. Jayant and Peter Noll, *Digital Coding of Waveforms: Principles and Applications to Speech and Video*, Prentice-Hall, 1984. The standard text on quantization, predictive coding, transform coding and subband coding, with the high-rate formulas worked out.
- Allen Gersho and Robert M. Gray, *Vector Quantization and Signal Compression*, Kluwer, 1992. The reference for scalar and vector quantizers, the Lloyd conditions and bit allocation.
- Vivek K. Goyal, "Theoretical foundations of transform coding", IEEE Signal Processing Magazine 18(5), 2001. A short survey of when the KLT is optimal, when it is not, and why the high-rate model matters.
- Martin Vetterli and Jelena Kovačević, *Wavelets and Subband Coding*, Prentice Hall, 1995. Filter banks, perfect reconstruction and wavelets from the signal processing side, with the polyphase view throughout.
- David S. Taubman and Michael W. Marcellin, *JPEG2000: Image Compression Fundamentals, Standards and Practice*, Kluwer, 2002. The full JPEG 2000 coder, including EBCOT, the MQ coder and rate control, by the designer of EBCOT.
