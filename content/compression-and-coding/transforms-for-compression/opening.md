An entropy coder with a memoryless model codes each symbol by its frequency alone. Most data has structure that such a model misses: neighbouring pixels are close in value, a sorted list of IDs grows by small steps, and a text repeats the same words in the same contexts many times. A transform rewrites the data so that this structure becomes plain: small numbers, long runs, or a few large coefficients among many near zero. A bzip2 file is a Burrows-Wheeler transform, a move-to-front pass and run lengths in front of a Huffman coder. A JPEG photograph is a grid of $8 \times 8$ discrete cosine transforms, and an AAC track is a sequence of modified discrete cosine transforms. MP3 applies the same transform to the bands of a polyphase filter bank.

The chapter separates two kinds of transform. A reversible transform loses nothing, and the decoder gets the input back exactly. A lossy coder adds a quantizer after the transform, and then the question is how much distortion each bit of rate buys. The entries state the model behind each claim. A coding gain is a ratio of variances for a stationary source at high rate, usually a Gaussian one or a first-order autoregressive one with correlation $\rho$. Where an entry implements a standard, it says which part: the baseline subset of JPEG, or only the transform stages of JPEG 2000.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Differences and runs.** [Delta encoding](/delta-encoding/), [predictive coding](/predictive-coding/), [run-length encoding](/run-length-encoding/), and Golomb codes for runs in [run-length compression](/run-length-compression/).
2. **Reversible transforms for text.** The [Burrows-Wheeler transform](/burrows-wheeler-transform/), its [inverse](/inverse-burrows-wheeler-transform/) by the LF mapping, [move-to-front](/move-to-front-transform/) and the other [locality recodings](/locality-recoding/), and the [bijective variant](/bijective-burrows-wheeler-transform/) that needs no end marker.
3. **Lossy compression and quantization.** The rate-distortion trade of [lossy compression](/lossy-compression/), [scalar quantization](/scalar-quantization/) with its $\Delta^2/12$ error, and [vector quantization](/vector-quantization/) by the generalized Lloyd algorithm.
4. **Orthogonal transforms.** [Orthogonal transforms](/orthogonal-transform/) and the two things they are asked to do: [energy compaction](/energy-compaction/) and [decorrelation](/signal-decorrelation/).
5. **Transform coding and the Karhunen-Loève transform.** The [transform coding](/transform-coding/) pipeline and its coding gain, the [Karhunen-Loève transform](/karhunen-loeve-transform/) that maximizes it, and the [Karhunen-Loève theorem](/karhunen-loeve-theorem/) for processes in continuous time.
6. **The DCT and JPEG.** The problem of [lossy image compression](/lossy-image-compression/), the [discrete cosine transform](/discrete-cosine-transform/) and its fast algorithms, the [zigzag scan](/zigzag-scan/), and baseline [JPEG](/jpeg-compression/).
7. **Filter banks and subband coding.** Two-channel [filter banks](/filter-bank/), the condition for [perfect reconstruction](/perfect-reconstruction/), bit allocation in [subband coding](/subband-coding/), and the [modified discrete cosine transform](/modified-discrete-cosine-transform/) of audio coders.
8. **Embedded wavelet image coding.** [Bit-plane coding](/bit-plane-coding/), the tree-based sorting of [SPIHT](/spiht/), and the transform stages and coding model of [JPEG 2000](/jpeg-2000/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: delta encoding, run-length encoding, the Burrows-Wheeler transform, its inverse, move-to-front, lossy compression, scalar quantization, orthogonal transform, transform coding, the discrete cosine transform, the zigzag scan and JPEG compression. They give one complete lossless pipeline, the one inside bzip2, and one complete lossy pipeline, the one inside a baseline JPEG file. Add energy compaction if the coding gain in transform coding feels abstract; its tables show the numbers for a real source.

Skip at first the Karhunen-Loève theorem, the bijective Burrows-Wheeler transform and SPIHT. The first is analysis rather than an algorithm, and the other two are research entries. Filter banks and perfect reconstruction need the $z$-transform. Read them before the MDCT and JPEG 2000, not before JPEG.

## Before you start

The transforms here feed an entropy coder, so you need [Shannon entropy](/shannon-entropy/), [Huffman coding](/huffman-coding/), [arithmetic coding](/arithmetic-coding/) and [Golomb coding](/golomb-coding/) from the chapter before. The Burrows-Wheeler entries use the [suffix array](/suffix-array/), and the bijective transform uses the [Lyndon factorization](/lyndon-factorization/). The transforms of the middle groups are linear algebra: the [spectral theorem](/spectral-theorem/) for symmetric matrices, the [covariance matrix](/covariance-matrix/), the [Jacobi eigenvalue algorithm](/jacobi-eigenvalue-algorithm/), and the [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/) with the [fast Fourier transform](/fast-fourier-transform/). The design algorithm of vector quantization is [k-means](/k-means/) under another name. For the last two groups you need the [z-transform](/z-transform/), the [Haar wavelet](/haar-wavelet/), the [discrete wavelet transform](/discrete-wavelet-transform/) and the [lifting scheme](/lifting-scheme/). You should be at ease with expectations, variances, logarithms in base 2 and decibels, where a factor of 2 in power is about $3.01$ dB.
