## History

The subject begins with Claude Shannon's paper *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* of 1948. It defined entropy, proved the source coding theorem for memoryless sources, and gave the code with lengths $\lceil \log_2(1/p) \rceil$ that now carries Shannon's name. Robert Fano described his top-down splitting code in an MIT report of 1949, and Leon Kraft proved the inequality on codeword lengths in his MIT master's thesis of the same year. Brockway McMillan extended it to all uniquely decodable codes in 1956, and Jack Karush gave the short proof by powers of the Kraft sum in 1961. McMillan also proved the equipartition property for stationary ergodic sources in 1953, and Leo Breiman strengthened it to almost sure convergence in 1957.

David Huffman, a student in Fano's course at MIT, published his bottom-up construction of minimum-redundancy codes in the Proceedings of the IRE in 1952 and closed the problem of the optimal symbol code. The variants came later. Gilbert and Moore studied alphabetic codes in 1959, Hu and Tucker gave a greedy algorithm for them in 1971, and Garsia and Wachs a simpler one in 1977. Larmore and Hirschberg found package-merge for length-limited codes in 1990. Faller in 1973 and Gallager in 1978 made Huffman coding adaptive, and Knuth and Vitter refined the method in the 1980s; Gallager's paper of 1978 also bounded the redundancy of a Huffman code by the largest probability. Tunstall turned the problem around in his thesis of 1967, with variable-length source words and fixed-length codewords.

Codes for the integers grew from run lengths. Solomon Golomb described his code in 1966, and Gallager and van Voorhis gave the exact rule for its parameter and proved it optimal for geometric sources in 1975. Peter Elias defined universal codes and gave the gamma, delta and omega codes in 1975. Robert Rice used the codes with power-of-two parameters for space data in 1979, and they now code the residuals of FLAC and JPEG-LS. Apostolico and Fraenkel proposed the Fibonacci code in 1987.

Arithmetic coding grew out of the Shannon-Fano-Elias code, which Elias described but did not publish. Jorma Rissanen and Richard Pasco found finite-precision versions in 1976, and G. N. N. Martin described range coding in 1979. Rissanen and Langdon split compression into a model and a coder in 1981, and the integer coder of Witten, Neal and Cleary, published in 1987, made the method practical. Modeling became the main topic. Cleary and Witten introduced PPM in 1984; Willems, Shtarkov and Tjalkens gave context tree weighting in 1995, with a proved bound; Matt Mahoney's PAQ series from 2002 mixed many models with a learned mixer. The binary arithmetic coder CABAC entered video with H.264 in 2003.

Jarek Duda proposed asymmetric numeral systems in 2009 and the tabled and range forms in 2013. Yann Collet's Finite State Entropy library of 2013 and Fabian Giesen's rANS coder of 2014 made them fast in practice, and tANS now codes the sequences of Zstandard. In a separate line, Slepian and Wolf showed in 1973 that two correlated sources coded apart need no more total rate than when coded together.

## Further reading

- Thomas M. Cover and Joy A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd edition, Wiley, 2006. The standard text on entropy, the equipartition property, the source coding theorem, Huffman and Shannon-Fano-Elias codes, and the Slepian-Wolf theorem.
- Alistair Moffat, "Huffman coding", *ACM Computing Surveys* 52(4), 2019. A survey of Huffman coding in practice: canonical codes, fast decoding, length limits and adaptive variants.
- Ian H. Witten, Alistair Moffat and Timothy C. Bell, *Managing Gigabytes: Compressing and Indexing Documents and Images*, 2nd edition, Morgan Kaufmann, 1999. Integer codes for inverted indexes, Golomb codes and arithmetic coding, from the side of an engineer.
- Giulio Ermanno Pibiri and Rossano Venturini, "Techniques for inverted index compression", *ACM Computing Surveys* 53(6), 2020. The modern codes for lists of integers, with measurements, from variable-byte to PForDelta and interpolative coding.
- Timothy C. Bell, John G. Cleary and Ian H. Witten, *Text Compression*, Prentice Hall, 1990. Modeling for compression: adaptive and context models, PPM and the coders they feed.
