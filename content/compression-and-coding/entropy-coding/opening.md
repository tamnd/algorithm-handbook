Entropy coding is the last step of almost every compressor. It takes a sequence of symbols and a probability for each one, and writes bits: few for a likely symbol, many for a rare one. A fixed code spends the same on every symbol, eight bits per byte in ASCII or three bits for eight letters. Take a text of 50 letters with the counts E 12, T 9, A 8, O 7, N 6, S 4, H 3 and R 1. A fixed three-bit code needs 150 bits. A Huffman code needs 141. The empirical entropy of the text is about 2.775 bits per letter, and no uniquely decodable code that gives each letter one codeword can write it in fewer than $50 \times 2.775 \approx 139$ bits. DEFLATE, the format inside zip, gzip and PNG, ends its work with Huffman codes, Zstandard uses Huffman codes and asymmetric numeral systems, and H.264 and HEVC video use a binary arithmetic coder. Search engines keep their lists of document numbers in codes for the integers.

The chapter keeps two questions apart. The first is how to turn probabilities into bits, which is a solved problem: Huffman codes are optimal among codes that give each symbol a whole number of bits, and arithmetic coding and ANS come within a few bits of the ideal length $\sum_i \log_2(1/p_i)$ of the whole message, plus a tiny cost per symbol for working in finite precision. The second is where the probabilities come from, which is the model, and that is where compressors differ. The bounds name their model. Shannon's theorem is about a memoryless source; it extends to a Markov or a stationary ergodic source by its entropy rate. The guarantees of adaptive coders hold for every input, measured against the empirical entropy or against the best model in a class.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Information and entropy.** [Self-information](/self-information/), [Shannon entropy](/shannon-entropy/) and its empirical form, the [binary entropy function](/binary-entropy-function/), [conditional entropy](/conditional-entropy/) and the [entropy rate](/entropy-rate/) of a source with memory.
2. **Codes and the limits of compression.** The [prefix code](/prefix-code/), the [Kraft inequality](/kraft-inequality/) and McMillan's theorem, the [incompressibility argument](/incompressibility-argument/), the [asymptotic equipartition property](/asymptotic-equipartition-property/), the [source coding theorem](/source-coding-theorem/) and the [Slepian-Wolf theorem](/slepian-wolf-theorem/).
3. **Optimal prefix codes.** The [optimal prefix code](/optimal-prefix-code/) problem, [Shannon-Fano](/shannon-fano-coding/) and [Huffman](/huffman-coding/) codes, the [canonical form](/canonical-huffman-code/) that formats store, and [adaptive Huffman coding](/adaptive-huffman-coding/).
4. **Prefix codes under constraints.** Codes with a [length limit](/length-limited-prefix-code/), solved by [package-merge](/package-merge/), [alphabetic codes](/optimal-alphabetic-code/) by [Hu-Tucker](/hu-tucker-algorithm/) and [Garsia-Wachs](/garsia-wachs-algorithm/), [unequal letter costs](/unequal-letter-cost-coding/), and the variable-to-fixed codes of [Tunstall](/tunstall-coding/).
5. **Codes for the integers.** [Unary](/unary-coding/) and the [universal codes](/universal-code/) of Elias, from [gamma](/elias-gamma-coding/) to [omega](/elias-omega-coding/), [Fibonacci codes](/fibonacci-coding/), and the parameter codes of [Golomb](/golomb-coding/), [Rice](/rice-coding/) and [Exp-Golomb](/exp-golomb-coding/).
6. **Coding lists of numbers.** [Variable-byte](/variable-byte-encoding/) codes, [PForDelta](/pfor-delta/), [interpolative coding](/interpolative-coding/), [enumerative coding](/enumerative-coding/) and the [encoding of genomes](/genomic-data-encoding/).
7. **Interval coding.** [Shannon-Fano-Elias](/shannon-fano-elias-coding/) and [Gilbert-Moore](/gilbert-moore-code/) codes, [arithmetic coding](/arithmetic-coding/) and the byte-wise [range coder](/range-coding/) of LZMA.
8. **Asymmetric numeral systems.** [ANS](/asymmetric-numeral-systems/) and its two practical forms, [rANS](/rans-coding/) and the tabled [tANS](/tabled-asymmetric-numeral-systems/).
9. **Modeling.** [Adaptive](/adaptive-modeling/) and [context models](/context-modeling/), [CABAC](/cabac/), [PPM](/prediction-by-partial-matching/), [context tree weighting](/context-tree-weighting/) and [context mixing](/context-mixing/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these thirteen entries in order: self-information, Shannon entropy, the prefix code, the [uniquely decodable code](/uniquely-decodable-code/), the Kraft inequality, the optimal prefix code, Huffman coding, the canonical Huffman code, the source coding theorem, Elias gamma coding, Golomb coding, arithmetic coding and adaptive modeling. They give the measure, the limit, the optimal symbol code, the codes for integers and the coder that reaches the limit for any model.

Skip at first the codes under extra constraints of group 4, the coders of groups 6 and 8, and the research entries: the Slepian-Wolf theorem, unequal letter costs, context tree weighting and context mixing. Group 4 matters when a format fixes a length limit or an order; group 8 matters when you build a fast coder.

## Before you start

You need [discrete probability](/discrete-probability-distribution/) and [expected value](/expected-value/), with logarithms to base 2 throughout, and the [binary tree](/binary-tree/), which is the shape of every prefix code. Huffman's algorithm is a [greedy algorithm](/greedy-algorithm/) on a [priority queue](/priority-queue/). The integer codes use the [geometric distribution](/geometric-distribution/), [$\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor$](/floor-log2/) and the [iterated logarithm](/iterated-logarithm/); the Fibonacci code rests on [Zeckendorf's theorem](/zeckendorf-theorem/). The limits use the [pigeonhole principle](/pigeonhole-principle/), [binomial coefficients](/binomial-coefficient/) and the [law of large numbers](/law-of-large-numbers/), and the entropy rate needs a [Markov chain](/markov-chain/) and its [stationary distribution](/stationary-distribution/). Alphabetic codes use [interval dynamic programming](/interval-dp/), enumerative coding uses [ranking and unranking](/combinatorial-ranking-and-unranking/), and the lists of group 6 use [bit packing](/bit-packing/) and [delta encoding](/delta-encoding/). Context mixing is [logistic regression](/logistic-regression/) trained online. You should be at ease with [bit operations](/bitwise-operations/) on machine words.
