## History

Fitting started long before CAD. Karl Pearson fitted lines and planes by least orthogonal distance in 1901, the method behind every least-squares reference feature. The algebraic circle fit of Kåsa appeared in 1976, Bookstein fitted conics in 1979, and Sampson refined that fit by a first-order distance in 1982. Pratt gave his normalization for algebraic surfaces in 1987, and Taubin in 1991 divided the algebraic distance by its gradient. Gander, Golub and Strebel compared algebraic and geometric fits of circles and ellipses in 1994, and Fitzgibbon, Pilu and Fisher made the ellipse fit always return an ellipse. Lukács, Martin and Marshall gave faithful distances for spheres, cylinders, cones and tori in 1998, the year Shakarji described the fits of the NIST algorithm testing system; Chernov's book of 2010 collected the theory of circle fitting.

Reverse engineering grew out of range imaging. Besl and Jain classified surface points by curvature signs in 1986 and segmented range images by variable-order fitting in 1988, the year Hoschek proposed parameter correction for curve fitting. Besl and McKay published the iterative closest point method in 1992, the same year as the point-to-plane variant of Chen and Medioni. Várady, Martin and Cox surveyed the field in 1997, and Várady and Martin, with Benkő and others, went on to B-rep reconstruction in 2001 and constrained fitting in 2002. Pottmann and Hofer studied the squared distance function in 2003, which led to the squared distance minimization of Wang, Pottmann and Liu in 2006. Gelfand and Guibas used slippage for segmentation in 2004, and Schnabel, Wahl and Klein gave efficient RANSAC for primitives in 2007. Datasets such as ABC (2019) now train and test learned methods.

Tolerancing got its mathematics from solid modeling. Requicha's 1983 paper "Toward a theory of geometric tolerancing" defined zones as offsets of an r-set, and Rossignac and Requicha studied the offsets in 1986. Bourdet and Clément introduced the small displacement screw in 1988. Davidson, Mujezinović and Shah built Tolerance-Maps for round faces in 2002, and Desrochers, Ghie and Laperrière published the unified Jacobian-torsor model in 2003. The standards moved in parallel: ASME Y14.5-2018 removed concentricity and symmetry, and ISO 1101:2017 kept fourteen characteristics.

Inspection and analysis matured with the coordinate measuring machine. Hammersley's points of 1960 were brought to CMM sampling by Woo and Liang in 1993. Menq, Yau and Lai described CAD-directed inspection of surface profiles in 1992, and Spitz, Spyridi and Requicha planned probe accessibility in 1999. Houle and Toussaint computed the width of a point set in 1988, the core of minimum-zone straightness and flatness, and Carr and Ferreira posed form verification as a sequence of linear programs in 1995. Agarwal, Aronov and Sharir gave exact and approximation algorithms for minimum-width cylindrical shells in 2001. On the analysis side, Spotts allocated tolerances by Lagrange multipliers in 1973, Chase and coauthors added process selection in 1990, and Chase, Gao and Magleby gave the direct linearization method in 1995.

## Further reading

- Tamás Várady, Ralph R. Martin and Jordan Cox, "Reverse engineering of geometric models, an introduction", *Computer-Aided Design* 29(4), 1997. The survey that set out the steps of scan-to-CAD, from data capture to the B-rep.
- Nikolai Chernov, *Circular and Linear Regression: Fitting Circles and Lines by Least Squares* (CRC Press, 2010). The statistics of algebraic and geometric fits, with the bias of each method worked out.
- Yadong Li and Peihua Gu, "Free-form surface inspection techniques state of the art review", *Computer-Aided Design* 36(13), 2004. Sampling, localization and comparison for inspection against a CAD model.
- Y. S. Hong and T. C. Chang, "A comprehensive review of tolerancing research", *International Journal of Production Research* 40(11), 2002. Specification, representation, analysis and allocation in one review.
- Hua Chen, Sun Jin, Zhimin Li and Xinmin Lai, "A comprehensive study of three dimensional tolerance analysis methods", *Computer-Aided Design* 53, 2014. A survey and comparison of the three-dimensional analysis models, among them torsors, Jacobians and T-Maps.
- Georg Henzold, *Geometrical Dimensioning and Tolerancing for Design, Manufacturing and Inspection* (Butterworth-Heinemann, second edition, 2006). The ISO system of geometrical tolerances explained feature by feature.
