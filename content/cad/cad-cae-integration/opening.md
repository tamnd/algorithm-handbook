A CAD system stores a quarter of a circle as a quadratic [NURBS](/nurbs/) curve with three control points, $(1, 0)$, $(1, 1)$ and $(0, 1)$, and the weights $1$, $\sqrt2/2$ and $1$. A classical finite element program cannot use that curve. It meshes the shape again with straight or quadratic elements, which only approximate the circle, and the error of the shape stays in every later answer. For a real part, building and checking that mesh takes much of the time of an analysis. This chapter is about closing the gap between the model that a designer builds and the model that an analysis needs.

The main idea is [isogeometric analysis](/isogeometric-analysis/), proposed by Hughes, Cottrell and Bazilevs in 2005: use the spline basis of the CAD model as the basis of the [finite element method](/finite-element-method/). The elements are the knot spans, refinement by knot insertion or degree elevation leaves the geometry unchanged, and the basis is smooth across elements. With $n$ elements of degree $p$, a spline of maximal smoothness has $n + p$ functions where a $C^0$ finite element space has $pn + 1$. The method needs new tools: operators that turn splines into the Bernstein form of a standard element code, quadrature rules for smooth spaces, error bounds for refined NURBS spaces, and splines that refine locally, because a tensor product grid refines whole rows at once.

The second half of the chapter deals with what CAD models really are. A CAD solid is a [boundary representation](/boundary-representation/) made of trimmed patches, not a spline map of its volume. One route builds such a map, which is still an open problem in general. Another analyzes the trimmed patches directly or immerses the part in a background grid, and then the small cut cells need a stabilization. The oldest route simplifies the model for meshing, by removing small features or by replacing a thin wall with its mid-surface.

The guarantees differ in kind. The convergence rates of isogeometric analysis are proved under assumptions on the mesh and the smoothness of the solution. T-splines are linearly independent when they are analysis-suitable, and general T-splines can be dependent. Trimmed and immersed methods need a stabilization to stay well conditioned. Defeaturing and mid-surfaces are geometric procedures, and their effect on the answer is judged case by case.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **The isogeometric idea.** Isogeometric analysis, [Bézier extraction](/bezier-extraction/), [k-refinement](/k-refinement/), [quadrature for isogeometric analysis](/isogeometric-quadrature/), the [approximation estimates for NURBS spaces](/nurbs-approximation-estimates/) and [isogeometric collocation](/isogeometric-collocation/).
2. **T-splines.** The [T-spline](/t-spline/), the [analysis-suitable T-spline](/analysis-suitable-t-spline/), the theorem on their [linear independence](/t-spline-linear-independence/) and their [local refinement](/t-spline-local-refinement/).
3. **Hierarchical and locally refined splines.** The [hierarchical B-spline](/hierarchical-b-spline/), the [truncated hierarchical B-spline](/truncated-hierarchical-b-spline/), the [LR B-spline](/lr-b-spline/) and the [PHT-spline](/pht-spline/).
4. **Volumes, trims and immersed methods.** [Volumetric spline parameterization](/volumetric-spline-parameterization/), [trimmed isogeometric analysis](/trimmed-isogeometric-analysis/), the [finite cell method](/finite-cell-method/), the [cut finite element method](/cut-finite-element-method/) and [immersogeometric analysis](/immersogeometric-analysis/).
5. **Shells, boundary elements and shape optimization.** The [isogeometric Kirchhoff-Love shell](/isogeometric-kirchhoff-love-shell/), [subdivision shell elements](/subdivision-shell-elements/), the [isogeometric boundary element method](/isogeometric-boundary-element-method/) and [isogeometric shape optimization](/isogeometric-shape-optimization/).
6. **Preparing CAD models for analysis.** [Defeaturing](/defeaturing/) and [mid-surface extraction](/midsurface-extraction/).

## A first reading

Take these twelve entries in order: isogeometric analysis, Bézier extraction, k-refinement, quadrature for isogeometric analysis, approximation estimates for NURBS spaces, the T-spline, the analysis-suitable T-spline, the hierarchical B-spline, the truncated hierarchical B-spline, trimmed isogeometric analysis, the finite cell method and defeaturing. They give the method and how a code runs it, the error bound, the two main ways to refine locally, the two ways to handle trimmed and immersed geometry, and the simplest model preparation.

Leave for later the proofs and the refinement algorithm for T-splines, LR B-splines and PHT-splines, which are other answers to the same question. The shells, the boundary element method, collocation, the cut finite element method, immersogeometric analysis, volumetric parameterization, shape optimization and mid-surfaces are research entries that build on the first reading.
