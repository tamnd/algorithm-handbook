## History

Model preparation came first. Rezayat named [mid-surface abstraction](/midsurface-extraction/) in 1996, and medial methods go back to Blum's [medial axis](/medial-axis/) of 1967. Thakur, Banerjee and Gupta surveyed model simplification for simulation in 2009. Gopalakrishnan and Suresh estimated the error of [defeaturing](/defeaturing/) in 2007, Li and Gao extended the estimate in 2011, and Buffa, Chanon and Vázquez made defeaturing analysis-aware in 2022.

Hughes, Cottrell and Bazilevs proposed [isogeometric analysis](/isogeometric-analysis/) in 2005 and wrote the first book on it in 2009. Bazilevs, Beirão da Veiga, Cottrell, Hughes and Sangalli proved the first [error estimates](/nurbs-approximation-estimates/) in 2006, and Cottrell, Hughes and Reali studied [k-refinement](/k-refinement/) in 2007. Hughes, Reali and Sangalli designed [quadrature](/isogeometric-quadrature/) for spline spaces in 2010, the year Auricchio and coauthors proposed [collocation](/isogeometric-collocation/). Borden, Scott, Evans and Hughes gave [Bézier extraction](/bezier-extraction/) in 2011, which let existing finite element codes run the method.

Local refinement has two lines. Sederberg, Zheng, Bakenov and Nasri introduced [T-splines](/t-spline/) in 2003 and a [local refinement](/t-spline-local-refinement/) in 2004, and Bazilevs and coauthors used them for analysis in 2010. That year Buffa, Cho and Sangalli found a T-mesh with [linearly dependent](/t-spline-linear-independence/) blending functions. Li, Zheng, Sederberg, Hughes and Scott answered with [analysis-suitable T-splines](/analysis-suitable-t-spline/) in 2012. The other line starts with Forsey and Bartels's [hierarchical B-splines](/hierarchical-b-spline/) of 1988 and Kraft's selection rule of 1997. Giannelli, Jüttler and Speleers added the [truncation](/truncated-hierarchical-b-spline/) in 2012, Deng and coauthors defined [PHT-splines](/pht-spline/) in 2008, and Dokken, Lyche and Pettersen [LR B-splines](/lr-b-spline/) in 2013.

Cirak, Ortiz and Schröder built [thin shell elements](/subdivision-shell-elements/) on Loop subdivision surfaces in 2000, before the name isogeometric existed. Kiendl, Bletzinger, Linhard and Wüchner gave the [isogeometric Kirchhoff-Love shell](/isogeometric-kirchhoff-love-shell/) in 2009. Politis and coauthors gave an [isogeometric boundary element method](/isogeometric-boundary-element-method/) in 2009, and Wall, Frenzel and Cyron introduced [isogeometric shape optimization](/isogeometric-shape-optimization/) in 2008.

Trimmed and immersed geometry came last. Parvizian, Düster and Rank introduced the [finite cell method](/finite-cell-method/) in 2007. Hansbo and Hansbo gave an unfitted method in 2002, Burman added the ghost penalty in 2010, and Burman, Claus, Hansbo, Larson and Massing set out [CutFEM](/cut-finite-element-method/) in 2015. Kim, Seo and Youn analyzed [trimmed surfaces](/trimmed-isogeometric-analysis/) in 2009, and Kamensky and coauthors applied [immersogeometric analysis](/immersogeometric-analysis/) to heart valves in 2015. Building a valid [spline map of a solid](/volumetric-spline-parameterization/), studied by Martin, Cohen and Kirby in 2009, is still hard.

## Further reading

- J. Austin Cottrell, Thomas J. R. Hughes and Yuri Bazilevs, Isogeometric Analysis: Toward Integration of CAD and FEA, Wiley, 2009. The method, refinement, shells and fluids, from its authors.
- L. Beirão da Veiga, A. Buffa, G. Sangalli and R. Vázquez, Mathematical analysis of variational isogeometric methods, Acta Numerica 23, 2014. The error estimates, T-splines and stability in one survey.
- B. Marussig and T. J. R. Hughes, A review of trimming in isogeometric analysis: challenges, data exchange and simulation aspects, Archives of Computational Methods in Engineering 25(4), 2018. What trimmed CAD models are and how analysis copes with them.
- D. Schillinger and M. Ruess, The finite cell method: a review in the context of higher-order structural analysis of CAD and image-based geometric models, Archives of Computational Methods in Engineering 22(3), 2015. The finite cell method with spline bases and weak boundary conditions.
- E. Burman, S. Claus, P. Hansbo, M. G. Larson and A. Massing, CutFEM: discretizing geometry and partial differential equations, International Journal for Numerical Methods in Engineering 104(7), 2015. The ghost penalty and its analysis in one framework.
- A. Thakur, A. G. Banerjee and S. K. Gupta, A survey of CAD model simplification techniques for physics-based simulation applications, Computer-Aided Design 41(2), 2009. Defeaturing, dimensional reduction and their tools.
