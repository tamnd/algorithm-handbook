## History

Euler stated $V - E + F = 2$ for polyhedra in 1752 and published a proof in 1758, and Poincaré proved the general relation from 1895 on. Solid modeling began in the early 1970s. Baumgart published the winged-edge structure with the first Euler operators in 1975, Braid, Hillyard and Stroud added rings and holes for the BUILD modeler in 1980, and Mäntylä proved in 1984 that the Euler operators reach every valid topology.

The theory of solids came from the Production Automation Project at the University of Rochester, led by Voelcker and Requicha. Requicha and Tilove defined the regularized set operations in 1978. Requicha's survey of 1980 defined r-sets and compared the representations, sweeps among them, and Tilove made set membership classification the common tool the same year. Roth rendered CSG trees by ray casting and Meagher used octrees in 1982. Requicha and Voelcker described boundary evaluation in 1985, Mäntylä gave Booleans by vertex neighborhood classification in 1986, and Weiler's radial edge structure of 1986 allowed non-manifold boundaries. For sets that are not regular, Nef defined his polyhedra in 1978, Rossignac and O'Connor proposed the selective geometric complex in 1989, and Hachenberger, Kettner and Mehlhorn published exact Nef polyhedra in three dimensions in 2007.

Lane and Riesenfeld gave subdivision of polynomial surfaces in 1980, Barnhill, Farin, Jordan and Piper a marching method in 1987, and Sederberg and Meyers their loop test in 1988. Rossignac and Requicha defined offsets of solids in 1986 as Minkowski sums with a ball, and Choi and Ju gave the rolling-ball blend in 1989. Fogel and Halperin built sums of convex polyhedra exactly in 2007, and Hachenberger published the nonconvex case, through Nef polyhedra, in 2009. Sherbrooke, Patrikalakis and Brisson traced the medial surface of a polyhedron in 1996. Mirtich published exact mass properties of polyhedra in 1996.

In 1989 Hoffmann separated accuracy from robustness and Hoffmann, Hopcroft and Karasick gave set operations with consistent decisions. Edelsbrunner and Mücke published simulation of simplicity in 1990, and Yap set out exact geometric computation in 1997, as Shewchuk gave adaptive predicates. Segal used tolerances to keep polyhedral results valid in 1990, and Jackson gave each edge and vertex its own tolerance in 1995. Bøhn and Wozny closed the shells of imported models in 1992, and Barequet with Sharir in 1995 and with Kumar in 1997 filled their gaps.

Features came last. Woo proposed the alternating sum of volumes in 1982, Tang and Woo studied in 1991 why it may not end, and Kim and Wilde added partitioning in 1992 so that it always ends. Joshi and Chang introduced the attributed adjacency graph in 1988, Vandenbrande and Requicha published hint-based recognition in 1993, Tseng and Joshi cut the removal volume into blocks in 1994, and Sakurai into maximal convex cells in 1995. Shapiro and Vossler studied the conversion from boundaries to CSG in the early 1990s, and Pasko and coauthors described function representation in 1995. History-based modelers raised the persistent naming problem. Capoyleas, Chen and Hoffmann published generic naming in 1996, and Marcheix and Pierra argued in 2002 that every known scheme fails on some edits. Direct modeling edits the boundary with no history, and Zou and Feng treated its push-pull edits with topology changes in 2019.

## Further reading

- Aristides A. G. Requicha, Representations for rigid solids, theory, methods, and systems, ACM Computing Surveys 12(4), 1980. The definitions of r-sets and the comparison of the representations.
- Martti Mäntylä, An Introduction to Solid Modeling, Computer Science Press, 1988. Boundary structures, Euler operators and Boolean operations, with code.
- Christoph M. Hoffmann, Geometric and Solid Modeling, An Introduction, Morgan Kaufmann, 1989. Booleans, robustness and surface intersection.
- Jami J. Shah and Martti Mäntylä, Parametric and Feature-Based CAD/CAM, Wiley, 1995. Features, recognition and naming.
- JungHyun Han, Mike Pratt and William C. Regli, Manufacturing feature recognition from solid models, a status report, IEEE Transactions on Robotics and Automation 16(6), 2000. Graph-based, hint-based and volumetric recognition compared.
- Lutz Kettner, Kurt Mehlhorn, Sylvain Pion, Stefan Schirra and Chee Yap, Classroom examples of robustness problems in geometric computations, Computational Geometry 40(1), 2008. Small inputs that break floating-point geometry.
