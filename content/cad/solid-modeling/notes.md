## History

The counting rule is the oldest part. Euler stated $V - E + F = 2$ for polyhedra in 1752 and published a proof in 1758, and Poincaré proved the general relation between cells and Betti numbers in his papers on analysis situs from 1895 on. Solid modeling as a subject began in the early 1970s. Baumgart designed the winged-edge structure for computer vision and published it in 1975, with the first Euler operators. Braid, Hillyard and Stroud wrote the operators with rings and holes for the BUILD modeler in 1980, and Mäntylä built the GWB modeler with Sulonen in 1982 and proved in 1984 that the Euler operators reach every valid topology.

The mathematics of solids came from the Production Automation Project at the University of Rochester, led by Voelcker and Requicha, whose PADL systems were built on CSG. Requicha and Tilove defined the regularized set operations in 1978. Requicha's survey of 1980 defined r-sets and compared the representations, and Tilove's paper of the same year made set membership classification the common tool. Requicha and Voelcker described boundary evaluation in 1985. Roth rendered CSG trees by ray casting in 1982, and Meagher used octrees for solids in the same year. Mäntylä gave Booleans on manifold boundaries by vertex neighborhood classification in 1986, Weiler described edge-based structures for curved surfaces in 1985 and the radial edge structure in his thesis of 1986, and Mäntylä's book of 1988 collected the field.

Curved faces brought numerical analysis in. Lane and Riesenfeld gave subdivision of polynomial surfaces in 1980, Barnhill, Farin, Jordan and Piper gave a marching method for surface intersection in 1987, and Sederberg and Meyers proved their loop test in 1988. Rossignac and Requicha defined offsetting of solids in 1986, and Choi and Ju gave the rolling-ball blend in 1989. On mass properties, Lee and Requicha compared the methods in 1982, and Mirtich published his exact formulas for polyhedra in 1996.

Robustness became a field of its own around 1989, when Hoffmann's paper separated accuracy from robustness and Hoffmann, Hopcroft and Karasick gave set operations with consistent decisions. Edelsbrunner and Mücke published simulation of simplicity in 1990. Yap set out exact geometric computation in 1997, the same year that Shewchuk gave adaptive exact predicates, and Kettner, Mehlhorn, Pion, Schirra and Yap collected classroom examples of the failures in 2008.

Features came last. Joshi and Chang introduced the attributed adjacency graph in 1988, Shah assessed feature technology in 1991, and Vandenbrande and Requicha gave hint-based recognition in 1993. Pasko and coauthors described function representation in 1995, building on Rvachev's R-functions, and Shapiro and Vossler studied the conversion from boundaries to CSG in the early 1990s. The persistent naming problem appeared with history-based parametric modelers and is still open.

## Further reading

- Aristides A. G. Requicha, Representations for rigid solids, theory, methods, and systems, ACM Computing Surveys 12(4), 1980. The definitions of r-sets and the comparison of the representations.
- Martti Mäntylä, An Introduction to Solid Modeling, Computer Science Press, 1988. Boundary structures, Euler operators and Boolean operations, with code.
- Christoph M. Hoffmann, Geometric and Solid Modeling, An Introduction, Morgan Kaufmann, 1989. Booleans, robustness and surface intersection.
- Nicholas M. Patrikalakis and Takashi Maekawa, Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and Manufacturing, Springer, 2002. Surface intersection and offsets in detail.
- Jami J. Shah and Martti Mäntylä, Parametric and Feature-Based CAD/CAM, Wiley, 1995. Features, recognition and naming.
- Lutz Kettner, Kurt Mehlhorn, Sylvain Pion, Stefan Schirra and Chee Yap, Classroom examples of robustness problems in geometric computations, Computational Geometry 40(1), 2008. Small inputs that break floating-point geometry.
