A designer draws a bracket. She sketches a profile of four points with five distances between them, and the sketch is rigid at almost every placement because $5 = 2 \cdot 4 - 3$ and no three points carry more than three of the distances. She extrudes the profile, subtracts two holes and rounds the edges, and the modeler turns these steps into faces, edges and vertices whose counts satisfy $V - E + F - R = 2(S - H)$. The part goes out as a triangle mesh with a few cracks and flipped triangles, which must be repaired before anything else can use it. A slicer then cuts it into layers for a printer, or a CAM system computes the path of a milling cutter around it. Each of these steps is an algorithm, and each can fail on inputs that look harmless.

This volume is about those algorithms. Its ten chapters cover the life of a mechanical part from sketch to machined or printed object. In this edition four of them are written, with 125 entries: solid modeling, geometric constraint solving, mesh processing and repair, and CAM and additive manufacturing. The other six chapters are not written yet; their opening pages say what they will cover and which written entries to read meanwhile.

## Why CAD gets a volume of its own

CAD uses the geometry of Volume 13, the curves and surfaces of Volume 28 and the numerical methods of Volume 12, but it asks them to work together on real parts, with tolerances, and without failing. A few ideas come back in every chapter:

- **A solid is a set, and a model is a finite description of it.** Trees of operations, boundaries, meshes, voxels and constraint systems are different descriptions of one shape. Much of the volume converts between them and checks that a description is valid.
- **Exact proofs meet rounded arithmetic.** Most geometric algorithms are proved on the [real RAM](/real-ram/). With floating point, an [orientation test](/orientation-test/) can contradict itself and a Boolean operation can return an open boundary. The volume says which results hold only with exact predicates.
- **Numerical methods stop at a tolerance.** [Marching](/marching-surface-intersection/) methods for surface intersection can miss a small closed loop unless a loop test is added. [Subdivision](/subdivision-surface-intersection/) finds every piece but stops at a tolerance. Each such guarantee says which one it is.
- **Generic is not always.** Rigidity results such as [Laman's theorem](/laman-theorem/) hold for almost every placement of the points, not for every placement. A sketch that is rigid in general can flex at a special one.
- **Much quality is measured, not proved.** Toolpaths, slicing, support structures, remeshing and feature recognition are judged by length, time, surface error or success on test parts. The entries say so, and they keep proved bounds apart from measured results.

## The chapters

**Chapter 31.1, Solid modeling**, is written. It defines solids as [r-sets](/r-set/) and covers [CSG](/constructive-solid-geometry/), [boundary representations](/boundary-representation/), the [winged edge](/winged-edge-data-structure/) and the [Euler-Poincaré formula](/euler-poincare-formula/); [set membership classification](/set-membership-classification/), [boundary evaluation](/boundary-evaluation/) and [Boolean operations](/solid-boolean-operations/); [surface-surface intersection](/surface-surface-intersection/) for curved faces; offsets and [blends](/blending-surface/); the [robustness problem](/geometric-robustness-problem/) and [exact geometric computation](/exact-geometric-computation/); [mass properties](/mass-properties-of-solids/); and [feature recognition](/feature-recognition/) and the [persistent naming problem](/persistent-naming-problem/).

**Chapter 31.2, Surface interrogation and design**, is not written yet. It will cover curvature and fairing of freeform surfaces, special surfaces such as developable surfaces and Pythagorean hodograph curves, patches with $n$ sides and the continuity between patches, and point projection and intersection problems on parametric surfaces. Meanwhile, read [surface curvature](/surface-curvature/), [parametric continuity](/parametric-continuity/) and [Bézier clipping](/bezier-clipping/) in Volume 28.

**Chapter 31.3, Geometric constraint solving**, is written. It starts from the [geometric constraint](/geometric-constraint/) and the [solving problem](/geometric-constraint-problem/), which is NP-hard in general, and covers [variational geometry](/variational-geometry/), [generic rigidity](/generic-rigidity/) and Laman's theorem with the $O(n^2)$ [pebble game](/pebble-game-rigidity/) that tests it, decomposition solvers such as the [Fudos-Hoffmann reduction](/fudos-hoffmann-reduction/), the [root identification problem](/root-identification-problem/) of choosing among exponentially many solutions, and [assembly constraint solving](/assembly-constraint-solving/).

**Chapter 31.4, Mechanism and assembly design**, is not written yet. It will cover linkages and their synthesis, cams and gears, and the planning of assembly and disassembly sequences. Meanwhile, the constraint solvers and assembly constraints of Chapter 31.3 are the nearest written material.

**Chapter 31.5, Mesh processing and repair**, is written. It covers [indexed face sets](/indexed-face-set/) and [manifold meshes](/manifold-mesh/), the [mesh repair problem](/mesh-repair-problem/) and [hole filling](/hole-filling/), the [generalized winding number](/generalized-winding-number/) and [mesh Boolean operations](/mesh-boolean-operations/), smoothing with the [cotangent Laplacian](/cotangent-laplacian/), discrete curvature and the [discrete Gauss-Bonnet theorem](/discrete-gauss-bonnet-theorem/), geodesics with the [MMP algorithm](/mmp-geodesic-algorithm/) and the [heat method](/heat-method/), and [mesh parameterization](/mesh-parameterization/) from the [Tutte embedding](/tutte-embedding/) on.

**Chapter 31.6, Mesh generation for analysis**, is not written yet. It will cover triangle and tetrahedral meshes by Delaunay refinement and advancing fronts, quadrilateral and hexahedral meshing, mesh quality measures, and smoothing and untangling. Meanwhile, read [Delaunay triangulation](/delaunay-triangulation/), [constrained Delaunay triangulation](/constrained-delaunay-triangulation/) and [Ruppert's algorithm](/ruppert-algorithm/) in Volume 13, and the [quadtree](/quadtree/) and [octree](/octree/) in Volume 3.

**Chapter 31.7, Isogeometric analysis and CAD-CAE integration**, is not written yet. It will cover analysis with the spline bases of the design itself, T-splines and hierarchical splines with local refinement, trimmed and immersed geometry, and the cleanup of models for simulation. Meanwhile, read [B-splines](/b-spline/) and [NURBS](/nurbs/) in Volume 28 and the [finite element method](/finite-element-method/) in Volume 12.

**Chapter 31.8, CAM and additive manufacturing**, is written. It covers [slicing](/slicing-for-additive-manufacturing/) and the [sweep](/triangle-mesh-slicing/) that slices a mesh in $O(n \log k + k + m)$ expected time, build orientation and [support structures](/support-structure-generation/), [toolpath planning](/toolpath-planning/), [scallop height](/scallop-height/) and [pocket machining](/pocket-machining/), [NC verification](/nc-verification/) of a [swept volume](/swept-volume/), [tolerance stack-up](/tolerance-stack-up/), the [nesting problem](/nesting-problem/), which is NP-hard in the strong sense, and [topology optimization](/topology-optimization/) with the [SIMP method](/simp-method/).

**Chapter 31.9, Manufacturing process planning**, is not written yet. It will cover the choice and order of machining operations and setups, fixture design, sheet-metal bending, and casting and molding checks. Meanwhile, read the [attributed adjacency graph](/attributed-adjacency-graph/) in Chapter 31.1 and [mold parting directions](/mold-parting-direction/) and the [accessibility cone](/accessibility-cone/) in Chapter 31.8.

**Chapter 31.10, Reverse engineering, inspection and tolerancing**, is not written yet. It will cover fitting planes, spheres, cylinders and freeform surfaces to measured points, segmentation of scans and B-rep reconstruction, form error by minimum zone fitting, and tolerance models and analysis. Meanwhile, read the [least squares problem](/least-squares-problem/) in Volume 12, [mesh segmentation](/mesh-segmentation/) and the [Metro mesh comparison](/metro-mesh-comparison/) in Chapter 31.5, and tolerance stack-up in Chapter 31.8.

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: r-set, constructive solid geometry, boundary representation, the Euler-Poincaré formula, set membership classification, the geometric robustness problem, geometric constraint, generic rigidity, Laman's theorem, manifold mesh, mesh repair, slicing for additive manufacturing, toolpath planning and pocket machining. Do the exercises marked ◦ at the end of each chapter you touch.

If you already know the basics, start with the opening page of each chapter. It names the groups in the chapter and the order to read them. The four written chapters can be read in any order. Chapter 31.1 gives the vocabulary of solids that the others use, Chapter 31.5 repairs the meshes that Chapter 31.8 slices and machines, and Chapter 31.3 stands closest to graph theory.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.

## What you need first

The geometry comes from Volume 13: the orientation test, [general position](/general-position/), [Minkowski sums](/minkowski-sum/), the [Voronoi diagram](/voronoi-diagram/) and the [straight skeleton](/straight-skeleton/). Curves, surfaces and meshes come from Volume 28: [Bézier curves](/bezier-curve/), B-splines, NURBS, [implicit surfaces](/implicit-surface/) and the [polygon mesh](/polygon-mesh/). From Volume 12 you need [floating-point arithmetic](/floating-point-arithmetic/), [Newton's method for systems](/newton-method-for-systems/) and sparse linear systems. Volume 7 gives the [doubly connected edge list](/doubly-connected-edge-list/), [Euler's formula](/euler-formula/) and [spanning trees](/spanning-tree/), and Volume 18 gives [bin packing](/bin-packing/), the base case of nesting.

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely; you should be able to do them in a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use an idea in a new place; each takes some thought, and some need a short program. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some are results from research papers. Hints for many exercises are in the appendix *Hints to the exercises* at the end of the volume.
