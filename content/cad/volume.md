A designer draws a bracket. She sketches a profile of four points with five distances between them, and the sketch is rigid at almost every placement because $5 = 2 \cdot 4 - 3$ and no three points carry more than three of the distances. She extrudes the profile, subtracts two holes and rounds the edges, and the modeler turns these steps into faces, edges and vertices. An engineer fills the part with tetrahedra for a stress analysis. The part goes out as a triangle mesh with a few cracks and flipped triangles that must be repaired. A planner groups its holes into setups and chooses a fixture, and a CAM system computes the path of a milling cutter around it. Last, a measuring machine probes the finished part, and a program fits planes and cylinders to the points to decide whether it is within tolerance. Each of these steps is an algorithm, and each can fail on harmless-looking inputs.

This volume is about those algorithms. Its ten chapters, with 374 entries, follow the life of a mechanical part. The first four describe the design: the solid, its freeform surfaces, the constraints of its sketches, and the mechanisms and assemblies it belongs to. The next three prepare it for computation: meshes and their repair, meshes for analysis, and analysis on the splines of the design. The last three make and check it: machining and printing, process planning, and inspection and tolerancing.

## Why CAD gets a volume of its own

CAD uses the geometry of Volume 13, the curves and surfaces of Volume 28 and the numerical methods of Volume 12, but it asks them to work together on real parts, with tolerances, and without failing. A few ideas come back in every chapter:

- **A solid is a set, and a model is a finite description of it.** Trees of operations, boundaries, meshes, splines and constraint systems describe one shape in different ways.
- **Exact proofs meet rounded arithmetic.** Most geometric algorithms are proved on the [real RAM](/real-ram/). With floating point, [orientation tests](/orientation-test/) can contradict each other.
- **Numerical methods need a good start or stop at a tolerance.** Newton's method finds the nearest point of a surface only from a start near it, marching can miss a small loop of an intersection, and subdivision finds every piece but only to a tolerance.
- **Generic is not always.** [Laman's theorem](/laman-theorem/) and the theorems on global rigidity hold for almost every placement, not for every one.
- **Exact for a model, or measured.** Burmester's curves, blocking graphs for assembly and the rank test for fixtures are exact for their models. Hexahedral meshers, toolpaths and process plans are judged by measurement, and Chvorinov's rule, Taylor's tool-life equation and the K-factor are empirical laws with fitted constants.

## The chapters

**Chapter 31.1, Solid modeling**, defines solids as [r-sets](/r-set/) and covers [CSG](/constructive-solid-geometry/), [boundary representations](/boundary-representation/) and the [Euler-Poincaré formula](/euler-poincare-formula/). It goes on to [set membership classification](/set-membership-classification/), [Boolean operations](/solid-boolean-operations/) and [surface-surface intersection](/surface-surface-intersection/) by [marching](/marching-surface-intersection/) and [subdivision](/subdivision-surface-intersection/). The [robustness problem](/geometric-robustness-problem/) is met by [exact geometric computation](/exact-geometric-computation/), which makes every predicate exact, or by [tolerant modeling](/tolerant-modeling/), which stores a tolerance on each edge and vertex.

**Chapter 31.2, Surface interrogation and design**, works on freeform faces. [Isophotes](/isophote/) and [reflection lines](/reflection-lines/) show breaks in continuity but do not measure them, and [energy-based fairing](/energy-based-surface-fairing/) is fair only in the sense of its energy. [Newton point inversion](/newton-point-inversion/) converges quadratically near the foot point but finds the nearest point only from a fine enough start grid. [Lattice evaluation](/lattice-evaluation-intersection/) misses loops inside one cell, the [Sederberg-Meyers test](/sederberg-meyers-loop-detection/) rules loops out, and [tangential intersections](/tangential-surface-intersection/) defeat marching. [Implicitization](/implicitization/) is exact, but a bicubic patch has an implicit degree up to 18.

**Chapter 31.3, Geometric constraint solving**, starts from the [solving problem](/geometric-constraint-problem/), NP-hard already for distances on a line. Its theory is rigidity: [generic rigidity](/generic-rigidity/), Laman's theorem and the $O(n^2)$ [pebble game](/pebble-game-rigidity/), the [Asimow-Roth theorem](/asimow-roth-theorem/), which makes rigidity a rank test at regular placements, the [double banana](/double-banana-graph/), on which counting fails in space, and global rigidity, which [Connelly](/connelly-global-rigidity-theorem/) and [Gortler, Healy and Thurston](/gortler-healy-thurston-theorem/) settled for generic placements. Solvers decompose the constraint graph and still face the [root identification problem](/root-identification-problem/).

**Chapter 31.4, Mechanism and assembly design**, covers the [four-bar linkage](/four-bar-linkage/) and its [Grashof condition](/grashof-condition/), cams and [gears](/fundamental-law-of-gearing/). Synthesis at precision points is exact: [Freudenstein's equation](/freudenstein-equation/) gives three-point function generation by one linear solve, and [Burmester theory](/burmester-theory/) gives at most four Burmester points for five positions. [Optimization-based synthesis](/optimization-based-linkage-synthesis/) finds local optima. Assembly planning uses the [AND/OR graph](/and-or-graph-of-assembly-plans/); [assembly partitioning](/assembly-partitioning/) is NP-complete in the plane for general motions, and the [non-directional blocking graph](/non-directional-blocking-graph/) solves it in polynomial time for one translation.

**Chapter 31.5, Mesh processing and repair**, covers [manifold meshes](/manifold-mesh/), [mesh repair](/mesh-repair-problem/), which is ill-posed because the input does not say what shape was meant, the [generalized winding number](/generalized-winding-number/), [mesh Booleans](/mesh-boolean-operations/), the [cotangent Laplacian](/cotangent-laplacian/) and geodesics, exact by the [MMP algorithm](/mmp-geodesic-algorithm/) and approximate by the [heat method](/heat-method/).

**Chapter 31.6, Mesh generation for analysis**, makes elements a solver can use, rated by [quality measures](/mesh-quality-measures/). [Ruppert's algorithm](/ruppert-algorithm/) in Volume 13 and [Shewchuk's tetrahedral refinement](/tetrahedral-delaunay-refinement/) have proved bounds when input angles are not too small, but a radius-edge bound lets [slivers](/sliver-tetrahedron/) through, and [sliver exudation](/sliver-exudation/) is needed. [Isosurface stuffing](/isosurface-stuffing/) has proved dihedral angle bounds. [Advancing front](/advancing-front-mesh-generation/), [paving](/paving/), [Q-Morph](/q-morph/), [whisker weaving](/whisker-weaving/) and [frame-field hex meshing](/frame-field-hex-meshing/) are heuristics whose quality is measured. The [hexahedral existence theorem](/hexahedral-mesh-existence-theorem/) is topological: it gives some hex mesh, not a good one.

**Chapter 31.7, Isogeometric analysis and CAD-CAE integration**, runs the [finite element method](/finite-element-method/) on the splines of the design. [Isogeometric analysis](/isogeometric-analysis/) converges at the optimal rate under the shape-regularity and smoothness assumptions of its [approximation estimates](/nurbs-approximation-estimates/). [T-splines](/t-spline/) refine locally, and their blending functions are [linearly independent](/t-spline-linear-independence/) when the T-mesh is [analysis-suitable](/analysis-suitable-t-spline/). [Trimmed analysis](/trimmed-isogeometric-analysis/) and [CutFEM](/cut-finite-element-method/) work on cut elements and need stabilization. [Volumetric parameterization](/volumetric-spline-parameterization/) builds a spline volume inside a B-rep, the main gap between design and analysis.

**Chapter 31.8, CAM and additive manufacturing**, covers [slicing](/slicing-for-additive-manufacturing/), with a [mesh slicing sweep](/triangle-mesh-slicing/) in $O(n \log k + k + m)$ expected time, [support structures](/support-structure-generation/), [toolpath planning](/toolpath-planning/), [pocket machining](/pocket-machining/), [five-axis tool orientation](/five-axis-tool-orientation/) and [NC verification](/nc-verification/), all judged by measured time and surface error. The [nesting problem](/nesting-problem/) is NP-hard in the strong sense, and [topology optimization](/topology-optimization/) by the [SIMP method](/simp-method/) finds a local optimum.

**Chapter 31.9, Manufacturing process planning**, decides how the part is made. [Setup planning](/setup-planning/) has set cover at its core and is NP-hard, [operation sequencing](/operation-sequencing/) is a traveling salesman problem with precedence, and [A* bend sequencing](/a-star-bend-sequencing/) is exact for its cost model. The [3-2-1 principle](/three-two-one-locating-principle/) and [kinematic fixture analysis](/kinematic-fixture-analysis/) are exact rank tests for frictionless point locators. The [K-factor](/k-factor/), [Chvorinov's rule](/chvorinov-rule/) and the [Taylor tool-life equation](/taylor-tool-life-equation/) are empirical.

**Chapter 31.10, Reverse engineering, inspection and tolerancing**, checks the part. [Form error evaluation](/form-error-evaluation/) is a minimax problem: [minimum zone flatness](/minimum-zone-flatness/) is exact, [minimum zone cylindricity](/minimum-zone-cylindricity/) finds a local optimum, and the [least-squares feature](/least-squares-reference-feature/), exact for its own objective, never gives a narrower zone. [Localization](/inspection-localization/) without point pairs reaches a local minimum, and [algebraic distance fitting](/algebraic-distance-fitting/) is biased under noise, which [geometric fitting](/geometric-primitive-fitting/) corrects. The worst-case [stack-up](/tolerance-stack-up/) gives the exact range, so it says whether every assembly works; [Monte Carlo analysis](/monte-carlo-tolerance-analysis/) estimates how many do.

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: r-set, constructive solid geometry, boundary representation, the geometric robustness problem, [point projection onto a surface](/point-projection-onto-a-surface/), [geometric constraint](/geometric-constraint/), Laman's theorem, the four-bar linkage, [assembly sequence planning](/assembly-sequence-planning/), mesh repair, mesh quality measures, isogeometric analysis, slicing for additive manufacturing, toolpath planning, the 3-2-1 locating principle and form error evaluation. Do the exercises marked ◦ and • at the end of each chapter you touch.

If you already know the basics, start with the opening page of a chapter. It names the groups in the chapter and which results are proved and which are measured.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.

## What you need first

The geometry comes from Volume 13: [general position](/general-position/), [convex hulls](/convex-hull/), the [Delaunay triangulation](/delaunay-triangulation/), the [Voronoi diagram](/voronoi-diagram/) and the [medial axis](/medial-axis/). Curves and surfaces come from Volume 28: [Bézier curves](/bezier-curve/), [B-splines](/b-spline/), [NURBS](/nurbs/), the [fundamental forms](/fundamental-forms/) and [curvature](/surface-curvature/) of a surface, and the [polygon mesh](/polygon-mesh/). From Volume 12 you need [floating-point arithmetic](/floating-point-arithmetic/), [Newton's method for systems](/newton-method-for-systems/), [linear least squares](/least-squares-problem/) and [interval arithmetic](/interval-arithmetic/). Volume 11 gives the [resultant](/resultant/), Volume 15 [linear programming](/linear-programming-problem/) and the [traveling salesman problem](/traveling-salesman-problem/).

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely; you should be able to do them in a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use an idea in a new place; each takes some thought, and some need a short program. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some are results from research papers. Hints for many exercises are in the appendix *Hints to the exercises* at the end of the volume.
