1. $r - p = (\cos t - 2, \sin t)$ and $r' = (-\sin t, \cos t)$, so the product is $2 \sin t$, with derivative $2 \cos t$, and the Newton step is $t - \tan t$. From $t_0 = 1$ the run is $-0.5574$, $0.0659$, $-0.0001$, and it tends to $0$, the foot point $(1, 0)$ at distance $1$. From $t_0 = 2$ the run is $4.185$, $2.468$, $3.266$, and it tends to $\pi$, the point $(-1, 0)$ at distance $3$. That is the farthest point: Newton finds stationary points of the distance, not only minima.

2. On $y = x^2$ the curvature at the origin is $2$, against $0$ on the line, so the tip curve jumps by $2s$ and the join is $G^1$ only. On $y = x^3$ the curvature $6x / (1 + 9x^4)^{3/2}$ tends to $0$, so the tip curve is continuous and the join is $G^2$; it is even $C^2$ as a graph, since $y'' = 6x \to 0$.

3. The outward normal is $N = (x, 0, z)$, so $N \cdot l = z = c$: the two lines $z = c$, $x = \pm \sqrt{1 - c^2}$, with $x = \pm 0.436$, $\pm 0.600$ and $\pm 0.714$. Seen from above, the lines crowd toward the silhouette $x = \pm 1$. For any $l$, $N \cdot l = x l_1 + z l_3$ does not depend on $y$, so each isophote is a union of rulings. On a developable surface the tangent plane, and so the normal, is constant along each ruling, which gives the same result.

4. For the first patch $b_u(0, v) = (1, -1, v)$, which has a $z$ component, while $a_u = (1, 0, 0)$ and $c' = (0, 1, 0)$ do not. So condition (1) holds only at $v = 0$. The normal of $b$ on the edge is $(-v, 0, 1)$, which leans by the angle $\arctan v$. For the second patch $b_u(0, v) = (3, 2, 0) = 3 a_u + 2 c'$, so $\alpha = 3 > 0$ and $\beta = 2$. The patch $b(u/3,\ v - 2u/3) = (1 + u,\ v,\ u^2/9)$ joins $a$ with $C^1$ continuity.

5. From the first equation $t^2 = x + 1$, so $y = t(t^2 - 1) = t x$ and $y^2 = t^2 x^2 = x^2 (x + 1)$. The resultant gives the same polynomial up to a constant factor. The parameters $t = 1$ and $t = -1$ both map to the origin, which is a node. The tangent vectors $(2t, 3t^2 - 1)$ are $(2, 2)$ and $(-2, 2)$ there, so the tangent lines are $y = x$ and $y = -x$, as $y^2 \approx x^2$ near the origin also shows.

6. The slant height is $5$, so the sector has radius $5$ and angle $2\pi \cdot 3/5 = 6\pi/5$, that is $216°$. The cut at height $2$ is a circle of radius $3/2$ at slant distance $5/2$ from the apex. The lower part unrolls to the annular sector between radii $5/2$ and $5$ with the same angle. Its arcs have lengths $3\pi$ and $6\pi$, the two circumferences.

7. The legs are $L_1 = (1, 0)$, $L_2 = (1, 1)$, $L_3 = (0, 2)$. Then $|L_2| = \sqrt 2 = \sqrt{|L_1| |L_3|}$, and the angle from $L_1$ to $L_2$ and from $L_2$ to $L_3$ is $45°$ each, so the curve is PH. With $r' = w^2$ in complex form, $w_0^2 = 3$, $w_0 w_1 = 3(1 + i)$ and $w_1^2 = 6i$, and $|r'| = |w|^2 = 3 + 3t^2$. The arc length is $\int_0^1 (3 + 3t^2) \, dt = 4$. The control polygon has length $3 + \sqrt 2 \approx 4.414$, and the chord $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.606$.

8. With $\alpha_1 = 30°$, $\alpha_2 = 0$ and $\varphi = 40°$, $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 < \varphi$, so the test excludes a loop. With $\varphi = 20°$ the sum $30°$ is not below $\varphi$, so the test fails. Let $a_2$ be the normal and take the plane $x \cdot a_2 = \cos 5°$. It cuts the sphere in a circle of angular radius $5°$ about $a_2$, and each point of the circle is within $25° < 30°$ of $(0, 0, 1)$, so the circle lies in the cap and is a closed loop.

9. For $n = 6$ and $d = 3$, $l = \lceil d/2 \rceil = 2$, and the generalized Bézier patch has $n \cdot l (d + 1 - l) + 1 = 6 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 25$ control points. The S-patch has $\binom{d + n - 1}{n - 1} = \binom{8}{5} = 56$. The generalized Bézier patch needs fewer than half as many.

12. A line $u = k/N$ finds the loop when $k/N$ lies strictly inside $[\tfrac12 - \tfrac1{30}, \tfrac12 + \tfrac1{30}]$, and the lines in $v$ behave the same way. For even $N$, $k/N = \tfrac12$ works. For odd $N$ the nearest value is at the distance $1/(2N)$ from $\tfrac12$, which is less than $1/30$ only for $N \ge 17$. For $N = 15$ the lines $u = 7/15$ and $u = 8/15$ touch the loop at one point each, where $g$ has a double root and no sign change, so a sign test misses the loop. The loop is found for even $N$ and for $N = 17$ and $19$.

17. With $\kappa = 2 / (1 + 4x^2)^{3/2}$, the cusps are where $d\kappa = 1$, at $x = \pm \tfrac12 \sqrt{2^{2/3} - 1} \approx \pm 0.3832$, and they are the points $(\mp 0.2251, 0.9406)$. The offset point of $x$ is $(x - 2x/q,\ x^2 + 1/q)$ with $q = \sqrt{1 + 4x^2}$, so it is on the axis when $q = 2$, at $x = \pm \sqrt 3 / 2$, both giving $(0, 5/4)$. Trimming removes the swallowtail, the piece with $|x| < \sqrt 3 / 2$.

21. Write the hodograph as $w^2$ with $w$ a complex polynomial of degree $1$, so $w = c(t - t_0)$. If $t_0$ is real, $w^2$ has a fixed direction and the curve is a line. Otherwise substitute $t = \operatorname{Re} t_0 + s \operatorname{Im} t_0$, and note that multiplying by a complex constant, and conjugating, are similarities.

22. Unroll the cone and lay copies of the sector side by side, turned by multiples of $\Theta$. A geodesic is a straight segment from the first point to a copy of the second, and it stays on the surface only if the angle between them is less than $\pi$. Here $k = 0$ gives the ruling segment of length $1$, and $k = \pm 1$ give two paths around the apex of length $\sqrt{1 + 4 - 4 \cos(\pi/2)} = \sqrt 5$.

23. Use $\gamma' = (1 - d\kappa) c'$ with $1 - d\kappa > 0$: the offset has the same unit tangent as $c$, its curvature is $\kappa / (1 - d\kappa) > 0$, and its tangent turns once. A closed curve with positive curvature and total turning $2\pi$ is simple and convex. On the ellipse the largest curvature is $2$, at $(\pm 2, 0)$, so $d < 1/2$, and the first cusps appear at $(\pm 3/2, 0)$.

24. Differentiate $D = \tfrac12 |r - p|^2$ twice: $D_{uu} = r_u \cdot r_u + (r - p) \cdot r_{uu}$, and $r - p = -hN$ at the foot point. For the saddle at the origin, $E = G = 1$, $F = 0$, the second form has the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, so the principal curvatures are $\pm 1$ and the Hessian $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -h \\ -h & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is positive definite exactly when $|h| < 1$.
