## History

The design side came first, from the car and aircraft industries of the 1960s. Coons described his patches at MIT, and Gordon, at General Motors Research, extended them in 1969 to surfaces through a whole network of curves. Gregory introduced his rational corner blend in 1974, so that a patch could match cross-boundary data whose twists disagree. Chiyokura and Kimura gave its form with twenty control points in 1983, and used it to change one patch of a network without touching its neighbors.

Intersection became an algorithmic problem with the solid modelers of the 1970s and 1980s. Levin gave his method for two quadrics in 1976. Sederberg, Anderson and Goldman brought resultants to implicitization in 1984, and Sederberg and Chen replaced them with moving lines in 1995. Rossignac and Requicha, and Lien and others, reduced a surface intersection to curve intersections along lattice lines in 1987. Sederberg and Meyers gave the normal cone test for closed loops in 1988, Patrikalakis and Prakash traced the intersection of a patch with an algebraic surface in 1990, and Sherbrooke and Patrikalakis gave the interval projected polyhedron solver in 1993.

Interrogation by light began on the car body. Klass introduced reflection lines in 1980 to find and correct irregularities, and Poeschl used isophotes in 1984. Hagen, Hahmann and coauthors surveyed the methods in 1992, and Maekawa, Wolter and Patrikalakis treated umbilics and lines of curvature in 1996. Fairing became a computation in the same period. Farin, Rein, Sapidis and Worsey removed and reinserted knots in 1987, and Moreton and Séquin minimized the variation of curvature in 1992.

The special curves and the many-sided patches are more recent. Farouki and Sakkalis introduced Pythagorean hodograph curves in 1990, building on a result of Kubota from 1972, and Farouki and Neff solved Hermite interpolation with PH quintics in 1995. Gregory filled a pentagon in 1984, Loop and DeRose gave S-patches in 1989, Várady and Rockwood gave setback vertex blends in 1997, and Várady, Salvi and Karikó gave generalized Bézier patches in 2016. Pottmann and Wallner put developable surfaces on the basis of line geometry around 1999, and the approximation of shapes by developable pieces is still an active field.

## Further reading

- Nicholas M. Patrikalakis and Takashi Maekawa, Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and Manufacturing, Springer, 2002. Intersections, distances, umbilics, offsets and their trimming, with exact and interval methods.
- Gerald Farin, Curves and Surfaces for CAGD, A Practical Guide, fifth edition, Morgan Kaufmann, 2002. Patches, joins, Gregory patches, fairing and interrogation from the designer's side.
- Manfredo P. do Carmo, Differential Geometry of Curves and Surfaces, Prentice-Hall, 1976. The curvature, geodesics and lines of curvature that the interrogation methods compute.
- Helmut Pottmann and Johannes Wallner, Computational Line Geometry, Springer, 2001. Ruled and developable surfaces, with the dual representation used for their approximation.
- Rida T. Farouki, Pythagorean-Hodograph Curves, Algebra and Geometry Inseparable, Springer, 2008. The theory of PH curves, their interpolants and their offsets.
