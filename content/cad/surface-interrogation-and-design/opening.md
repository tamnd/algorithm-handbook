A car door is built from a dozen patches. Before the stamping tools are cut, an engineer measures points on a clay model and asks for the nearest point of each patch. Where two panels meet, the engineer needs the curve of their intersection, and under a row of light tubes, the reflected lines must cross each patch boundary without a kink. Interrogation answers such questions about a surface that exists. Design builds new surfaces with control over how they join: patches through a network of curves, smooth fillings of holes with three or five sides, fair shapes that minimize an energy, and sheets that unroll onto the plane.

The methods rest on different kinds of guarantee, and the entries keep them apart. Implicitization by resultants is exact algebra, but its cost grows fast with the degree. Point projection by Newton steps converges only from a good start. Lattice evaluation misses a small closed loop of an intersection that falls inside one cell, while the loop test of Sederberg and Meyers proves when no such loop exists. Interval methods lose no root but stop at a tolerance. A fairing energy is a model of fairness, not its definition, and isophotes show a defect to the eye without measuring it.

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

## A first reading

Take these twelve entries in order: point projection onto a surface, Newton point inversion, curve-surface intersection, implicitization, lattice evaluation, Sederberg-Meyers loop detection, isophotes, lines of curvature, tangent plane continuity, the Gregory patch, energy-based fairing and developable surfaces. They give the basic distance problem and its Newton solution, one exact and one sampling method for intersections, the test that makes sampling safe, the two main tools for inspecting a surface, the condition for a smooth join and a patch that meets it, the standard fairing model, and the surfaces that sheet metal can form.

Leave for later the generalized Bézier patch, the minimum variation surface and the PH quintic. They assume the first reading and are closer to current research.
