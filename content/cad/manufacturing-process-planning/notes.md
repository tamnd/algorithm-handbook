## History

The oldest results in the chapter are empirical laws. F. W. Taylor ran cutting tests for many years at Midvale Steel and Bethlehem Steel, reported them to the American Society of Mechanical Engineers in December 1906, and published them in 1907; the law $V T^n = C$ comes from that work. Gilbert (1950) turned it into the tool life of least cost and of least time. Ermer (1971) wrote the constrained economics of one pass as a geometric program, and Ermer and Kromodihardjo (1981) treated several passes.

Casting followed the same path. Chvorinov published his rule in 1940 in Die Giesserei, from the conduction of heat into a sand mold. Caine (1948) related the freezing ratio of riser and casting to their volume ratio by a hyperbola, and Wlodawer built the modulus method of feeder design on Chvorinov's rule; his book on steel castings appeared in English in 1966. Ravi and Srinivasan (1996) computed hot spots on solid models, where foundry engineers had fitted the largest circle into a section by hand.

Computer-aided process planning grew from group technology, with variant systems that retrieve the plan of a similar part and generative systems that reason from features. Alting and Zhang (1989) surveyed the systems of the time, and Halevi and Weill (1995) wrote a textbook on the logic of planning. Gupta and Nau (1995) rated a machined part by its best plan, and Gupta, Regli, Das and Nau (1997) surveyed manufacturability analysis. On sheet metal, Shpitalni and Saddan (1994) stated bend and tool choice as one graph search, Radin, Shpitalni and Hartman (1997) gave a two-stage method, and Gupta, Bourne, Kim and Krishnan (1998) used A* in a planner for a robotic press brake. Gan and Wagoner (2004) gave the displacement adjustment method for die design under springback.

Fixtures became a geometric subject with Asada and By (1985), who wrote the gap change at each locator as a row of a matrix and reduced determinate location to its rank. The idea of form closure goes back to Reuleaux (1876). Markenscoff, Ni and Papadimitriou (1990) showed that four frictionless contacts suffice in the plane and seven in space, except for parts with rotational symmetry. Brost and Goldberg gave the first complete algorithm for modular fixtures at ICRA in 1994 and in full in 1996, and Zhuang and Goldberg (1996) gave convex polygons that have no fixture of that class. For molds, Ahn and coauthors decided at SoCG 1997 whether a polyhedron can be cast in a two-piece mold, Fu, Nee and Fuh (2002) chose parting lines by visibility, and Wang, Yu, Wang and Zhang (2011) designed conformal cooling circuits automatically.

## Further reading

- Serope Kalpakjian and Steven R. Schmid, *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology*, 7th edition, Pearson, 2014. The processes behind every entry, with the empirical constants for K-factors, Chvorinov's rule and tool life.
- Gideon Halevi and Roland D. Weill, *Principles of Process Planning, a Logical Approach*, Chapman and Hall, 1995. Process planning as a sequence of decisions, from the part drawing to the operation sheet.
- John Campbell, *Complete Casting Handbook*, 2nd edition, Butterworth-Heinemann, 2015. Feeding rules, the modulus method and the limits of both, from a foundry view.
- Merton C. Flemings, *Solidification Processing*, McGraw-Hill, 1974. The heat flow model from which Chvorinov's rule follows, and when it fails.
- Randy C. Brost and Kenneth Y. Goldberg, A complete algorithm for designing planar fixtures using modular components, *IEEE Transactions on Robotics and Automation* 12(1), 1996, 31 to 46. The model of fixtures as lattice points and contact wrenches, and an exact algorithm over it.
- Satyandra K. Gupta, William C. Regli, Diganta Das and Dana S. Nau, Automated manufacturability analysis, a survey, *Research in Engineering Design* 9(3), 1997, 168 to 190. The direct and plan-based ways of judging a design.
