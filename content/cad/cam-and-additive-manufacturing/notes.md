## History

Numerical control and its geometry grew up together. Robert O. Anderson's 1978 paper on collisions in NC machining is credited with the grid of heights behind the z-map, and in the same year Persson cleared pockets of any shape with offset curves built from the bisectors of the boundary. Tim Van Hook showed milling on screen as it ran, with dexels, in 1986, and Wang and Wang described the swept volume of a moving tool in the same year. Loney and Ozsoy wrote about iso-parametric paths on free-form patches in 1987. Kondo, Kishinami and Saito built gouge-free paths from the inverse offset of the tool in 1988, and Jerard, Drysdale and their colleagues gave methods to find errors in sculptured surface paths in 1989. Martin Held's 1991 monograph put pocket machining on the footing of computational geometry, with offsets read from the Voronoi diagram of segments and arcs.

The 1990s refined both paths and their analysis. Huang and Oliver gave iso-planar paths in 1994, and Suresh and Yang placed passes so that the scallop height between them is constant, also in 1994; Lin and Koren made that method efficient in 1996. Arkin, Held and Smith proved in 2000 that minimizing the retractions of a zigzag path is NP-hard for pockets with holes, and Arkin, Fekete and Mitchell proved the same year that milling with holes along a shortest path is NP-hard. Chen, Chou and Woo used visibility to choose mold parting directions in 1993, and Woo described visibility maps on the sphere in 1994. Bose, Bremner and van Kreveld studied planar parting surfaces in 1997, and Ahn and his coauthors allowed any parting surface for two-part molds in 2002.

Layered manufacturing began with the stereolithography machines of the late 1980s and their STL meshes. Dolenc and Mäkelä defined the cusp height and adaptive layer thickness in 1994, and Frank and Fadel ranked build directions with expert rules in 1995. Minetto, Volpato, Stolfi, Gregori and da Silva published an optimal slicing algorithm in 2017. Supports moved from pillars and walls to trees with the Clever Support method of Vanek, Garcia Galicia and Benes in 2014. Zhao and his coauthors drew a whole layer as one connected Fermat spiral in 2016.

Topology optimization starts with the homogenization method of Martin Bendsøe and Noboru Kikuchi in 1988. Bendsøe proposed the power law of SIMP in 1989, and Ole Sigmund's 99-line program of 2001, followed by the 88-line program of Andreassen and coauthors in 2011, made the method a standard teaching tool. Wang, Wang and Guo in 2003 and Allaire, Jouve and Toader in 2004 moved the boundary with the level set method of Osher and Sethian.

Nesting and tolerancing come from operations research and design. Baker, Coffman and Rivest analyzed the bottom-left rule for rectangles in 1980, and Chazelle gave a fast implementation in 1983. Fischetti and Luzzi wrote exact mixed-integer models of nesting in 2009, and Abrahamsen, Miltzow and Seiferth showed in 2020 that packing with curved pieces is complete for the existential theory of the reals. Greenwood and Chase studied mean shifts in statistical tolerancing in 1987, and Chase and Parkinson surveyed tolerance analysis in 1991.

## Further reading

- Byoung K. Choi and Robert B. Jerard, *Sculptured Surface Machining: Theory and Applications* (Kluwer, 1998). The cutter location, scallop, gouge and simulation methods of the chapter, with the three kinds of finishing paths compared.
- Martin Held, *On the Computational Geometry of Pocket Machining*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 500 (Springer, 1991). Offsets, Voronoi diagrams and zigzag paths for pockets, worked out in full.
- Ian Gibson, David Rosen, Brent Stucker and Mahyar Khorasani, *Additive Manufacturing Technologies*, 3rd ed. (Springer, 2021). The printing processes, their data flow from the mesh to the layers, and the role of orientation and supports.
- Martin P. Bendsøe and Ole Sigmund, *Topology Optimization: Theory, Methods and Applications* (Springer, 2003). The standard text on density methods, filters and the mathematics of the relaxed problem.
- Julia A. Bennell and José F. Oliveira, "The geometry of nesting problems: a tutorial", *European Journal of Operational Research* 184(2), 2008. No-fit polygons, phi-functions and the overlap tests behind nesting heuristics.
