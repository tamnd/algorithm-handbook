## History

Anderson's 1978 paper on NC collisions is credited with the z-map, and Persson cleared pockets with offsets built from bisectors the same year. Van Hook showed milling on screen with dexels in 1986, when Wang and Wang also described the swept volume of a tool. Kondo, Kishinami and Saito built gouge-free paths from the inverse offset in 1988, and Held's 1991 monograph read pocket offsets from the Voronoi diagram. Controllers learned to follow curves: Shpitalni, Koren and Lo stepped the parameter of a curve from its derivatives in 1994, and Farouki and Shah used the polynomial arc length of Pythagorean hodograph curves in 1996. Time-optimal feeds go back to the phase plane methods for robots of Bobrow, Dubowsky and Gibson and of Shin and McKay (1985), and Verscheure and coauthors showed in 2009 that without jerk limits the problem is convex.

The 1990s refined the paths. Huang and Oliver gave iso-planar paths, and Suresh and Yang kept the scallop height constant, both in 1994. Choi and Jerard's 1998 book described pencil cuts. For five axes, Rao, Bedi and coauthors tied the tilt to the principal curvatures in 1996 and 1997, Morishige, Kase and Takeuchi searched a space of tool directions for collisions in 1997, and Liu placed flank-milling cutters by offsets in 1995. High-speed machining made tool load the concern: Stori and Wright posed constant engagement in 2000, Elber, Cohen and Drake built spirals from the medial axis in 2005, and Otkur and Lazoglu analyzed trochoidal milling in 2007. Arkin, Held and Smith proved minimizing retractions NP-hard for pockets with holes in 2000.

Layered manufacturing began with stereolithography and STL meshes in the late 1980s. Guduri, Crawford and Beaman sliced CSG solids of quadrics exactly in 1992, and Jamieson and Hacker sliced inside the CAD system in 1995. Dolenc and Mäkelä defined the cusp height and adaptive layers in 1994, and Minetto and coauthors published an optimal slicing algorithm in 2017. Vanek, Garcia Galicia and Benes grew tree supports in 2014. Curved layers came from Chakraborty, Reddy and Roy Choudhury in 2008 and CurviSlicer in 2019; Wu, Dai, Fang, Liu and Wang cut models into support-free parts for a robot arm in 2017, and Dai and coauthors printed curved layers in 2018.

Topology optimization starts with the homogenization method of Bendsøe and Kikuchi in 1988, built on the relaxation theory of the 1970s and 1980s; Allaire gave a full account in 2002. Bendsøe's power law of 1989 became SIMP, and Sigmund's 99-line program of 2001 made it a teaching tool. Bruns and Tortorelli filtered the densities in 2001, and Bourdin proved that the filtered problem has a solution. Xie and Steven removed lightly stressed elements in 1993, Querin, Steven and Xie made the method bidirectional in 1998, and after the counterexample of Zhou and Rozvany (2001), Huang and Xie gave BESO its present form in 2007. Level set methods followed in 2003 and 2004.

Chen, Chou and Woo chose parting directions by visibility in 1993, and Ahn and coauthors allowed any parting surface in 2002. Baker, Coffman and Rivest analyzed the bottom-left rule in 1980, Fischetti and Luzzi wrote exact nesting models in 2009, and Abrahamsen, Miltzow and Seiferth showed in 2020 that packing curved pieces is complete for the existential theory of the reals. Greenwood and Chase studied mean shifts in statistical tolerancing in 1987.

## Further reading

- Byoung K. Choi and Robert B. Jerard, *Sculptured Surface Machining: Theory and Applications* (Kluwer, 1998). Cutter locations, scallops, gouges, simulation and finishing paths.
- Martin Held, *On the Computational Geometry of Pocket Machining*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 500 (Springer, 1991). Offsets, Voronoi diagrams and zigzag paths for pockets.
- Ian Gibson, David Rosen, Brent Stucker and Mahyar Khorasani, *Additive Manufacturing Technologies*, 3rd ed. (Springer, 2021). The processes and their data flow from the mesh to the layers.
- Martin P. Bendsøe and Ole Sigmund, *Topology Optimization: Theory, Methods and Applications* (Springer, 2003). The standard text on density methods, filters and relaxation.
- Julia A. Bennell and José F. Oliveira, "The geometry of nesting problems: a tutorial", *European Journal of Operational Research* 184(2), 2008. No-fit polygons and the overlap tests behind nesting heuristics.
