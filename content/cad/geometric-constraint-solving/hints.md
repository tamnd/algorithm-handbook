In the plane a trivial motion is $S(q) = \omega J q + c$, with $J$ the turn by a right angle, and a bar with end $a$ and direction $u$ gives the functional $S \mapsto \omega\,(a \times u) + c \cdot u$. The three functionals are dependent exactly when some $S \ne 0$ is killed by all of them. If $\omega \ne 0$, $S$ is a rotation about a point $z$ and it is killed exactly by the lines through $z$; if $\omega = 0$, $S$ is a translation and it is killed by the lines perpendicular to $c$. So the bad cases are a relative rotation about the common point and a relative translation across parallel bars.

21. A cut vertex $v$ lets one side be reflected about $p_v$, which keeps all edge lengths and changes a distance across the cut. For the converse, use an ear decomposition. On a cycle, a second placement differs from the first by signs $\epsilon_t$ with $\sum_t \epsilon_t (p_{t+1} - p_t) = 0$, a linear relation with integer coefficients among generic coordinates, so all $\epsilon_t$ are equal and the placement is the original or its mirror. The same argument fixes each ear between two placed vertices.

22. By Mantel's theorem, a triangle-free graph on $n$ vertices has at most $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ edges, with equality only for the balanced complete bipartite graph. Since $2n - 3 > \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ for $n = 3, 4, 5$, the first possible size is $n = 6$ with 9 edges, which forces $K_{3,3}$, and $K_{3,3}$ is a Laman graph.
