## History

The counting side of the subject is older than computers. Maxwell gave the rule in 1864 that a stiff plane frame of $n$ joints needs $2n - 3$ bars, and $3n - 6$ in space and saw that the count can fail at special positions. Henneberg described in 1911 how plane trusses are built up by adding joints of degree two and by splitting a bar with a joint of degree three. Pollaczek-Geiringer proved in 1927 that the plane count, applied to every subset of joints, characterizes generic minimal rigidity, and Laman proved the same theorem again in 1970, using Henneberg's moves; for decades the result carried only his name.

Solving constraints on a screen began with Sutherland's Sketchpad in 1963, which ordered the variables in one pass when it could and fell back on relaxation when it could not. Local propagation became the first method of constraint-based user interfaces. Engineering design took the numerical route: Lin, Gossard and Light in 1981, and Light and Gossard in 1982, treated a part as characteristic points tied by dimension equations, solved them by Newton-Raphson iteration and used the Jacobian to find missing and redundant dimensions. Kramer's book of 1992 solved assemblies and linkages of rigid bodies by degrees of freedom analysis, with closed-form steps in place of iteration.

Rigidity theory grew into a theory of matroids and graphs; Lovász and Yemini gave the matroid view of the plane counts in 1982. Asimow and Roth showed in 1978 that rigidity and infinitesimal rigidity agree at generic placements, which makes rigidity a property of the graph. Tay proved in 1984 that body-and-bar frameworks in every dimension are generically rigid exactly when the multigraph of bodies and bars contains $D = d(d + 1)/2$ edge-disjoint spanning trees. Jacobs and Hendrickson published the pebble game in 1997 as a fast exact test of Laman's counts, and Lee and Streinu extended it to all $(k, \ell)$ counts in 2008.

The decomposition solvers of CAD came at the same time. Owen in 1991 split the constraint graph at separation pairs with the triconnected components algorithm and solved the pieces by quadratic equations. At Purdue, Bouma, Fudos, Hoffmann, Cai and Paige built a solver around a bottom-up merge of clusters, and Fudos and Hoffmann published the reduction and its quadratic bound in 1997. Joan-Arinyo, Soto-Riera, Vila-Marta and Vilaplana-Pastó proved in 2002 that the two methods solve the same graphs. Hoffmann, Lomonosov and Sitharam set out in 2001 what a general decomposition plan should achieve and how to measure it.

The question of unique placement has its own line. Saxe showed in 1979 that deciding whether a weighted graph can be placed at all is NP-complete on the line and strongly NP-hard in every dimension. Hendrickson gave the necessary conditions for generic global rigidity in 1992, redundant rigidity and $(d + 1)$-connectivity. Jackson and Jordán proved in 2005 that they are sufficient in the plane, and Connelly's sufficient condition by stress matrices, published in 2005, was shown to be necessary as well by Gortler, Healy and Thurston in 2010.

## Further reading

- Jack Graver, Brigitte Servatius and Herman Servatius, *Combinatorial Rigidity*, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 2, American Mathematical Society, 1993. The textbook on frameworks, infinitesimal rigidity, the rigidity matroid and the plane counts.
- Meera Sitharam, Audrey St. John and Jessica Sidman (editors), *Handbook of Geometric Constraint Systems Principles*, Chapman and Hall/CRC, 2018. A survey of the whole field, from constraint types and decomposition plans to rigidity and the algebra of solution counts.
- Glenn A. Kramer, *Solving Geometric Constraint Systems: A Case Study in Kinematics*, MIT Press, 1992. Degrees of freedom analysis for assemblies of rigid bodies.
- Ioannis Fudos and Christoph M. Hoffmann, "A graph-constructive approach to solving systems of geometric constraints", *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 16(2), 1997. The cluster-merging solver for points and lines in the plane, with its proof, its running time and a careful section on root identification.
- Christoph M. Hoffmann, Andrew Lomonosov and Meera Sitharam, "Decomposition plans for geometric constraint systems, Part I: Performance measures for CAD", *Journal of Symbolic Computation* 31(4), 2001. The definition of DR-plans and the measures by which older planners are compared.
