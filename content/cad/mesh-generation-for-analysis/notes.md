## History

Mesh generation began with structured grids for finite difference codes. Winslow solved a Laplace system for the grid coordinates in 1966, and Thompson, Thames and Mastin made elliptic grid generation a standard tool in 1974. Gordon introduced transfinite interpolation in 1971, and Gordon and Hall applied it to finite element meshes in 1973. The theory of element shape came from the finite element side: Synge in 1957 and Zlámal in 1968 worked with angle conditions, and Babuška and Aziz showed in 1976 that a bound on the largest angle is enough for the interpolation estimate.

Unstructured meshes of general domains followed in the 1980s. Yerry and Shephard used octrees in 1983, Lo described an advancing front method in 1985, and Peraire, Vahdati, Morgan and Zienkiewicz added adaptive sizing to it in 1987. Bern, Eppstein and Gilbert gave the first quadtree method with proved angle and size bounds in 1990. Shewchuk extended Delaunay refinement to tetrahedra in 1997 and 1998 with a radius-edge bound, and Cheng, Dey, Edelsbrunner, Facello and Teng removed slivers by sliver exudation in 1999. Persson and Strang published DistMesh in 2004, Chen and Xu analysed optimal Delaunay triangulations the same year, and Labelle and Shewchuk proved dihedral angle bounds for isosurface stuffing in 2007.

Surface meshing gained a proof of topology through the restricted Delaunay triangulation. Edelsbrunner and Shah gave the closed ball property in 1997, Amenta and Bern proved that dense samples give the right surface in 1999, and Boissonnat and Oudot turned Chew's 1993 surface mesher into an algorithm with guarantees in 2005. Cheng, Dey and Ramos used protecting balls to handle sharp features in 2007.

Quadrilateral and hexahedral meshing grew at Sandia around the CUBIT code. Blacker and Stephenson introduced paving in 1991 and Blacker and Meyers plastering in 1993. Tautges, Blacker and Mitchell described whisker weaving in 1996, and Murdoch, Benzley, Blacker and Mitchell the spatial twist continuum in 1997. Thurston in 1993 and Mitchell in 1996 proved that a ball with an even number of boundary quads has a topological hex mesh, and Eppstein gave a linear size construction in 1999. Owen, Staten, Canann and Saigal published Q-Morph in 1999. Later methods came from geometry processing: polycubes from Tarini and coauthors in 2004, frame fields from Huang, Tong, Wei and Bao and from Nieser, Reitebuch and Polthier in 2011, and Blossom-Quad from Remacle and coauthors in 2012.

Mesh improvement became a separate step. Joe studied local transformations of tetrahedral meshes in 1995, Freitag and Ollivier-Gooch compared flips and smoothing in 1997, Freitag and Plassmann combined max-min smoothing with a linear program for untangling in 2000, and Klingner and Shewchuk combined smoothing, flips and edge removal in 2007.

## Further reading

- Siu-Wing Cheng, Tamal K. Dey and Jonathan Richard Shewchuk, *Delaunay Mesh Generation* (CRC Press, 2013). The proofs of Delaunay refinement in two and three dimensions, sliver exudation, protecting balls and surface meshing.
- Pascal Jean Frey and Paul-Louis George, *Mesh Generation: Application to Finite Elements* (Hermes Science, 2000). A broad engineering text on structured, advancing front, Delaunay and anisotropic meshing, with the metric view of adaptation.
- Joe F. Thompson, Z. U. A. Warsi and C. Wayne Mastin, *Numerical Grid Generation: Foundations and Applications* (North-Holland, 1985). The classical account of algebraic and elliptic structured grids.
- Steven J. Owen, "A survey of unstructured mesh generation technology", *Proceedings of the 7th International Meshing Roundtable*, 1998. A short map of the tetrahedral, quadrilateral and hexahedral methods used in practice.
- Nico Pietroni and coauthors, "Hex-mesh generation and processing: a survey", *ACM Transactions on Graphics*, 2022. The current methods for hexahedral meshes, including polycubes and frame fields, with their measured quality.
