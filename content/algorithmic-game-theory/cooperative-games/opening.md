One player owns a left glove and two others each own a right glove. A matched pair sells for 1, a single glove for nothing. The question is how the three should share the 1 they can earn together. The [core](/core-of-a-cooperative-game/), the set of divisions that no group can improve on by leaving, gives everything to the owner of the left glove: if a right-glove owner received anything, the left-glove owner and the other right-glove owner could earn 1 on their own and split it. The [Shapley value](/shapley-value/), which averages each player's marginal contribution over all orders of arrival, gives the left-glove owner $2/3$ and each right-glove owner $1/6$. One answer measures stability, the other a kind of fairness, and they disagree.

In a cooperative game the players can make binding agreements, so the questions are which coalitions form and how they share what they earn. The model is a [transferable utility game](/transferable-utility-game/): a worth $v(S)$ for every coalition $S$ of the $n$ players. The same mathematics shares the cost of a runway among the airlines that use it, the cost of a network among the towns it connects, an estate among creditors whose claims exceed it, and the voting power of council members with unequal weights. A game on $n$ players has $2^n$ coalitions, so the algorithmic side of the subject is about linear programs with exponentially many constraints, games with short descriptions, counting, and sampling.

The last group of the chapter adds private information. A [cost-sharing mechanism](/cost-sharing-mechanism/) asks the players what a service is worth to them, and it must decide whom to serve and what to charge so that lying does not pay and the payments cover the cost. This is mechanism design, with cooperative game theory supplying the shares.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Games and stable divisions.** TU games, the core and the [Bondareva-Shapley theorem](/bondareva-shapley-theorem/), which says by LP duality exactly when the core is empty. The [least core](/least-core/) is one linear program, and the [nucleolus](/nucleolus/) is a sequence of them ([the sequential LP](/nucleolus-sequential-lp/)).
2. **The Shapley value.** Its definition, the [uniqueness theorem](/shapley-uniqueness-theorem/), the [multilinear extension](/owen-multilinear-extension/), [estimation by sampling](/shapley-value-sampling/), and [marginal contribution nets](/marginal-contribution-nets/), on which the value takes linear time.
3. **Convex games and games from optimization.** [Convex games](/convex-game/) and [Shapley's theorem](/shapley-convex-games-theorem/) on their core, then the [airport game](/airport-game/), the [assignment game](/assignment-game/), the [minimum cost spanning tree game](/minimum-cost-spanning-tree-game/) and [Bird's rule](/bird-rule/).
4. **Voting power.** [Weighted voting games](/weighted-voting-game/), the [Shapley-Shubik index](/shapley-shubik-index/) and the [Banzhaf index](/banzhaf-index/), their computation by [dynamic programming](/power-index-dynamic-programming/), and the [hardness](/power-index-hardness/) of computing them exactly.
5. **Claims and bargaining.** The [bankruptcy problem](/bankruptcy-problem/) and the [Talmud rule](/talmud-rule/), the [Nash bargaining solution](/nash-bargaining-solution/) and the [Rubinstein bargaining model](/rubinstein-bargaining-model/).
6. **Cost-sharing mechanisms.** [Cross-monotonicity](/cross-monotonicity/), the [Moulin mechanism](/moulin-mechanism/) and [Jain-Vazirani cost sharing](/jain-vazirani-steiner-cost-sharing/) for Steiner trees.
7. **Forming coalitions.** [Coalition structure generation](/coalition-structure-generation/) and [hedonic games](/hedonic-game/).

## Before you start

The core is a polyhedron, and the main tools are [linear programming](/linear-programming-problem/), [LP duality](/lp-duality/) and [complementary slackness](/complementary-slackness/) from Volume 15. The Shapley value is an average over the [permutations](/permutation/) of the players (Volume 6), and its estimates use [Hoeffding's inequality](/hoeffding-inequality/) from Volume 1. The games from optimization rest on the [assignment problem](/assignment-problem/), the [minimum spanning tree](/minimum-spanning-tree/) and the [Steiner tree problem](/steiner-tree-problem/) of Volume 7. Power indices are counted like the [subset sum problem](/subset-sum-problem/) of Volume 39, and their hardness uses [NP-completeness](/np-complete/) from the same volume. The mechanisms need [quasilinear utility](/quasilinear-utility/) and [dominant-strategy incentive compatibility](/dominant-strategy-incentive-compatibility/) from chapter 42.4, and the bargaining and hedonic entries use the [subgame perfect equilibrium](/subgame-perfect-equilibrium/) and the [Nash equilibrium](/nash-equilibrium/) of chapter 42.1.
