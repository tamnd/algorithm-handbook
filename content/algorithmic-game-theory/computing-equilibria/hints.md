4. Against $y$ the rows pay $36/13$, $40/13$ and $40/13$, so rows 2 and 3, the support of $x$, are best replies. Against $x$ the columns pay $40/11$, $40/11$ and $36/11$, so columns 1 and 2, the support of $y$, are best replies. The payoffs are $40/13$ to the row player and $40/11$ to the column player.

6. The symmetric equilibria are $z = (3/5, 0, 2/5, 0)$, $(0, 2/5, 0, 3/5)$ and $(1/3, 1/6, 1/6, 1/3)$. They give (row 1, column 1), (row 2, column 2) and $((2/3, 1/3), (1/3, 2/3))$: the three equilibria of the battle of the sexes, which adding a constant does not change. If $z$ had weight only on the first block, it would earn 0, and a strategy of the second block would earn a positive amount against it.

7. With variables $p_{DD}, p_{DC}, p_{CD}, p_{CC}$, the four constraints say that a player told D does not prefer C and a player told C does not prefer D. The optimum puts $1/4$ on (D, C), $1/4$ on (C, D) and $1/2$ on (C, C), for a total of $21/2$, so each player gets $21/4 = 5.25$. A player told C then expects the other to dare with probability $1/3$ and is exactly indifferent.

9. The column player plays $j$, a best reply to $i$, and the row player mixes $i$ with $k$, a best reply to $j$. The row player gets at least $A_{kj}/2$ and can get at most $A_{kj}$, a gain of at most $1/2$. A deviation of the column player to $j'$ gives $(B_{ij'} + B_{kj'})/2 \le (B_{ij} + 1)/2$, while it gets $(B_{ij} + B_{kj})/2$, again a gain of at most $1/2$.

10. If $x$ has support of size $k$, every column in the support of $y$ is a best reply to $x$, and there are at most $k$ of them; so $|\operatorname{supp} y| \le |\operatorname{supp} x|$. The same argument with the roles exchanged gives the other inequality.
