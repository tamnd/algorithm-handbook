1. Around the cycle (U, L), (D, L), (D, R), (U, R), (U, L) the movers' cost changes are $3 - 1 = 2$, $2 - 5 = -3$, $4 - 2 = 2$ and $2 - 3 = -1$, which sum to 0. The potential is $\Phi(U, L) = 0$, $\Phi(D, L) = 2$, $\Phi(U, R) = 1$, $\Phi(D, R) = -1$. Its local minima (U, L) and (D, R) are the pure equilibria, with social costs 3 and 4. The optimum is 3, so the price of anarchy is $4/3$ and the price of stability is 1.

2. The equilibrium equalizes $2x = (2 - x) + 1$, so $x = 1$: the flow is $(1, 1)$, the common latency 2 and the cost 4. The optimum equalizes the marginal costs $4x = 2(2 - x) + 1$, so $x = 5/6$: the flow is $(5/6, 7/6)$ with cost $25/18 + 91/36 = 47/12$. The price of anarchy is $48/47$.

5. Without the shortcut each route carries $r/2$ at latency $r/2 + 1$, that is $5/4$ and $7/4$. With it, at $r = 1/2$ all traffic takes the zigzag, at latency $2r = 1$, while the other two routes would cost $3/2$. At $r = 3/2$ each of the three paths carries $1/2$, each edge of latency $x$ carries 1, and every path has latency 2. So the shortcut helps at $r = 1/2$ and hurts at $r = 3/2$.

6. The profiles $(\{e\}, \{f\})$, $(\{e\}, \{e, g\})$, $(\{f, g\}, \{f\})$, $(\{f, g\}, \{e, g\})$ have player costs $(1, 5)$, $(3, 4)$, $(6, 5)$, $(6, 2)$, totals 6, 7, 11, 8 and potentials 6, 5, 11, 8. Only $(\{e\}, \{e, g\})$ has no improving move; it is the minimum of $\Phi$. The optimum costs 6 but player 2 leaves it for $\{e, g\}$, which costs 4 instead of 5. The price of anarchy and the price of stability are both $7/6$.

7. With $k$ players on the edge of cost 3: for $k = 3$ each pays 1, and a player alone on the other edge would pay 2; for $k = 0$ each pays $2/3$, and alone on the edge of cost 3 a player would pay 3. Both are equilibria. For $k = 1$ the lone player pays 3 and would pay $2/3$; for $k = 2$ the lone player pays 2 and would pay 1. The potentials for $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$ are $11/3$, $6$, $13/2$, $11/2$. The equilibria cost 2 and 3, so the price of anarchy is $3/2$ and the price of stability is 1.

8. The optimum is 6, with $\{3, 3\}$ and $\{2, 2, 2\}$, and it is an equilibrium. The only other equilibrium is $\{3, 2, 2\}$ against $\{3, 2\}$, with loads 7 and 5: a job of weight 2 that moves pays $5 + 2 = 7$, no less than now, and a job of weight 3 would pay 8. The schedule $\{3, 3, 2\}$ against $\{2, 2\}$ is not one, since a job of weight 2 moves and pays 6 instead of 8. The price of anarchy is $7/6$, below $4/3$.

11. With the shortcut, for $r \le 1$ all traffic takes the zigzag at latency $2r$. For $1 \le r \le 2$ the two outer paths carry $r - 1$ each and the zigzag $2 - r$, and the latency is 2. For $r \ge 2$ the zigzag is unused and the latency is $r/2 + 1$. Now $2r > r/2 + 1$ exactly when $r > 2/3$, and $2 > r/2 + 1$ exactly when $r < 2$. The ratio $2r/(r/2 + 1)$ grows up to $r = 1$ and $2/(r/2 + 1)$ falls after it, so the largest ratio is $4/3$, at $r = 1$, the most that adding edges can cost with affine latencies.

14. At the optimum $(5/6, 7/6)$ link 2 has latency $13/6$ and link 1 has $5/3$, so the leader fills link 2 first, with $L = 2\alpha$ up to $7/6$. For $L \le 1$ the followers' equilibrium still puts 1 on link 1, the total flow stays $(1, 1)$ and the cost is 4. For $1 \le L \le 7/6$ all followers use link 1, and the cost is $2(2 - L)^2 + L(L + 1) = 12\alpha^2 - 14\alpha + 8$. From $\alpha = 7/12$ on, the outcome is the optimum $47/12$. At $\alpha = 1/2$ the ratio $48/47$ is well below $4/(3 + 1/2) = 8/7$.

16. Round 1: the paths $s$-$a$-$t$, $s$-$a$-$b$-$t$, $s$-$b$-$t$ have lengths 9, 6 and 5, so $s$-$b$-$t$ is routed. Round 2: $s$-$a$-$b$-$t$ has length $1 + 2 + 4 = 7$, the shortest. Round 3: $s$-$b$-$t$ has length $4 + 5 = 9$; the path $s$-$b$-$a$-$t$ that cancels the unit on $a \to b$ costs $4 - 2 + 8 = 10$. The potential is $5 + 7 + 9 = 21$. One player uses $s$-$a$-$b$-$t$ at cost 8 and two use $s$-$b$-$t$ at cost 9, a total of 26; this is the only pure equilibrium. The optimum, with one player on each of the three paths, costs $10 + 8 + 6 = 24$, and the player on $s$-$a$-$t$ would move to $s$-$b$-$t$ and pay 9. Both prices are $13/12$.

17. The total weight is 15 and $\Phi = 15 - \text{cut}$. (a) $b$ pays 3 and moves to pay 0, $\Phi = 12$; $d$ pays 7 and moves to pay 0, $\Phi = 5$. In $\{a, c\}$ against $\{b, d\}$, $a$ and $c$ pay 5 on either side and $b$, $d$ pay 0, so no one gains; the cut is 10. (b) $a$ pays 10 and moves, $\Phi = 5$; $b$ pays 2 and would pay 1, so it moves, $\Phi = 4$. In $\{a, b\}$ against $\{c, d\}$ the cut is 11, the largest of the eight partitions.

21. For concave $\ell \ge 0$ and $0 \le x \le r$, $\ell(x) \ge \frac{x}{r}\ell(r) + \left(1 - \frac{x}{r}\right)\ell(0) \ge \frac{x}{r}\ell(r)$. So the denominator of the Pigou ratio is at least $\ell(r)\left(x^2/r - x + r\right) \ge \frac34 r\,\ell(r)$, with the minimum at $x = r/2$. The function $\ell(x) = x$ is concave and reaches $4/3$. The upper bound of the Pigou bound theorem then gives the claim for every network.

22. On link 1 alone the latency is $1/(u - r)$, the latency of the empty link 2. The marginal cost condition $u/(u - x)^2 = 1/(u - r)$ gives $x^* = u - \sqrt{u(u - r)}$, which lies in $[0, r]$. With $q = \sqrt{u/(u - r)}$, the optimum costs $2(q - 1)$ and the equilibrium costs $r/(u - r) = q^2 - 1$, so the ratio is $(q + 1)/2$. For $u = 2$, $r = 1$ it is $(1 + \sqrt2)/2 \approx 1.207$.

23. Let $L = \ln(d + 1)$. Then $1/\alpha_d = 1 - \frac{d}{d + 1}e^{-L/d}$, and $e^{-y} = 1 - y + O(y^2)$ with $y = L/d$ gives $1/\alpha_d = (L + 1)/d + O(L^2/d^2)$. So $\alpha_d \ln d/d = \ln d/(L + 1 + O(L^2/d)) \to 1$. The $+1$ is what slows the approach: $\ln d/(\ln d + 1)$ is about $0.93$ at $d = 10^6$, and indeed $67497 \cdot 13.8/10^6 \approx 0.93$.

24. (a) Keep the invariant that every used link $e$ has $c_e(x_e) \le c_f(x_f + 1)$ for every link $f$. If the next player joins link $g$ with $m = c_g(x_g + 1)$ the smallest such value, the new cost on $g$ is $m$, which is at most every $c_f(x_f + 1)$ with $f \ne g$ and at most $c_g(x_g + 2)$, because costs do not decrease; the other used links had costs at most $m$. At the end the invariant says that no player gains by moving. (b) The first player takes $b$ (2 against 3), the second takes $a$ (3 against 4); then the player on $b$ pays 2 but would pay $c_a(2) = 1$ on $a$.

25. For $\alpha = 3/2$ the equilibria are the 4-cycle, with each player buying the next edge around the cycle (social cost 22), and the star, bought in any way (cost $45/2$). The optimum $K_4$ costs 21 and is not an equilibrium, so the price of anarchy is $15/14$ and the price of stability $22/21$. For $\alpha = 1$ the equilibrium graphs are $K_4$ (18), $K_4$ minus an edge (19), the 4-cycle and the triangle with a pendant edge (20 each) and the star (21), so the price of anarchy is $7/6$ and the price of stability 1.
