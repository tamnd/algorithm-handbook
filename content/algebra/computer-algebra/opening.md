A computer algebra system computes with formulas the way a numerical library computes with floating-point numbers, but without rounding. Ask it for $\int 1/(x^2 + x - 1)\,dx$ and it returns a logarithm with $\sqrt{5}$ in it, or tells you that $\int e^{x^2}\,dx$ has no answer of that kind. Ask it whether three polynomial equations have a common solution and it decides, then lists the solutions as roots of one polynomial in one variable. Ask it for $\sum_k \binom{n}{k}^2$ and it finds $\binom{2n}{n}$, with a certificate that proves it. The same methods sit inside theorem provers, compilers that simplify expressions, solvers for robot kinematics and the attacks on multivariate public-key cryptosystems.

This chapter collects the algorithms behind those answers. The earlier chapters of the volume give the arithmetic on integers, polynomials and matrices. This one builds on them to manipulate expressions, to solve systems of polynomial equations, and to integrate and sum in closed form. Two problems run through the whole chapter. Intermediate results can grow far beyond the size of the input and the output, and much of the design goes into keeping them small. And some questions, such as whether an expression is zero, are undecidable in general, so each method states exactly the class of inputs it handles.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Formulas as data.** The [symbolic expression](/expression/) type, stored as an [expression tree](/expression-tree/), a DAG with shared parts, or a [straight-line program](/straight-line-program/). [Canonical forms](/canonical-form/) make equality a comparison of data; [Richardson's theorem](/richardson-theorem/) shows that they cannot exist for all elementary expressions.
2. **Algebraic groundwork.** Groups, minimal polynomials, [monomials](/monomial/), [monomial orders](/monomial-ordering/), [leading terms](/leading-term/) and [polynomial ideals](/polynomial-ideal/).
3. **Storing polynomials and recovering them from values.** Dense and [sparse](/sparse-polynomial/) representations, and the interpolation methods of [Ben-Or and Tiwari](/ben-or-tiwari-algorithm/) and [Zippel](/zippel-sparse-interpolation/).
4. **Greatest common divisors and factors over the integers.** The [Sylvester matrix](/sylvester-matrix/), the [primitive](/primitive-prs/) and [subresultant](/subresultant-prs/) remainder sequences, [modular methods](/modular-methods/), the [heuristic gcd](/heuristic-gcd/) and [Kronecker's factorization method](/kronecker-factorization-method/).
5. **Gröbner bases.** [Multivariate division](/multivariate-division-algorithm/), the [Gröbner basis](/groebner-basis/), [S-polynomials](/s-polynomial/) and [Buchberger's algorithm](/buchberger-algorithm/), with [ideal membership](/ideal-membership-problem/) as the first application.
6. **Faster Gröbner bases and changing the order.** Pair criteria, [F4](/f4-algorithm/) and [F5](/f5-algorithm/), and conversion between orders by [FGLM](/fglm-algorithm/) and the [Gröbner walk](/groebner-walk/).
7. **Solving polynomial systems.** The [elimination theorem](/elimination-theorem/), [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](/hilbert-nullstellensatz/), [polynomial system solving](/polynomial-system-solving/) and the triangular methods of Wu and of regular chains.
8. **Real algebra and quantifier elimination.** [Semialgebraic sets](/semialgebraic-set/), the [Tarski-Seidenberg theorem](/tarski-seidenberg-theorem/) and [cylindrical algebraic decomposition](/cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition/).
9. **Integration and differential equations.** [Integration of rational functions](/integration-of-rational-functions/) by [Hermite reduction](/hermite-reduction/) and the [Rothstein-Trager](/rothstein-trager/) resultant, [Liouville's theorem](/liouville-theorem/), the [Risch algorithm](/risch-algorithm/), and [Kovacic's algorithm](/kovacic-algorithm/) for second-order equations.
10. **Symbolic summation.** [Hypergeometric terms](/hypergeometric-term/), [Gosper's algorithm](/gosper-algorithm/), [Zeilberger's algorithm](/zeilberger-algorithm/), the [WZ method](/wz-method/) and [Petkovšek's Hyper](/petkovsek-hyper-algorithm/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fifteen entries in order: [expression tree](/expression-tree/), [canonical form](/canonical-form/), [expression swell](/expression-swell/), [primitive PRS](/primitive-prs/), [modular methods](/modular-methods/), [monomial ordering](/monomial-ordering/), [multivariate division](/multivariate-division-algorithm/), [Gröbner basis](/groebner-basis/), [S-polynomial](/s-polynomial/), [Buchberger's algorithm](/buchberger-algorithm/), [elimination theorem](/elimination-theorem/), [integration of rational functions](/integration-of-rational-functions/), [Hermite reduction](/hermite-reduction/), [Rothstein-Trager](/rothstein-trager/) and [Gosper's algorithm](/gosper-algorithm/). Look up [leading term](/leading-term/) and [polynomial ideal](/polynomial-ideal/) when the Gröbner entries need them. That path shows the three main threads: controlling growth, Gröbner bases, and closed forms for integrals and sums.

Skip the group theory, real algebra, triangular sets and the differential equations at first. The entries marked *Research*, such as the Risch algorithm in full, Kovacic's algorithm and cylindrical algebraic decomposition, are for a second pass.

## Before you start

Most prerequisites come from Chapter 11.2: the [polynomial ring](/polynomial-ring/), [polynomial long division](/polynomial-long-division/), the [polynomial gcd](/polynomial-gcd/) and [extended Euclidean algorithm](/polynomial-extended-euclidean-algorithm/), [pseudo-division](/pseudo-division/), the [resultant](/resultant/) and [square-free factorization](/square-free-factorization/). From Chapter 11.5 you need [Gaussian elimination](/gaussian-elimination/), the [determinant](/determinant/) and the [Hadamard bound](/hadamard-bound/). From Volume 10 you need the [Chinese remainder theorem](/chinese-remainder-theorem/). The expression entries use [hash consing](/hash-consing/) from Volume 3 and [directed acyclic graphs](/directed-acyclic-graph/) from Volume 7, and the summation entries use [recurrence relations](/recurrence-relation/) and [telescoping sums](/telescoping-sum/). Some comfort with rings, fields and ideals helps; the entries define what they use.
