## History

Arthur Cayley defined the matrix product in 1858, in his memoir on the theory of matrices, and the triple loop is that definition. For more than a century the only savings were in constants. Shmuel Winograd showed in 1968 that pairing the terms of an inner product halves the multiplications when the entries commute, and the next year Volker Strassen published *Gaussian elimination is not optimal*, with seven products for $2 \times 2$ blocks and the bound $O(n^{\lg 7})$. The paper also showed that inversion, determinants and linear systems inherit the same exponent. Winograd proved in 1971 that seven products is the least for $2 \times 2$ matrices, and Robert Probert showed in 1976 that 15 additions is the least for such a scheme. Julian Laderman found 23 products for $3 \times 3$ matrices in 1976.

The exponent then fell in steps. Victor Pan reached $2.795$ in 1978. Dario Bini, Milvio Capovani, Francesco Romani and Grazia Lotti brought in approximate algorithms in 1979, the idea behind border rank, and reached $2.7799$. Arnold Schönhage proved his asymptotic sum inequality in 1981 and reached $2.522$; Romani reached $2.517$ in 1982, and Don Coppersmith and Winograd $2.496$ in the same year. Strassen's laser method of 1986 gave $2.479$. Coppersmith and Winograd announced $2.375477$ at STOC in 1987 and published it in 1990.

That bound stood for twenty years. Andrew Stothers improved it to $2.3737$ in 2010 and Virginia Vassilevska Williams to $2.372873$ in 2012, both by applying the laser method to higher tensor powers of the Coppersmith-Winograd tensor. François Le Gall reached $2.3728639$ in 2014, and Josh Alman and Vassilevska Williams $2.3728596$ in 2021. Ran Duan, Hongxun Wu and Renfei Zhou reached $2.371866$ in 2023, and Vassilevska Williams, Yinzhan Xu, Zixuan Xu and Zhou $2.371552$ in 2024. On the other side, Andris Ambainis, Yuval Filmus and Le Gall showed in 2015 that this line of work cannot go below $2.3078$. Joseph Landsberg proved in 2006 that the border rank of the $2 \times 2$ product is 7, and Markus Bläser that the rank of the $3 \times 3$ product is at least 19. In 2022 Alhussein Fawzi and colleagues at DeepMind used reinforcement learning to find a $4 \times 4$ scheme with 47 products over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$.

The practical line runs separately. Lynn Cannon gave his algorithm for a mesh of processors in his 1969 thesis at Montana State University. Fred Gustavson's 1978 algorithm for sparse products is still the standard. Jia-Wei Hong and H. T. Kung proved in 1981 that the $n^3$ method must move $\Omega(n^3/\sqrt M)$ words through a memory of size $M$, which blocked products achieve, and Matteo Frigo, Charles Leiserson, Harald Prokop and Sridhar Ramachandran showed in 1999 that recursion achieves it without knowing $M$. For the min-plus product, Ryan Williams gave the first time below $n^3 / \log^c n$ for every $c$ in 2014.

## Further reading

- Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest and Clifford Stein, *Introduction to Algorithms*, fourth edition (2022). Chapter 4 derives Strassen's algorithm from the divide-and-conquer recurrence.
- Peter Bürgisser, Michael Clausen and M. Amin Shokrollahi, *Algebraic Complexity Theory* (Springer, 1997). The standard account of tensor rank, border rank, Schönhage's inequality and the laser method.
- Joachim von zur Gathen and Jürgen Gerhard, *Modern Computer Algebra*, third edition (2013). Chapter 12 covers fast linear algebra and its reductions to matrix multiplication.
- Gene H. Golub and Charles F. Van Loan, *Matrix Computations*, fourth edition (2013). Chapter 1 explains blocked matrix products as they are written in numerical libraries.
- Volker Strassen, *Gaussian Elimination is not Optimal*, Numerische Mathematik 13, 1969. Three pages long.
