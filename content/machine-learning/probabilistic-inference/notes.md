## History

The rule for turning a prior into a posterior comes from Thomas Bayes, whose essay Richard Price published in 1763, and from Laplace, who stated it in general form in 1774. Ronald Fisher made maximum likelihood a method of its own in 1922. The link between graphs and independence came two centuries after Bayes. Hammersley and Clifford proved in an unpublished 1971 note that positive distributions with the Markov property of a graph factorize over its cliques, and Julian Besag published a proof in 1974. Judea Pearl gave message passing on trees in 1982 and put Bayesian networks, d-separation and belief propagation together in his 1988 book. In the same year Lauritzen and Spiegelhalter gave exact inference on any network through a tree of cliques, the start of the [junction tree algorithm](/junction-tree-algorithm/). The limits came soon after: Cooper showed in 1990 that exact inference in Bayesian networks is NP-hard, and Dagum and Luby showed in 1993 that approximating it is NP-hard too.

Hidden Markov models grew up separately. Baum and his colleagues at the Institute for Defense Analyses developed the forward-backward recursions and the [Baum-Welch](/baum-welch-algorithm/) re-estimation in papers from 1966 to 1970, and Andrew Viterbi gave his decoding algorithm in 1967 for convolutional codes. Dempster, Laird and Rubin named and unified the [EM algorithm](/expectation-maximization/) in 1977. Lawrence Rabiner's 1989 tutorial brought the whole toolkit to speech recognition. Rudolf Kalman published his filter in 1960, and Rauch, Tung and Striebel gave the smoother in 1965. Gordon, Salmond and Smith introduced the bootstrap [particle filter](/particle-filter/) in 1993, and Julier and Uhlmann the [unscented filter](/unscented-kalman-filter/) in 1997. Lafferty, McCallum and Pereira introduced [conditional random fields](/conditional-random-field/) in 2001.

Running belief propagation on graphs with cycles became respectable when McEliece, MacKay and Cheng showed in 1998 that turbo decoding is an instance of it. Yedidia, Freeman and Weiss then showed that the fixed points of [loopy belief propagation](/loopy-belief-propagation/) are stationary points of the Bethe free energy, which led to generalized belief propagation. Wainwright, Jaakkola and Willsky gave the tree-reweighted upper bound on the log partition function in 2005. The variational view goes back to the mean-field theory of Pierre Weiss in 1907; Jordan, Ghahramani, Jaakkola and Saul set it out for graphical models in 1999, and Thomas Minka introduced [expectation propagation](/expectation-propagation/) in 2001. Radford Neal published [annealed importance sampling](/annealed-importance-sampling/) in 2001. Blei, Ng and Jordan introduced [latent Dirichlet allocation](/latent-dirichlet-allocation/) in 2003, and Hoffman, Blei, Wang and Paisley scaled such models to large data with [stochastic variational inference](/stochastic-variational-inference/) in 2013.

Structure and causation form the last thread. Chow and Liu found the best tree-shaped approximation of a distribution in 1968. Pearl stated the [back-door criterion](/backdoor-criterion/) in 1993 and the rules of the [do-calculus](/do-calculus/) in 1995. In 2006 Huang and Valtorta, and independently Shpitser and Pearl, proved that the three rules are complete for deciding when a causal effect can be identified.

## Further reading

- Daphne Koller and Nir Friedman, *Probabilistic Graphical Models: Principles and Techniques*, MIT Press, 2009. The standard reference for representation, exact and approximate inference, and learning.
- Judea Pearl, *Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems: Networks of Plausible Inference*, Morgan Kaufmann, 1988. The book that introduced Bayesian networks and belief propagation to a wide audience.
- Martin J. Wainwright and Michael I. Jordan, "Graphical models, exponential families, and variational inference", *Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning* 1(1–2), 2008. One framework for mean field, Bethe, tree-reweighted and other variational methods.
- Lawrence R. Rabiner, "A tutorial on hidden Markov models and selected applications in speech recognition", *Proceedings of the IEEE* 77(2), 1989. The classic account of evaluation, decoding and learning.
- Simo Särkkä, *Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing*, Cambridge University Press, 2013. Kalman, extended, unscented and particle filters and their smoothers, in one notation.
- Judea Pearl, *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference*, 2nd edition, Cambridge University Press, 2009. Structural causal models, the back-door criterion and the do-calculus.
