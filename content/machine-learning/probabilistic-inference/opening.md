Probabilistic inference answers questions about the variables you cannot see, given the ones you can. A model gives a joint distribution over many variables; inference computes a marginal, a most probable assignment, a normalizing constant or a posterior over parameters from it. In the sprinkler network of the [Bayesian network](/bayesian-network/) entry, wet grass raises the probability of rain from its prior $1/2$ to $509/719 \approx 0.708$, and learning that it rained then lowers the probability that the sprinkler ran from $309/719$ to $99/509$. A speech recognizer finds the most likely words behind a sound with a hidden Markov model, a tracker estimates the position of a vehicle from noisy readings with a filter, and a decoder of turbo codes runs loopy belief propagation. The same few ideas serve all of them.

The difficulty is size. A joint distribution over $n$ binary variables has $2^n$ entries, and a direct sum over them doubles in cost with each new variable. Graphs make the problem smaller: when the joint factorizes over a graph, sums can be pushed inside products, and the cost depends on the width of the graph rather than on $n$. In general no method escapes the size: exact marginal inference is #P-hard and the most probable assignment is NP-hard. So the chapter keeps three kinds of answer apart. Some methods are exact, on trees or on graphs of small treewidth. Some give a bound, such as the lower bound on $\log Z$ from mean field or the upper bound from tree-reweighted messages. Some are approximate with no guarantee of convergence or of accuracy on a general graph, as loopy belief propagation and expectation propagation are. Sampling methods sit apart: their estimates converge as the number of samples grows, and some, such as annealed importance sampling, are unbiased for every sample size.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Bayesian reasoning and estimation.** [Bayes' theorem](/bayes-theorem/), [Bayesian inference](/bayesian-inference/), [conjugate priors](/conjugate-prior/) and the [marginal likelihood](/marginal-likelihood/); point estimates by [maximum likelihood](/maximum-likelihood-estimation/) and [MAP](/maximum-a-posteriori-estimation/); [expectation-maximization](/expectation-maximization/); and the [Gaussian process](/gaussian-process/) and [Dirichlet process](/dirichlet-process/) priors.
2. **Graphical models and independence.** [Conditional independence](/conditional-independence/), [Bayesian networks](/bayesian-network/) and [d-separation](/d-separation/), [Markov random fields](/markov-random-field/), the [Markov blanket](/markov-blanket/) and the [factor graph](/factor-graph/).
3. **Inference problems and their hardness.** [Marginal](/marginal-inference/) and [MAP inference](/map-inference/), [Cooper's theorem](/cooper-theorem/), the [Dagum-Luby theorem](/dagum-luby-theorem/) and the [partition function](/partition-function/).
4. **Exact inference.** [Variable elimination](/variable-elimination/) and the [elimination ordering](/elimination-ordering/), [belief propagation](/belief-propagation/) and [max-product](/max-product/) on trees, and the [junction tree algorithm](/junction-tree-algorithm/).
5. **Loopy and Gaussian message passing.** [Loopy belief propagation](/loopy-belief-propagation/), the [Bethe free energy](/bethe-free-energy/), [tree-reweighted belief propagation](/tree-reweighted-belief-propagation/) and [Gaussian belief propagation](/gaussian-belief-propagation/).
6. **Hidden Markov models.** The [model](/hidden-markov-model/), the [forward](/forward-algorithm/), [Viterbi](/viterbi-algorithm/) and [forward-backward](/forward-backward-algorithm/) algorithms, [Baum-Welch](/baum-welch-algorithm/) training, and the [conditional random field](/conditional-random-field/).
7. **Sampling.** [Ancestral sampling](/ancestral-sampling/), [likelihood weighting](/likelihood-weighting/), [resampling](/resampling/), [annealed importance sampling](/annealed-importance-sampling/) and [probabilistic programming](/probabilistic-programming/).
8. **State-space models and filtering.** [Filtering](/filtering/) and [smoothing](/smoothing/), the [extended](/extended-kalman-filter/) and [unscented](/unscented-kalman-filter/) Kalman filters, the [particle filter](/particle-filter/) and [sequential Monte Carlo](/sequential-monte-carlo/).
9. **Variational inference.** The [evidence lower bound](/evidence-lower-bound/), [variational inference](/variational-inference/) and [mean field](/mean-field-approximation/), [expectation propagation](/expectation-propagation/), the stochastic methods, and [latent Dirichlet allocation](/latent-dirichlet-allocation/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fifteen entries in order: Bayes' theorem, conditional independence, the Bayesian network, d-separation, the Markov random field, the factor graph, variable elimination, belief propagation, the hidden Markov model, the forward algorithm, the Viterbi algorithm, expectation-maximization, filtering, likelihood weighting and variational inference. They give the models, the exact methods for trees and chains, one way to learn, one way to sample and one way to approximate.

Skip at first the hardness theorems of group 3, the variants of loopy belief propagation in group 5, and the research entries: annealed importance sampling, the stochastic variational methods, the wake-sleep algorithm and the Dirichlet process. Read the causal entries of group 10 when you need to reason about interventions rather than observations.

## Before you start

You need [conditional probability](/conditional-probability/), [probability distributions](/probability-distribution/) and [independent random variables](/independent-random-variables/), and a [Markov chain](/markov-chain/) with its transition matrix. The graph entries use [directed acyclic graphs](/directed-acyclic-graph/), [topological sort](/topological-sort/) and [graph reachability](/graph-reachability/); exact inference uses [treewidth](/treewidth/), [chordal graphs](/chordal-graph/), [tree decompositions](/tree-decomposition/) and a [maximum spanning tree](/maximum-spanning-tree/). The sampling entries build on [importance sampling](/importance-sampling/), [inverse transform sampling](/inverse-transform-sampling/), [Gibbs sampling](/gibbs-sampling/) and [Metropolis-Hastings](/metropolis-hastings/). The variational entries use [Jensen's inequality](/jensens-inequality/), [Shannon entropy](/shannon-entropy/) and [stochastic gradient descent](/stochastic-gradient-descent/). The Gaussian entries need a [covariance matrix](/covariance-matrix/) and the [Cholesky decomposition](/cholesky-decomposition/). Viterbi decoding is a shortest path in a [DAG](/dag-shortest-paths/), and the conditional random field extends [logistic regression](/logistic-regression/).
