## History

The core algorithm is older than its use for networks. Robert Wengert described forward-mode [automatic differentiation](/automatic-differentiation/) in 1964. Seppo Linnainmaa gave the reverse mode in his master's thesis of 1970, and Paul Werbos proposed using it to train networks in his thesis of 1974. David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton and Ronald Williams made [backpropagation](/backpropagation/) widely known with their *Nature* paper of 1986. Andreas Griewank showed in 1992 how checkpoints trade time for memory in the reverse mode, and Tianqi Chen and coauthors brought [gradient checkpointing](/gradient-checkpointing/) to deep networks in 2016.

Depth brought its own problems. Sepp Hochreiter analyzed the [vanishing gradient problem](/vanishing-gradient-problem/) in his diploma thesis of 1991, and Yoshua Bengio, Patrice Simard and Paolo Frasconi studied it for recurrent networks in 1994. Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber answered it with the LSTM in 1997. Xavier Glorot and Bengio derived their initialization in 2010, the same year Vinod Nair and Hinton used the [ReLU](/relu/) in restricted Boltzmann machines. Kaiming He, Xiangyu Zhang, Shaoqing Ren and Jian Sun adapted the initialization to ReLU networks in 2015. Normalization layers followed quickly: Sergey Ioffe and Christian Szegedy introduced [batch normalization](/batch-normalization/) in 2015, Jimmy Ba, Jamie Kiros and Hinton [layer normalization](/layer-normalization/) in 2016, Tim Salimans and Diederik Kingma weight normalization in 2016, and Takeru Miyato and coauthors spectral normalization in 2018.

Most optimizers of the chapter refine Adam. Ilya Loshchilov and Frank Hutter separated weight decay from the adaptive step in [AdamW](/adamw/), first as a preprint of 2017 and then at ICLR in 2019. Sashank Reddi, Satyen Kale and Sanjiv Kumar found a convex problem on which Adam fails and proposed AMSGrad in 2018. Noam Shazeer and Mitchell Stern factored the second moment in Adafactor in 2018. Yang You, Igor Gitman and Boris Ginsburg introduced LARS in 2017, and You and coauthors LAMB in 2020. James Martens and Roger Grosse introduced K-FAC in 2015, and Pierre Foret and coauthors [sharpness-aware minimization](/sharpness-aware-minimization/) in 2021. Paulius Micikevicius and coauthors described [mixed precision training](/mixed-precision-training/) with loss scaling in 2018.

[Dropout](/dropout/) appeared in a preprint by Hinton and coauthors in 2012, with the full paper by Nitish Srivastava and coauthors in 2014. Hinton introduced [contrastive divergence](/contrastive-divergence/) in 2002, and with Oriol Vinyals and Jeff Dean gave [knowledge distillation](/knowledge-distillation/) its name in 2015. Ian Goodfellow, Jonathon Shlens and Szegedy proposed the fast gradient sign attack in 2015, and Aleksander Madry and coauthors wrote [adversarial training](/adversarial-training/) as a min-max problem in 2018. Yann LeCun, John Denker and Sara Solla pruned networks with second-order saliencies in 1990.

The last entries come from recent theory and from search. Arthur Jacot, Franck Gabriel and Clément Hongler defined the [neural tangent kernel](/neural-tangent-kernel/) in 2018. Mikhail Belkin, Daniel Hsu, Siyuan Ma and Soumik Mandal named [double descent](/double-descent/) in 2019, the year Jonathan Frankle and Michael Carbin stated the [lottery ticket hypothesis](/lottery-ticket-hypothesis/). James Bergstra and Bengio argued for random search over grids in 2012, and Barret Zoph and Quoc Le started modern [neural architecture search](/neural-architecture-search/) with a reinforcement learning controller in 2017.

## Further reading

- Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio and Aaron Courville, *Deep Learning*, MIT Press (2016). Part II covers backpropagation, regularization, optimization and normalization at the level of this chapter.
- Andreas Griewank and Andrea Walther, *Evaluating Derivatives*, second edition, SIAM (2008). The standard text on automatic differentiation, with the cost bounds and checkpointing in full.
- Grégoire Montavon, Geneviève Orr and Klaus-Robert Müller (eds.), *Neural Networks: Tricks of the Trade*, second edition, LNCS 7700, Springer (2012). Practical chapters on initialization, learning rates, early stopping and tuning, written by the people who found the tricks.
- Jürgen Schmidhuber, *Deep Learning in Neural Networks: An Overview*, Neural Networks 61 (2015). A long survey with detailed credit for who found what and when.
- Thomas Elsken, Jan Hendrik Metzen and Frank Hutter, *Neural Architecture Search: A Survey*, Journal of Machine Learning Research 20 (2019). A short map of search spaces, search strategies and the cost of evaluating candidates.
