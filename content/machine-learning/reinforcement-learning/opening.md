Reinforcement learning is learning to act from the consequences of acting. An agent sees a state, chooses an action, and gets a reward and a next state; nobody tells it which action was right. It must find a policy whose rewards add up to a large total, often when a good move pays off only many steps later. A controller that learns to balance a pole on a cart, as the first [actor-critic](/actor-critic/) did in 1983, a program that learns Atari games from the pixels and the score, as [DQN](/dqn/) did on 49 games, and a doctor who must assign the next patient to one of two treatments while the evidence is still coming in, as in Thompson's question of 1933, all face this problem. So does the fine tuning of language models from human preferences, which has been done with [PPO](/ppo/).

The model behind the chapter is the [Markov decision process](/markov-decision-process/): finite or continuous, with a discount or with average reward, and either known to the algorithm or only sampled by it. Its one-state special case is the [multi-armed bandit](/multi-armed-bandit/), stochastic or chosen by an adversary. Methods differ in what they assume about the model and in what they promise. Some guarantees hold for every finite MDP: with $\gamma < 1$, [value iteration](/value-iteration/) finds an $\varepsilon$-optimal policy after $O\big(\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\log\frac{R_{\max}}{\varepsilon(1-\gamma)}\big)$ sweeps. Some bound an expectation: on every bandit with rewards in $[0, 1]$, the expected regret of [UCB1](/ucb1/) after $n$ rounds is at most $\sum_i 8\ln n/\Delta_i$ plus a constant, where $\Delta_i$ is the gap of arm $i$. Some hold only in the limit: tabular [Q-learning](/q-learning/) reaches the optimal action values with probability 1 if every pair is tried infinitely often and the step sizes meet the Robbins-Monro conditions. And the methods that train neural networks, such as DQN and PPO, have no convergence guarantee at all; their entries describe the update rule and the invariants it keeps. Each entry says which of these it offers, and the pages of this chapter keep the same care.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in ten groups, read in order.

1. **Decisions, rewards and values.** The [MDP](/markov-decision-process/), the [discount factor](/discount-factor/), [policies](/policy/), the [state](/state-value-function/) and [action](/action-value-function/) value functions, and the [Bellman expectation](/bellman-expectation-equation/) and [optimality](/bellman-optimality-equation/) equations.
2. **Planning with a known model.** [Iterative policy evaluation](/iterative-policy-evaluation/), [value iteration](/value-iteration/), [policy iteration](/policy-iteration/) and the [linear program](/linear-programming-for-mdps/), with the [contraction](/bellman-contraction/) and [policy improvement](/policy-improvement-theorem/) theorems that prove them.
3. **Bandits.** [ε-greedy](/epsilon-greedy/), [UCB1](/ucb1/), [Thompson sampling](/thompson-sampling/), the [Lai-Robbins lower bound](/lai-robbins-lower-bound/), [EXP3](/exp3/) for adversarial rewards, [contextual bandits](/contextual-bandit/) and the [Gittins index](/gittins-index/).
4. **Learning values from experience.** [Monte Carlo evaluation](/monte-carlo-policy-evaluation/), [TD learning](/temporal-difference-learning/), [$n$-step TD](/n-step-td/), [TD(λ)](/td-lambda/), and the [stochastic approximation](/stochastic-approximation/) theory behind their convergence.
5. **Control from experience.** [SARSA](/sarsa/), [Q-learning](/q-learning/), [Expected SARSA](/expected-sarsa/), [Double Q-learning](/double-q-learning/), the [convergence of tabular Q-learning](/tabular-q-learning-convergence/), and [importance sampling](/importance-sampling-off-policy/) for learning off-policy.
6. **Learning and using a model.** [Dyna-Q](/dyna-q/), [prioritized sweeping](/prioritized-sweeping/), the [successor representation](/successor-representation/) and [MuZero](/muzero/).
7. **Value function approximation.** [Linear TD](/value-function-approximation/) and [LSTD](/least-squares-td/), the [deadly triad](/deadly-triad/) and [gradient TD](/gradient-temporal-difference/), [fitted Q-iteration](/fitted-q-iteration/), and [DQN](/dqn/) with its refinements.
8. **Policy gradient methods.** The [policy gradient theorem](/policy-gradient-theorem/), [REINFORCE](/reinforce/), [actor-critic](/actor-critic/), [GAE](/generalized-advantage-estimation/), [TRPO](/trpo/), [PPO](/ppo/), [DDPG](/ddpg/), [TD3](/td3/) and [soft actor-critic](/soft-actor-critic/).
9. **Off-policy evaluation and offline learning.** [Off-policy evaluation](/off-policy-evaluation/), [Retrace](/retrace/), [V-trace](/v-trace/), [offline RL](/offline-reinforcement-learning/) and [conservative Q-learning](/conservative-q-learning/).
10. **Beyond one agent and a given reward.** [POMDPs](/pomdp/), [options](/options-framework/), [Markov games](/markov-game/), [imitation learning](/imitation-learning/), [inverse RL](/inverse-reinforcement-learning/) and [reward shaping](/potential-based-reward-shaping/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fifteen entries in order: the Markov decision process, the discounted return, the state value function, the action-value function, the Bellman expectation equation, the Bellman optimality equation, value iteration, policy iteration, the multi-armed bandit, ε-greedy, UCB1, Monte Carlo policy evaluation, TD learning, SARSA and Q-learning. They give the model, the two planning methods, the simplest exploration rules and the two basic learning rules. Then read [value function approximation](/value-function-approximation/), DQN, REINFORCE and actor-critic for the step to large problems.

Skip at first the theorems on stochastic approximation, the linear programming formulation, the Gittins index and the research entries of the last two groups. Come back to the [contraction theorem](/bellman-contraction/) early, though: most convergence proofs in the chapter reduce to it.

## Before you start

You need [expected value](/expected-value/) and [conditional expectation](/conditional-expectation/), the [law of large numbers](/law-of-large-numbers/) and [Hoeffding's inequality](/hoeffding-inequality/), and the [Markov chain](/markov-chain/) with its [stationary distribution](/stationary-distribution/). The contraction proofs use the [Banach fixed-point theorem](/banach-fixed-point-theorem/), and the bandit entries measure [regret](/regret/) as in online learning, where [EXP3](/exp3/) builds on [multiplicative weights](/multiplicative-weights-update/). Off-policy methods use [importance sampling](/importance-sampling/). The convergence theory starts from the [Robbins-Monro algorithm](/robbins-monro-algorithm/) and [martingales](/martingale/). The methods that learn with features need [least squares](/least-squares-problem/), [stochastic gradient descent](/stochastic-gradient-descent/) and, for policy gradients, the [gradient](/gradient/); TRPO and the natural gradient use the [Fisher information matrix](/fisher-information-matrix/) and the [conjugate gradient method](/conjugate-gradient-method/). Thompson sampling uses [Bayes' theorem](/bayes-theorem/) and [conjugate priors](/conjugate-prior/), and MuZero uses [Monte Carlo tree search](/monte-carlo-tree-search/).
