A circuit layout program checks that no two wires on one layer touch. A map overlay program puts a road map on top of a river map and needs every bridge, that is, every place where a road crosses a river. A game checks, sixty times a second, whether the player's ship overlaps a rock. All three ask one question about a set of geometric objects: which of them meet, and where.

For two objects the answer is a short calculation. For $n$ objects, testing every pair costs $\Theta(n^2)$ time, and most of the chapter is about doing better. The main tool is the sweep line. A vertical line moves from left to right across the plane and stops at the end points of the segments. At each moment it keeps the segments it crosses in vertical order, and the key fact is that the leftmost crossing is between two segments that are next to each other in that order just before it. So the sweep tests only neighbors, and a set of $n$ segments with no crossing is checked in $O(n \log n)$ time instead of $\Theta(n^2)$.

The chapter also covers intersections that are not between segments: half-planes, convex polygons, rectangles and lines. In each case some structure of the objects, convexity or axis-parallel sides, is what makes a fast algorithm possible.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Two objects at a time.** How to write a [line](/line-representation/), and how to intersect [two lines](/line-line-intersection/), [two segments](/segment-intersection-point/), a [line and a circle](/circle-line-intersection/) and [two circles](/circle-circle-intersection/). Exact arithmetic and the degenerate cases get as much attention as the formulas.
2. **Many segments.** [Detecting](/segment-intersection-detection/) whether any two of $n$ segments meet, with the [Shamos-Hoey sweep](/shamos-hoey-algorithm/) in $O(n \log n)$ time and a [matching lower bound](/segment-intersection-lower-bound/). Then [reporting all $k$ crossings](/all-segment-intersections/), with the [Bentley-Ottmann sweep](/bentley-ottmann-algorithm/) in $O((n + k) \log n)$ time and the optimal $O(n \log n + k)$ method of [Chazelle and Edelsbrunner](/chazelle-edelsbrunner-algorithm/).
3. **Axis-parallel segments and boxes.** [Horizontal and vertical segments](/orthogonal-segment-intersection/) and [rectangles](/rectangle-intersection-reporting/) meet in $O(n \log n + k)$ time with simpler structures. [Klee's measure problem](/klee-measure-problem/) asks for the area or volume of a union of boxes.
4. **Half-planes and convex polygons.** The [intersection of half-planes](/half-plane-intersection/) by [sorting and a deque](/half-plane-intersection-deque/), and the [intersection of two convex polygons](/convex-polygon-intersection/) in linear time.
5. **Collision tests for convex objects.** The [separating axis theorem](/separating-axis-theorem/), the [GJK algorithm](/gjk-algorithm/) and the logarithmic test of [Chazelle and Dobkin](/chazelle-dobkin-algorithm/).
6. **Arrangements of lines.** The subdivision cut out by $n$ lines, its [counts](/line-arrangement/), and its [construction](/line-arrangement-construction/) in $O(n^2)$ time by the [zone theorem](/edelsbrunner-o-rourke-seidel-algorithm/).
