## History

Michael Shamos and Dan Hoey started the subject in a 1975 paper on closest-point problems. They solved the closest pair by divide and conquer in $O(n \log n)$ time, and read the nearest neighbors and the Euclidean minimum spanning tree from the Voronoi diagram. Bentley and Shamos carried divide and conquer to every fixed dimension in 1976. In the same year Michael Rabin gave a randomized grid algorithm with linear expected time, and in 1979 Fortune and Hopcroft gave a deterministic $O(n \log \log n)$ method; both use the floor function. The lower bounds came next: Dobkin and Lipton (1979), Steele and Yao (1982) and Ben-Or (1983) showed that algebraic decision and computation trees need $\Omega(n \log n)$ steps. Hinrichs, Nievergelt and Schorn gave the sweep line version in 1988, and in 1995 Khuller and Matias, and Golin, Raman, Schwarz and Smid, gave the simple randomized algorithms used today.

The proximity graphs came from outside computer science. Gabriel and Sokal defined their graph in 1969 to study geographic variation in biology, and Matula and Sokal placed it between the minimum spanning tree and the Delaunay triangulation in 1980. Toussaint introduced the relative neighborhood graph in 1980 to describe the shape of a set of points. Urquhart proposed in the same year to compute it by removing the longest side of every Delaunay triangle; the shortcut gives a slightly larger graph, which now carries his name. Supowit computed the RNG in $O(n \log n)$ time in 1983, Kirkpatrick and Radke put the Gabriel graph and the RNG on one scale with the $\beta$-skeletons in 1985, and Jaromczyk and Toussaint surveyed the family in 1992.

Spanners grew out of the minimum spanning tree. Yao used the nearest point in each of a few cones in 1982 to find minimum spanning trees in higher dimensions. Clarkson in 1987 and Keil in 1988 built the cone graphs now called Theta-graphs, Chew showed in 1989 that planar graphs can approximate all distances, and Keil and Gutwin bounded the stretch of the Delaunay triangulation in 1992; Xia brought the bound below 1.998 in 2013. Althöfer, Das, Dobkin, Joseph and Soares described the greedy spanner in 1993. Callahan and Kosaraju published the well-separated pair decomposition in 1995 with nearest neighbors, spanners and n-body simulation as applications.

Bentley invented the k-d tree in 1975, and Friedman, Bentley and Finkel analyzed its nearest neighbor search in 1977. Clarkson in 1983 and Vaidya in 1989 solved all nearest neighbors in $O(n \log n)$ time in every fixed dimension. In 1998 Arya, Mount, Netanyahu, Silverman and Wu gave the balanced box-decomposition tree for approximate queries, and Indyk and Motwani introduced locality-sensitive hashing.

The distances between sets and curves are older than any algorithm: Felix Hausdorff defined his distance in 1914, and Maurice Fréchet his distance between curves in 1906. Huttenlocher, Klanderman and Rucklidge used the Hausdorff distance to compare images in 1993. Eiter and Mannila gave the dynamic program for the discrete Fréchet distance in 1994, and Alt and Godau the free space algorithm for the continuous one in 1995. In 2014 Bringmann showed that a strongly subquadratic algorithm would refute the strong exponential time hypothesis.

## Further reading

- Franco P. Preparata and Michael Ian Shamos, *Computational Geometry: An Introduction* (1985). Chapter 5 treats the closest pair, all nearest neighbors and the minimum spanning tree through the Voronoi diagram, with the lower bounds.
- Mark de Berg, Otfried Cheong, Marc van Kreveld and Mark Overmars, *Computational Geometry: Algorithms and Applications*, third edition (2008). Its chapters on Voronoi diagrams and Delaunay triangulations build the structures that most proximity graphs are cut from.
- Jerzy W. Jaromczyk and Godfried T. Toussaint, *Relative Neighborhood Graphs and Their Relatives*, Proceedings of the IEEE 80 (1992). A survey of the RNG, the Gabriel graph, the $\beta$-skeletons and their algorithms.
- Giri Narasimhan and Michiel Smid, *Geometric Spanner Networks* (2007). The standard book on Theta-graphs, the well-separated pair decomposition, the greedy spanner and their lower bounds.
- Helmut Alt and Michael Godau, *Computing the Fréchet Distance Between Two Polygonal Curves*, International Journal of Computational Geometry and Applications 5 (1995). The paper that introduced the free space diagram.
