An air traffic system tracks hundreds of aircraft and must warn when two of them come too close. A map service must find the gas station nearest to a driver. A biologist wants to know how far a measured protein backbone strays from a predicted one. All three ask which objects are near which, and how near. These are proximity problems, and the oldest of them is the closest pair: of $n$ points, which two are nearest each other? Testing all pairs takes $\Theta(n^2)$ time. Sorting-based methods take $\Theta(n \log n)$, which is optimal in the algebraic model, and a random grid takes linear expected time. The closest pair shows in a small space the main ideas of geometric algorithms: divide and conquer, sweeping, bucketing, randomization and lower bounds by reduction.

The same point set carries a family of graphs. Join each point to its nearest neighbor, or keep the edges whose diametral disk is empty, or keep the cheapest tree that connects everything, and you get graphs that nest inside one another and inside the Delaunay triangulation. Graphs of a different kind, spanners, keep every distance within a constant factor using only $O(n)$ edges.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Distances.** The [Euclidean distance](/euclidean-distance/) and why to compare it squared, the distance from a point to a [line](/point-to-line-distance/) and to a [segment](/point-to-segment-distance/), and the [Hausdorff distance](/hausdorff-distance/) between two sets.
2. **The closest pair.** The family of [proximity problems](/proximity-problems/), the [closest pair](/closest-pair-of-points/) itself, and its three algorithms: [divide and conquer](/closest-pair-divide-and-conquer/), the [sweep line](/closest-pair-sweep-line/) and the [randomized grid](/randomized-closest-pair/). The group ends with the [$\Omega(n \log n)$ lower bound](/closest-pair-lower-bound/).
3. **Other questions about pairs.** The [farthest pair](/farthest-pair/), the test for [collinear points](/collinear-points/), and the [bichromatic closest pair](/bichromatic-closest-pair/).
4. **Nearest neighbors.** The [nearest neighbor graph](/nearest-neighbor-graph/), [search in a k-d tree](/kd-tree-nearest-neighbor-search/), [all nearest neighbors](/all-nearest-neighbors/), [approximate](/approximate-nearest-neighbor/) and [reverse](/reverse-nearest-neighbor/) nearest neighbors.
5. **Proximity graphs.** The [chain of proximity graphs](/proximity-graphs/), the [Euclidean minimum spanning tree](/euclidean-minimum-spanning-tree/), the [Gabriel graph](/gabriel-graph/), the [relative neighborhood graph](/relative-neighborhood-graph/), the [Urquhart graph](/urquhart-graph/) and the [$\beta$-skeletons](/beta-skeleton/).
6. **Spanners and well-separated pairs.** [Geometric spanners](/geometric-spanner/), the [Yao graph](/yao-graph/) and the [well-separated pair decomposition](/well-separated-pair-decomposition/).
7. **Distances between curves.** The [Fréchet distance](/frechet-distance/) and the [algorithm of Alt and Godau](/alt-godau-algorithm/) that computes it.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these eleven entries in order: Euclidean distance, point to line, point to segment, Hausdorff distance, proximity problems, closest pair, closest pair by divide and conquer, farthest pair, collinear points, the nearest neighbor graph and search in a k-d tree. They are the six *Intro* and five *Core* entries of the chapter. Then read the lower bound and the randomized grid, which show why $n \log n$ is the right answer in one model and not in another, and the proximity graph overview, which ties groups 4 and 5 together. The sweep line, the Urquhart graph, the $\beta$-skeletons and the curve group can wait. The well-separated pair decomposition is the main research tool of the chapter; read it after the spanners.
