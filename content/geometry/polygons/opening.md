A polygon is the shape a program stores most often: a parcel on a map, a room in a floor plan, a glyph of a font, an obstacle for a robot, a window on a screen. It is given as a list of corners, and almost every question about it comes down to a few arithmetic formulas on their coordinates. Whether a point is inside, what the area is, which way the boundary runs and where two outlines cross are all answered this way. The formulas are short, but they must be exact on the awkward inputs, a point on an edge or three corners on a line, or the algorithms built on them give wrong answers without any warning.

This chapter has two jobs. Its first group is the toolkit of the whole volume: [points and vectors](/point-and-vector/), the [cross product](/cross-product/), the [dot product](/dot-product/) and the [geometry primitives](/geometry-primitives/) built from them. Every other chapter of the volume uses them, so a first-time reader of the volume should start here. The rest of the chapter is about polygons themselves: their kinds, their area, containment, cutting them up, combining them, and seeing inside them.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Points, vectors and the basic tests.** Points, vectors, the [cross](/cross-product/) and [dot](/dot-product/) products, the [oriented area of a triangle](/oriented-triangle-area/), [projection and reflection](/point-projection-and-reflection/), [rotation](/vector-rotation/), and the catalog of exact [primitives](/geometry-primitives/).
2. **Kinds of polygons.** [Polygons](/polygon/), [simple](/simple-polygon/), [convex](/convex-polygon/), [rectilinear](/rectilinear-polygon/), [star-shaped](/star-shaped-polygon/) and [monotone](/monotone-polygon/) polygons, and [polygons with holes](/polygon-with-holes/).
3. **Area, orientation and lattice points.** The [area problem](/polygon-area/), the [shoelace formula](/shoelace-formula/), [orientation](/polygon-orientation/), [Pick's theorem](/picks-theorem/) and [lattice points in polygons with rational vertices](/lattice-points-in-non-lattice-polygon/).
4. **Containment and simplicity tests.** [Point in polygon](/point-in-polygon/) by the [crossing number](/crossing-number-algorithm/) and the [winding number](/winding-number-algorithm/), the $O(\log n)$ test for [convex polygons](/point-in-convex-polygon/), and the [simplicity test](/polygon-simplicity-test/).
5. **Cutting polygons into pieces.** [Monotone partition](/monotone-polygon-partition/), [convex decomposition](/convex-decomposition/) and the [Hertel-Mehlhorn](/hertel-mehlhorn/) heuristic.
6. **Clipping, union and Minkowski sums.** [Sutherland-Hodgman](/sutherland-hodgman/), the general [clipping problem](/polygon-clipping/) with the algorithms of [Weiler and Atherton](/weiler-atherton-algorithm/), [Greiner and Hormann](/greiner-hormann-algorithm/) and [Vatti](/vatti-clipping/), [polygon union](/polygon-union/), and [Minkowski sums](/minkowski-sum/), with the linear merge for [convex polygons](/minkowski-sum-convex-polygons/).
7. **Visibility, guards and shortest paths.** The [kernel](/polygon-kernel/), the [art gallery problem](/art-gallery-problem/) and [theorem](/art-gallery-theorem/), the [visibility graph](/visibility-graph-of-polygon/), [shortest paths](/euclidean-shortest-path-in-polygon/) by the [funnel algorithm](/funnel-algorithm/), and the [watchman route](/watchman-route-problem/).
8. **Simplifying and shrinking.** [Polygon simplification](/polygon-simplification/) and the [straight skeleton](/straight-skeleton/).

## A first reading

Readers new to computational geometry should take the first five entries before any other chapter of the volume: [point and vector](/point-and-vector/), [cross product](/cross-product/), [dot product](/dot-product/), then the [orientation test](/orientation-test/) from the chapter on convex hulls, and [geometry primitives](/geometry-primitives/). Then continue here with [oriented area of a triangle](/oriented-triangle-area/), [polygon](/polygon/), [simple polygon](/simple-polygon/), [convex polygon](/convex-polygon/), [shoelace formula](/shoelace-formula/), [polygon orientation](/polygon-orientation/), [point in polygon](/point-in-polygon/), [crossing number algorithm](/crossing-number-algorithm/) and [Pick's theorem](/picks-theorem/). All fourteen are marked *Intro*. Projection, rotation and Sutherland-Hodgman are also *Intro* and can be read when needed. Groups 5 to 8 can wait until polygon triangulation is familiar, since many of their entries start from a triangulation.

The toolkit needs only coordinates and school algebra. Later entries use the [Jordan curve theorem](/jordan-curve-theorem/) from Volume 7, the [gcd](/gcd-of-two-integers/) and the [Euclidean algorithm](/euclidean-algorithm/) from Volume 10, [binary search](/binary-search/) from Volume 5, the [sweep line technique](/sweep-line-technique/) from Volume 2, and the [deque](/deque/) and [linked lists](/linked-list/) from Volume 3.
