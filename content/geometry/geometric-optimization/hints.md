The circle passes through $(0,0)$, $(8,0)$ and $(4,6)$, which form an acute triangle, so it is their circumcircle; $(1,3)$ and $(7,2)$ are at squared distances $97/9$ and $82/9$, below $169/9$. 4. All angles of the first triangle are below 120 degrees (about $78.7$, $78.7$ and $22.6$). The Fermat point is $(1, 1/\sqrt{3})$, where each side subtends 120 degrees, and the sum is $2 \cdot 2/\sqrt{3} + (5 - 1/\sqrt{3}) = 5 + \sqrt{3} \approx 6.732$; the formula $\sqrt{28 + 10\sqrt{3}}$ gives the same number. In the second triangle the angle at $(0,0)$ is about $161.6$ degrees, so the Fermat point is $(0,0)$ and the sum is $6 + \sqrt{10} \approx 9.162$.

8. $c_1 = (0,0)$ with $r^2 = 64$, $c_2 = (0,4)$ with $r^2 = 52$, $c_3 = (2, 8/3)$ with $r^2 = 292/9 \approx 32.4$, $c_4 = (3/2, 4)$ with $r^2 = 145/4 = 36.25$. The radius grows from step 3 to step 4, so it is not monotone. The optimum is the midpoint $c^* = (3,4)$ of the hypotenuse with $r^{*2} = 25$, and $|c_i - c^*|^2 = 25, 9, 25/9, 9/4$ stays below $25/i$.

9. The points are $(0,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(2,4)$, $(3,2)$, $(4,2)$, $(5,4)$, $(6,1)$. Twice the area of each triangle is a nonzero integer, so the smallest area is $1/2$, and it occurs. Scaling $[0,6]^2$ to the unit square divides areas by 36, so $\Delta(7) \ge 1/72$. Three strips of width $1/3$ hold seven points, so one holds three, and their triangle has area at most $1/6$. Hence $1/72 \le \Delta(7) \le 1/6$.

11. Removing $(1,3)$ or $(7,2)$ leaves the circle unchanged. Removing any of $(0,0)$, $(8,0)$, $(4,6)$ gives a smaller circle that misses the removed point; without $(4,6)$, for example, the circle has center $(4, 1/3)$ and squared radius $145/9$. So the probability is $3/5$, exactly the bound $3/n$, because three points fix the circle.

14. Pair each short side: each Steiner point sits at distance $1/(2\sqrt{3})$ inside a short side, at $(\sqrt{3}/6, 1/2)$ and $(2 - \sqrt{3}/6, 1/2)$. The four outer edges have length $1/\sqrt{3}$ and the middle edge $2 - \sqrt{3}/3$, so $\mathrm{SMT} = 2 + \sqrt{3} \approx 3.732$, against $\mathrm{MST} = 4$, a ratio of about $0.933$. For the other pairing the 120 degree rule puts the Steiner point of the bottom pair $1/\sqrt{3} \approx 0.577$ above the bottom side and that of the top pair $0.577$ below the top side, so they would cross over in a rectangle of height 1.

18. $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(4/5, 4/5, 1)$, since the weighted mean of the points is 0. Each corner has $w = 5/4 + 5/4 + 1 = 7/2$ and the center has $w = 1$; the weighted mean is $(4 \cdot 7/2 + 1)/5 = 3 = d + 1$. So $\kappa = 7/2$ and $\beta = (1/2)/(3 \cdot 5/2) = 1/15$; the chosen corner gets $19/75$ and the other four points $14/75$. The center is never the farthest point, so its weight shrinks by the factor $1 - \beta$ at every step and tends to 0, the corners tend to $1/4$, and the ellipse tends to the circle $x^2 + y^2 \le 2$ of area $2\pi$.

19. The shortest path is $s, (4,2), (8,2), t$, of length $4 + 2\sqrt{13} \approx 11.211$ and with 3 links. The segment $st$ crosses the notch above $y = 2$ between $x = 4$ and $x = 8$, so the link distance is at least 2. The line from $s$ through $(4,2)$ and the line from $t$ through $(8,2)$ meet at $(6, 2/3)$, and $s, (6, 2/3), t$ has 2 links and length $10\sqrt{13}/3 \approx 12.019$.

22. From $(1, R)$ the middle point is at distance $R$ and the two outer points at $\sqrt{1 + R^2}$, so the width is $\sqrt{1 + R^2} - R = 1/(\sqrt{1 + R^2} + R)$, which tends to 0. Width 0 would put the three points on one circle, which collinear points never are: the perpendicular bisectors of the two segments are parallel.

23. For three points with $x_1 \le x_2 \le x_3$, the $x$-parts of the three pairwise distances add to $2(x_3 - x_1)$, and likewise for $y$; so the three $L_1$ distances add to $2(\Delta x + \Delta y)$, and the two smallest, which form the spanning tree, add to at most $\frac{4}{3}(\Delta x + \Delta y)$. The Steiner tree has length exactly $\Delta x + \Delta y$. The points $(0,0)$, $(3,1)$, $(1,3)$ have all distances 4, $\mathrm{MST} = 8$ and $\mathrm{RSMT} = 6$. The plus shape has four terminals.

24. No point of $K$ projects beyond $a$ or $b$ on the line $ab$, or it would be farther than $D$ from the other end; so the rectangle has length $D$ and height $h_1 + h_2$, the largest distances of $K$ from the line on its two sides. The points at those distances and $a$, $b$ span a quadrilateral inside $K$ of area $D(h_1 + h_2)/2$. For a triangle, a rectangle flush with a side of length $\ell$ must also reach the opposite corner at height $h$, so its area is at least $\ell h$, twice the triangle.

25. If two points fix the circle, $r^* = |pq|/2 \le D/2$. Otherwise three points on the circle form a triangle with no obtuse angle; its largest angle $A$ is between 60 and 90 degrees, and the side opposite it has length $2r^* \sin A \ge 2r^* \sin 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}\, r^*$. That side is at most $D$, and equality needs all angles equal to 60 degrees.

26. Count the pairs $(R, x)$ with $x \notin R$ and $x < \min R$. Each gives the $(r+1)$-set $S = R \cup \{x\}$ with $x = \min S$, and each $(r+1)$-set arises from exactly one pair. So the violators summed over all $\binom{n}{r}$ samples number $\binom{n}{r+1}$, and the ratio is $(n - r)/(r + 1)$.

27. Since $|p - c|^2 = |p|^2 - 2p \cdot c + |c|^2$, the conditions $r^2 \le |p - c|^2 \le R^2$ read $b \le v_p \le a$ with $v_p = |p|^2 - 2p \cdot c$, $a = R^2 - |c|^2$, $b = r^2 - |c|^2$, and $R^2 - r^2 = a - b$. The center $(0,0)$ gives $R = 5$, $r = 3$ and area $16\pi$. For a lower bound, $0.4\,v_{(5,0)} + 0.4\,v_{(0,5)} + 0.1\,v_{(-5,0)} + 0.1\,v_{(0,-5)} - 0.5\,v_{(3,0)} - 0.5\,v_{(0,3)} = 25 - 9 = 16$ for every $c$, because the terms in $c$ cancel. The minimum-width annulus has $R^2 - r^2 = 37 - 17 = 20$, while the minimum-area one has width $2 > \sqrt{37} - \sqrt{17} \approx 1.960$.
