Put $n$ post offices on a map and give every point of the plane to the office nearest to it. The plane splits into $n$ convex cells, one per office, and the border between two cells is a piece of the perpendicular bisector of their offices. This partition is the Voronoi diagram. Where three cells meet, the corner is equally far from three offices, so it is the center of a circle through them with no office inside. Join two offices whenever their cells share an edge, and the result is a triangulation of the offices, the Delaunay triangulation, in which no office lies inside the circumcircle of any triangle. The two structures are dual: each Voronoi vertex is the circumcenter of one Delaunay triangle, and each gives the other in linear time.

Many questions about nearness reduce to them. The nearest office of a query point, the nearest neighbour of every site, the closest pair, the Euclidean minimum spanning tree and the largest empty circle all come from one of the two structures. Mesh generators and interpolation schemes start from the Delaunay triangulation, because among all triangulations of the points it maximizes the smallest angle. A third view ties the chapter together: lift each point $(x, y)$ to $(x, y, x^2 + y^2)$ on a paraboloid, and the Delaunay triangulation is the shadow of the lower convex hull of the lifted points.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Nearest sites and empty circles.** The [circumcircle](/circumcircle/) and [circumcenter](/circumcenter/), the [Voronoi cell](/voronoi-cell/) and [Voronoi diagram](/voronoi-diagram/), the [in-circle test](/in-circle-test/), the [empty circle property](/empty-circle-property/) and the [Delaunay triangulation](/delaunay-triangulation/).
2. **The structure theorems.** [Duality](/voronoi-delaunay-duality/), the [size of the diagram](/voronoi-complexity-theorem/), the role of [general position](/general-position-delaunay/), [uniqueness](/delaunay-triangulation-uniqueness/), the [local Delaunay criterion](/local-delaunay-criterion/), the [max-min angle property](/delaunay-max-min-angle/) and the [lifting map](/paraboloid-lifting-map/).
3. **Building the triangulation.** The two construction problems, for the [triangulation](/delaunay-triangulation-construction/) and for the [diagram](/voronoi-diagram-construction/); [Lawson's flips](/lawson-flip-algorithm/), the [Bowyer-Watson](/bowyer-watson-algorithm/) insertion, the divide and conquer of [Guibas and Stolfi](/guibas-stolfi-algorithm/) and its strip variant by [Dwyer](/dwyer-algorithm/); and the $\Omega(n \log n)$ [lower bound](/voronoi-construction-lower-bound/).
4. **Fortune's sweep.** The [beach line](/beach-line/) of parabolic arcs and [Fortune's algorithm](/fortunes-algorithm/), which builds the diagram directly.
5. **Updates, constraints and higher dimensions.** The [dynamic](/dynamic-delaunay-triangulation/) and [constrained](/constrained-delaunay-triangulation/) Delaunay triangulations, and [Delaunay triangulations in $d$ dimensions](/higher-dimensional-delaunay/).
6. **Other distances and other diagrams.** The [farthest-point](/farthest-point-voronoi-diagram/) and [order-$k$](/order-k-voronoi-diagram/) diagrams, the [power diagram](/power-diagram/) with its dual [regular triangulation](/regular-triangulation/), and the [additively and multiplicatively weighted](/weighted-voronoi-diagram/) diagrams.
7. **Using the diagrams.** The [largest empty circle](/largest-empty-circle/), the [medial axis](/medial-axis/), [alpha shapes](/alpha-shape/), [centroidal Voronoi tessellations](/centroidal-voronoi-tessellation/) and [Lloyd's algorithm](/lloyds-algorithm/).

## A first reading

Take these thirteen entries in order: [circumcircle](/circumcircle/), [Voronoi cell](/voronoi-cell/), [Voronoi diagram](/voronoi-diagram/), [in-circle test](/in-circle-test/), [empty circle property](/empty-circle-property/), [Delaunay triangulation](/delaunay-triangulation/), [duality](/voronoi-delaunay-duality/), [the size theorem](/voronoi-complexity-theorem/), [the local criterion](/local-delaunay-criterion/), [the max-min angle property](/delaunay-max-min-angle/), [the lifting map](/paraboloid-lifting-map/), [Lawson's flips](/lawson-flip-algorithm/) and [the Bowyer-Watson insertion](/bowyer-watson-algorithm/). Six of them are marked *Intro*; the in-circle test and the six entries after duality are *Core*. Together they give the definitions, the two counts $2n - 2 - h$ and $3n - 3 - h$, the local test that every algorithm runs, and two short algorithms that are easy to code. Fortune's algorithm and Guibas-Stolfi make a good second pass for $O(n \log n)$ worst-case time. Groups 5 and 6 can wait until a problem needs them. Dwyer's algorithm is the one *Research* entry, and the lower bound proof can be skipped by readers who know the [convex hull lower bound](/convex-hull-lower-bound/).
