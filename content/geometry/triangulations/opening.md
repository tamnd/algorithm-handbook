A graphics card draws triangles and nothing else. To fill the outline of a letter, a lake on a map or a room in a floor plan, a program first cuts the shape into triangles whose corners are the shape's own vertices. A simple polygon with $n$ vertices always splits into exactly $n - 2$ triangles by $n - 3$ diagonals, but in many ways, and the questions of this chapter are how to find one such cut quickly, how many there are, and how to choose a good one. The same questions come back for a set of points, where the triangles cover the convex hull, and for the meshes of engineering simulations, where the triangles must also be well shaped.

A triangulation is also a data structure. Once a polygon is cut into triangles, its area is a sum, its guards can be placed by coloring corners, a shortest path inside it follows a chain of triangles, and a point can be located by walking from triangle to triangle. Several later chapters start from a triangulation.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **What a triangulation is.** The definitions for [polygons](/polygon-triangulation/) and [point sets](/triangulation-of-point-set/), [ears](/ear/), the [dual tree](/triangulation-dual-tree/) of a polygon triangulation, and the [counting theorem](/triangulation-counting-theorem/), which says that the number of triangles depends only on $n$ and on the number $h$ of points on the hull boundary.
2. **Triangulating a simple polygon.** The [two ears theorem](/two-ears-theorem/) and [ear clipping](/ear-clipping/), the simple $\Theta(n^2)$ method; the $O(n \log n)$ method through [monotone pieces](/monotone-polygon-triangulation/); and [polygons with holes](/polygon-with-holes-triangulation/).
3. **Counting and optimizing.** The [Catalan count](/catalan-triangulation-theorem/) for convex polygons, the cubic [dynamic program](/polygon-triangulation-dp/) for the cheapest triangulation of a polygon, the [greedy triangulation](/greedy-triangulation/), the NP-hard [minimum-weight triangulation](/min-weight-triangulation/) of a point set, and [bounds on the number of triangulations](/number-of-triangulations/).
4. **Edge flips.** The [flip](/edge-flip/) of one diagonal, the [max-min angle triangulation](/max-min-angle-triangulation/) that flips reach, the [flip graph](/flip-graph/), [flip distance](/flip-distance/) and the [diameter theorem](/flip-graph-diameter-theorem/) for convex polygons.
5. **Constraints, Steiner points and meshes.** [Constrained triangulations](/constrained-triangulation/), [Steiner points](/steiner-points/), [mesh generation](/mesh-generation/) and [Delaunay refinement](/delaunay-refinement/), with the algorithms of [Ruppert](/ruppert-algorithm/) and [Chew](/chew-second-algorithm/).
6. **Faster polygon triangulation.** [Seidel's](/seidel-polygon-triangulation/) randomized $O(n \log^* n)$ method, the $O(n \log \log n)$ algorithm of [Tarjan and Van Wyk](/tarjan-van-wyk-triangulation/), and [Chazelle's](/chazelle-polygon-triangulation/) linear-time algorithm.
7. **Storage and three dimensions.** The [quad-edge structure](/quad-edge-data-structure/), [tetrahedralizations](/tetrahedralization/) and the [Schönhardt polyhedron](/schonhardt-polyhedron/).

Many entries in groups 4 and 5 lean on the Delaunay triangulation, the triangulation whose triangles have empty circumcircles. Its own chapter, [Chapter 13.4](/delaunay-triangulation/) on Voronoi diagrams and Delaunay triangulations, proves those facts; the entries here state the ones they need, so you can read this chapter first.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: [triangulation](/triangulation/), [ear](/ear/), [polygon triangulation](/polygon-triangulation/), [the dual tree](/triangulation-dual-tree/), [triangulation of a point set](/triangulation-of-point-set/), [the counting theorem](/triangulation-counting-theorem/), [the two ears theorem](/two-ears-theorem/), [ear clipping](/ear-clipping/), [the Catalan count](/catalan-triangulation-theorem/), [monotone polygon triangulation](/monotone-polygon-triangulation/), [edge flip](/edge-flip/) and [the max-min angle triangulation](/max-min-angle-triangulation/). The first nine are marked *Intro* and the last three *Core*. The other *Core* entries, the dynamic program, the greedy triangulation and mesh generation, make a good second pass. Groups 6 and 7 and the research entries of groups 3 and 4 can wait; the faster triangulation algorithms are worth reading for their ideas, not for use.
