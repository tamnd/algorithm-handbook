## History

Johann Radon showed in 1917 that a smooth function on the plane is determined by its integrals along lines, and gave an inversion formula. The paper was pure mathematics, and it stayed unknown in medicine for decades. Ronald Bracewell found the slice relation in radio astronomy in 1956, and Allan Cormack found an inversion again in 1963 by circular harmonics, without knowing of Radon's work, and applied it to the attenuation of X-rays. Godfrey Hounsfield built the first clinical CT scanner at EMI and described it in 1973. Cormack and Hounsfield shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1979.

The analytic methods took their working form in the early 1970s. Ramachandran and Lakshminarayanan replaced the Fourier transforms by a convolution with the sampled band-limited ramp in 1971, and Shepp and Logan proposed their smoother kernel and their head phantom in 1974. Fan beams came next, with Parker's short-scan weights in 1982. For cone beams, Tuy gave an inversion formula and the condition on the orbit in 1983, Feldkamp, Davis and Kress published their approximate circular-orbit method in 1984, and Grangeat linked cone-beam data to the 3D Radon transform in 1991. Katsevich's exact filtered back-projection for the helix appeared in 2002.

The algebraic methods started in electron microscopy. Gordon, Bender and Herman published ART in 1970, Gilbert published SIRT in 1972, and Andersen and Kak proposed SART in 1984. Joseph's interpolating projector appeared in 1982 and Siddon's exact ray tracing in 1985. In emission tomography Shepp and Vardi applied the EM algorithm to the Poisson model in 1982, Hudson and Larkin made it fast with ordered subsets in 1994, and Novikov published an exact inversion of the attenuated transform in 2002.

Compressed sensing changed the questions asked about incomplete data after 2006. Sidky and Pan published ASD-POCS for sparse-view cone-beam CT in 2008, and total variation priors became a standard reference in low-dose work. The related problems are older: Gale and Ryser characterized 0-1 matrices with given row and column sums in 1957, and the Calderón problem of impedance tomography was posed by Calderón in 1980.

## Further reading

- Frank Natterer, *The Mathematics of Computerized Tomography*, SIAM Classics in Applied Mathematics 32 (2001). The theory of the Radon transform, sampling, stability and the analytic algorithms.
- Avinash C. Kak and Malcolm Slaney, *Principles of Computerized Tomographic Imaging*, SIAM Classics in Applied Mathematics 33 (2001). An engineering text on parallel, fan and algebraic reconstruction, with the discrete filter kernels.
- Gabor T. Herman, *Fundamentals of Computerized Tomography: Image Reconstruction from Projections*, second edition, Springer (2009). The discrete model, projectors and the algebraic methods.
- Frank Natterer and Frank Wübbeling, *Mathematical Methods in Image Reconstruction*, SIAM (2001). Cone-beam and attenuated problems and their numerical methods.
- L. A. Shepp and Y. Vardi, *Maximum likelihood reconstruction for emission tomography*, IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging 1(2) (1982). The paper that brought EM to PET.
