## History

Seismology located sources before it imaged anything. Geiger proposed in 1910 to locate an earthquake from arrival times alone, by linearizing the travel times around a trial hypocentre and correcting it by least squares, step by step; an English translation appeared in 1912. Exploration seismology turned the same waves to the search for oil. Dix related the velocity that fits a reflection hyperbola to the rms velocity of flat layers in 1955, and Mayne described common-depth-point stacking in 1962. Digital recording in the 1960s made the stacked section the standard product. Robinson applied Wiener prediction to seismic traces in 1957 and wrote the trace as a wavelet convolved with reflectivity, and Peacock and Treitel showed in 1969 how the gap of [predictive deconvolution](/predictive-deconvolution/) controls the output and the removal of multiples. Taner and Koehler computed velocity spectra in 1969, and Neidell and Taner defined semblance in 1971.

Migration became a computation in the 1970s, largely through the work of Claerbout and his students at Stanford. Claerbout stated the imaging principle in 1971: reflectors are where the downgoing and upgoing waves coincide in time. He introduced finite-difference migration with a one-way equation, and Claerbout and Doherty gave the 15-degree scheme in 1972. Loewenthal, Lu, Roberson and Sherwood introduced the [exploding reflector model](/exploding-reflector-model/) in 1976. In 1978 three methods appeared: Gazdag's phase shift, Stolt's Fourier migration, and Schneider's integral form of [Kirchhoff migration](/kirchhoff-migration/). Bleistein gave the weights that make Kirchhoff migration an asymptotic inverse in 1987.

In 1983 Baysal, Kosloff and Sherwood, McMechan, and Whitmore each published [reverse time migration](/reverse-time-migration/). In the same years Lailly (1983) and Tarantola (1984) posed seismic inversion as the least-squares fit of full waveforms, with the gradient computed by correlating a forward field with a back-propagated residual. This is the adjoint-state method, and it is the root of [full waveform inversion](/full-waveform-inversion/) and least-squares migration. Bunks and colleagues inverted low frequencies first in 1995 to widen the basin of attraction, Pratt worked the method out in the frequency domain in 1999, and Virieux and Operto surveyed the field in 2009. Nemeth, Wu and Schuster showed in 1999 that least-squares migration reduces the artifacts of incomplete data.

Multiples and passive data came last. Thorson and Claerbout posed the velocity stack as a least-squares problem in 1985, and Hampson replaced its hyperbolas by parabolas in 1986. Verschuur, Berkhout and Wapenaar gave [surface-related multiple elimination](/surface-related-multiple-elimination/) in 1992. Stoffa, Fokkema, de Luna Freire and Kessinger published split-step Fourier migration in 1990. Claerbout had shown in 1968 that the autocorrelation of a transmission record gives the reflection response; Campillo and Paul retrieved surface waves from correlations of earthquake coda in 2003, and Shapiro and colleagues imaged the crust from ambient noise in 2005, the start of [seismic interferometry](/seismic-interferometry/) at large scale.

## Further reading

- Öz Yilmaz, *Seismic Data Analysis*, Society of Exploration Geophysicists, 2001. The standard reference for processing, from deconvolution and velocity analysis to migration, with many field examples.
- Jon F. Claerbout, *Imaging the Earth's Interior*, Blackwell, 1985. The one-way wave equation, downward continuation and finite-difference migration from the person who introduced them.
- Norman Bleistein, Jack K. Cohen and John W. Stockwell Jr., *Mathematics of Multidimensional Seismic Imaging, Migration, and Inversion*, Springer, 2001. Kirchhoff migration and inversion as high-frequency asymptotics, with the weights derived.
- Jean Virieux and Stéphane Operto, An overview of full-waveform inversion in exploration geophysics, *Geophysics* 74(6), 2009. A survey of the method, its gradient, cycle skipping and the ways around it.
- R.-E. Plessix, A review of the adjoint-state method for computing the gradient of a functional with geophysical applications, *Geophysical Journal International* 167(2), 2006. A short derivation of the adjoint state with worked examples.
