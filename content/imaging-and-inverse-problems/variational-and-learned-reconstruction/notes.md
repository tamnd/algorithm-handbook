## History

Variational imaging took its modern form with the total variation model of Leonid Rudin, Stanley Osher and Emad Fatemi in 1992, which kept edges that quadratic penalties blur. Acar and Vogel (1994) proved existence and stability for such problems, and Chambolle and Lions (1997) studied them further. The solvers came next: Chambolle's dual projection algorithm in 2004, Bregman iteration by Osher, Burger, Goldfarb, Xu and Yin in 2005, which used an idea of Lev Bregman from 1967, the split Bregman method of Goldstein and Osher in 2009, and the primal-dual algorithm of Chambolle and Pock in 2011. Burger and Osher (2004) gave the first convergence rates in the Bregman distance, and Bredies, Kunisch and Pock introduced total generalized variation in 2010.

Sparse recovery has an older root in uncertainty principles. Donoho and Stark (1989) proved the discrete uncertainty principle $|\mathrm{supp}\,f|\cdot|\mathrm{supp}\,\hat f| \ge N$ and used it to recover sparse signals from partial Fourier data, and Donoho and Elad (2003) and Tropp (2004) proved recovery by basis pursuit and greedy methods under a coherence condition. The step from coherence to random measurements was made in 2004 and 2006, when Candès, Romberg and Tao proved exact recovery with high probability from random Fourier samples and Donoho named compressed sensing. Donoho and Tanner computed the phase transition of $\ell_1$ recovery from the face counts of random polytopes from 2005 on. Blumensath and Davies analyzed iterative hard thresholding and Needell and Tropp published CoSaMP, both in 2009, and Donoho, Maleki and Montanari derived approximate message passing in the same year, with state evolution proved by Bayati and Montanari in 2011.

Learned representations began with dictionaries. Olshausen and Field (1996) learned a sparse code for natural image patches and found atoms like the receptive fields of simple cells in the primary visual cortex. Aharon, Elad and Bruckstein published K-SVD in 2006, and Zeiler and coauthors learned convolutional filters in 2010. The imaging hardware is older: Dicke proposed masks with many random holes for X-rays and gamma rays in 1968, Fenimore and Cannon gave the uniformly redundant arrays in 1978, and Duarte and coauthors built the single-pixel camera in 2008.

Learning entered the reconstruction itself after 2010. Gregor and LeCun trained the layers of a truncated ISTA in 2010, and Haber and Tenorio (2003) had already posed the choice of a regularizer as a bilevel problem. Venkatakrishnan, Bouman and Wohlberg proposed plug-and-play priors in 2013, and Romano, Elad and Milanfar regularization by denoising in 2017. Bora, Jalal, Price and Dimakis used generative networks for compressed sensing in 2017, Adler and Öktem introduced learned primal-dual reconstruction and Ulyanov, Vedaldi and Lempitsky the deep image prior in 2018, and Chung and coauthors published diffusion posterior sampling in 2023.

## Further reading

- Otmar Scherzer, Markus Grasmair, Harald Grossauer, Markus Haltmeier and Frank Lenzen, *Variational Methods in Imaging* (Springer, 2009). The analysis of variational regularization: existence, stability, convergence and rates.
- Antonin Chambolle and Thomas Pock, "An introduction to continuous optimization for imaging", *Acta Numerica* 25 (2016). The solvers of this chapter, with their convergence results and many imaging models.
- Simon Foucart and Holger Rauhut, *A Mathematical Introduction to Compressive Sensing* (Birkhäuser, 2013). Null space property, coherence, restricted isometry, random matrices and the greedy algorithms, with full proofs.
- Martin Benning and Martin Burger, "Modern regularization methods for inverse problems", *Acta Numerica* 27 (2018). Variational and iterative regularization with Bregman distances, from the inverse problems side.
- Simon Arridge, Peter Maass, Ozan Öktem and Carola-Bibiane Schönlieb, "Solving inverse problems using data-driven models", *Acta Numerica* 28 (2019). A survey of learned reconstruction and of what is known about it.
