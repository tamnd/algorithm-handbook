An ultrasound probe on the skin and a radar on an aircraft solve the same problem. Each sends a pulse, listens for echoes, and turns the time of each echo into a distance. In soft tissue sound travels at about 1540 m/s, so an echo that comes back after 26 µs left a reflector 20 mm deep. One pulse gives one line of the image; an array of elements, or a sensor that moves, gives many lines, and the work of the algorithms is to combine them so that each pixel collects the echoes that belong to it. A synthetic aperture radar on an aircraft sends a pulse at least every half antenna length of its flight and combines the echoes into the image that an antenna hundreds of metres long would give.

The tools are shared. Delay-and-sum focuses an ultrasound array and, in the form of back-projection, a radar. The matched filter compresses a long pulse into a short one. Phase carries the fine information in both fields: the motion of blood, the height of a mountain from two radar passes. The images are coherent, so they show speckle, and the phase is known only modulo $2\pi$, so it must be unwrapped. The chapter ends with two methods where the wave itself is the image, photoacoustic and diffraction tomography.

## A first reading

For ultrasound, read the pulse-echo model, B-mode image formation and speckle, then dynamic receive focusing and plane-wave compounding. These five pages explain how a scanner makes a frame and why it looks grainy. The Kasai estimator adds colour Doppler.

For radar, read synthetic aperture radar, SAR image formation and range cell migration, then back-projection and the range-Doppler algorithm. Back-projection is the simplest focusing method and the one to check others against; the range-Doppler algorithm shows how the frequency-domain methods gain their speed. Phase unwrapping and SAR interferometry finish a first pass.

Skip at first the adaptive beamformers, chirp scaling, omega-k and the polar format algorithm, autofocus, the two unwrapping algorithms and the wave tomography group. They refine the methods above and assume them. Omega-k also uses the Stolt mapping of [Stolt migration](/stolt-migration/) from the chapter on seismic imaging.
