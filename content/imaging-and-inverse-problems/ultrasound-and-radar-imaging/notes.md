## History

Radar imaging grew from an idea of 1951: Carl Wiley noticed that the Doppler shift of the echoes from a moving radar tells targets apart along the flight track, and so sharpens a beam far beyond the width of the antenna. This is the principle of [synthetic aperture radar](/synthetic-aperture-radar/). The first processors were optical. Wu described a digital processor in 1976, and his steps of range compression, migration correction in the range-Doppler domain and azimuth compression became the standard [range-Doppler algorithm](/range-doppler-algorithm/). Walker described polar format processing of rotating objects in 1980, and Munson, O'Brien and Jenkins showed in 1983 that spotlight SAR is a tomography problem. Cafforio, Prati and Rocca derived the [omega-k algorithm](/omega-k-algorithm/) from the seismic migration of Stolt in 1991, and Raney, Runge, Bamler, Cumming and Wong published the [chirp scaling algorithm](/chirp-scaling-algorithm/) in 1994. Back-projection came back as computers grew: Desai and Jenkins used it for spotlight SAR in 1992, and Ulander, Hellsten and Stenström made it fast by factorization in 2003. Eichel, Ghiglia and Jakowatz proposed [phase gradient autofocus](/phase-gradient-autofocus/) in 1989.

Interferometry adds height. Graham proposed a radar interferometer for topographic mapping in 1974, Zebker and Goldstein made topographic maps from airborne data in 1986, and Goldstein, Zebker and Werner unwrapped satellite interferograms in 1988 with the residues and branch cuts that still carry their name. Itoh analysed one-dimensional unwrapping in 1982. Ghiglia and Romero solved the least-squares problem with fast cosine transforms in 1994, Costantini wrote the $L^1$ problem as a network flow in 1998, and Chen and Zebker proved the $L^0$ problem NP-hard in 2000.

Medical ultrasound images appeared in the 1950s, made with one transducer moved over the skin. Burckhardt in 1978 and Wagner, Smith, Sandrik and Lopez in 1983 gave the statistics of [speckle](/speckle/) in B-mode scans. Kasai, Namekawa, Koyano and Omoto used the lag-one autocorrelation for real-time colour flow imaging in 1985; the same pulse-pair estimate was known in weather radar. Capon introduced the minimum variance method for seismometer arrays in 1969, and Synnevåg, Austeng and Holm adapted it to medical ultrasound in 2007. Mallart and Fink defined the coherence factor in 1994.

Ultrafast imaging changed the field after 2000. Montaldo, Tanter, Bercoff, Benech and Fink showed in 2009 that a coherent sum of a few tilted plane waves reaches the quality of focused imaging at thousands of frames per second, and Demene and colleagues applied the [SVD clutter filter](/svd-clutter-filter/) to such data in 2015. In nondestructive testing, Doctor, Hall and Reid described SAFT in 1986, and Holmes, Drinkwater and Wilcox named the [total focusing method](/total-focusing-method/) in 2005.

Wave tomography rests on older mathematics. Wolf showed in 1969 that, under the first Born approximation, the scattered field gives the Fourier transform of a weak scatterer on spheres, and Mueller, Kaveh and Wade applied the idea to ultrasound in 1979. For photoacoustic tomography, Finch, Patch and Rakesh proved inversion formulas for the sphere in odd dimensions in 2004, and Xu and Wang gave [universal back-projection](/universal-back-projection/) in 2005.

## Further reading

- Thomas L. Szabo, *Diagnostic Ultrasound Imaging: Inside Out*, 2nd edition, Academic Press, 2014. The physics of medical ultrasound and the signal chain of a scanner, from the transducer to the B-mode and Doppler image.
- Ian G. Cumming and Frank H. Wong, *Digital Processing of Synthetic Aperture Radar Data*, Artech House, 2005. The standard text on the stripmap algorithms: range-Doppler, chirp scaling and omega-k, with worked parameters.
- Charles V. Jakowatz Jr. and others, *Spotlight-Mode Synthetic Aperture Radar: A Signal Processing Approach*, Kluwer, 1996. Spotlight SAR as tomography, with the polar format algorithm and phase gradient autofocus.
- Dennis C. Ghiglia and Mark D. Pritt, *Two-Dimensional Phase Unwrapping: Theory, Algorithms, and Software*, Wiley, 1998. The path-following and minimum-norm methods of unwrapping, with code.
- Joseph W. Goodman, *Speckle Phenomena in Optics: Theory and Applications*, Roberts and Company, 2007. The statistics of speckle from the random phasor sum, for optics and beyond.
