## History

The mathematics of homogenization took shape in the 1970s. Alain Bensoussan, Jacques-Louis Lions and George Papanicolaou collected the asymptotic expansions and their proofs in a book in 1978, and Gabriel Nguetseng (1989) and Grégoire Allaire (1992) gave two-scale convergence, which made the limits short to prove. On the engineering side, Rodney Hill had set out in 1963 what a representative volume element must satisfy. Frédéric Feyel turned the nested computation into the FE² method in 1999, and Thomas Hou and Xiao-Hui Wu put fine solves into coarse basis functions in 1997. Thomas Hughes explained stabilized finite elements as variational multiscale methods in 1995, and Axel Målqvist and Daniel Peterseim gave localized orthogonal decomposition in 2014.

Two general frameworks appeared in 2003: the heterogeneous multiscale method of Weinan E and Björn Engquist, and the equation-free methods of Ioannis Kevrekidis, William Gear and their coauthors, which include coarse projective integration. On the atomistic side, Ellad Tadmor, Michael Ortiz and Rob Phillips gave the quasicontinuum method in 1996, and Arieh Warshel and Michael Levitt the first QM/MM model of an enzyme reaction in 1976. Martin Karplus, Levitt and Warshel shared the 2013 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for multiscale models of complex chemical systems.

Rate theory is older. Henry Eyring wrote down transition state theory in 1935, and George Vineyard gave the harmonic formula for solids in 1957. Alfred Bortz, Malvin Kalos and Joel Lebowitz gave the rejection-free kinetic Monte Carlo step in 1975, and Daniel Gillespie the same direct method for chemical reactions in 1976. The tools for finding saddles came in the 1990s: the nudged elastic band of Greg Mills and Hannes Jónsson (1994), with the climbing image of Graeme Henkelman, Blas Uberuaga and Jónsson (2000), the dimer method of Henkelman and Jónsson (1999), and the string method of E, Weiqing Ren and Eric Vanden-Eijnden (2002). Arthur Voter gave hyperdynamics in 1997 and parallel replica dynamics in 1998, and with Mads Sørensen temperature-accelerated dynamics in 2000.

Coarse-graining rests on the projection operators of Robert Zwanzig (1961) and Hazime Mori (1965). Hoogerbrugge and Koelman introduced dissipative particle dynamics in 1992, and Pep Español and Patrick Warren found its fluctuation-dissipation relation in 1995. John Cahn and John Hilliard wrote the gradient free energy behind phase-field models in 1958, Samuel Allen and Cahn the equation for moving boundaries in 1979, and Stewart Silling proposed peridynamics in 2000.

## Further reading

- Weinan E, *Principles of Multiscale Modeling*, Cambridge University Press, 2011. The whole field from one point of view, from homogenization to rare events and the heterogeneous multiscale method.
- Grigorios A. Pavliotis and Andrew M. Stuart, *Multiscale Methods: Averaging and Homogenization*, Springer, 2008. Periodic homogenization and averaging with complete proofs, at the level of a graduate course.
- Ellad B. Tadmor and Ronald E. Miller, *Modeling Materials: Continuum, Atomistic and Multiscale Techniques*, Cambridge University Press, 2011. The Cauchy-Born rule, the quasicontinuum method and atomistic-continuum coupling from the materials side.
- Arthur F. Voter, Francesco Montalenti and Timothy C. Germann, "Extending the time scale in atomistic simulation of materials", *Annual Review of Materials Research* 32, 2002. A survey of hyperdynamics, parallel replica dynamics and temperature-accelerated dynamics by their authors.
- Peter Hänggi, Peter Talkner and Michal Borkovec, "Reaction-rate theory: fifty years after Kramers", *Reviews of Modern Physics* 62, 1990. The standard review of transition state theory and its corrections.
