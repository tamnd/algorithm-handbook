Most methods for fluid flow start from the [Navier-Stokes equations](/navier-stokes-equations/) and discretize them on a mesh: finite differences, finite volumes, finite elements. The velocity and pressure live at the nodes or in the cells, and for an incompressible fluid every time step must solve a Poisson equation for the pressure. This chapter's two written entries take other routes. Neither writes down a discrete Navier-Stokes operator, and both recover fluid behaviour from simpler local rules.

The [lattice Boltzmann method](/lattice-boltzmann-method/) keeps, at each node of a regular lattice, a handful of particle populations, one for each lattice velocity. A time step relaxes them toward an equilibrium built from the local density and momentum and then moves each population one node along its velocity. No linear system is solved; the pressure is just the density divided by 3. At low Mach number the density and velocity obey the Navier-Stokes equations with a viscosity set by the relaxation time. [Smoothed particle hydrodynamics](/smoothed-particle-hydrodynamics/) has no mesh at all. The fluid is a set of particles that move with the flow; the density at a particle is a kernel-weighted sum of the masses of its neighbours, and the pressure force between two particles is written so that it is equal and opposite. Free surfaces, splashes and fragments need no special treatment, because there is no mesh to tangle.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter has two groups, one for each method.

1. **Populations on a lattice.** The [lattice Boltzmann method](/lattice-boltzmann-method/) gives the D2Q9 lattice and its equilibrium, the BGK collision with a body force, and halfway bounce-back walls. It works the D1Q3 diffusion scheme by hand, where $\tau = 1$ gives exactly the [FTCS scheme](/ftcs-method/), and measures second-order convergence on the Taylor-Green vortex, a $\tau$-dependent slip in Poiseuille flow, and the instability of BGK as $\tau$ approaches $\frac12$.
2. **Particles without a mesh.** [Smoothed particle hydrodynamics](/smoothed-particle-hydrodynamics/) gives the cubic spline kernel, the symmetric pressure force and the Monaghan artificial viscosity, integrated by kick-drift-kick leapfrog, and runs the Sod shock tube against the exact solution of the [Riemann problem](/riemann-problem/).

## A first reading

Read the lattice Boltzmann entry first, through the two examples by hand; then the correctness section, which separates what is proved (exact conservation, the D1Q3 case) from what is cited (the Navier-Stokes limit, by the Chapman-Enskog expansion) and what is only observed. Then read the SPH entry, where the same care applies: the conservation laws are proved, but convergence to the Euler equations is observed, and on irregular particles the kernel sums are not even zeroth-order consistent.

## Before you start

From Volume 12 you need the [Navier-Stokes equations](/navier-stokes-equations/), the [heat equation](/heat-equation/), the [finite difference method](/finite-difference-method/) with the [FTCS scheme](/ftcs-method/), and for SPH the [Riemann problem](/riemann-problem/) and [Verlet integration](/verlet-integration/). The neighbour search of SPH is the [fixed-radius near neighbors](/fixed-radius-near-neighbors/) problem of Volume 5. The graphics versions of these ideas, in [fluid simulation for animation](/fluid-simulation-for-animation/) in Volume 28, trade accuracy for speed.
