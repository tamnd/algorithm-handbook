Water in a pipe, air around a car at road speed, blood in an artery and the ocean below its surface all move far slower than sound in them. Their density hardly changes, and the Navier-Stokes equations can be solved with the constraint $\nabla \cdot u = 0$ in place of an equation for the density. That constraint changes the numerical problem. The pressure no longer follows from an equation of state; it is the field that keeps the velocity free of divergence, and finding it means solving a Poisson equation, or something like one, at every step. Most of the methods of the first half of this chapter are ways to do that well.

The second half is about turbulence. At the Reynolds numbers of most engineering and natural flows the velocity is chaotic, with eddies from the size of the body down to sizes where viscosity takes over, and resolving all of them is far too expensive. So the flow is averaged or filtered, and a model stands in for the scales that are not resolved. These turbulence models are not derived from the equations: they are closures whose constants are mostly fitted to experiments, and the entries say where each one is known to work and where it fails. Two families of methods close the chapter. Lattice Boltzmann and smoothed particle hydrodynamics do not discretize the Navier-Stokes equations at all, and recover fluid behavior from particle populations on a lattice or from particles that move with the flow.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Incompressibility and the pressure.** The [Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition](/helmholtz-hodge-decomposition/) splits a field into a gradient and a divergence-free part; the [projection method](/chorin-projection-method/) of Chorin is built on it. The [vorticity-streamfunction formulation](/vorticity-streamfunction-formulation/) removes the pressure in 2D, [artificial compressibility](/artificial-compressibility-method/) marches to a steady state in pseudo-time, and the [marker-and-cell method](/marker-and-cell-method/) adds a free surface.
2. **Pressure-velocity coupling on finite volumes.** The [SIMPLE](/simple-algorithm/) and [PISO](/piso-algorithm/) algorithms of engineering codes, and [Rhie-Chow interpolation](/rhie-chow-interpolation/) for grids where pressure and velocity share the cell centers.
3. **Interfaces and immersed bodies.** The [volume of fluid method](/volume-of-fluid-method/) with [piecewise linear interface calculation](/piecewise-linear-interface-calculation/), the [continuum surface force](/continuum-surface-force/) model of surface tension, the [immersed boundary method](/immersed-boundary-method/) and [front tracking](/front-tracking-method/).
4. **Turbulence and its scales.** [Kolmogorov's 1941 theory](/kolmogorov-1941-theory/) and the cost of [direct numerical simulation](/direct-numerical-simulation/).
5. **Reynolds-averaged models.** The [Reynolds-averaged equations](/reynolds-averaged-navier-stokes/) and the [Boussinesq eddy viscosity](/boussinesq-eddy-viscosity/), then the [mixing length](/mixing-length-model/), [k-epsilon](/k-epsilon-model/), [k-omega](/k-omega-model/), [SST](/k-omega-sst-model/) and [Spalart-Allmaras](/spalart-allmaras-model/) models, and [wall functions](/wall-functions/).
6. **Large eddy simulation.** [Large eddy simulation](/large-eddy-simulation/) with the [Smagorinsky](/smagorinsky-model/) and [dynamic](/dynamic-subgrid-model/) subgrid models.
7. **Lattice Boltzmann methods.** The [lattice Boltzmann method](/lattice-boltzmann-method/), the [BGK collision operator](/bgk-collision-operator/), [bounce-back walls](/bounce-back-boundary-condition/), the [Chapman-Enskog expansion](/chapman-enskog-expansion/) and [multiple relaxation times](/multiple-relaxation-time-lattice-boltzmann/).
8. **Smoothed particle hydrodynamics.** [SPH](/smoothed-particle-hydrodynamics/), its [smoothing kernel](/sph-smoothing-kernel/), [Monaghan's artificial viscosity](/monaghan-artificial-viscosity/) and [incompressible SPH](/incompressible-sph/).

## A first reading

Start with the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, the vorticity-streamfunction formulation, Chorin's projection method and the SIMPLE algorithm: these carry the incompressible solvers. For interfaces, read the volume of fluid method. For turbulence, read Kolmogorov's theory, direct numerical simulation, the Reynolds-averaged equations, the Boussinesq hypothesis, the k-epsilon model, large eddy simulation and the Smagorinsky model. Then read the lattice Boltzmann method and SPH, one entry each. That makes fourteen entries.

Artificial compressibility, PISO, Rhie-Chow interpolation, PLIC, surface tension, immersed boundaries, front tracking, the other RANS models, wall functions, the dynamic model and the specialized lattice Boltzmann and SPH entries can wait. Each one refines a method of the first reading.

## Before you start

From Volume 12 you need the [Navier-Stokes equations](/navier-stokes-equations/), the [Poisson equation](/poisson-equation/), the [finite difference](/finite-difference-method/) and [finite volume](/finite-volume-method/) methods, the [CFL condition](/courant-friedrichs-lewy-condition/), [operator splitting](/operator-splitting/) and [spectral methods](/spectral-methods/); for the pressure solves, the [Gauss-Seidel method](/gauss-seidel-method/), [successive over-relaxation](/successive-over-relaxation/) and the [conjugate gradient method](/conjugate-gradient-method/). PLIC uses [polygon clipping](/polygon-clipping/) and the [shoelace formula](/shoelace-formula/) of Volume 13, and SPH uses the [fixed-radius near neighbors](/fixed-radius-near-neighbors/) search of Volume 5. The animation versions of several methods here, such as [stable fluids](/stable-fluids/), [FLIP](/fluid-implicit-particle/) and [weakly compressible SPH](/weakly-compressible-sph/), are in Chapter 28.12; they give up accuracy for speed at large time steps.
