## History

Sampling came first. Metropolis and Ulam named the Monte Carlo method in 1949, and its $N^{-1/2}$ rate has set the baseline for every later method. Structural engineers needed small failure probabilities long before the computing budget allowed plain sampling for them, so Hasofer and Lind gave the invariant reliability index in 1974, and Rackwitz and Fiessler turned it into the iteration that the [first-order reliability method](/first-order-reliability-method/) still uses (1978). Au and Beck introduced [subset simulation](/subset-simulation/) in 2001. Heinrich used a multilevel idea for parametric integration in 1998, and Giles made [multilevel Monte Carlo](/multilevel-monte-carlo/) a standard tool for stochastic differential equations in 2008. Peherstorfer, Willcox and Gunzburger gave the optimal allocation of [multifidelity Monte Carlo](/multifidelity-monte-carlo/) in 2016.

Spectral methods have older roots. Wiener's "homogeneous chaos" of 1938 expanded random functionals in Hermite polynomials of Gaussian variables, and Cameron and Martin proved in 1947 that the expansion converges in mean square. Ghanem and Spanos brought it to engineering in their 1991 book on stochastic finite elements, which is the start of the [stochastic Galerkin method](/stochastic-galerkin-method/). Xiu and Karniadakis matched other distributions to the Askey families of orthogonal polynomials in 2002. Xiu and Hesthaven (2005) and Babuška, Nobile and Tempone (2007) set out [stochastic collocation](/stochastic-collocation-method/), which reuses deterministic solvers.

Global sensitivity analysis rests on Hoeffding's 1948 decomposition. Cukier and coauthors proposed the [Fourier amplitude sensitivity test](/fourier-amplitude-sensitivity-test/) in 1973. Morris published his screening design in 1991. Sobol defined his indices in a Russian paper of 1990, translated in 1993, and Homma and Saltelli added the total indices in 1996. Saltelli's 2002 sampling scheme and Jansen's 1999 estimator are the usual way to compute them. Kennedy and O'Hagan gave the Bayesian treatment of calibration with model discrepancy in 2001, and Stuart's 2010 survey in Acta Numerica set out the function-space view of Bayesian inverse problems and their well-posedness.

Reduced models grew out of turbulence and structural mechanics. Lumley brought proper orthogonal decomposition to turbulence in 1967, and Sirovich's method of snapshots (1987) made it cheap. The [reduced basis method](/reduced-basis-method/) took its modern form with the a posteriori bounds of Prud'homme and coauthors in 2002. Barrault, Maday, Nguyen and Patera introduced the [empirical interpolation method](/empirical-interpolation-method/) in 2004, and Chaturantabut and Sorensen gave its discrete form in 2010. Binev and coauthors and Buffa and coauthors proved the convergence rates of the weak greedy algorithm in 2011 and 2012. Ammar and coauthors introduced the [proper generalized decomposition](/proper-generalized-decomposition/) in 2006, and Peherstorfer and Willcox introduced [operator inference](/operator-inference/) in 2016.

## Further reading

- Ralph C. Smith, *Uncertainty Quantification: Theory, Implementation, and Applications*, SIAM, 2014. A broad first text, from random fields and sensitivity analysis to surrogates and calibration, with engineering examples.
- T. J. Sullivan, *Introduction to Uncertainty Quantification*, Springer, Texts in Applied Mathematics 63, 2015. A mathematical text that covers Bayesian inverse problems, polynomial chaos and the proofs this chapter only cites.
- Dongbin Xiu, *Numerical Methods for Stochastic Computations: A Spectral Method Approach*, Princeton University Press, 2010. A short and clear account of generalized polynomial chaos, stochastic Galerkin and collocation.
- Andrea Saltelli and coauthors, *Global Sensitivity Analysis: The Primer*, Wiley, 2008. The practical guide to Morris screening, Sobol indices and their estimators.
- Michael B. Giles, "Multilevel Monte Carlo methods", *Acta Numerica* 24, 2015. The survey of the multilevel method, its complexity theorem and its many variants.
- Jan S. Hesthaven, Gianluigi Rozza and Benjamin Stamm, *Certified Reduced Basis Methods for Parametrized Partial Differential Equations*, Springer, 2016. A compact text on reduced bases, greedy sampling, error bounds and empirical interpolation.
