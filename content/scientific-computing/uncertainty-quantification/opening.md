The permeability of the rock around a planned waste repository is known at a few boreholes and nowhere else. A groundwater model needs it at every cell, so it is uncertain, and so is every answer the model gives: the travel time of a particle to the surface, the peak concentration in a well. Running the model once at the most likely permeability is not enough, because the output at the mean input is not the mean output. For $Q = e^{\xi}$ with $\xi$ standard normal, the model at the mean gives 1, while $\mathbb{E}[Q] = 1.649$, the variance is 4.67, and $P(Q > 5) = 0.054$. The same questions arise for an aircraft wing whose material properties vary from batch to batch, a climate model with uncertain parameters, or a reactor whose failure must be shown to be rare.

Uncertainty quantification computes such answers at a cost a model that takes an hour per run can bear. Plain Monte Carlo always works, with an error $\sigma/\sqrt N$ that does not depend on the number of inputs, but it needs many runs. The methods of this chapter cut that number by using structure: a hierarchy of grids, a smooth dependence of the output on the inputs, a few inputs that matter much more than the rest, or a model that can be replaced by a small surrogate. Each structure brings its own assumption, and the entries say what is proved under it and what is measured.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **The problem.** [Forward uncertainty propagation](/forward-uncertainty-propagation/) states what is to be computed, and [random fields](/random-field/) describe inputs that vary in space.
2. **Sampling methods.** [Multilevel Monte Carlo](/multilevel-monte-carlo/) and its [complexity theorem](/mlmc-complexity-theorem/) bring the cost of an SDE expectation from $\varepsilon^{-3}$ to $\varepsilon^{-2}(\log\varepsilon)^2$; [multifidelity Monte Carlo](/multifidelity-monte-carlo/) uses cheap models as control variates.
3. **Rare failures.** The [first-order reliability method](/first-order-reliability-method/) and [subset simulation](/subset-simulation/).
4. **Polynomial chaos.** The [polynomial chaos expansion](/polynomial-chaos-expansion/), its [generalized](/generalized-polynomial-chaos/) form for other input laws, and the intrusive [stochastic Galerkin method](/stochastic-galerkin-method/).
5. **Non-intrusive spectral methods.** [Spectral projection](/non-intrusive-spectral-projection/), [regression](/regression-polynomial-chaos/), [sparse polynomial chaos](/sparse-polynomial-chaos/) and [stochastic collocation](/stochastic-collocation-method/) compute the expansion from model runs.
6. **Sensitivity analysis.** The [Morris method](/morris-method/) screens inputs; the [Hoeffding-Sobol decomposition](/hoeffding-sobol-decomposition/) defines [Sobol indices](/sobol-indices/), which the [Saltelli scheme](/saltelli-sampling-scheme/) and the [Fourier amplitude sensitivity test](/fourier-amplitude-sensitivity-test/) estimate; [active subspaces](/active-subspaces/) find important directions.
7. **Calibration and inverse problems.** [Kennedy-O'Hagan calibration](/kennedy-ohagan-calibration/) and the [well-posedness of Bayesian inverse problems](/bayesian-inverse-problem-well-posedness/).
8. **Projection-based reduced models.** [POD-Galerkin](/pod-galerkin-reduced-model/), the [reduced basis method](/reduced-basis-method/) and the [weak greedy algorithm](/weak-greedy-reduced-basis/).
9. **Nonlinear terms and data-driven reduction.** The [empirical interpolation method](/empirical-interpolation-method/), [DEIM](/discrete-empirical-interpolation-method/), [operator inference](/operator-inference/) and the [proper generalized decomposition](/proper-generalized-decomposition/).

## A first reading

Read forward uncertainty propagation first, then multilevel Monte Carlo, the first-order reliability method and the polynomial chaos expansion. Continue with non-intrusive spectral projection and stochastic collocation, which compute the expansion from model runs. For sensitivity, read the Hoeffding-Sobol decomposition, Sobol indices and the Saltelli scheme. For surrogates, read POD-Galerkin and the reduced basis method. That makes eleven entries, and with them most of the chapter's ideas.

The complexity theorem, the stochastic Galerkin method, sparse chaos, the Fourier amplitude test and the well-posedness theorem can wait for a second reading. Random fields matter as soon as an input varies in space. Operator inference and the proper generalized decomposition are research methods with fewer guarantees.
