A protein simulation moves tens of thousands of atoms by Newton's laws. The fastest motion, the stretch of a bond to a hydrogen atom, limits the time step to about 1 fs, or 2 fs when those bonds are held fixed by constraints, so one microsecond of motion takes $5 \times 10^8$ steps. Each step needs the force on every atom, and the electric forces reach across the whole periodic box. A sum over all pairs is exact but costs $O(N^2)$ per step; particle mesh Ewald computes the long-range part with fast Fourier transforms in $O(N \log N)$ time. If the integrator lets the energy drift, or the thermostat gives the wrong distribution of states, the measured averages are wrong. A weather forecast meets the same questions at another scale: conservation laws on a grid that cannot resolve the smallest eddies, and millions of observations a day to blend into a model state of $10^7$ to $10^9$ numbers.

This volume collects the algorithms that simulate physical systems: particles, fluids with shocks and turbulence, solids by finite elements, the transport of neutrons and photons, models that couple scales, and the methods that measure the uncertainty of a simulation and correct it with data. It has nine chapters and 292 entries.

## Why scientific computing gets a volume of its own

A few ideas run through the whole volume:

- **Keep the structure of the physics.** A [symplectic integrator](/symplectic-integrators/) such as [Verlet integration](/verlet-integration/) does not conserve energy exactly. For a fixed step it keeps a nearby modified energy bounded over times exponentially long in $1/h$, the [shadow Hamiltonian](/shadow-hamiltonian/) theorem; adaptive steps break this. Conservative schemes for a [conservation law](/hyperbolic-conservation-law/) change the totals of mass, momentum and energy only by boundary fluxes.
- **Far away is cheaper than near.** [Direct summation](/direct-n-body-summation/) is exact up to rounding at $\Theta(N^2)$ per step. The far field is smooth, so the [Barnes-Hut algorithm](/barnes-hut-algorithm/) needs $O(N \log N)$ time for non-pathological distributions and the [fast multipole method](/fast-multipole-method/) $O(N)$ for a fixed accuracy, with constants that grow with the number of digits.
- **Shocks need weak solutions.** Weak solutions are not unique, and an entropy condition selects the physical one. By the [Lax-Wendroff theorem](/lax-wendroff-theorem/) a consistent conservative scheme that converges has a weak solution as its limit, not necessarily the entropy one. By [Godunov's order barrier](/godunov-order-barrier/) a linear monotone scheme is at most first order, so high-resolution schemes are nonlinear.
- **Error is measured in the right norm.** [Cea's lemma](/cea-lemma/) puts the Galerkin error within a constant factor of the best approximation, under its conditions; mixed methods are stable only when the [inf-sup condition](/inf-sup-condition/) holds uniformly in the mesh size; adaptive refinement reaches the best rate only under the conditions of [its theorem](/afem-optimality-theorem/).
- **Sampling has a known error.** A Monte Carlo average is unbiased and its error falls like $N^{-1/2}$ whatever the dimension. Variance reduction in transport keeps the estimator unbiased when done as stated, and [multilevel Monte Carlo](/multilevel-monte-carlo/) reaches the cost bound of its theorem under its rate assumptions. Thermostats sample the ensembles their entries state, and only under their conditions: [Langevin dynamics](/langevin-dynamics/) samples the canonical ensemble, and the [Berendsen thermostat](/berendsen-thermostat/) does not.
- **Models are judged by measurement.** Turbulence closures and force fields are calibrated against data. The $k^{-5/3}$ spectrum of [Kolmogorov's 1941 theory](/kolmogorov-1941-theory/) is a prediction from hypotheses that experiments confirm, and the global regularity of the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations is open. The Kalman filter is exact only for linear Gaussian models; the [ensemble Kalman filter](/ensemble-kalman-filter/) is an approximation tested in twin experiments.

## The chapters

**Chapter 43.1, N-body and particle methods** (30 entries), starts from the [gravitational N-body problem](/gravitational-n-body-problem/) and the [multipole expansion](/multipole-expansion/). It covers tree codes, the fast multipole method and its kernel-independent and black-box forms, the [particle-mesh method](/particle-mesh-method/) in $O(N + M \log M)$ time, [P3M](/p3m-method/) and [TreePM](/treepm-method/), [particle-in-cell](/plasma-particle-in-cell/) plasma simulation with the [Boris pusher](/boris-pusher/), the [Hermite](/hermite-n-body-integrator/) and [Wisdom-Holman](/wisdom-holman-integrator/) integrators, and the [material point method](/material-point-method/) for solids.

**Chapter 43.2, Molecular simulation** (40 entries), covers [molecular dynamics](/molecular-dynamics/) with [periodic boundary conditions](/periodic-boundary-conditions/) and [cell lists](/linked-cell-list/); thermostats and barostats, from the [Nose-Hoover thermostat](/nose-hoover-thermostat/), which samples the canonical ensemble if the dynamics is ergodic, to the [Parrinello-Rahman barostat](/parrinello-rahman-barostat/), which alone samples NPH; [Ewald summation](/ewald-summation/) and [particle mesh Ewald](/particle-mesh-ewald/); constraints by [SHAKE](/shake-constraint-algorithm/); and free energy methods such as [umbrella sampling](/umbrella-sampling/) and [metadynamics](/metadynamics/), estimators with statistical error.

**Chapter 43.3, Conservation laws and shock capturing** (34 entries), covers [weak solutions](/weak-solution-of-conservation-law/), the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](/rankine-hugoniot-conditions/) and the entropy condition, then the [exact Riemann solver](/exact-riemann-solver/) and the approximate ones, [Roe](/roe-solver/), which needs an entropy fix, and [HLL](/hll-riemann-solver/), which smears contacts. High-resolution schemes follow: [MUSCL](/muscl-scheme/), ENO, the piecewise parabolic method and [discontinuous Galerkin](/discontinuous-galerkin-method/), with [SSP Runge-Kutta](/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta/) time stepping and [adaptive mesh refinement](/adaptive-mesh-refinement/).

**Chapter 43.4, Incompressible flow and turbulence** (35 entries), covers the [Chorin projection method](/chorin-projection-method/), the [SIMPLE algorithm](/simple-algorithm/) and the [marker-and-cell method](/marker-and-cell-method/); interfaces by [volume of fluid](/volume-of-fluid-method/) and the [immersed boundary method](/immersed-boundary-method/); the [lattice Boltzmann method](/lattice-boltzmann-method/) and [smoothed particle hydrodynamics](/smoothed-particle-hydrodynamics/); and turbulence, from [direct numerical simulation](/direct-numerical-simulation/), whose cost grows like $Re^3$, to [RANS](/reynolds-averaged-navier-stokes/) closures such as the [k-epsilon model](/k-epsilon-model/) and [large eddy simulation](/large-eddy-simulation/).

**Chapter 43.5, Finite element practice** (39 entries), goes from the [weak formulation](/weak-formulation/) and [assembly](/finite-element-assembly/) through the [Lax-Milgram theorem](/lax-milgram-theorem/) and Cea's lemma to [mixed methods](/mixed-finite-element-method/) and the [Taylor-Hood element](/taylor-hood-element/). It covers [a posteriori estimators](/residual-error-estimator/) and the [adaptive loop](/adaptive-finite-element-method/), [locking](/finite-element-locking/) and the [patch test](/patch-test/), time stepping by [Newmark's method](/newmark-beta-method/), [SUPG](/streamline-upwind-petrov-galerkin/) and the [boundary element method](/boundary-element-method/).

**Chapter 43.6, Particle transport** (27 entries), starts from the [linear Boltzmann transport equation](/linear-boltzmann-transport-equation/). Monte Carlo methods follow [analog histories](/analog-monte-carlo-transport/), with [implicit capture](/implicit-capture/), [weight windows](/weight-window/) and the [track-length estimator](/track-length-estimator/), and solve the [k-eigenvalue problem](/k-eigenvalue-problem/) for reactors. Deterministic methods use [discrete ordinates](/discrete-ordinates-method/), which suffer ray effects, with [source iteration](/source-iteration/) and [diffusion synthetic acceleration](/diffusion-synthetic-acceleration/). [CADIS](/cadis/) joins the two, and [direct simulation Monte Carlo](/direct-simulation-monte-carlo/) treats dilute gases.

**Chapter 43.7, Multiscale methods** (29 entries), covers coupling of atoms and continuum by the [Cauchy-Born rule](/cauchy-born-rule/), the [quasicontinuum method](/quasicontinuum-method/), whose energy-based form has ghost forces, and [QM/MM](/qm-mm-method/); [periodic homogenization](/periodic-homogenization/), [FE²](/fe2-method/) and the [heterogeneous multiscale method](/heterogeneous-multiscale-method/); rare events by [kinetic Monte Carlo](/kinetic-monte-carlo/), the [nudged elastic band](/nudged-elastic-band/) and accelerated dynamics; and coarse-graining and the [Mori-Zwanzig formalism](/mori-zwanzig-formalism/).

**Chapter 43.8, Uncertainty quantification** (29 entries), covers [forward propagation](/forward-uncertainty-propagation/) by [polynomial chaos](/polynomial-chaos-expansion/), [stochastic collocation](/stochastic-collocation-method/) and the [stochastic Galerkin method](/stochastic-galerkin-method/); multilevel and [multifidelity](/multifidelity-monte-carlo/) Monte Carlo; sensitivity by [Sobol indices](/sobol-indices/) and the [Morris method](/morris-method/); rare failures by [FORM](/first-order-reliability-method/) and [subset simulation](/subset-simulation/); [reduced basis](/reduced-basis-method/) and [POD-Galerkin](/pod-galerkin-reduced-model/) models; and [Kennedy-O'Hagan calibration](/kennedy-ohagan-calibration/).

**Chapter 43.9, Data assimilation** (29 entries), states the [data assimilation problem](/data-assimilation-problem/) and covers [optimal interpolation](/optimal-interpolation/), [3D-Var](/three-dimensional-variational-assimilation/) and [4D-Var](/four-dimensional-variational-assimilation/) with the [adjoint model](/adjoint-model/), ensemble filters with [covariance inflation](/covariance-inflation/) and [localization](/covariance-localization/), the [LETKF](/local-ensemble-transform-kalman-filter/), hybrid methods, and the [collapse of particle filter weights](/particle-filter-weight-collapse/). The [Lorenz-96 model](/lorenz-96-model/) is its test bed.

## How to read this volume

The chapters lean on the numerical methods of Volume 12 more than on each other, and most can be read in any order. Chapters 43.1 and 43.2 share integrators and long-range force methods. Two entries of Chapter 43.5 build on the discontinuous Galerkin method of 43.3. Chapter 43.7 draws most on Chapter 43.2, and some of it on 43.5. Chapter 43.6 needs sampling, 43.8 probability and polynomials, and 43.9 the Kalman filter.

For a first course, take these entries in order: [direct N-body summation](/direct-n-body-summation/), [Barnes-Hut](/barnes-hut-algorithm/), [particle-mesh](/particle-mesh-method/), [molecular dynamics](/molecular-dynamics/), [Langevin dynamics](/langevin-dynamics/), [Ewald summation](/ewald-summation/), [hyperbolic conservation law](/hyperbolic-conservation-law/), [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](/rankine-hugoniot-conditions/), [Roe solver](/roe-solver/), [MUSCL](/muscl-scheme/), [Chorin projection](/chorin-projection-method/), [Kolmogorov 1941](/kolmogorov-1941-theory/), [weak formulation](/weak-formulation/), [Cea's lemma](/cea-lemma/), [analog Monte Carlo transport](/analog-monte-carlo-transport/), [source iteration](/source-iteration/), [kinetic Monte Carlo](/kinetic-monte-carlo/), [polynomial chaos](/polynomial-chaos-expansion/), [multilevel Monte Carlo](/multilevel-monte-carlo/) and the [ensemble Kalman filter](/ensemble-kalman-filter/). A second course can add the fast multipole method, particle mesh Ewald, discontinuous Galerkin, large eddy simulation, the inf-sup condition, the adaptive loop, the quasicontinuum method, Sobol indices and 4D-Var. The learning path *Simulation of physical systems* goes through the volume step by step.

Each entry stands on its own; its facts box lists what it needs, by entry number.
