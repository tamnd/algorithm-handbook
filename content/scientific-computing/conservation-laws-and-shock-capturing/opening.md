Start Burgers' equation $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$ with a smooth hump. Each point of the profile moves at a speed equal to its height, so the top overtakes the foot ahead of it, the front steepens, and after a finite time the slope is infinite: a shock has formed and the smooth solution has ended. The same happens to a sound wave of large amplitude, to a flood wave in a river and to a jam in traffic. A program that simulates a supernova, a jet engine, a dam break or the air around a reentering capsule must carry such jumps for thousands of steps, keep them sharp, put them at the right place, and not ring around them.

The chapter is about the theory that says which discontinuous solution is the right one, and the schemes that capture it on a fixed grid. Three facts frame it. Solutions with jumps are weak solutions, and they are not unique; an entropy condition picks the physical one. A scheme in conservation form that converges converges to a weak solution, so conservation form puts shocks at the right speed, though not always the entropy solution. And a linear scheme that creates no new oscillations is at most first order, so every sharp, accurate scheme of the chapter is nonlinear, even for the linear advection equation.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Conservation laws and their solutions.** A [hyperbolic conservation law](/hyperbolic-conservation-law/), its [weak solutions](/weak-solution-of-conservation-law/), the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](/rankine-hugoniot-conditions/) for the speed of a jump and the [Lax entropy condition](/lax-entropy-condition/) that rules out expansion shocks. The [Lax-Wendroff theorem](/lax-wendroff-theorem/) and [Godunov's order barrier](/godunov-order-barrier/) say what a scheme can and cannot do.
2. **Gas dynamics and shallow water.** The [Euler equations of gas dynamics](/euler-equations-of-gas-dynamics/), their [exact Riemann solver](/exact-riemann-solver/), and the [shallow water equations](/shallow-water-equations/).
3. **Numerical fluxes and approximate Riemann solvers.** The [Rusanov flux](/rusanov-flux/), the [Roe solver](/roe-solver/) with the [Harten-Hyman entropy fix](/harten-hyman-entropy-fix/), the [HLL](/hll-riemann-solver/) and [HLLC](/hllc-riemann-solver/) solvers, [flux vector splitting](/flux-vector-splitting/) and the [AUSM scheme](/ausm-scheme/). Each gives the flux between two cells; they differ in cost, in how much they smear contacts, and in how they fail.
4. **High-resolution schemes.** The [MacCormack method](/maccormack-method/), second order and oscillating at shocks; [strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta methods](/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta/) for the time step; and the nonlinear reconstructions of [MUSCL](/muscl-scheme/), the [piecewise parabolic method](/piecewise-parabolic-method/) and [ENO](/eno-scheme/), with the central schemes of [Nessyahu and Tadmor](/nessyahu-tadmor-scheme/) and [Kurganov and Tadmor](/kurganov-tadmor-scheme/) that need no Riemann solver.
5. **Discontinuous Galerkin and robust high order.** The [discontinuous Galerkin method](/discontinuous-galerkin-method/), its [Runge-Kutta form with a limiter](/runge-kutta-discontinuous-galerkin/), the [positivity-preserving limiter](/positivity-preserving-limiter/) and [entropy stable schemes](/entropy-stable-scheme/).
6. **Sources, interfaces and adaptive grids.** [Well-balanced schemes](/well-balanced-scheme/) for a varying bottom, the [ghost fluid method](/ghost-fluid-method/) for material interfaces, [adaptive mesh refinement](/adaptive-mesh-refinement/) and the [Berger-Colella method](/berger-colella-amr/).
7. **Linear waves, energy and boundaries.** The [Yee scheme](/yee-fdtd-scheme/) for Maxwell's equations, the [perfectly matched layer](/perfectly-matched-layer/) that absorbs outgoing waves, and [summation-by-parts operators](/summation-by-parts-operators/) that make stability provable by the energy method.

## A first reading

Read the first group in order: the hyperbolic conservation law, weak solutions, the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions, the Lax entropy condition, the Lax-Wendroff theorem and Godunov's order barrier. Then read the Euler equations and the exact Riemann solver, followed by the Rusanov flux, the Roe solver, the HLL solver and the HLLC solver. Finish with SSP Runge-Kutta methods, the MUSCL scheme and the discontinuous Galerkin method. These fifteen entries give a working shock-capturing code: MUSCL reconstruction, an HLLC flux and a third-order SSP time step.

The entropy fix, flux vector splitting, AUSM, PPM, ENO, the central schemes and the research entries on positivity and entropy stability can wait for a second pass; each refines one part of the code above. The last two groups stand apart. Read the well-balanced schemes after the shallow water equations if you work on rivers or oceans, and the Yee scheme and the perfectly matched layer if you work on waves in electromagnetics or acoustics.
