## History

Rankine published the conditions for a wave of finite amplitude in a gas in 1870, and Hugoniot added the energy relation in 1887 and 1889, which fixed the entropy jump across a shock. The general theory of systems came with Lax's 1957 paper on hyperbolic systems of conservation laws, which gave the entropy condition that carries his name and solved the Riemann problem for weak waves. Glimm proved existence for systems with small total variation in 1965, and Kruzkov in 1970 gave the entropy inequalities that make the solution of a scalar law unique.

Shock capturing began with Godunov. His 1959 paper solved the Riemann problem between every pair of cells, which is the start of the exact Riemann solver and of Godunov's method, and proved that a linear scheme that keeps monotone profiles monotone is at most first order. Lax and Wendroff gave their second-order scheme and their convergence theorem in 1960, Rusanov his flux in 1961 and MacCormack his predictor-corrector scheme in 1969. Sod's survey of 1978 compared them on the shock tube that is still the first test of every code.

Most codes still use methods from the years around 1980. Van Leer's series of papers ended in 1979 with MUSCL. Roe published his approximate Riemann solver in 1981, the same year as the flux vector splitting of Steger and Warming; van Leer's splitting followed in 1982. In 1983 Harten defined TVD schemes, and Harten, Lax and van Leer introduced the HLL solver, with wave speed estimates from Davis in 1988 and Einfeldt in 1988. Harten and Hyman gave their entropy fix the same year. Colella and Woodward published the piecewise parabolic method in 1984, and Sweby the TVD region for flux limiters. ENO followed in 1987, from Harten, Engquist, Osher and Chakravarthy, and Shu and Osher gave it a finite difference form and TVD Runge-Kutta time steps in 1988 and 1989; Gottlieb, Shu and Tadmor named these strong stability preserving in 2001. Berger and Oliger introduced block-structured adaptive refinement in 1984, and Berger and Colella made it conservative in 1989.

Nessyahu and Tadmor gave a central scheme without Riemann solvers in 1990, and Kurganov and Tadmor its semi-discrete form in 2000. Liou and Steffen introduced AUSM in 1993, and Toro, Spruce and Speares restored the contact wave in HLLC in 1994. The discontinuous Galerkin method, created by Reed and Hill in 1973 for neutron transport, became a method for conservation laws in the series of papers by Cockburn and Shu from 1989 to 1998. Fedkiw, Aslam, Merriman and Osher published the ghost fluid method in 1999, and Audusse and coauthors the hydrostatic reconstruction for shallow water in 2004, ten years after Bermúdez and Vázquez stated the property that well-balanced schemes keep. Tadmor's entropy conservative fluxes of 1987 led to the entropy stable schemes of the 2000s, and Zhang and Shu's positivity-preserving limiter appeared in 2010.

Linear waves had their own line. Yee published his staggered scheme for Maxwell's equations in 1966, and Taflove and his coworkers made it the standard tool of computational electromagnetics. Berenger's perfectly matched layer of 1994 removed the reflections from the edge of the grid. Kreiss and Scherer introduced summation-by-parts operators in 1974; penalty terms later made their energy estimates hold with boundary conditions.

## Further reading

- Randall J. LeVeque, *Finite Volume Methods for Hyperbolic Problems*, Cambridge University Press, 2002. The theory and the finite volume schemes, with careful statements of the Lax-Wendroff theorem and the entropy conditions.
- Eleuterio F. Toro, *Riemann Solvers and Numerical Methods for Fluid Dynamics*, third edition, Springer, 2009. The exact and approximate Riemann solvers for the Euler equations in full detail, with test problems.
- Sigal Gottlieb, David Ketcheson and Chi-Wang Shu, *Strong Stability Preserving Runge-Kutta and Multistep Time Discretizations*, World Scientific, 2011. The theory of SSP time stepping, the optimal methods and the barriers.
- Bernardo Cockburn and Chi-Wang Shu, Runge-Kutta discontinuous Galerkin methods for convection-dominated problems, *Journal of Scientific Computing* 16(3) (2001). The survey of the RKDG method by its authors.
- Allen Taflove and Susan C. Hagness, *Computational Electrodynamics: The Finite-Difference Time-Domain Method*, third edition, Artech House, 2005. The standard reference on the Yee scheme and absorbing layers.
