A lidar on a car returns hundreds of thousands of points a second, each a position in space with no link to its neighbors. A depth camera gives a grid of distances thirty times a second, and a museum scanner takes dozens of scans of one statue from different sides. None of these come as surfaces. Each is a [point cloud](/point-cloud/), and each scan sits in the coordinates of the sensor at the moment it was taken. Before a car can compare its scan with a map, or a scanner can merge its views of a statue, the clouds must be cleaned, described and brought into one frame.

The central problem is [point cloud registration](/point-cloud-registration/): find the rigid motion that best aligns one cloud to another when nobody says which point matches which. If the matches were known, the best motion would follow in closed form from the [Kabsch algorithm](/kabsch-algorithm/). They are not known, and that turns a least squares problem into a hard search. The chapter shows the two ways around it. Local methods such as iterative closest point guess the matches from the current pose and improve both in turn; they are fast and accurate but need a good start. Global methods match local shape descriptors or search the whole space of motions, and give a start pose, a probability of success, or in one case a proved gap to the optimum.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Point clouds and their cleanup.** The [point cloud](/point-cloud/) and the [range image](/range-image/) that keeps the sensor's grid; [voxel grid downsampling](/voxel-grid-downsampling/) and [statistical outlier removal](/statistical-outlier-removal/) as the usual first steps; and the [chamfer distance](/point-set-chamfer-distance/) for comparing two clouds, which is not a metric.
2. **Normals and local descriptors.** [Normal estimation](/point-cloud-normal-estimation/) by the covariance of the nearest neighbors, [consistent orientation](/consistent-normal-orientation/) along a minimum spanning tree, and three descriptors that do not change under rigid motion: [spin images](/spin-images/), [FPFH](/point-feature-histograms/) and [SHOT](/shot-descriptor/).
3. **Iterative closest point.** [ICP](/iterative-closest-point/) and its [monotone convergence theorem](/icp-convergence-theorem/), the [taxonomy of variants](/icp-variants-taxonomy/), [point-to-plane ICP](/point-to-plane-icp/), [trimmed ICP](/trimmed-icp/) for partial overlap and [generalized ICP](/generalized-icp/).
4. **Soft matches and deformations.** The [normal distributions transform](/normal-distributions-transform/), [robust point matching](/robust-point-matching/), [non-rigid registration](/non-rigid-registration/) and [coherent point drift](/coherent-point-drift/).
5. **Global registration and shape detection.** [Geometric hashing](/geometric-hashing/), [efficient RANSAC](/efficient-ransac-shape-detection/) for planes, spheres and cylinders, [4PCS](/four-points-congruent-sets/), [fast global registration](/fast-global-registration/), [TEASER](/teaser-registration/) and [Go-ICP](/go-icp/).

## A first reading

Start with [point cloud](/point-cloud/), [range image](/range-image/), [voxel grid downsampling](/voxel-grid-downsampling/), [statistical outlier removal](/statistical-outlier-removal/) and [normal estimation](/point-cloud-normal-estimation/). Then read [point cloud registration](/point-cloud-registration/), [iterative closest point](/iterative-closest-point/), the [convergence theorem](/icp-convergence-theorem/), [point-to-plane ICP](/point-to-plane-icp/) and [trimmed ICP](/trimmed-icp/). Finish with [FPFH](/point-feature-histograms/) and [fast global registration](/fast-global-registration/), which together give the usual two-stage pipeline: a coarse global pose from descriptor matches, refined by ICP. On a first pass, skip SHOT, spin images, robust point matching, non-rigid registration, geometric hashing, TEASER and Go-ICP.

## Before you start

The fit step of ICP is the [Kabsch algorithm](/kabsch-algorithm/) of Volume 27. Closest points come from [nearest neighbor search](/nearest-neighbor-search/) in Volume 5, usually with a [k-d tree](/kd-tree/), and the voxel grid and efficient RANSAC use the [hash table](/hash-table/) and the [octree](/octree/) of Volume 3. Normals use [principal component analysis](/principal-component-analysis/), and coherent point drift uses [expectation maximization](/expectation-maximization/) and the [Gaussian mixture model](/gaussian-mixture-model/), all in Volume 25. Consistent orientation needs the [minimum spanning tree](/minimum-spanning-tree/) of Volume 7, and Go-ICP [best-first branch and bound](/best-first-branch-and-bound/) from Volume 2. From this volume, the global methods use [RANSAC](/ransac/) from Chapter 38.3, and point-to-plane ICP the [Gauss-Newton algorithm](/gauss-newton-algorithm/) from Chapter 38.4. Robust costs such as Geman-McClure are [M-estimators](/m-estimator/), from Volume 33.
