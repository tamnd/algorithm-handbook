## History

Registration of 3D shapes became a standard problem around 1991, once range sensors gave dense data; Besl surveyed them in 1988. Chen and Medioni presented registration by point-to-plane distances at ICRA 1991, with a journal version in 1992. Besl and McKay published the iterative closest point algorithm in 1992 and showed that its mean square error never rises. Zhang gave a closely related method in a 1992 report and in the *International Journal of Computer Vision* in 1994, with a rule that rejects far pairs. The fit step of ICP rests on earlier closed forms for the best rigid motion. Arun, Huang and Blostein (1987) used the singular value decomposition, Horn (1987) used unit quaternions, and Umeyama (1991) fixed the reflection case and added scale. Low (2004) linearized the point-to-plane step. Rusinkiewicz and Levoy (2001) split ICP into stages and compared the choices for each stage. Pottmann, Huang, Yang and Hu (2006) explained the rates: point-to-point ICP is linear, and point-to-plane ICP is close to quadratic near a zero-residual fit.

Robust and probabilistic forms followed. Chetverikov and co-authors gave trimmed ICP at ICPR 2002, with a journal version in 2005. Biber and Straßer introduced the normal distributions transform for laser scan matching at IROS 2003, and Magnusson, Lilienthal and Duckett extended it to 3D in 2007. Segal, Haehnel and Thrun presented generalized ICP at RSS 2009. Soft matches came from the work of Gold, Rangarajan, Lu, Pappu and Mjolsness (1998) on robust point matching, and Chui and Rangarajan (2003) added thin-plate splines for non-rigid shapes. Myronenko, Song and Carreira-Perpiñán presented coherent point drift at NIPS 2006, and Myronenko and Song gave the full journal version in 2010.

Cleanup and local description grew with the size of scans. Hoppe, DeRose, Duchamp, McDonald and Stuetzle (1992) estimated normals by local principal component analysis and oriented them along a minimum spanning tree. Pauly, Gross and Kobbelt (2002) and Mitra and Nguyen (2003) studied neighborhood size and noise. Rossignac and Borrel (1993) simplified models by clustering vertices in a grid, the idea behind voxel grid downsampling. Rusu and co-authors gave statistical outlier removal in 2008, point feature histograms in 2008 and their fast version in 2009. Johnson and Hebert published spin images in 1999, and Tombari, Salti and Di Stefano gave the SHOT descriptor in 2010. Chamfer matching goes back to Barrow, Tenenbaum, Bolles and Wolf (1977); Fan, Su and Guibas used the chamfer distance as a training loss for point sets.

Global methods avoid the need for a good start. Lamdan and Wolfson presented geometric hashing at ICCV 1988, and Grimson and Huttenlocher (1990) analyzed its sensitivity to noise. Schnabel, Wahl and Klein (2007) made RANSAC fast for shape detection in large clouds. Aiger, Mitra and Cohen-Or introduced 4-points congruent sets at SIGGRAPH 2008, and Mellado, Aiger and Mitra (2014) made the search much faster. Yang, Li and Jia presented Go-ICP at ICCV 2013, with a journal version with Campbell in 2016. Zhou, Park and Koltun gave fast global registration at ECCV 2016. Yang, Shi and Carlone published TEASER in *IEEE Transactions on Robotics* in 2021, with a certificate of optimality when its relaxation is tight.

## Further reading

- Richard Szeliski, *Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications*, second edition, Springer, 2022. The chapter on 3D reconstruction covers range data, point clouds and their alignment at textbook level.
- Szymon Rusinkiewicz and Marc Levoy, "Efficient variants of the ICP algorithm", *3DIM*, 2001. The paper that split ICP into stages; read it before any of the variants.
- Helmut Pottmann, Qi-Xing Huang, Yong-Liang Yang and Shi-Min Hu, "Geometry and convergence analysis of algorithms for registration of 3D shapes", *International Journal of Computer Vision* 67(3), 2006. Why point-to-point ICP is slow and point-to-plane ICP is fast near the answer.
- Gary K. L. Tam and co-authors, "Registration of 3D point clouds and meshes: a survey from rigid to nonrigid", *IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics* 19(7), 2013. A broad survey of rigid and non-rigid methods.
- Radu Bogdan Rusu and Steve Cousins, "3D is here: Point Cloud Library (PCL)", *ICRA*, 2011. The library where most of the filters and descriptors in this chapter have standard implementations.
