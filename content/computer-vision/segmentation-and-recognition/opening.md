A photo editor wants to cut a dog out of a picture with three clicks. A car must put a box around every pedestrian in each frame, and a phone must find the one photo of a building among ten thousand. A medical program must give every pixel of a scan a tissue label. These are four different questions about the same kind of input: which pixels belong together, what they are, and where the known objects are.

The chapter answers them with three families of methods. Segmentation groups pixels, either with help from a user, as in [Boykov-Jolly graph cuts](/boykov-jolly-graph-cuts/), or for the whole image, as in [semantic segmentation](/semantic-segmentation/). [Object detection](/object-detection/) finds every object of known classes and returns a box, a class and a score; its methods run from [sliding windows](/sliding-window-detection/) with a linear classifier to convolutional networks such as [Faster R-CNN](/faster-r-cnn/). [Image retrieval](/image-retrieval/) ranks a collection by how likely each image is to show the query's object. Many of the classical methods are exact for the problem they state: a minimum cut, a shortest path, a sparse linear system or a dynamic program over a tree. The learned detectors are not exact for anything a theorem can state. They are judged by measurements on benchmarks, so the chapter starts with the measures.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Measuring detections and segmentations.** [Intersection over union](/intersection-over-union/), [mean average precision](/detection-mean-average-precision/) for detection, [mean intersection over union](/mean-intersection-over-union/) for semantic segmentation and the [boundary F-measure](/boundary-f-measure/).
2. **Recognition by models and votes.** [Object recognition](/object-recognition/) as a problem, [eigenfaces](/eigenfaces/), the [generalized Hough transform](/generalized-hough-transform/), the [implicit shape model](/implicit-shape-model/), [recognition by alignment](/alignment-recognition/), and the [active shape](/active-shape-model/) and [active appearance](/active-appearance-model/) models.
3. **Windows, parts and suppression.** [Sliding window detection](/sliding-window-detection/), [non-maximum suppression](/box-non-maximum-suppression/) and [Soft-NMS](/soft-nms/), the [Dalal-Triggs detector](/dalal-triggs-detector/), [pictorial structures](/pictorial-structures/) and the [deformable part model](/deformable-part-model/).
4. **Proposals and two-stage networks.** [Object proposals](/object-proposals/) and [selective search](/selective-search/), [bounding box regression](/bounding-box-regression/), [R-CNN](/r-cnn/), [RoI pooling](/roi-pooling/), [Fast R-CNN](/fast-r-cnn/), [anchor boxes](/anchor-boxes/) and their [assignment](/anchor-assignment/), Faster R-CNN and the [feature pyramid network](/feature-pyramid-network/).
5. **Detectors in one pass.** [SSD](/single-shot-multibox-detector/), [YOLO](/yolo-detector/) and [DETR](/detr/).
6. **Retrieval and image representations.** The [bag of visual words](/bag-of-visual-words/), the [vocabulary tree](/vocabulary-tree/), [spatial verification](/spatial-verification/), [query expansion](/query-expansion/), [visual place recognition](/visual-place-recognition/), [spatial pyramid matching](/spatial-pyramid-matching/), [VLAD](/vlad/) and the [Fisher vector](/fisher-vector/).
7. **Interactive segmentation and matting.** [Interactive segmentation](/interactive-segmentation/) as a problem, [intelligent scissors](/intelligent-scissors/), Boykov-Jolly graph cuts, the [random walker](/random-walker-segmentation/), [image matting](/image-matting/) and [closed-form matting](/closed-form-matting/).
8. **Segmenting the whole image.** The [Mumford-Shah functional](/mumford-shah-functional/), [quick shift](/quick-shift/), the [ultrametric contour map](/ultrametric-contour-map/), semantic segmentation with a [dense CRF](/dense-crf/), [instance segmentation](/instance-segmentation/), [Mask R-CNN](/mask-r-cnn/) and [panoptic segmentation](/panoptic-segmentation/).

## A first reading

Read intersection over union and mean average precision first, since every detection result is quoted in them. Then read object detection, sliding window detection, non-maximum suppression and the Dalal-Triggs detector, which show the whole pipeline with a classifier simple enough to follow by hand. Continue with bounding box regression, anchor boxes and Faster R-CNN for the network form of the same pipeline, and with Boykov-Jolly graph cuts and the random walker for interactive segmentation. Finish with the bag of visual words and spatial verification, which together make a retrieval system. On a first pass, skip the active appearance model, the implicit shape model, quick shift, the ultrametric contour map, the Fisher vector, the Mumford-Shah functional and closed-form matting.

## Before you start

The measures need [precision and recall](/precision-and-recall/) and the [confusion matrix](/confusion-matrix/) of Volume 25, and boxes are the [axis-aligned bounding boxes](/axis-aligned-bounding-box/) of Volume 28. The image side comes from Volume 28 as well: the [digital image](/digital-image/), the [image pyramid](/image-pyramid/), [image segmentation](/image-segmentation/) and the [Hough transform](/hough-transform/). The learning tools are in Volume 25: [principal component analysis](/principal-component-analysis/), [k-means](/k-means/), the [support vector machine](/support-vector-machine/), the [Gaussian mixture model](/gaussian-mixture-model/) and the [conditional random field](/conditional-random-field/). The graph algorithms are in Volume 7: the [max-flow min-cut theorem](/max-flow-min-cut-theorem/), [Dijkstra's algorithm](/dijkstra/), the [Laplacian matrix](/laplacian-matrix/) and the [Hungarian algorithm](/hungarian-algorithm/). Retrieval borrows [tf-idf](/tf-idf/) from Volume 35. From this volume, the detectors use [HOG features](/histogram-of-oriented-gradients/) from Chapter 38.1, and spatial verification uses [RANSAC](/ransac/) from Chapter 38.3.
