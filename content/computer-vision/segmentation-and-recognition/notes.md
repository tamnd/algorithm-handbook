## History

Early recognition matched explicit models. Fischler and Elschlager (1973) described objects as pieces held together by springs, and Ballard (1981) generalized the Hough transform to any shape given by an example. Lowe (1987) and Huttenlocher and Ullman (1990) recognized objects by aligning a model pose from a few matches and verifying it. Sirovich and Kirby (1987) represented faces by principal components, and Turk and Pentland (1991) used these eigenfaces for recognition. Cootes, Taylor, Cooper and Graham described active shape models in 1995, and Cootes, Edwards and Taylor added appearance in 1998. Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher (2000, 2005) restricted pictorial structures to trees with quadratic springs and made matching linear in the number of locations.

Segmentation grew from energies and graphs. Mumford and Shah (1989) posed segmentation as the minimum of an energy over a smooth image and its edges. Mortensen and Barrett (1995) traced boundaries by shortest paths, Boykov and Jolly (2001) cut objects from user seeds by a minimum cut, and Grady (2006) labeled pixels by random walks. Levin, Lischinski and Weiss (2006) found a soft matte from one linear system. Vedaldi and Soatto (2008) proposed quick shift, and Arbelaez, Maire, Fowlkes and Malik (2011) built hierarchical segmentations from ultrametric contour maps. Martin, Fowlkes and Malik (2004) set the boundary benchmark with its F-measure, and Krähenbühl and Koltun (2011) made mean field in the fully connected CRF fast.

Retrieval borrowed from text search. Sivic and Zisserman (2003) built Video Google with visual words, tf-idf weights and an inverted file, and Nister and Stewenius (2006) made the vocabulary a tree. Philbin, Chum, Isard, Sivic and Zisserman (2007) added fast spatial verification, and Chum and co-authors (2007) added query expansion. Lazebnik, Schmid and Ponce (2006) put spatial pyramids on the bag of words for classification. Perronnin and Dance (2007) applied the Fisher kernel of Jaakkola and Haussler (1998) to local descriptors, and Jegou, Douze, Schmid and Perez (2010) proposed VLAD.

Detection went from windows to networks. Viola and Jones (2001) made sliding windows fast with a boosted cascade, Dalal and Triggs (2005) introduced HOG with a linear SVM, and Felzenszwalb, McAllester and Ramanan (2008) added deformable parts. The PASCAL VOC challenge, described by Everingham and co-authors (2010), fixed the IoU threshold and the AP measure. Van de Sande, Uijlings, Gevers and Smeulders (2011) gave selective search, and Girshick, Donahue, Darrell and Malik (2014) used it in R-CNN. Girshick (2015) gave Fast R-CNN, and Ren, He, Girshick and Sun (2015) gave Faster R-CNN with a region proposal network. Redmon, Divvala, Girshick and Farhadi presented YOLO in 2016, and Liu and co-authors presented SSD in the same year. Lin and co-authors gave the COCO data set (2014) and feature pyramid networks (2017). He, Gkioxari, Dollar and Girshick presented Mask R-CNN at ICCV 2017, Bodla, Singh, Chellappa and Davis gave Soft-NMS the same year, Kirillov and co-authors defined panoptic segmentation in 2019, and Carion and co-authors presented DETR in 2020.

## Further reading

- Richard Szeliski, *Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications*, second edition, Springer, 2022. The chapters on recognition and segmentation cover most of this chapter at textbook level.
- Mark Everingham, Luc Van Gool, Christopher K. I. Williams, John Winn and Andrew Zisserman, "The PASCAL Visual Object Classes (VOC) challenge", *International Journal of Computer Vision*, 2010. The source of the detection and segmentation measures, with a discussion of their choices.
- Pedro F. Felzenszwalb, Ross B. Girshick, David McAllester and Deva Ramanan, "Object detection with discriminatively trained part-based models", *IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence*, 2010. The fullest account of a classical detector, from HOG features to suppression.
- Jan Hosang, Rodrigo Benenson, Piotr Dollar and Bernt Schiele, "What makes for effective detection proposals?", *IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence* 38(4), 2016. A careful comparison of proposal methods by recall and detection quality.
- Jue Wang and Michael F. Cohen, "Image and video matting: a survey", *Foundations and Trends in Computer Graphics and Vision* 3(2), 2008. Matting from blue screens to closed-form solutions.
