A camera over a shop door films people coming and going. To count them, a program must find each person in each frame and know that the person in frame 200 is the one who entered in frame 150. A drone that follows a cyclist has the opposite problem: one target, given by a box in the first frame, that turns, changes size and passes behind trees. A radar watching aircraft returns a few points per sweep, some of them noise, and must decide which point belongs to which plane. All three are tracking: estimating, from a sequence of images or measurements, where things are and which thing is which.

Two questions run through the chapter. The first is how to find the target in a new frame: by a model of the background, by a template, by a color histogram, by a learned filter or by a detector. The second is how to link what was found to what was there before. For one target this is a local search near the last position. For many targets it is [data association](/data-association/), which is easy in one frame and hard over many: one frame is an [assignment problem](/assignment-problem/), links between consecutive detections give a min-cost flow, and costs on three frames make the problem NP-hard. The [Kalman filter](/kalman-filter/) predicts each track between frames, and it is exact only when the motion and the noise are linear and Gaussian. The learned trackers are judged by their scores on benchmarks, and the chapter also shows how those scores are computed.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Tracking problems and their scores.** [Visual object tracking](/visual-object-tracking/) of one target and [multi-object tracking](/multi-object-tracking/) of many; the [CLEAR MOT metrics](/clear-mot-metrics/), the [IDF1 score](/idf1-score/) and [HOTA](/hota-metric/).
2. **Background subtraction.** The [problem](/background-subtraction/) for a fixed camera, the [adaptive Gaussian mixture](/stauffer-grimson-background-model/) of Stauffer and Grimson, and [ViBe](/vibe-background-subtraction/) with its random sample sets.
3. **Points, templates and motion fields.** The [Kanade-Lucas-Tomasi tracker](/kanade-lucas-tomasi-tracker/), [inverse compositional alignment](/inverse-compositional-alignment/), the [forward-backward error](/forward-backward-error/) and [scene flow](/scene-flow/).
4. **Following one target by its appearance.** [Mean shift tracking](/mean-shift-tracking/) and [CAMShift](/camshift/), the [CONDENSATION algorithm](/condensation-algorithm/), [correlation filters](/correlation-filter-tracking/) with the [MOSSE filter](/mosse-filter/) and the [kernelized correlation filter](/kernelized-correlation-filter/), [Siamese tracking](/siamese-tracking/) and [tracking-learning-detection](/tracking-learning-detection/).
5. **Gating and data association.** [Gating](/track-gating/), data association, [global nearest neighbor](/global-nearest-neighbor-association/), [JPDA](/joint-probabilistic-data-association/), [multiple hypothesis tracking](/multiple-hypothesis-tracking/) and the [PHD filter](/phd-filter/).
6. **Tracking by detection.** The [template](/tracking-by-detection/), [network flow](/network-flow-tracking/) over a whole video, and the online trackers [SORT](/sort-tracker/), [Deep SORT](/deep-sort/) and [ByteTrack](/bytetrack/).

## A first reading

Start with visual object tracking and multi-object tracking, then the CLEAR MOT metrics and IDF1, so that the scores in the later pages mean something. Read background subtraction and the Stauffer-Grimson model for the fixed camera. For one target, read the KLT tracker, mean shift tracking and correlation filter tracking. For many targets, read gating, data association, global nearest neighbor, tracking by detection, SORT and network flow tracking. That is fifteen pages and covers the ideas that most trackers combine. On a first pass, skip HOTA, ViBe, inverse compositional alignment, scene flow, the PHD filter, multiple hypothesis tracking and the learned trackers, Siamese tracking and Deep SORT; come back to them once the basic pipeline is clear.
