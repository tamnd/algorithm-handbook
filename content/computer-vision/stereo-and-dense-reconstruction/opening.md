Hold a finger in front of your face and close each eye in turn: the finger jumps against the background, and it jumps more the nearer it is. Two cameras do the same. After the images of a stereo pair are rectified, so that matching points lie in the same row, the jump of a point is one number, its [disparity](/disparity/) $d$, and the point's depth is $Z = fb/d$ for a focal length $f$ in pixels and a baseline $b$. With $f = 500$ and $b = 0.1$ m, a disparity of 20 pixels means 2.5 m and a disparity of 2 pixels means 25 m. The formula is exact; the hard part is the matching. A pixel on a white wall looks like every other pixel on the wall, a pixel near the edge of a chair may have no match at all because the other camera cannot see it, and a shiny surface shows different colors to the two cameras.

The chapter is about how to find matches anyway and how to build shapes from them. The methods run the cameras of cars and drones, the depth sensors of phones and robots, the 3D scanners of factories and museums, and the photogrammetry that turns a set of tourist photographs into a model of a building. Some methods are local and fast; some minimize one energy over the whole image; some use more than two images, or no second camera at all. The chapter says each time what a method guarantees: an exact minimum for its own energy, a bound, a local minimum, or only measured accuracy on benchmark scenes.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Disparity and the stereo problem.** [Disparity](/disparity/), [depth from disparity](/depth-from-disparity/) with its error law $|\delta Z| \approx Z^2 |\delta d| / (fb)$, [stereo rectification](/stereo-rectification/) and the [stereo matching](/stereo-matching/) problem.
2. **Matching costs and local methods.** A [matching cost](/stereo-matching-cost/) such as the [census transform](/census-transform/) fills a [cost volume](/cost-volume/). [Block matching](/block-matching-stereo/) sums it over a box and takes the best disparity; [cost aggregation](/cost-aggregation/) and [adaptive support weights](/adaptive-support-weights/) choose better windows. [Subpixel refinement](/subpixel-disparity-refinement/) and the [left-right check](/left-right-consistency-check/) finish the map.
3. **Optimizing along lines.** [Scanline dynamic programming](/scanline-dynamic-programming-stereo/) is exact for one row at a time; [semi-global matching](/semi-global-matching/) sums 8 or 16 such paths per pixel.
4. **Random search for slanted windows.** [PatchMatch](/patchmatch/) and [PatchMatch stereo](/patchmatch-stereo/) search an infinite set of planes by random guesses and propagation.
5. **Energies and graph cuts.** [Iterated conditional modes](/iterated-conditional-modes/), the [Kolmogorov-Zabih theorem](/graph-representable-energies/) on which binary energies a cut can minimize, the [Boykov-Kolmogorov max-flow algorithm](/boykov-kolmogorov-max-flow/), [alpha-beta swap](/alpha-beta-swap/), [alpha-expansion](/alpha-expansion/), [QPBO](/qpbo/) and [graph cut stereo](/graph-cut-stereo/).
6. **Shape from light and focus.** [Structured light](/structured-light-scanning/), [photometric stereo](/photometric-stereo/), [shape from shading](/shape-from-shading/) and [shape from focus](/shape-from-focus/).
7. **Many views.** [Photo-consistency](/photo-consistency/) is the test, [multi-view stereo](/multi-view-stereo/) the problem; [plane sweep stereo](/plane-sweep-stereo/) and [PMVS](/patch-based-multi-view-stereo/) solve it, and [depth map fusion](/depth-map-fusion/) and [TSDF fusion](/tsdf-fusion/) merge the results.
8. **Shapes from voxels.** The [visual hull](/visual-hull/) from silhouettes, and the photo hull from colors by [voxel coloring](/voxel-coloring/) and [space carving](/space-carving/).
9. **Learned scene representations.** [Neural radiance fields](/neural-radiance-field/) and [3D Gaussian splatting](/gaussian-splatting/).

## A first reading

Read these thirteen entries in order: [disparity](/disparity/), [depth from disparity](/depth-from-disparity/), [stereo rectification](/stereo-rectification/), [stereo matching](/stereo-matching/), [stereo matching cost](/stereo-matching-cost/), [cost volume](/cost-volume/), [block matching stereo](/block-matching-stereo/), [left-right consistency check](/left-right-consistency-check/), [semi-global matching](/semi-global-matching/), [iterated conditional modes](/iterated-conditional-modes/), [alpha-expansion](/alpha-expansion/), [photo-consistency](/photo-consistency/) and [plane sweep stereo](/plane-sweep-stereo/). Together they give a working two-view stereo pipeline, the idea of a global energy, and the step to many views. On a first pass, skip QPBO, graph cut stereo with occlusions, PMVS, the voxel methods and the two learned representations. The Kolmogorov-Zabih theorem is worth reading before alpha-expansion, but its proof can wait.
