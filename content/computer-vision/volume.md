A visitor walks around a statue and takes 200 photographs with a phone. A program finds a few thousand distinctive points in each photograph and describes the patch around each point by a short vector. It matches the vectors between pairs of photographs, and most matches are right but many are not. For each pair, a random sample of five matches gives a guess at the relative pose of the two cameras, and the guess that most matches agree with wins. The program adds cameras one at a time, triangulates points, and refines all cameras and points together by nonlinear least squares. Stereo matching then fills in a depth for every pixel, and the depth maps are fused into one surface. A car does the same work thirty times a second: it follows its own motion from video, aligns laser scans, finds the people in front of it and keeps track of each one.

This volume is about those algorithms. Its eight chapters hold 277 entries. The first four chapters go from pixels to cameras: local features, camera models, multiple view geometry, and structure from motion. The next two build dense geometry, by stereo and by the registration of point clouds. The last two ask what an image shows and where it moves: segmentation and recognition, and tracking.

## Why computer vision gets a volume of its own

Computer vision draws on image processing, linear algebra, optimization, geometry, statistics and machine learning. What it adds is inversion: it recovers the scene and the cameras from images that lost depth, under noise and wrong matches. A few ideas come back in every chapter:

- **Exact without noise, local with it.** The [eight-point algorithm](/eight-point-algorithm/), the [five-point algorithm](/five-point-algorithm/) and the [DLT for homographies](/dlt-homography/) are exact for noise-free data, and with noise they minimize an [algebraic error](/algebraic-and-geometric-error/), not the geometric one. [Bundle adjustment](/bundle-adjustment/) minimizes the [reprojection error](/reprojection-error/) but finds a local minimum, so it needs a good start.
- **Outliers are the rule.** Many putative matches are wrong. [RANSAC](/ransac/) finds a model supported by the inliers with a probability set by the [number of samples](/ransac-iteration-bound/), so its success is probabilistic, not certain.
- **Degeneracies are real.** Points on a plane leave the fundamental matrix undetermined, and some [critical configurations](/critical-configurations/) of points and cameras have more than one reconstruction. One camera cannot see absolute scale, so monocular odometry suffers [scale drift](/scale-drift/).
- **Invariance is designed, repeatability is measured.** A detector is invariant to the changes its entry proves, and [detector repeatability](/detector-repeatability/) is a figure measured on test images.
- **Global optima where the structure allows.** A minimum s-t cut minimizes a binary energy exactly when the energy is submodular, by the [graph-representability theorem](/graph-representable-energies/). [Alpha-expansion](/alpha-expansion/) is within a factor $2c$ of the optimum. [ICP](/iterative-closest-point/) never increases its error but stops at a local minimum, by the [ICP convergence theorem](/icp-convergence-theorem/).
- **Learned methods are judged by benchmarks.** [SuperGlue](/superglue/), [Faster R-CNN](/faster-r-cnn/) and [neural radiance fields](/neural-radiance-field/) come with no guarantee of correctness; scores such as [mean average precision](/detection-mean-average-precision/) measure them.

The [pinhole camera model](/pinhole-camera-model/), [SIFT](/sift/), the [Harris corner detector](/harris-corner-detector/), [optical flow](/optical-flow/) and the [Kalman filter](/kalman-filter/) live in Volume 28. The [singular value decomposition](/singular-value-decomposition/) and [Cholesky decomposition](/cholesky-decomposition/) that solve the estimation problems come from Volume 12. [Minimum cuts](/min-cut-problem/) and the [Hungarian algorithm](/hungarian-algorithm/) come from Volume 7. [Markov random fields](/markov-random-field/), [k-means](/k-means/) and the [particle filter](/particle-filter/) come from Volume 25. [M-estimators](/m-estimator/) come from Volume 33. Robotics, Volume 37, builds on this volume in [ORB-SLAM](/orb-slam/) and [loop closure detection](/loop-closure-detection/).

## The chapters

**Chapter 38.1, Local features and matching**, finds points that can be found again in another image. Corners come from the [Moravec](/moravec-corner-detector/), [Shi-Tomasi](/shi-tomasi-corner-detector/) and [FAST](/fast-corner-detector/) detectors, blobs from a [scale space](/scale-space-representation/) with the [difference of Gaussians](/difference-of-gaussians-detector/), and affine covariant regions from [MSER](/maximally-stable-extremal-regions/). Descriptors range from [RootSIFT](/rootsift/) and [HOG](/histogram-of-oriented-gradients/) to binary strings such as [ORB](/orb-features/), compared by Hamming distance. Matches are filtered by [Lowe's ratio test](/lowe-ratio-test/) and found fast with [randomized k-d forests](/randomized-kd-forest/). [SuperPoint](/superpoint/) and [LoFTR](/loftr/) are learned.

**Chapter 38.2, Camera models and calibration**, builds the [camera projection matrix](/camera-projection-matrix/) from [homogeneous coordinates](/homogeneous-coordinates/), the [intrinsic matrix](/camera-intrinsic-matrix/) and the [extrinsic parameters](/camera-extrinsic-parameters/). [DLT resectioning](/dlt-camera-resectioning/) and [Zhang's method](/zhang-calibration-method/) calibrate from a known target; Zhang's method needs 3 views of a plane in general, or 2 with zero skew. [Brown-Conrady](/brown-conrady-distortion-model/) and [Kannala-Brandt](/kannala-brandt-fisheye-model/) models describe lens distortion. [Vanishing points](/vanishing-point-calibration/) and the [image of the absolute conic](/image-of-the-absolute-conic/) calibrate from scene structure, and [self-calibration](/self-calibration/) needs no target at all.

**Chapter 38.3, Projective and multiple view geometry**, starts in the [projective plane](/projective-plane/) and estimates [homographies](/homography/) with [Hartley normalization](/hartley-normalization/) for [image stitching](/image-stitching/). [Epipolar geometry](/epipolar-geometry/) leads to the [fundamental matrix](/fundamental-matrix/), found by the [seven-point](/seven-point-algorithm/) and [normalized eight-point](/normalized-eight-point-algorithm/) algorithms, and the [essential matrix](/essential-matrix/). [P3P](/p3p/) and [EPnP](/epnp/) give pose, and [optimal triangulation](/optimal-triangulation/) gives points. [MSAC](/msac/), [LO-RANSAC](/lo-ransac/), [PROSAC](/prosac/) and [MAGSAC](/magsac/) improve RANSAC, and the [projective reconstruction theorem](/projective-reconstruction-theorem/) says what uncalibrated views determine.

**Chapter 38.4, Structure from motion and visual odometry**, solves [nonlinear least squares](/nonlinear-least-squares/) by [Gauss-Newton](/gauss-newton-algorithm/), [Levenberg-Marquardt](/levenberg-marquardt/) and [dog leg](/powell-dogleg/) steps. [Sparse bundle adjustment](/sparse-bundle-adjustment/) eliminates the points through the [reduced camera system](/reduced-camera-system/), after fixing the [gauge](/gauge-freedom/). [Tomasi-Kanade factorization](/tomasi-kanade-factorization/) recovers an orthographic camera and the shape exactly from noise-free tracks seen in every frame. [Incremental](/incremental-structure-from-motion/) pipelines add one camera at a time, while [global](/global-structure-from-motion/) ones place all cameras at once by [rotation averaging](/rotation-averaging/) and translation averaging. [Visual odometry](/visual-odometry/) runs on video, and [direct sparse odometry](/direct-sparse-odometry/) minimizes [photometric error](/photometric-error/).

**Chapter 38.5, Stereo and dense reconstruction**, starts from [disparity](/disparity/) and [depth from disparity](/depth-from-disparity/): depth is $fb/d$, so its error grows as the square of depth. [Stereo matching](/stereo-matching/) uses costs such as the [census transform](/census-transform/), then [semi-global matching](/semi-global-matching/) or [PatchMatch stereo](/patchmatch-stereo/). [Iterated conditional modes](/iterated-conditional-modes/) stops at a local minimum, while [QPBO](/qpbo/) labels part of a non-submodular energy with values some optimum shares. Many views give [multi-view stereo](/multi-view-stereo/), [space carving](/space-carving/) and [TSDF fusion](/tsdf-fusion/), and [Gaussian splatting](/gaussian-splatting/) is fitted to posed images and judged by benchmarks.

**Chapter 38.6, 3D registration and point clouds**, works with [point clouds](/point-cloud/), their [normals](/point-cloud-normal-estimation/) and descriptors such as [point feature histograms](/point-feature-histograms/) and [spin images](/spin-images/). [Point-to-plane ICP](/point-to-plane-icp/), [generalized ICP](/generalized-icp/) and [trimmed ICP](/trimmed-icp/) change the error or the pairs. [Go-ICP](/go-icp/) finds the global optimum within a gap by branch and bound, and [TEASER](/teaser-registration/) can certify its rotation when its relaxation is tight. [Coherent point drift](/coherent-point-drift/) handles [non-rigid registration](/non-rigid-registration/).

**Chapter 38.7, Segmentation and recognition**, measures with [intersection over union](/intersection-over-union/). Retrieval uses the [bag of visual words](/bag-of-visual-words/), the [vocabulary tree](/vocabulary-tree/) and [spatial verification](/spatial-verification/). Detection runs from the [Dalal-Triggs detector](/dalal-triggs-detector/) and [deformable part models](/deformable-part-model/) to [Mask R-CNN](/mask-r-cnn/), [YOLO](/yolo-detector/) and [DETR](/detr/). [Boykov-Jolly graph cuts](/boykov-jolly-graph-cuts/) segment exactly for their energy by one minimum cut, and the [random walker](/random-walker-segmentation/) and [dense CRFs](/dense-crf/) give soft alternatives.

**Chapter 38.8, Tracking**, separates moving pixels by [background subtraction](/background-subtraction/) with the [Stauffer-Grimson model](/stauffer-grimson-background-model/). Single targets are followed by the [KLT tracker](/kanade-lucas-tomasi-tracker/), [mean shift](/mean-shift-tracking/) and correlation filters such as [MOSSE](/mosse-filter/) and [KCF](/kernelized-correlation-filter/). [Data association](/data-association/) runs from [global nearest neighbour](/global-nearest-neighbor-association/) to [JPDA](/joint-probabilistic-data-association/), [multiple hypothesis tracking](/multiple-hypothesis-tracking/) and the global [network flow tracking](/network-flow-tracking/). [SORT](/sort-tracker/) and [ByteTrack](/bytetrack/) are scored by [CLEAR MOT](/clear-mot-metrics/) and [HOTA](/hota-metric/).

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: homogeneous coordinates, the camera projection matrix, Zhang's calibration method, the Shi-Tomasi corner detector, the difference of Gaussians detector, feature matching, Lowe's ratio test, homography estimation, RANSAC, epipolar geometry, the normalized eight-point algorithm, the five-point algorithm, linear triangulation, bundle adjustment, incremental structure from motion, depth from disparity, semi-global matching, iterative closest point, object detection and multi-object tracking.

If you already know the basics, start with a chapter's opening page. Read Chapters 38.1 to 38.3 first, since most later entries match features, use camera models or estimate geometry from matches. Chapter 38.4 builds on Chapter 38.3, and Chapter 38.5 uses the calibration and epipolar geometry of both earlier ones. Chapters 38.6, 38.7 and 38.8 stand mostly on their own, with links back to the features of Chapter 38.1.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.
