## History

The problem appeared as soon as lock-free structures did. IBM's System/370 had COMPARE DOUBLE AND SWAP, which let a pointer carry a counter, and R. Kent Treiber's 1986 IBM research report used such counters for the stack that bears his name. Tags stop the ABA problem only if a removed node stays a node, so early lock-free code kept nodes in free lists of their own type. Jeff Bonwick's slab allocator (1994) and Greenwald and Cheriton's type-stable memory in the Cache Kernel (1996) made that discipline part of an allocator. John Valois gave lock-free reference counting over a type-stable pool at PODC in 1995, and Maged Michael and Michael Scott corrected it the same year. Detlefs, Martin, Moir and Steele removed the pool in 2001 with a double compare-and-swap.

Grace periods came from operating systems. Paul McKenney and John Slingwine described read-copy update in 1998, from code that had run in the DYNIX/ptx kernel since the early 1990s, and RCU entered Linux in 2002. Keir Fraser's 2004 Cambridge thesis, *Practical lock-freedom*, gave epoch-based reclamation in the form used by most libraries since then.

The first robust schemes appeared in 2002. Michael presented hazard pointers at PODC that year and the full method in *IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems* in 2004. In the same year Herlihy, Luchangco and Moir posed the repeat offender problem at DISC, and with Martin they published pass the buck in *ACM Transactions on Computer Systems* in 2005. Hart, McKenney, Demke Brown and Walpole compared QSBR, epochs, hazard pointers and reference counting in 2007; QSBR had the fastest reads, but only while no thread stalled.

The next decade tried to get cheap reads and robustness together. Braginsky, Kogan and Petrank published drop the anchor in 2013. In 2015 Trevor Brown gave DEBRA and DEBRA+, and Cohen and Petrank gave optimistic access. Ramalhete and Correia announced hazard eras in 2017, Wen, Izraelevitz, Cai, Beadle and Scott presented interval-based reclamation in 2018, and Nikolaev and Ravindran presented Hyaline in 2019 and its full family in 2021. Singh, Brown and Mashtizadeh's NBR (2021) replaced the recovery code of DEBRA+ with a restart of the read phase. In 2023 Sheffi and Petrank's ERA theorem explained why each of these schemes gives something up. Hazard pointers reached the C++26 standard library through proposal P2530.

## Further reading

- Maurice Herlihy, Nir Shavit, Victor Luchangco and Michael Spear, *The Art of Multiprocessor Programming*, second edition (2020). Introduces the ABA problem, tagged references and the lock-free structures that the schemes of this chapter protect.
- Maged M. Michael, *Hazard pointers: safe memory reclamation for lock-free objects*, IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems 15(6), 2004. The method, its proof and its use on stacks, queues and lists, in a short and clear paper.
- Keir Fraser, *Practical lock-freedom*, PhD thesis, University of Cambridge, Technical Report UCAM-CL-TR-579, 2004. The source of epoch-based reclamation, with the lock-free skip lists and trees that use it.
- Thomas E. Hart, Paul E. McKenney, Angela Demke Brown and Jonathan Walpole, *Performance of memory reclamation for lockless synchronization*, Journal of Parallel and Distributed Computing 67(12), 2007. Measures four schemes under the same workloads and shows where each one wins.
- Trevor Brown, *Reclaiming memory for lock-free data structures: there has to be a better way*, PODC 2015. Surveys the schemes known at the time by their robustness and cost, and introduces DEBRA.
- Mathieu Desnoyers, Paul E. McKenney, Alan S. Stern, Michel R. Dagenais and Jonathan Walpole, *User-level implementations of read-copy update*, IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems 23(2), 2012. RCU outside the kernel, with the QSBR flavour and offline threads.
