## History

The mathematics came first, from epidemics. Kermack and McKendrick (1927) gave the mathematical theory of epidemics behind the SIR model, with the threshold $R_0 = 1$. Daley and Kendall studied a model of rumours in which spreaders stop when they meet people who already know; in the later model of Maki and Thompson only the caller stops, and Sudbury (1985) proved that a fraction of about 0.203 never hears the rumour. Frieze and Grimmett (1985) met push gossip as a first passage problem in graphs with random arc lengths and found the time $\log_2 n + \ln n$ to leading order, and Pittel (1987) sharpened the error to $O(1)$.

Demers, Greene, Hauser, Irish, Larson, Shenker, Sturgis, Swinehart and Terry brought the ideas into computing at PODC 1987, to keep the replicas of the Clearinghouse name service at Xerox PARC in step. Their paper named anti-entropy and rumour mongering, compared push, pull and push-pull, found the relation $s = e^{-m}$, and introduced death certificates. Feige, Peleg, Raghavan and Upfal (1990) studied push on general graphs. Birman, Hayden, Ozkasap, Xiao, Budiu and Minsky (1999) took the term anti-entropy and the idea of gossip repair into bimodal multicast.

Karp, Schindelhauer, Shenker and Vöcking (FOCS 2000) defined the random phone call model, proved the $\log_3 n + O(\log \log n)$ rounds and $O(n \log \log n)$ transmissions of push-pull, and showed that no address-oblivious algorithm uses fewer than $\Omega(n \log \log n)$. Chierichetti, Lattanzi and Panconesi asked in 2010 whether push-pull is fast on every graph of high conductance and gave the first bounds, and Giakkoupis (2011) proved the tight $O(\log n / \Phi)$. On the epidemic side, Pastor-Satorras and Vespignani (2001) found that the threshold of scale-free networks tends to 0, and Wang, Chakrabarti, Wang and Faloutsos (2003) and Ganesh, Massoulié and Towsley (2005) tied the threshold to $\lambda_1(A)$.

The systems of the following decade made gossip practical at scale. Eugster, Guerraoui, Handurukande, Kermarrec and Kouznetsov presented lpbcast at DSN 2001, and Ganesh, Kermarrec and Massoulié presented SCAMP in the same year, resting on their analysis of the fanout $\ln n + c$. Gupta, Chandra and Goldszmidt (2001) stated what a scalable failure detector must achieve, and Das, Gupta and Motivala built SWIM on it at DSN 2002; Hayashibara, Défago, Yared and Katayama introduced the phi accrual detector at SRDS 2004. Newscast (2003) and Cyclon (2005) kept random partial views, and Jelasity, Voulgaris, Guerraoui, Kermarrec and van Steen (2007) placed both in one framework of peer sampling. Leitão, Pereira and Rodrigues presented HyParView and Plumtree in 2007, and van Renesse, Dumitriu, Gough and Thomas presented Scuttlebutt at LADIS 2008.

Aggregation grew in parallel. Kempe, Dobra and Gehrke gave push-sum at FOCS 2003, Xiao and Boyd (2004) studied synchronous averaging with optimal weights, Jelasity, Montresor and Babaoglu (2005) built push-pull aggregation over peer sampling, and Boyd, Ghosh, Prabhakar and Shah (2006) tied the time of pairwise gossip averaging to the spectral gap.

## Further reading

- Alan Demers et al., "Epidemic algorithms for replicated database maintenance", PODC 1987. The source of anti-entropy, rumour mongering and death certificates.
- Richard Karp, Christian Schindelhauer, Scott Shenker and Berthold Vöcking, "Randomized rumor spreading", FOCS 2000. The random phone call model, push-pull and the message lower bound.
- Anne-Marie Kermarrec and Maarten van Steen, "Gossiping in distributed systems", ACM SIGOPS Operating Systems Review 41(5), 2007. A short survey built around peer selection, data exchanged and data processing.
- Patrick Eugster, Rachid Guerraoui, Anne-Marie Kermarrec and Laurent Massoulié, "Epidemic information dissemination in distributed systems", IEEE Computer 37(5), 2004. A survey of gossip for broadcast and membership.
- Márk Jelasity, Spyros Voulgaris, Rachid Guerraoui, Anne-Marie Kermarrec and Maarten van Steen, "Gossip-based peer sampling", ACM Transactions on Computer Systems 25(3), 2007. The framework behind Newscast and Cyclon.
- Stephen Boyd, Arpita Ghosh, Balaji Prabhakar and Devavrat Shah, "Randomized gossip algorithms", IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 52(6), 2006. The spectral analysis of gossip averaging.
