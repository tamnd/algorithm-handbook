## History

Replicated services came first. In RFC 677 of 1975, Johnson and Thomas kept duplicate databases in step with timestamps, each copy keeping the latest update, which is the origin of [last-writer-wins](/last-writer-wins/). Alsberg and Day (1976) described a primary host that keeps backups up to date, and Lamport (1978) ran one state machine at each process on commands in a total order. Thomas (1979) asked a majority of copies to approve each update, and Gifford (1979) generalized this to weighted voting with read and write quorums. Oki and Liskov's Viewstamped Replication (1988) joined a primary to view changes decided by a majority, and Schneider's 1990 tutorial set out the [state machine approach](/state-machine-replication/). Budhiraja, Marzullo, Schneider and Toueg defined primary-backup precisely and proved its bounds. Attiya, Bar-Noy and Dolev (1995) built an atomic register from majorities, Lynch and Shvartsman (1997) gave its multi-writer form, and van Renesse and Schneider introduced [chain replication](/chain-replication/) in 2004.

Weaker stores grew alongside. Version vectors came from the Locus file system work of Parker et al. (1983), and Wuu and Bernstein (1984) used a matrix of clocks to tell when a log entry may be dropped. Demers et al. (1987) named [anti-entropy](/anti-entropy/) for the Clearinghouse name service at Xerox PARC, analysed push, pull and push-pull, and kept deletions as death certificates. Lipton and Sandberg (1988) defined PRAM, Herlihy and Wing (1990) linearizability, and Ahamad et al. (1995) causal memory. Terry and colleagues defined the session guarantees in 1994 for the Bayou system. Brewer stated his conjecture at PODC 2000, Gilbert and Lynch proved it in 2002, and Abadi added the latency side as PACELC in 2012. Amazon's Dynamo (DeCandia et al., 2007) put sloppy quorums, hinted handoff and Merkle-tree anti-entropy into one store, Vogels described eventual consistency in 2009, and COPS (Lloyd et al., 2011) showed causal consistency at scale.

The theory of merging came next. Baquero and Moura described replicated types with a merge in a semilattice in 1997. Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero and Zawirski named CRDTs in 2011, with the convergence theorem for both forms and a catalogue of counters, registers and sets. Bieniusa et al. (2012) made the OR-Set compact with dots and version vectors, Almeida, Shoker and Baquero introduced delta-state CRDTs in 2015, and Gomes et al. (2017) checked convergence in Isabelle. Hellerstein stated the CALM conjecture in 2010, and Ameloot, Neven and Van den Bussche proved it in 2011. For comparing replicas cheaply, Minsky, Trachtenberg and Zippel gave set reconciliation by characteristic polynomials, and Eppstein et al. (2011) used invertible Bloom lookup tables.

Shared text has its own line. Ellis and Gibbs gave operational transformation with dOPT in 1989. Jupiter (Nichols et al., 1995) used a central server, Ressel et al. (1996) stated TP1 and TP2, and Sun et al. (1998) set out the convergence, causality and intention model. Imine et al. (2003) checked published functions with a theorem prover and found counterexamples, and Randolph et al. (2013) showed that no transformation function on the classical parameters meets both conditions. Sequence CRDTs replaced transformation with identity: WOOT (Oster et al., 2006), Treedoc (Preguiça et al., 2009), Logoot (Weiss, Urso and Molli, 2009), the causal tree (Grishchenko, 2010), RGA (Roh et al., 2011), LSEQ (Nédelec et al., 2013) and YATA (Nicolaescu et al., 2016). Attiya et al. (2016) gave a precise list specification and bounds on metadata, and Kleppmann et al. (2019) showed that Logoot and LSEQ can interleave concurrent words.

## Further reading

- Marc Shapiro, Nuno Preguiça, Carlos Baquero and Marek Zawirski, A comprehensive study of convergent and commutative replicated data types, INRIA research report RR-7506, 2011. The specifications of every basic CRDT in this chapter.
- Martin Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications, O'Reilly, 2017. Leaders, leaderless quorums and conflicts in real systems.
- Paolo Viotti and Marko Vukolić, Consistency in non-transactional distributed storage systems, ACM Computing Surveys, 2016. A map of the consistency models and how they relate.
- Alan Demers et al., Epidemic algorithms for replicated database maintenance, PODC 1987. Anti-entropy, rumour mongering and death certificates in one paper.
- Hagit Attiya and Jennifer Welch, Distributed Computing: Fundamentals, Simulations and Advanced Topics, second edition, Wiley, 2004. The lower bounds behind PACELC and the quorum register.
