Two phones hold copies of one shopping list. One is offline on a train, and its owner adds milk. The other is in a shop, and its owner removes milk, because it is already in the basket. When the phones next talk, both copies must end the same, and something has to decide whether milk stays on the list. A replicated store meets this question for every key. It keeps copies on several machines so that it survives crashes and answers nearby clients fast, and the copies drift apart whenever writes arrive at different places. This chapter is about the methods that bring the copies back together, and about what a reader may see while they differ.

There are two broad answers. The first makes all copies act as one. The replicas agree on one order of writes, through consensus or through quorums that overlap, and a reader never sees an old value. The cost is time: a write and a read together take at least one message delay, and during a network partition one side must stop answering. The second lets every replica accept writes at once and merges them later. It stays available, but it needs a merge that gives the same result whatever order the replicas exchange their states in. A [conflict-free replicated data type](/crdt/) (CRDT) is a data type built to have such a merge. In a [g-counter](/g-counter/) on three replicas that hold $(2,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$ and $(0,0,1)$, the merge is the entrywise maximum $(2,1,1)$, whatever the order of the exchanges and however often they repeat, and the value is $4$.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **What a replicated store promises.** [Consistency models](/consistency-models/) from linearizable to [causal](/causal-consistency/) and [eventual](/eventual-consistency/), the [session guarantees](/session-guarantees/) [read your writes](/read-your-writes/) and [monotonic reads](/monotonic-reads/), and the costs set by the [CAP theorem](/cap-theorem/) and [PACELC](/pacelc/).
2. **Replicating a service in one order.** [State machine replication](/state-machine-replication/) and the [replicated log](/replicated-log/), run as [active](/active-replication/), [primary-backup](/primary-backup-replication/) or [chain](/chain-replication/) replication.
3. **Quorums.** [Quorum intersection](/quorum-intersection/), [quorum replication](/quorum-replication/) and [leaderless replication](/leaderless-replication/), with [read repair](/read-repair/), [sloppy quorums](/sloppy-quorum/) and [hinted handoff](/hinted-handoff/).
4. **Conflicts and background repair.** [Last-writer-wins](/last-writer-wins/), [multi-master replication](/multi-master-replication/), [anti-entropy](/anti-entropy/), [set reconciliation](/set-reconciliation/) and [tombstones](/tombstone/), joined up in [Dynamo-style replication](/dynamo-style-replication/).
5. **Why CRDTs converge.** The [join-semilattice](/join-semilattice/), [strong eventual consistency](/strong-eventual-consistency/), [state-based](/state-based-crdt/) and [op-based](/op-based-crdt/) CRDTs, the [convergence theorem](/crdt-convergence-theorem/) and the [CALM theorem](/calm-theorem/).
6. **Counters, registers and sets.** The [PN-counter](/pn-counter/), the [LWW-register](/lww-register/) and [MV-register](/mv-register/), the [G-Set](/g-set/), [2P-Set](/two-phase-set/), [LWW-Element-Set](/lww-element-set/) and [OR-Set](/or-set/), and [delta-state CRDTs](/delta-state-crdt/).
7. **Shared text.** [Collaborative text editing](/collaborative-text-editing/), [operational transformation](/operational-transformation/) with the [TP1 and TP2 conditions](/tp1-tp2-conditions/), and the sequence CRDTs [WOOT](/woot/), [Treedoc](/treedoc/), [Logoot](/logoot/), [LSEQ](/lseq/), [RGA](/rga/), the [causal tree](/causal-tree/) and [YATA](/yata/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these fourteen entries in order: consistency models, eventual consistency, the CAP theorem, state machine replication, primary-backup replication, quorum intersection, leaderless replication, last-writer-wins, anti-entropy, the join-semilattice, the CRDT, the g-counter, the OR-Set and RGA. They give the contracts, the cost of the strong ones, the two ways to keep one order, the counting fact behind every quorum store, the simplest conflict rule and its flaw, the repair process that makes weak stores converge, and the algebra that makes CRDTs converge, with one counter, one set and one sequence built on it. Run the OR-Set by hand on two replicas with an add and a concurrent remove, and compare the result with the 2P-Set and the LWW-Element-Set on the same run. The three sets differ only in that case, and it is the case that matters.

PACELC, the CALM theorem and the TP1 and TP2 conditions are short but abstract, and can wait until the rest is familiar. Set reconciliation is a self-contained piece of coding theory. Of the sequence CRDTs, LSEQ only changes the allocation of Logoot, and the causal tree is RGA seen as a tree, so each can be read with its partner.
