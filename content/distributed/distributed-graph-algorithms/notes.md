## History

The first distributed graph algorithms came from computer networks. [Flooding](/flooding/) is folklore of early packet networks, and the ARPANET from 1969 routed by a distributed form of [Bellman-Ford](/distributed-bellman-ford/), in which each node sent its distance table to its neighbours, until count-to-infinity pushed it to a link-state method in 1979. Dijkstra and Scholten (1980) gave the [detection of termination](/dijkstra-scholten/) for diffusing computations, Chang (1982) named the [echo algorithm](/echo-algorithm/), and Segall (1983) treated propagation of information with and without feedback. Chandy and Misra (1986) showed that the Dijkstra-Scholten count of control messages is optimal. Awerbuch (1985) gave the [synchronizers](/synchronizer/) α, β and γ, with BFS as the main application, and Awerbuch and Peleg (1990) later reached polylogarithmic overheads.

Spira (1977) and Dalal (1977) asked what a distributed MST must cost in messages. Gallager, Humblet and Spira wrote [their algorithm](/gallager-humblet-spira/) at MIT in 1979 and published it in 1983, with levels for the fragments and the bound $5n \log_2 n + 2m$; it received the Dijkstra Prize in 2004. Awerbuch (1987) brought the time to $O(n)$. Garay, Kutten and Peleg gave the first sublinear algorithm in 1993, and Kutten and Peleg reached $O(D + \sqrt n \log^* n)$ in 1995. Peleg and Rubinovich showed at FOCS 1999 that this is close to optimal, and Das Sarma et al. (STOC 2011) extended the [bound](/peleg-rubinovich-lower-bound/) to randomized and approximate algorithms and to many other problems through set disjointness. Frischknecht, Holzer and Wattenhofer (SODA 2012) showed that the [diameter](/diameter-lower-bound-distributed/) needs almost linear time, and $O(n)$ algorithms by Holzer and Wattenhofer and by Peleg, Roditty and Tal appeared in the same year.

Locality has its own line. Angluin (STOC 1980) showed how symmetry limits anonymous networks, the setting of [port numbering](/port-numbering/). Cole and Vishkin (1986) invented deterministic coin tossing for list ranking on the PRAM, and Goldberg, Plotkin and Shannon (1988) applied it to rooted trees and bounded-degree graphs. Linial's FOCS 1987 paper, published in the journal in 1992, set up the [LOCAL model](/local-model/), proved the $\Omega(\log^* n)$ bound for the ring and gave $O(\Delta^2)$ colours on all graphs; Naor (1991) extended the bound to randomized algorithms. Luby and, independently, Alon, Babai and Itai found randomized MIS algorithms in 1986. Awerbuch, Goldberg, Luby and Plotkin (FOCS 1989) introduced [network decomposition](/network-decomposition/), and Peleg's book (2000) named the LOCAL and [CONGEST](/congest-model/) models.

The lower bounds for local problems came later. Naor and Stockmeyer (1995) asked what can be computed in a constant number of rounds and defined [locally checkable labelings](/locally-checkable-labeling/). Kuhn, Moscibroda and Wattenhofer proved the [KMW bound](/kmw-lower-bound/) at PODC 2004, improved it at SODA 2006 and published the full version in 2016. Brandt et al. (STOC 2016) used [sinkless orientation](/sinkless-orientation/) to start round elimination, and Chang, Kopelowitz and Pettie separated randomized from deterministic complexity. Rozhoň and Ghaffari (STOC 2020) found a deterministic network decomposition in polylogarithmic time, which derandomizes every polylogarithmic LCL algorithm, and Ghaffari and Haeupler (SODA 2016) introduced [low-congestion shortcuts](/low-congestion-shortcuts/) to beat $\sqrt n$ on planar networks.

## Further reading

- Nancy A. Lynch, Distributed Algorithms, Morgan Kaufmann, 1996. Synchronous and asynchronous BFS, Bellman-Ford, GHS and the synchronizers, with full proofs.
- David Peleg, Distributed Computing: A Locality-Sensitive Approach, SIAM, 2000. The LOCAL and CONGEST models, network decompositions, covers and MST algorithms.
- Juho Hirvonen and Jukka Suomela, Distributed Algorithms 2020, online textbook. Port numbering, colouring and lower bounds by round elimination, with many exercises.
- Leonid Barenboim and Michael Elkin, Distributed Graph Coloring: Fundamentals and Recent Developments, Morgan and Claypool, 2013. Colouring, MIS and matching from Cole-Vishkin and Linial to the algorithms of the early 2010s.
- Gerard Tel, Introduction to Distributed Algorithms, second edition, Cambridge University Press, 2000. Wave algorithms, echo and termination detection in an asynchronous setting.
