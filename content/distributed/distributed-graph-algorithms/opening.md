Six computers sit on a ring with the identifiers 213, 87, 215, 154, 138 and 14, and each one can talk only to its two neighbours. They want colours from $\{0, 1, 2\}$ with no two neighbours alike. In 6 rounds of messages, [Cole-Vishkin](/cole-vishkin/) gives them the colours 0, 2, 0, 2, 1, 2. On a ring of a million nodes it needs only one round more, yet no algorithm can do it in a fixed number of rounds as the ring grows. A network of routers that wants a [minimum spanning tree](/distributed-mst/) of its own links faces a different limit. Some node must hear about edges $D$ hops away, and when a message carries only $O(\log n)$ bits, about $\sqrt n$ rounds are needed even if $D$ is small.

These are distributed graph algorithms: the network is the input graph, every node is a processor that starts out knowing only its own edges, and the cost is the number of synchronous rounds or the number of messages. Two kinds of problem fill the chapter. Local problems such as colouring, [maximal independent set](/distributed-mis/) and matching need only a small neighbourhood, and their study is about breaking symmetry fast. Global problems such as spanning trees, shortest paths and the diameter need information from across the network, and their study is about bandwidth.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **Models and measures.** The [port numbering](/port-numbering/) model, [message complexity](/message-complexity/), the [LOCAL model](/local-model/) and [round complexity](/round-complexity/), with the rule that $r$ rounds tell a node exactly its ball of radius $r$.
2. **Broadcast and waves.** [Flooding](/flooding/), [spanning trees](/distributed-spanning-tree/), [convergecast](/convergecast/), the [echo algorithm](/echo-algorithm/), [distributed BFS](/breadth-first-search-distributed/), [synchronizers](/synchronizer/) and [termination detection](/termination-detection/) with [Dijkstra-Scholten](/dijkstra-scholten/).
3. **Limited bandwidth and changing links.** The [CONGEST model](/congest-model/), the [congested clique](/congested-clique-model/) and [dynamic networks](/distributed-graph-dynamics/).
4. **Colouring and the log-star barrier.** [Ring colouring](/ring-coloring/), Cole-Vishkin, [Linial's lower bound](/linial-coloring-lower-bound/), [Linial's algorithm](/linial-algorithm/), and [vertex](/distributed-vertex-coloring/) and [edge colouring](/distributed-edge-coloring/) of general graphs.
5. **Independent sets, matchings and locally checkable problems.** [Locally checkable labelings](/locally-checkable-labeling/), MIS, [ruling sets](/ruling-set/), [maximal matching](/distributed-maximal-matching/) and [sinkless orientation](/sinkless-orientation/).
6. **Clustering the network.** [Low-diameter decompositions](/low-diameter-decomposition/), [network decompositions](/network-decomposition/) and [sparse neighbourhood covers](/sparse-neighborhood-cover/).
7. **Approximation and the KMW bound.** [Vertex cover](/distributed-minimum-vertex-cover/), [dominating set](/distributed-dominating-set/), [weighted matching](/distributed-weighted-matching/) and the [KMW lower bound](/kmw-lower-bound/) on what $k$ rounds can approximate.
8. **Spanning trees and cuts in CONGEST.** [Connected components](/distributed-connected-components/), MST, [Gallager-Humblet-Spira](/gallager-humblet-spira/), the [Peleg-Rubinovich bound](/peleg-rubinovich-lower-bound/), [low-congestion shortcuts](/low-congestion-shortcuts/), [min cut](/distributed-min-cut/) and [max flow](/distributed-max-flow/).
9. **Distances and counting.** [Distributed Bellman-Ford](/distributed-bellman-ford/), [exact](/distributed-shortest-paths/) and [approximate](/distributed-approximate-sssp/) single-source distances, [all pairs](/distributed-apsp/), the [diameter](/distributed-diameter/) and its [lower bound](/diameter-lower-bound-distributed/), [triangle enumeration](/distributed-triangle-enumeration/) and [PageRank](/distributed-pagerank/).

## A first reading

Read these twelve entries in order: [the LOCAL model](/local-model/), [round complexity](/round-complexity/), [flooding](/flooding/), [the echo algorithm](/echo-algorithm/), [distributed BFS](/breadth-first-search-distributed/), [the CONGEST model](/congest-model/), [Cole-Vishkin](/cole-vishkin/), [Linial's lower bound](/linial-coloring-lower-bound/), [distributed MIS](/distributed-mis/), [distributed MST](/distributed-mst/), [the Peleg-Rubinovich bound](/peleg-rubinovich-lower-bound/) and [distributed Bellman-Ford](/distributed-bellman-ford/). They give the two models, the basic waves, one tight local bound ($\Theta(\log^* n)$ for 3-colouring a ring), the central local problem, and the $\sqrt n$ barrier for global problems in CONGEST. Work the Cole-Vishkin example by hand before reading the lower bound; the proof makes sense only once you have seen how a colour shrinks.

On a first pass you can skip the synchronizer, termination detection and Dijkstra-Scholten, which matter for asynchronous networks; the clustering group, which is used by the best deterministic algorithms; and the research-level entries on min cut, max flow, approximate SSSP and shortcuts. The GHS entry is long and asynchronous. Read the MST entry first, and come back to GHS for its level rule and its message bound $5n\log_2 n + 2m$.
