Before a program can count words, tag them or feed them to a model, it has to decide what the words are. The string "Don't stop." is two tokens if you split at white space (Don't, stop.), four under the Penn Treebank rules (Do, n't, stop, .) and three under the Unicode word rules (Don't, stop, .). A Chinese sentence has no spaces at all, a period may end a sentence or an abbreviation, and the same word appears as "walk", "walks" and "walked". The choices made at this first step decide what every later step can see. A search engine that keeps "U.S." and "us" apart, or a translation model that has never seen the token "unaffable", fails because of choices made here.

The chapter covers the steps that turn raw text into units: finding the language, cutting text into tokens and sentences, restoring case, segmenting text written without spaces, analysing the inner structure of words, and building the subword vocabularies that neural models use. Most of the methods are rules or greedy procedures. A few have exact algorithms behind them, such as dynamic programming for word segmentation and finite-state transducers for morphology. The last entry shows why the subword methods do not try to be optimal.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Which language.** [Language identification](/language-identification/) as a classification problem over character or byte n-grams, and the [Cavnar-Trenkle method](/cavnar-trenkle-language-identification/), which compares ranked n-gram profiles with the out-of-place distance.
2. **Tokens and words.** [Tokenization](/text-tokenization/), specified by rules, by a vocabulary with an encoding rule, or by an objective; the rules of [Penn Treebank tokenization](/penn-treebank-tokenization/); and the [out-of-vocabulary word](/out-of-vocabulary-word/), the test token that training never saw.
3. **Sentences and case.** [Sentence boundary detection](/sentence-boundary-detection/), the [Punkt tokenizer](/punkt-sentence-tokenizer/), which learns abbreviations from raw text with likelihood ratios, and [truecasing](/truecasing/), which restores the case of text written in one case.
4. **Text without spaces.** [Word segmentation](/word-segmentation/) by dynamic programming over a unigram model, and [maximum matching](/maximum-matching-segmentation/), the greedy dictionary method, with the inputs on which it fails.
5. **Morphology.** [Lemmatization](/lemmatization/), [morphological analysis](/morphological-analysis/) as a regular relation, [two-level morphology](/two-level-morphology/) with rules on pairs of letters, and [Morfessor](/morfessor/), which learns morphs with no labels under a description length cost.
6. **Subword vocabularies.** [Subword tokenization](/subword-tokenization/) and its three main methods: [WordPiece](/wordpiece/), [byte-level BPE](/byte-level-bpe/) and the [unigram language model](/unigram-language-model-tokenization/). [Fast WordPiece](/fast-wordpiece-tokenization/) encodes a word in linear time.
7. **Sampling and limits.** [Subword regularization](/subword-regularization/) and [BPE-dropout](/bpe-dropout/) train on sampled segmentations, and the [NP-hardness of optimal tokenization](/optimal-tokenization-hardness/) shows that choosing the vocabulary with the fewest tokens is NP-complete.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these ten entries in order: [tokenization](/text-tokenization/), [Penn Treebank tokenization](/penn-treebank-tokenization/), [out-of-vocabulary word](/out-of-vocabulary-word/), [sentence boundary detection](/sentence-boundary-detection/), [word segmentation](/word-segmentation/), [maximum matching](/maximum-matching-segmentation/), [lemmatization](/lemmatization/), [subword tokenization](/subword-tokenization/), [WordPiece](/wordpiece/) and [unigram language model tokenization](/unigram-language-model-tokenization/). They are marked *Intro* or *Core*. Work the examples by hand: the word segmentation of "thetabledownthere", the BPE merges on the corpus low, lower, newest and widest, and the seven segmentations of "hugs" under the unigram model are small enough to check with a pencil. Two-level morphology, Morfessor, the two sampling methods and the hardness proof can wait for a second pass.

You need the [Unicode code point](/unicode-code-point/) and [UTF-8](/utf-8/) from Chapter 8.8, and [regular expressions](/regular-expression/) and the [finite-state transducer](/finite-state-transducer/) from Volume 9. The statistical entries use [maximum likelihood estimation](/maximum-likelihood-estimation/), the [Viterbi algorithm](/viterbi-algorithm/), the [forward-backward algorithm](/forward-backward-algorithm/), [expectation maximization](/expectation-maximization/), [minimum description length](/minimum-description-length/) and [dropout](/dropout/) from Volume 25, and Punkt uses the [likelihood ratio test](/likelihood-ratio-test/) from Volume 33. Truecasing and the out-of-vocabulary page use [n-gram language models](/n-gram-language-model/) from Chapter 41.3, lemmatization uses [part-of-speech tagging](/part-of-speech-tagging/) from Chapter 41.5, and the exact sampler of subword regularization draws paths of a [word lattice](/word-lattice/), defined in Chapter 41.9.
