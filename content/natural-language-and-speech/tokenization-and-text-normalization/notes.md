## History

The rule-based side of the chapter grew with the first large corpora. The Penn Treebank of Mitchell Marcus, Beatrice Santorini and Mary Ann Marcinkiewicz (1993) fixed a set of token conventions for English, and Robert MacIntyre wrote the sed script that applies them in late 1995. Sentence splitting was learned from labeled data by the regression trees of Michael Riley (1989), by the Satz system of David Palmer and Marti Hearst (1997) and by the maximum entropy model of Jeffrey Reynar and Adwait Ratnaparkhi (1997). Tibor Kiss and Jan Strunk treated abbreviations as collocations of a word and a period in 2002, and published Punkt, which needs no labels, in *Computational Linguistics* in 2006. Lucian Lita and colleagues described truecasing with a trigram model and Viterbi decoding in 2003.

Language identification by character n-grams goes back at least to William Cavnar and John Trenkle, who presented their rank profiles and the out-of-place distance at SDAIR-94. Marco Lui and Timothy Baldwin later built langid.py (2012) on naive Bayes over selected byte n-grams. For text without spaces, maximum matching was a common method for Chinese by the early 1990s; Keh-Jiann Chen and Shing-Huan Liu (1992) and Pak-kwong Wong and Chorkin Chan (1996) describe variants. Richard Sproat, Chilin Shih, William Gale and Nancy Chang (1996) cast segmentation as a weighted finite-state problem and measured how often native speakers disagree.

Finite-state morphology began with Kimmo Koskenniemi's thesis of 1983 and his COLING paper of 1984, which described Finnish with rules on pairs of letters. Ronald Kaplan and Martin Kay showed in 1994 that ordered rewrite rules of the kind phonologists write define regular relations, so that a whole grammar compiles into one transducer. Kenneth Beesley and Lauri Karttunen described the tools in 2003. On the unsupervised side, Mathias Creutz and Krista Lagus proposed recursive splitting under a description length cost in 2002, the model later called the Morfessor Baseline.

Subword vocabularies came from speech and translation. Mike Schuster and Kaisuke Nakajima (2012) built word pieces for Japanese and Korean voice search, and Yonghui Wu and colleagues used them in Google's translation system in 2016. Rico Sennrich, Barry Haddow and Alexandra Birch (2016) took byte-pair encoding, a compression method that Philip Gage published in 1994, into neural translation. BERT (Jacob Devlin and colleagues, 2019) made WordPiece with greedy longest match the common form, and GPT-2 (Alec Radford and colleagues, 2019) ran BPE on bytes. Taku Kudo (2018) introduced the unigram model and subword regularization, and released SentencePiece with John Richardson the same year. Ivan Provilkov, Dmitrii Emelianenko and Elena Voita gave BPE-dropout in 2020, and Xinying Song and colleagues the linear-time WordPiece encoder in 2021.

The theory of tokenization is recent. László Kozma and Johannes Voderholzer showed in 2024 that maximizing the compression of a pair encoding is APX-complete and bounded the ratio that greedy BPE reaches, and Philip Whittington, Gregor Bachmann and Tiago Pimentel proved at ACL 2025 that choosing an optimal vocabulary, directly or by merges, is NP-complete. Violeta Kastreva and colleagues extended the hardness to bounded alphabets later in 2025.

## Further reading

- Daniel Jurafsky and James H. Martin, *Speech and Language Processing*, third edition draft. The standard textbook; its chapters on words, tokens and subwords cover this chapter at an introductory level.
- Kenneth R. Beesley and Lauri Karttunen, *Finite State Morphology* (CSLI Publications, 2003). The practical account of two-level rules, lexicons and the composition of transducers.
- Ronald M. Kaplan and Martin Kay, "Regular models of phonological rule systems", *Computational Linguistics* 20(3), 1994. The proof that rewrite rules compile to finite-state transducers.
- Tommi Jauhiainen, Marco Lui, Marcos Zampieri, Timothy Baldwin and Krister Lindén, "Automatic language identification in texts: a survey", *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* 65, 2019. A long survey of features, methods and evaluation for language identification.
- Taku Kudo, "Subword regularization: improving neural network translation models with multiple subword candidates", ACL 2018. Defines the unigram model and its training and sampling, with comparisons to BPE.
