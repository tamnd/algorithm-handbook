## History

Summarization is the oldest topic of the chapter. Hans Peter Luhn described his extracts from significant words in the IBM Journal of Research and Development in 1958, and H. P. Edmundson added cue words, title words and sentence position in 1969. Jaime Carbonell and Jade Goldstein introduced maximal marginal relevance in 1998 to trade relevance against redundancy. In 2004 Gunes Erkan and Dragomir Radev published LexRank and Rada Mihalcea and Paul Tarau published TextRank, both ranking sentences by a random walk in the manner of PageRank. The same year Chin-Yew Lin released ROUGE, which became the standard score at the Document Understanding Conferences, and Ryan McDonald showed in 2007 that selection with redundancy is NP-hard.

Generation from data grew in parallel. Robert Dale and Ehud Reiter published the incremental algorithm for referring expressions in Cognitive Science in 1995, and Reiter and Dale set out the pipeline of document planning, microplanning and realization in 1997 and in their book of 2000.

Neural sequence models made decoding a topic of its own. Beam search came to translation from speech recognition. The Google translation system of Yonghui Wu and colleagues (2016) used a length penalty and a coverage penalty, and Philipp Koehn and Rebecca Knowles (2017) listed the drop in quality with wide beams among six challenges of neural translation. Felix Stahlberg and Bill Byrne (2019) ran exact search and found that the mode was often the empty output, and Bryan Eikema and Wilker Aziz (2020) named the inadequacy of the mode and proposed sampling-based minimum Bayes risk decoding, which Vaibhava Goel and William Byrne (2000) and Shankar Kumar and William Byrne (2004) had used for speech and statistical translation. On the training side, Samy Bengio and colleagues proposed scheduled sampling in 2015, and Marc'Aurelio Ranzato and colleagues named exposure bias in 2016.

Open-ended generation needed sampling. Angela Fan, Mike Lewis and Yann Dauphin used top-k sampling for stories in 2018, and Ari Holtzman and colleagues introduced nucleus sampling at ICLR 2020. Nitish Keskar and colleagues proposed the repetition penalty with the CTRL model in 2019, Yixuan Su and colleagues contrastive search in 2022, and Clara Meister and colleagues typical sampling in 2023. Among the beam variants, Ashwin Vijayakumar and colleagues posted diverse beam search in 2016, Chris Hokamp and Qun Liu published grid beam search in 2017, and Wouter Kool, Herke van Hoof and Max Welling gave stochastic beam search in 2019.

Speed became the main concern with large transformers. The key-value cache follows from the causal attention of Vaswani and colleagues (2017); Noam Shazeer proposed multi-query attention in 2019 to shrink it, and Joshua Ainslie and colleagues grouped-query attention in 2023. Speculative decoding was published in 2023 by Yaniv Leviathan, Matan Kalman and Yossi Matias, and independently by Charlie Chen and colleagues at DeepMind as speculative sampling. Xuezhi Wang and colleagues introduced self-consistency in 2023, and Tianyi Zhang and colleagues BERTScore in 2020.

## Further reading

- Ani Nenkova and Kathleen McKeown, *Automatic Summarization*, Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval 5(2-3), 2011. A survey of extractive methods, their scores and their evaluation.
- Ehud Reiter and Robert Dale, *Building Natural Language Generation Systems* (Cambridge University Press, 2000). The standard account of the generation pipeline, with referring expressions and realization.
- Ari Holtzman, Jan Buys, Li Du, Maxwell Forbes and Yejin Choi, "The curious case of neural text degeneration", ICLR 2020. Shows why the mode and pure sampling both give poor text, and defines nucleus sampling.
- Bryan Eikema and Wilker Aziz, "Is MAP decoding all you need? The inadequacy of the mode in neural machine translation", COLING 2020. The case against the mode, and minimum Bayes risk over samples as the alternative.
- Yaniv Leviathan, Matan Kalman and Yossi Matias, "Fast inference from transformers via speculative decoding", ICML 2023. The algorithm, the proof that it samples exactly, and the analysis of its speed up.
