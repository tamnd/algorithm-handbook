A recognizer turns sound into words. One second of speech becomes about 100 frames of features, each a vector of 39 numbers or a row of 80 filter bank energies, and the recognizer must choose, among all sentences over a vocabulary of tens of thousands of words, the one that best explains those frames. The classical answer splits the problem in two: an acoustic model gives $P(O \mid W)$, the probability of the frames given a word sequence, and a language model gives $P(W)$; the recognizer searches for the $W$ that maximizes their product. Neural recognizers of the last decade learn the map from frames to characters or word pieces directly, but they still search over outputs, and they are still judged by the word error rate on a test set.

The chapter follows this history and adds the recognition of speakers. Some of its algorithms are exact for their model: the forward and Viterbi recursions of an HMM, forced alignment, the CTC sum over alignments, the composition and determinization of weighted transducers, and the posteriors of a word lattice. Training is not: EM and Viterbi training never lower the likelihood but reach only a stationary point, and neural models are trained by gradient methods. The search with a full language model is approximate, because pruning drops hypotheses that might have won. The error rates the entries quote are measurements on particular corpora.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups, read in order.

1. **The problem and its measure.** [Automatic speech recognition](/automatic-speech-recognition/) as a problem, the [source-channel formulation](/source-channel-speech-recognition/), the [pronunciation lexicon](/pronunciation-lexicon/) and the [word error rate](/word-error-rate/).
2. **Template matching.** [Dynamic time warping](/dynamic-time-warping/) of a spoken word against stored examples, and the [Sakoe-Chiba band](/sakoe-chiba-band/) that limits the warp.
3. **HMM acoustic models.** The [GMM-HMM acoustic model](/gmm-hmm-acoustic-model/), [forced alignment](/forced-alignment/), [Viterbi training](/viterbi-training/), the [triphone model](/triphone-model/) and [state tying by phonetic decision trees](/phonetic-decision-tree-state-tying/).
4. **Adaptation to the speaker.** [Vocal tract length normalization](/vocal-tract-length-normalization/), [MAP adaptation](/map-speaker-adaptation/) and [maximum likelihood linear regression](/maximum-likelihood-linear-regression/).
5. **Search and its output.** [Time-synchronous beam search](/time-synchronous-beam-search/), [token passing](/token-passing/) and [stack decoding](/stack-decoding/); the [word lattice](/word-lattice/), [lattice rescoring](/lattice-rescoring/), the [confusion network](/confusion-network/) and [keyword spotting](/keyword-spotting/).
6. **Weighted transducers.** [Composition](/weighted-transducer-composition/), [determinization](/weighted-determinization/), [weight pushing](/weight-pushing/) and the [HCLG decoding graph](/hclg-decoding-graph/) that combines them.
7. **Discriminative and neural acoustic models.** [MMI training](/maximum-mutual-information-training/), the [hybrid DNN-HMM](/hybrid-dnn-hmm/), [lattice-free MMI](/lattice-free-mmi/) and [SpecAugment](/specaugment/).
8. **End-to-end recognition.** [Connectionist temporal classification](/connectionist-temporal-classification/), [CTC prefix beam search](/ctc-prefix-beam-search/), the [RNN transducer](/rnn-transducer/), [Listen, Attend and Spell](/listen-attend-and-spell/), [joint CTC-attention](/joint-ctc-attention/), [shallow fusion](/shallow-fusion/), the [Conformer](/conformer/) and [wav2vec 2.0](/wav2vec-2/).
9. **Speakers.** [Speaker verification](/speaker-verification/), the [equal error rate](/equal-error-rate/), [GMM-UBM verification](/gmm-ubm-speaker-verification/), the [i-vector](/i-vector/), [PLDA scoring](/plda-scoring/), the [x-vector](/x-vector/) and [speaker diarization](/speaker-diarization/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these thirteen entries in order: automatic speech recognition, the source-channel formulation, the pronunciation lexicon, the word error rate, dynamic time warping, the GMM-HMM acoustic model, forced alignment, the triphone model, time-synchronous beam search, the word lattice, connectionist temporal classification, speaker verification and the equal error rate. They are marked *Intro* or *Core*, apart from CTC, which is the shortest way into the end-to-end models. Work the small cases by hand: the alignment of a reference and a hypothesis of seven words, the warping table of two sequences of four values, and the fifteen CTC paths that spell "ab" in four frames. The transducer algorithms, discriminative training and the speaker embeddings can wait for a second pass.
