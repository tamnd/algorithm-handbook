## History

Statistical part-of-speech tagging began in 1988, when Kenneth Church and Steven DeRose each published a tagger that combined tag transition and word emission probabilities estimated from a tagged corpus. Eric Brill answered in 1992 with a tagger that learns an ordered list of transformation rules from its own errors, and gave the full account of transformation-based learning in Computational Linguistics in 1995. Thorsten Brants's TnT tagger of 2000 showed that a well-smoothed trigram HMM matches the more elaborate models of its time. Slav Petrov, Dipanjan Das and Ryan McDonald proposed a universal set of twelve tags in 2012, and the Universal Dependencies project later extended it to the 17 tags used across its treebanks.

The discriminative models came next. Adwait Ratnaparkhi's maximum entropy tagger of 1996 conditioned each tag on rich features of the context. Andrew McCallum, Dayne Freitag and Fernando Pereira made the conditional Markov model explicit in 2000, and in 2001 John Lafferty, McCallum and Pereira introduced conditional random fields, with the label bias problem as the motivating example. Michael Collins's structured perceptron of 2002 trained the same kind of model with Viterbi decoding and additive updates, with a mistake bound carried over from Rosenblatt's perceptron. Sunita Sarawagi and William Cohen generalized the chain to segments with the semi-Markov CRF in 2004. Neural encoders took over the features: Zhiheng Huang, Wei Xu and Kai Yu put a CRF on a bidirectional LSTM in 2015, and Guillaume Lample and colleagues added character-level words in 2016.

The chunking and entity tasks were shaped by encodings and shared tasks. Lance Ramshaw and Mitchell Marcus cast base noun phrase chunking as tagging with inside, outside and begin tags in 1995. The CoNLL-2000 shared task on chunking and the CoNLL-2003 task on named entities in English and German fixed the data, the IOB encodings and the span F1 score that later work reports.

Coreference has a longer history. Jerry Hobbs published his tree-search algorithm for pronouns in 1978. Susan Brennan, Marilyn Friedman and Carl Pollard gave a pronoun algorithm based on centering in 1987, and Barbara Grosz, Aravind Joshi and Scott Weinstein published the full centering framework in 1995. Marc Vilain and colleagues defined the link-based MUC score in 1995, and Amit Bagga and Breck Baldwin proposed B-cubed in 1998 to address its weaknesses. Wee Meng Soon, Hwee Tou Ng and Daniel Lim built the standard learned mention-pair system in 2001, and Vincent Ng and Claire Cardie improved it with best-first linking in 2002. Kenton Lee and colleagues replaced the pipeline with end-to-end span ranking in 2017.

Extraction from the web began with Sergey Brin's DIPRE in 1998, which grew patterns and author-title pairs from five seed books. Razvan Bunescu and Marius Paşca used Wikipedia to disambiguate named entities in 2006, David Milne and Ian Witten learned to link Wikipedia mentions in 2008, and Lev Ratinov and colleagues combined local and global evidence in 2011. Mike Mintz, Steven Bills, Rion Snow and Dan Jurafsky introduced distant supervision for relation extraction in 2009, and Sebastian Riedel, Limin Yao and McCallum relaxed its assumption in 2010. On the unsupervised side, Sharon Goldwater and Tom Griffiths showed in 2007 that a Bayesian HMM sampled by Gibbs sampling tags better than one trained by EM.

## Further reading

- Daniel Jurafsky and James H. Martin, *Speech and Language Processing*, third edition draft. Its chapters on sequence labeling, extraction and coreference cover most of this chapter.
- Charles Sutton and Andrew McCallum, "An introduction to conditional random fields", *Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning* 4(4), 2012. A full account of linear-chain and general CRFs, their inference and their training.
- Michael Collins, "Discriminative training methods for hidden Markov models: theory and experiments with perceptron algorithms", EMNLP 2002. The structured perceptron, its convergence proof and the averaged variant.
- Vincent Ng, "Supervised noun phrase coreference research: the first fifteen years", ACL 2010. A survey of mention-pair, entity-mention and ranking models and of the evaluation scores.
- Erik F. Tjong Kim Sang and Fien De Meulder, "Introduction to the CoNLL-2003 shared task: language-independent named entity recognition", CoNLL 2003. The data, encoding and evaluation that most entity recognition papers still use.
