A speech signal is a list of numbers, 16000 of them per second at the usual rate for recognition. Very little can be read from the numbers themselves. The useful facts are a few slower quantities: the pitch of the voice, about 100 to 250 Hz for most adult speakers; the formants, the resonances of the vocal tract, which tell an "ee" from an "ah"; and the overall shape of the spectrum, which changes every few tens of milliseconds. Speech analysis computes these quantities from the samples. Speech synthesis goes the other way and makes samples from text, through the same quantities or, in recent systems, through a learned spectrogram.

The chapter covers both directions. On the analysis side it builds the features that recognizers read (the mel filter bank, MFCCs and their normalizations), the pitch and formant trackers, detectors that find speech in noise, and coders that send speech at a few kilobits per second. On the synthesis side it follows text-to-speech from the written text to the waveform: text normalization, grapheme-to-phoneme conversion, and four generations of synthesizers, from formant rules to neural networks. Most of these methods are signal procedures, and their accuracy is measured, not proved. A few have exact parts: linear prediction solves the Toeplitz normal equations exactly by the Levinson-Durbin recursion, the pitch trackers find the best path for their costs by dynamic programming, and parameter generation solves a banded linear system.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **The speech signal.** The [source-filter model](/source-filter-model/), which splits speech into an excitation and a vocal tract filter, the [zero-crossing rate](/zero-crossing-rate/) and [pre-emphasis](/pre-emphasis/), the first-order filter that boosts the high frequencies.
2. **Speech in noise.** [Voice activity detection](/voice-activity-detection/) as a problem, the [Rabiner-Sambur endpoint detector](/rabiner-sambur-endpoint-detection/) on energy and zero crossings, the [Ephraim-Malah estimator](/ephraim-malah-estimator/) for noise suppression, and the [likelihood-ratio detector](/likelihood-ratio-vad/), which tests each frame bin by bin.
3. **Spectral features.** The [mel scale](/mel-scale/), the [mel filter bank](/mel-filter-bank/), [MFCCs](/mel-frequency-cepstral-coefficients/), [cepstral mean normalization](/cepstral-mean-normalization/) and [delta features](/delta-features/); then [perceptual linear prediction](/perceptual-linear-prediction/) and [RASTA filtering](/rasta-filtering/).
4. **Pitch and formants.** [Cepstral pitch detection](/cepstral-pitch-detection/), the [RAPT](/rapt-pitch-tracker/) and [pYIN](/pyin/) trackers, and [formant estimation](/formant-estimation/) from the roots of the prediction polynomial.
5. **Coding and modification.** [Code-excited linear prediction](/code-excited-linear-prediction/), the [phase vocoder](/phase-vocoder/) and [PSOLA](/psola/).
6. **From text to sounds.** [Text-to-speech](/text-to-speech/) as a problem, the [mean opinion score](/mean-opinion-score/), [text normalization for TTS](/tts-text-normalization/), [grapheme-to-phoneme conversion](/grapheme-to-phoneme-conversion/) and the [joint-sequence model](/joint-sequence-g2p/).
7. **Classical synthesis.** [Formant synthesis](/formant-synthesis/), [unit selection](/unit-selection-synthesis/), [maximum likelihood parameter generation](/maximum-likelihood-parameter-generation/) and [HMM-based synthesis](/hmm-based-speech-synthesis/).
8. **Neural synthesis.** [Tacotron](/tacotron/), [FastSpeech](/fastspeech/), [WaveNet](/wavenet/), [HiFi-GAN](/hifi-gan/), [VITS](/vits/) and the [neural codec language model](/neural-codec-language-model/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: the source-filter model, pre-emphasis, voice activity detection, the mel scale, the mel filter bank, MFCCs, cepstral mean normalization, delta features, formant estimation, text-to-speech, the mean opinion score and grapheme-to-phoneme conversion. They are marked *Intro* or *Core*, and together they give the front end of a recognizer and the plan of a synthesizer. Work the small examples by hand: the filter edges of the mel filter bank for four filters at 8 kHz, the formant at 500 Hz from a second-order polynomial, and the deltas of the sequence 1, 3, 6, 10, 15. The advanced trackers and detectors, PLP and RASTA, and the neural synthesis models can wait for a second pass; the neural models need the attention and transformer entries of Chapters 41.3 and 41.7 first.

The neural models use the [transformer](/transformer/) and the [encoder-decoder model](/encoder-decoder-model/) of Chapters 41.3 and 41.7.
