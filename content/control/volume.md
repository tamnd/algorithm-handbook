A chemical reactor must hold its temperature near 350 K while the feed changes. Engineers fit a model of a few differential equations to data from a test in which the coolant valve opens and closes in a random pattern. They linearize the model at the operating point and design a PID loop, or a state feedback gain, that puts every closed-loop eigenvalue in the left half plane. A Kalman filter estimates the temperatures that no sensor measures. The valve cannot open past fully open, so a model predictive controller solves a quadratic program every few seconds to plan within that limit. A finite automaton decides when the reactor may start, stop or switch to a safe mode. Each step rests on a theorem about a model, and the reactor is never exactly the model.

This volume is about those algorithms and theorems. Its eight chapters hold 279 entries. The first chapter builds models and fits them to data, and the second asks when they are stable. Three chapters on linear design follow: classical loops, state feedback with observers, and optimal and predictive control. The last three cover uncertainty and adaptation, nonlinear systems, and systems with discrete events and switching.

## Why control gets a volume of its own

Control uses the signals of Volume 28, the matrix equations of Volume 12, the optimization of Volume 15 and the automata of Volume 16. What it adds is feedback: the output of the algorithm changes the system that produces its next input. A few ideas come back in every chapter:

- **A result holds for its model.** The [Routh-Hurwitz criterion](/routh-hurwitz-criterion/), the [Nyquist criterion](/nyquist-stability-criterion/) and the [Kalman rank condition](/kalman-rank-condition/) are exact for linear time-invariant models. Root locus sketches and Bode asymptotes are approximations, and gain and phase margins measure the distance to instability along one direction only.
- **Many tests are sufficient only.** [Lyapunov's direct method](/lyapunov-direct-method/) proves stability when it finds a function; failing to find one proves nothing. [Linearization](/lyapunov-indirect-method/) decides local stability only when no eigenvalue lies on the imaginary axis. The [small-gain theorem](/small-gain-theorem/), the [circle criterion](/circle-criterion/) and the [Popov criterion](/popov-criterion/) are sufficient conditions.
- **Optimal for a model is not robust.** In continuous time, a single-input [LQR](/infinite-horizon-lqr/) loop keeps a gain margin of $[1/2, \infty)$ and a phase margin of at least 60 degrees, by the [LQR margin theorem](/lqr-stability-margins/). Put a Kalman filter in the loop and the [LQG](/lqg-control/) controller has no guaranteed margins: [Doyle's 1978 example](/lqg-guaranteed-margins-counterexample/) loses stability under input gains arbitrarily close to 1 as the weights grow.
- **Exact, hard or measured.** H-infinity synthesis is exact, by [two Riccati equations](/dgkf-h-infinity-synthesis/) or by [linear matrix inequalities](/lmi-h-infinity-synthesis/). Computing the [structured singular value](/structured-singular-value/) exactly is [NP-hard](/np-hard/), so only bounds are computed. MPC solve times and the [Ziegler-Nichols rules](/ziegler-nichols-tuning/) are measurements and heuristics.

The [Kalman filter](/kalman-filter/) lives in Volume 28, where it is the minimum-variance estimator for linear Gaussian models and the best linear estimator otherwise. [Lyapunov equations](/lyapunov-equation/) and [Runge-Kutta methods](/runge-kutta-methods/) come from Volume 12. The [quadratic programs](/quadratic-programming/) of MPC and the [semidefinite programs](/semidefinite-programming/) of robust synthesis come from Volume 15. [Timed automata](/timed-automata/) and [hybrid automata](/hybrid-automata/) come from Volume 16. Robotics, Volume 37, builds on this volume, but it is not yet written on the site.

## The chapters

**Chapter 36.1, Modeling and identification**, builds the [state-space representation](/state-space-representation/), the [matrix exponential](/matrix-exponential/) and [Jacobian linearization](/jacobian-linearization/). [Zero-order hold discretization](/zero-order-hold-discretization/) is exact at the samples when the input is held between them. [Least-squares ARX identification](/least-squares-arx-identification/) is biased under colored noise, and the [prediction error method](/prediction-error-method/) is maximum likelihood for Gaussian noise but nonconvex. [Subspace identification](/subspace-identification/) needs no iterative search, and the [Ho-Kalman algorithm](/ho-kalman-algorithm/) is exact for noise-free data. Each method is consistent only under its noise assumptions and [persistent excitation](/persistent-excitation/), and [in closed loop](/closed-loop-identification/) open-loop methods can fail.

**Chapter 36.2, Stability**, decides stability of polynomials with the [Routh array](/routh-array/) and the [Jury test](/jury-stability-test/). From Lyapunov's method it goes to [LaSalle's invariance principle](/lasalle-invariance-principle/), the [converse theorems](/converse-lyapunov-theorem/), [input-to-state stability](/input-to-state-stability/) and the [KYP lemma](/kalman-yakubovich-popov-lemma/). [Sum-of-squares programs](/sum-of-squares-programming/) search for Lyapunov functions and give [inner estimates](/region-of-attraction-estimation/) of the region of attraction. The [describing function](/describing-function/) predicts limit cycles only approximately.

**Chapter 36.3, Classical control**, studies the [feedback loop](/feedback-control-loop/) with the [root locus](/root-locus/), the [Bode plot](/bode-plot/) and the Nyquist criterion, and reads [gain](/gain-margin/) and [phase margins](/phase-margin/) from them. The [Bode sensitivity integral](/bode-sensitivity-integral/) is an exact limit for loops of relative degree at least two: reducing sensitivity in one band raises it in another. The [PID controller](/pid-controller/) gets [anti-windup](/anti-windup/), the [Smith predictor](/smith-predictor/) handles delays, and the [internal model principle](/internal-model-principle/) says why integral action removes step errors.

**Chapter 36.4, State space control**, proves the [pole placement theorem](/pole-placement-theorem/): any closed-loop eigenvalues are possible exactly when the pair is [controllable](/controllability/). The [Luenberger observer](/luenberger-observer/) and the [separation principle](/separation-principle/) join estimation and feedback for linear models. [Minimal realization](/minimal-realization/) removes unneeded states, and [balanced truncation](/balanced-truncation/) reduces order with an error at most twice the sum of the deleted Hankel singular values.

**Chapter 36.5, Optimal and predictive control**, gives the [Pontryagin maximum principle](/pontryagin-maximum-principle/), which is necessary only, and the [Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation](/hamilton-jacobi-bellman-equation/), sufficient when the value function is smooth; otherwise the value function is its viscosity solution. The [Riccati equation](/continuous-algebraic-riccati-equation/) is solved by the [Schur method](/schur-method-riccati/) or by [Kleinman iteration](/kleinman-iteration/). [Linear MPC](/linear-mpc/) solves a quadratic program at each sample; it is stable and [recursively feasible](/recursive-feasibility/) only with a terminal cost and terminal set, by the [MPC stability theorem](/mpc-stability-theorem/). [Explicit MPC](/explicit-mpc/) can need exponentially many regions, [tube MPC](/tube-mpc/) handles bounded disturbances, and [nonlinear MPC](/nonlinear-mpc/) is nonconvex.

**Chapter 36.6, Robust and adaptive control**, computes the [H-infinity norm](/h-infinity-norm-computation/) with an exact Hamiltonian test inside a bisection. [Kharitonov's theorem](/kharitonov-theorem/) checks four polynomials when coefficients vary independently, and [D-K iteration](/dk-iteration/) lowers an upper bound on [mu](/mu-analysis/) with no guarantee of a global optimum. The [MIT rule](/mit-rule/) has no stability guarantee. [Model reference adaptive control](/model-reference-adaptive-control/) has a Lyapunov proof for the ideal model, its parameters converge only under persistent excitation, and the [Rohrs example](/rohrs-counterexample/) shows failure under unmodelled dynamics.

**Chapter 36.7, Nonlinear control**, covers [feedback linearization](/feedback-linearization/), which needs [relative degree and involutivity conditions](/feedback-linearizability-conditions/) and gives no robustness guarantee, then [backstepping](/backstepping/) and [control Lyapunov functions](/control-lyapunov-function/) with [Sontag's formula](/sontag-universal-formula/). [Brockett's condition](/brockett-condition/) shows that some controllable systems, such as the nonholonomic integrator, have no continuous time-invariant stabilizing feedback. [Sliding mode control](/sliding-mode-control/) rejects matched uncertainty but [chatters](/chattering/). [Control barrier functions](/control-barrier-function/) keep a state in a safe set.

**Chapter 36.8, Discrete-event and hybrid systems**, starts from [supervisory control](/supervisory-control-theory/): the [supremal controllable sublanguage](/supremal-controllable-sublanguage/) is computable for regular languages, and the [twin plant test](/twin-plant-diagnosability-test/) decides diagnosability in polynomial time. The [max-plus eigenvalue theorem](/max-plus-eigenvalue-theorem/) gives the cycle time of a [timed event graph](/timed-event-graph/). For [switched systems](/switched-system/) the [dwell time theorem](/dwell-time-theorem/) gives sufficient conditions, while deciding whether the [joint spectral radius](/joint-spectral-radius/) is at most 1 is undecidable. Reachability is PSPACE-complete for timed automata and undecidable for general hybrid automata, so [reachable-set tools](/reachable-set-computation/) such as [zonotopes](/zonotope-reachability/) over-approximate.

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: state-space representation, Jacobian linearization, the Routh-Hurwitz criterion, Lyapunov's direct method, the feedback control loop, the Nyquist criterion, the PID controller, the Kalman rank condition, the pole placement theorem, the Luenberger observer, the separation principle, infinite-horizon LQR, the LQR margins, LQG control, linear MPC, the MPC stability theorem, the [H-infinity control problem](/h-infinity-control-problem/), model reference adaptive control, feedback linearization and sliding mode control.

If you already know the basics, start with a chapter's opening page. Read Chapters 36.1 and 36.2 first, since every later chapter uses their models and stability tests. Chapter 36.5 builds on Chapter 36.4, and Chapter 36.6 on both. Chapter 36.8 stands mostly on its own.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely; you should be able to do them in a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use an idea in a new place; each takes some thought, and some need a short program. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some are results from research papers.
