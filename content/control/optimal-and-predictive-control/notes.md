## History

Optimal control grew out of the calculus of variations. Johann Bernoulli posed the brachistochrone problem in 1696. Euler derived the general equation in his *Methodus inveniendi* of 1744, and the young Lagrange gave the direct method of variations in a letter to Euler of 12 August 1755. Engineering problems of the early 1950s brought in bounded inputs, which the classical theory did not handle: Bushaw (1952) and Feldbaum (1953) studied switching rules for relay systems, and LaSalle (1960) proved the bang-bang principle for linear systems.

Two general answers came in the second half of the 1950s. Pontryagin, Boltyanskii and Gamkrelidze announced the maximum principle in 1956, and the book by Pontryagin and his coworkers on the mathematical theory of optimal processes appeared in Russian in 1961 and in English in 1962. Bellman's book on dynamic programming came out in 1957; its continuous-time form is the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation. That equation rarely has smooth solutions, and the right weak notion, the viscosity solution, came only with Crandall and Lions (1983).

Kalman solved the linear quadratic regulator in 1960, with the Riccati equation and the limit of an infinite horizon, and with Bucy he gave the continuous-time filter in 1961. In 1964 he showed that the single-input LQR loop has a return difference of modulus at least one, from which the guaranteed margins follow; Safonov and Athans (1977) extended them to every input channel when the input weight is diagonal. Doyle (1978) then gave a plant on which LQG loses stability under input gains arbitrarily close to 1, and Doyle and Stein (1979, 1981) gave loop transfer recovery in answer. The numerical side followed: Kleinman (1968) gave the Newton iteration for the Riccati equation, Hewer (1971) its discrete form, and Laub (1979) the Schur method.

Model predictive control began in industry. Richalet, Rault, Testud and Papon (1978) described model predictive heuristic control, Cutler and Ramaker (1980) presented dynamic matrix control from Shell, and García and Morshedi (1986) added constraints through a quadratic program. Clarke, Mohtadi and Tuffs (1987) gave generalized predictive control on transfer function models. The theory came after the practice. Keerthi and Gilbert (1988) proved stability with a terminal equality constraint, Gilbert and Tan (1991) computed maximal output admissible sets, and the survey of Mayne, Rawlings, Rao and Scokaert (2000) set out the terminal cost and terminal set conditions used today. Bemporad, Morari, Dua and Pistikopoulos (2002) showed that the constrained law is piecewise affine, which led to explicit MPC, and Mayne, Seron and Raković (2005) gave tube MPC for bounded disturbances. Diehl, Bock and Schlöder developed the real-time iteration for fast nonlinear MPC and published its contraction analysis in 2005.

## Further reading

- Brian D. O. Anderson and John B. Moore, *Optimal Control: Linear Quadratic Methods* (Prentice Hall, 1990; Dover reprint, 2007). The regulator, the Riccati equations and the robustness of LQ designs, worked out with care.
- Daniel Liberzon, *Calculus of Variations and Optimal Control Theory: A Concise Introduction* (Princeton University Press, 2012). A short and rigorous path from the Euler-Lagrange equation to the maximum principle, the HJB equation and LQR.
- Dimitri P. Bertsekas, *Dynamic Programming and Optimal Control*, Volume I, fourth edition (Athena Scientific, 2017). Dynamic programming for finite and infinite horizons, with the linear quadratic case as a running example.
- James B. Rawlings, David Q. Mayne and Moritz M. Diehl, *Model Predictive Control: Theory, Computation, and Design*, second edition (Nob Hill Publishing, 2017). The standard text on the theory of MPC, from the stability theorem to robust, economic and nonlinear MPC and their numerics.
- Francesco Borrelli, Alberto Bemporad and Manfred Morari, *Predictive Control for Linear and Hybrid Systems* (Cambridge University Press, 2017). Invariant sets, multiparametric programming and explicit MPC.
- S. Joe Qin and Thomas A. Badgwell, "A survey of industrial model predictive control technology", *Control Engineering Practice* 11(7), 2003. How MPC is used in industry and which products implement it.
