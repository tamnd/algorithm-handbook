## History

Supervisory control began with Peter Ramadge and Murray Wonham. Their 1987 paper in the SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization, "Supervisory control of a class of discrete event processes", split the events of an automaton into controllable and uncontrollable ones and proved that a specification can be enforced exactly when it is controllable. A second paper of the same year, by Wonham and Ramadge, showed that the supremal controllable sublanguage exists and is computable for regular languages, and their 1989 survey in the Proceedings of the IEEE made the theory widely known. Partial observation followed at once: Lin and Wonham (1988) and Cieslak, Desclaux, Fawaz and Varaiya (1988) introduced observability, and Brandt and coauthors (1990) gave closed formulas for the supremal controllable and normal sublanguages. For Petri nets, Giua, DiCesare and Silva (1992) introduced monitor places for mutual exclusion constraints, and Yamalidou, Moody, Lemmon and Antsaklis (1996) built them from place invariants.

Fault diagnosis came next. Sampath, Sengupta, Lafortune, Sinnamohideen and Teneketzis defined diagnosability in 1995 and built the diagnoser, which tests the property and detects faults online but can be exponential in the size of the plant. Jiang, Huang, Chandra and Kumar (2001) and Yoo and Lafortune (2002) replaced it, for the test, by polynomial checks on a product of the plant with itself.

Max-plus algebra has an older and separate root. Cuninghame-Green worked on the max-plus eigenproblem in the 1960s and wrote it up in *Minimax Algebra* (1979), and Karp's algorithm of 1978 computes the maximum cycle mean. Cohen, Dubois, Quadrat and Viot (1985) used the algebra for timed event graphs and proved the cyclicity of matrix powers. The book of Baccelli, Cohen, Olsder and Quadrat (1992) set out the linear system theory of these graphs.

On the continuous side, the stability of switched systems became a topic of its own in the 1990s. Narendra and Balakrishnan (1994) built a common quadratic Lyapunov function for commuting matrices, Morse (1996) used dwell time to switch among set-point controllers, Hespanha and Morse (1999) gave average dwell time, and Dayawansa and Martin (1999) proved the converse Lyapunov theorem for arbitrary switching. The joint spectral radius is older: Rota and Strang defined it in 1960. Blondel and Tsitsiklis (2000) showed that deciding whether it is at most 1 is undecidable, and Bousch and Mairesse (2002) disproved the finiteness conjecture. Sontag (1981) introduced piecewise linear models, Bemporad and Morari (1999) the mixed logical dynamical form and predictive control on it, and Heemels, De Schutter and Bemporad (2001) proved the two equivalent.

Henzinger, Kopke, Puri and Varaiya (1998) mapped which reachability problems for hybrid automata are decidable. Set-based tools followed: zonotopes after Kühn (1998) and Girard (2005), Hamilton-Jacobi reachability after Mitchell, Bayen and Tomlin (2005), and barrier certificates after Prajna and Jadbabaie (2004). Symbolic control, from Girard and Pappas (2007) and Pola, Girard and Tabuada (2008), brought the discrete methods back to continuous plants, and event-triggered control, from Åström and Bernhardsson (2002) and Tabuada (2007), made sampling itself an event.

## Further reading

- Christos G. Cassandras and Stéphane Lafortune, *Introduction to Discrete Event Systems*, third edition, Springer, 2021. The standard textbook on automata and Petri net models, supervisory control and diagnosis.
- W. Murray Wonham and Kai Cai, *Supervisory Control of Discrete-Event Systems*, Springer, 2019. The theory from its founder, with full proofs.
- François Baccelli, Guy Cohen, Geert Jan Olsder and Jean-Pierre Quadrat, *Synchronization and Linearity: An Algebra for Discrete Event Systems*, Wiley, 1992. The reference on max-plus algebra and timed event graphs; Heidergott, Olsder and van der Woude's *Max Plus at Work* (2006) is a gentler start.
- Daniel Liberzon, *Switching in Systems and Control*, Birkhäuser, 2003. Stability under arbitrary and constrained switching, common Lyapunov functions and dwell time, in a short book.
- Francesco Borrelli, Alberto Bemporad and Manfred Morari, *Predictive Control for Linear and Hybrid Systems*, Cambridge University Press, 2017. Piecewise affine and mixed logical dynamical models and the mixed-integer programs of hybrid MPC.
- Matthias Althoff, Goran Frehse and Antoine Girard, "Set propagation techniques for reachability analysis", *Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems* 4, 2021. A survey of zonotopes, support functions and the other set representations used by reachability tools.
