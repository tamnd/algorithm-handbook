## History

Feedback control is older than its theory. James Clerk Maxwell analysed the stability of governors in 1868 by the roots of a characteristic equation. Nicolas Minorsky studied the automatic steering of ships in 1922 and gave the three-term law that became the PID controller. Harold Black conceived the negative feedback amplifier in 1927 and published it in 1934; trading gain for insensitivity, his amplifier is the first worked case of the sensitivity function.

The frequency-domain theory came from Bell Telephone Laboratories. Harry Nyquist published his stability criterion in 1932, for amplifiers whose loop gain was known only as a measured curve. Hendrik Bode related the gain and phase of minimum-phase functions in 1940, and his 1945 book Network Analysis and Feedback Amplifier Design added the sensitivity integral and much of the theory of feedback design.

The war turned these ideas toward servomechanisms. In 1942 John Ziegler and Nathaniel Nichols published their tuning rules for industrial controllers. The Radiation Laboratory volume Theory of Servomechanisms, edited by Hubert James, Nathaniel Nichols and Ralph Phillips in 1947, introduced the Nichols chart, and Arnold Tustin published the bilinear map for sampled systems the same year. Walter Evans gave the root locus in 1948 and 1950.

Process control and robust control shaped the later entries. Otto Smith proposed his predictor for plants with delay in 1957, Isaac Horowitz developed two-degree-of-freedom design in 1963, and Edgar Bristol introduced the relative gain array in 1966. Bruce Francis and Murray Wonham stated the internal model principle in 1975 and 1976, and Carlos Garcia and Manfred Morari framed internal model control in 1982. Karl Johan Åström and Tore Hägglund proposed relay autotuning in 1984, and James Freudenberg and Douglas Looze extended Bode's integral to unstable plants in 1985.

## Further reading

- Karl Johan Åström and Richard M. Murray, *Feedback Systems: An Introduction for Scientists and Engineers*, second edition, Princeton University Press, 2021. A modern first course that covers the loop, the Nyquist criterion, margins, sensitivity and PID control with many worked examples.
- Gene F. Franklin, J. David Powell and Abbas Emami-Naeini, *Feedback Control of Dynamic Systems*, eighth edition, Pearson, 2019. The standard engineering text for root locus, Bode design with lead and lag networks, and digital implementation.
- Karl Johan Åström and Tore Hägglund, *Advanced PID Control*, ISA, 2006. Everything about PID controllers in practice: forms, tuning rules, relay autotuning, windup and set-point weighting.
- John C. Doyle, Bruce A. Francis and Allen R. Tannenbaum, *Feedback Control Theory*, Macmillan, 1992. A short and rigorous book on internal stability, loop shaping and the limits that Bode's integrals set.
- Sigurd Skogestad and Ian Postlethwaite, *Multivariable Feedback Control: Analysis and Design*, second edition, Wiley, 2005. Performance limits, the relative gain array and the step from one loop to several.
- Karl Johan Åström and Björn Wittenmark, *Computer-Controlled Systems*, third edition, Prentice Hall, 1997. Sampling, hold equivalents and the emulation of continuous controllers.
