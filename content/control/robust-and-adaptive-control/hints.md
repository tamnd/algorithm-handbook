1. With $t = \omega^2$, the denominator $(1 - t)^2 + t$ is smallest at $t = 1/2$, where it equals $3/4$. So $\|G\|_\infty = 2/\sqrt3 \approx 1.1547$ at $\omega = 1/\sqrt2 \approx 0.7071$. For $1/(s^2 + a_1 s + a_0)$ the squared H2 norm is $1/(2 a_1 a_0)$, here $1/2$, so $\|G\|_2 = 1/\sqrt2 \approx 0.7071$. The H-infinity norm is larger.

3. The eigenvalues of $M$ are $\pm 1$, so $\rho(M) = 1$, and $\bar\sigma(M) = 2$. Since $\det(I - M\Delta) = 1 - \delta_1 \delta_2$, the smallest destabilizing $\Delta$ has $|\delta_1| = |\delta_2| = 1$, so $\mu(M) = 1$. With $d = 1/2$, $D M D^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, whose largest singular value is 1.

5. The four polynomials have $(q_2, q_1, q_0)$ equal to $(4, 2, 1)$, $(3, 5, 7)$, $(4, 5, 1)$ and $(3, 2, 7)$. The products $q_2 q_1$ are 8, 15, 20 and 6, against $q_0$ = 1, 7, 1 and 7. The last test fails, so $s^3 + 3s^2 + 2s + 7$, a member of the family, is not Hurwitz, and the family is not robustly stable.

7. If the law works, $y(t) = e(t)$ in closed loop, so the term $-0.3\, e(t)$ can be replaced by $-0.3\, y(t)$, which is allowed because the noise polynomial $1 - 0.3 z^{-1}$ has its zero inside the unit circle. Setting the prediction $1.2\, y + 2u - 0.3\, y$ to zero gives $u = -0.45\, y$, and the output variance is $\sigma^2$. The open loop has its pole at 1.2 and is unstable. A seeded simulation with $\sigma^2 = 1$ (seeds 0 to 4, 100000 steps each) measured sample variances from 0.9916 to 1.0007.

9. Both have positive coefficients, and $a_2 a_1 - a_0 = 0.1$ for each. On the segment $a_2 = a_1 = 1 + 2\lambda$ and $a_0 = 0.9 + 8\lambda$, so $a_2 a_1 - a_0 = 4\lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 0.1$. This is negative for $\lambda \in ((1 - \sqrt{0.9})/2, (1 + \sqrt{0.9})/2) \approx (0.0257, 0.9743)$; the midpoint $s^3 + 2s^2 + 2s + 4.9$ fails. Corners are not enough. The family here is a single edge, and the edge theorem tests the edge itself. Kharitonov's theorem does not apply, because $a_2$ and $a_1$ are tied together and the family is not a box.

11. The characteristic polynomial is $(s + 1)(s + 10)^2 - 200 k_y = s^3 + 21 s^2 + 120 s + 100 - 200 k_y$. The Hurwitz conditions give $k_y < 0.5$ and $2520 > 100 - 200 k_y$, so $-12.1 < k_y < 0.5$. At $\omega = \sqrt{120} \approx 10.95$ the plant is real and equal to $-1/12.1$. In one RK4 run per frequency with step 0.001 (step 0.0005 at $\omega = 10.95$ gave the same time), $k_y$ passed $-12.1$ at about 7.4 s for $\omega = 9$, 11.5 s for $\omega = 10.95$, 19.2 s for $\omega = 13$ and 39.2 s for $\omega = 16$, and had not passed it by 60 s for $\omega = 3$ and $\omega = 6$. These times are measurements of the simulation.

12. For $P = 1/(s - 1)$, the control equation $A^T X + X A - X B B^T X + C^T C = 0$ becomes $2X - X^2 + 1 = 0$, so $X = 1 + \sqrt2$, and the filter equation gives the same $Z$. Then $\rho(XZ) = 3 + 2\sqrt2$, $\gamma_{\min} = \sqrt{4 + 2\sqrt2} \approx 2.6131$ and $\varepsilon_{\max} \approx 0.3827$, which is $\sin(\pi/8)$. For $k/s$ take $A = 0$, $B = 1$, $C = k$: then $X = k$, $Z = 1/k$, $XZ = 1$ and $\gamma_{\min} = \sqrt2$.

13. In lifted form $e_{j+1} = (I - \gamma G) e_j$, where $G$ is lower triangular Toeplitz with diagonal $CB = 2$. The eigenvalues are all $1 - 2\gamma$, so the error converges for every $N$ exactly when $0 < \gamma < 1$. The values $e^{j\theta} P(e^{j\theta}) = 2/(1 - 0.8 e^{-j\theta})$ fill a circle through $10/9$ and $10$, and the frequency condition holds for $0 < \gamma < 0.2$; at $\gamma = 0.1$ its maximum is $0.8889$. At $\gamma = 0.5$ the matrix $I - \gamma G$ is nilpotent, so the error is zero after at most $N$ trials, but its 2-norm for $N = 30$ is about 3.69 (computed by power iteration), so the error can grow first.

20. With $P = I$, $A_1^T + A_1 = A_2^T + A_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & a \\ a & -2 \end{pmatrix}$, negative definite for $|a| < 2$, and so is every convex combination. The member $\lambda A_1 + (1 - \lambda) A_2$ has eigenvalues $-1 \pm |a| \sqrt{\lambda(1 - \lambda)}$, so for $|a| \ge 2$ the midpoint is not Hurwitz and no common $P$ exists. Here both sets are $|a| < 2$. Frozen eigenvalues say nothing about fast switching; a common $P$ makes $x^T P x$ decrease for any switching.

21. $\det(I - u v^T \Delta) = 1 - \sum_i \delta_i u_i v_i$. Choosing every $\delta_i$ of modulus $1/\sum_i |u_i v_i|$ with the phase that cancels that of $u_i v_i$ gives the smallest destabilizing $\Delta$. Here $\mu = 3 + 2 + 2 = 7$, $\rho(M) = |v^T u| = 1$ and $\bar\sigma(M) = \|u\|\,\|v\| = \sqrt{84} \approx 9.165$. Since $\bar\sigma(D M D^{-1}) = \|Du\|\,\|D^{-1} v\|$, Cauchy-Schwarz gives at least $\sum_i |u_i v_i|$, with equality for $d_i^2 = |v_i|/|u_i|$, here $D = \mathrm{diag}(\sqrt3, \sqrt{1/2}, \sqrt2)$. A random search over the phases (seed 1, 200000 draws) reached 6.99998.

22. With $c = 1 - \gamma^{-2}$ the control equation is $-c X^2 + 2 a X + 1 = 0$, so $X = (a + \sqrt{a^2 + c})/c$, and the filter equation gives the same $Y$. The coupling condition $\rho(XY) = X^2 < \gamma^2$ reduces to $\gamma^2 - 2a\gamma - 2 > 0$, so $\gamma_{\mathrm{opt}} = a + \sqrt{a^2 + 2}$: $\sqrt2$ for $a = 0$, $1 + \sqrt3$ for $a = 1$ and $2 + \sqrt6 \approx 4.449$ for $a = 2$. For $\gamma \le 1$, $c \le 0$ and every term of the control equation is positive for $X \ge 0$, so there is no solution $X \ge 0$.

23. $|P_1 - P_2|^2 = 1/(1 + t)$ with $t = \omega^2$, and $1 + |P_1|^2 = (2 + t)/(1 + t)$, $1 + |P_2|^2 = (5 + t)/(1 + t)$. So $\kappa^2 = (1 + t)/((2 + t)(5 + t))$; with $v = 1 + t$ this is $v/(v^2 + 5v + 4)$, largest at $v = 2$, that is $\omega = 1$, where $\kappa = 1/3$. The function $1 + P_2(-s) P_1(s) = (3 - s^2)/(1 - s^2)$ has no zeros or poles on the imaginary axis and winding number 0, so $\delta_\nu = 1/3$. Every controller that stabilizes $P_1$ with $b_{P_1, C} > 1/3$ also stabilizes $P_2$.

24. Internal stability forbids cancelling the zero at 2, so $S(2) = 1$ for every stabilizing controller, and the maximum modulus principle gives $\|WS\|_\infty \ge |W(2)| = 5/3$. For a stable plant $S = 1 - PQ$. Ask for $WS \equiv 5/3$: then $S = 5(s + 1)/(3(s + 3))$, $Q = 2(s + 1)/(3(s + 3))$ and $C = Q/(1 - PQ) = 2/5$, with the closed-loop pole at $-3$. The optimal weighted sensitivity is flat over frequency, as H-infinity optima often are.
