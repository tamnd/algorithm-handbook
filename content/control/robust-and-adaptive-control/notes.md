## History

Adaptive control came first. Aircraft autopilots of the 1950s had to work across a wide range of speed and altitude, and Whitaker, Yamron and Kezer (1958) proposed model reference control with the [MIT rule](/mit-rule/), a gradient adjustment of the gains. The rule had no stability proof, and Parks (1966) replaced it by a law derived from a Lyapunov function. Kalman (1958) described a self-optimizing controller that estimates a model and designs from it, and Åström and Wittenmark (1973) gave the [self-tuning regulator](/self-tuning-regulator/) with least squares and minimum variance control; Ljung (1977) analysed its convergence. Around 1980 Morse, Narendra, Lin and Valavani proved global stability of output feedback [model reference adaptive control](/model-reference-adaptive-control/) for linear plants of any relative degree.

The proofs held for the ideal model only. Rohrs and co-authors showed in 1985 that a small unmodelled lag and a sinusoidal command can drive the gains of a standard law out of the stable region ([Rohrs counterexample](/rohrs-counterexample/)). Ioannou and Kokotović, Egardt, Narendra and Annaswamy and others answered with robust adaptive laws, among them leakage ([sigma modification](/sigma-modification/)) and [parameter projection](/parameter-projection/); Ioannou and Sun (1996) gave a unified account. Later variants such as [L1 adaptive control](/l1-adaptive-control/) (Cao and Hovakimyan, from 2006) claimed uniform transient bounds, and their status is still argued in the literature.

Robust control grew in the same years from the failure of [LQG control](/lqg-control/) to guarantee margins, shown by Doyle (1978). Zames (1981) posed the H-infinity sensitivity problem using the [Youla parametrization](/youla-parametrization/) of Youla, Jabr and Bongiorno (1976) and Kučera (1975). Doyle (1982) defined the [structured singular value](/structured-singular-value/), and Safonov (1982) gave the same quantity as a stability margin. The early H-infinity solutions used interpolation and gave controllers of high order, until Glover and Doyle (1988) and Doyle, Glover, Khargonekar and Francis (1989) reduced the problem to two Riccati equations of the plant's size. Glover and McFarlane (1989) added [H-infinity loop shaping](/h-infinity-loop-shaping/), and Vinnicombe (1993) the [nu-gap metric](/nu-gap-metric/). Braatz, Young, Doyle and Morari (1994) proved that exact $\mu$ is NP-hard.

A parametric line ran beside it. Kharitonov published his four-polynomial theorem in 1978, and it became widely known outside the Soviet Union in the 1980s; Bartlett, Hollot and Lin (1988) extended it to polytopes by the [edge theorem](/edge-theorem/). Matrix inequalities joined the two lines. Yakubovich (1971) proved the lossless S-lemma, Willems (1971) linked Riccati equations and matrix inequalities, and the interior-point methods of the early 1990s made LMIs solvable in practice. Gahinet and Apkarian (1994) and Iwasaki and Skelton (1994) then wrote H-infinity synthesis as LMIs without rank assumptions, and Boyd, El Ghaoui, Feron and Balakrishnan (1994) collected the methods in one book. [Extremum seeking](/extremum-seeking-control/) is older than all of these: Leblanc (1922) is often cited as its first use, but a general proof of local convergence for a plant with dynamics came only with Krstić and Wang (2000). [Iterative learning control](/iterative-learning-control/) took its standard form, with a convergence proof, from Arimoto, Kawamura and Miyazaki (1984).

## Further reading

- Kemin Zhou, John C. Doyle and Keith Glover, *Robust and Optimal Control*, Prentice Hall, 1996. The standard reference for norms, coprime factors, the Youla parametrization, $\mu$ and the Riccati solution of H-infinity control.
- Sigurd Skogestad and Ian Postlethwaite, *Multivariable Feedback Control: Analysis and Design*, 2nd ed., Wiley, 2005. Mixed sensitivity, loop shaping and $\mu$ with worked engineering examples.
- Stephen Boyd, Laurent El Ghaoui, Eric Feron and Venkataramanan Balakrishnan, *Linear Matrix Inequalities in System and Control Theory*, SIAM, 1994. LMIs, the S-procedure and quadratic stability.
- Shankar P. Bhattacharyya, Hervé Chapellat and Lee H. Keel, *Robust Control: The Parametric Approach*, Prentice Hall, 1995. Kharitonov's theorem, the edge theorem and their extensions.
- Karl Johan Åström and Björn Wittenmark, *Adaptive Control*, 2nd ed., Addison-Wesley, 1995. Self-tuning and model reference control as used in practice.
- Petros A. Ioannou and Jing Sun, *Robust Adaptive Control*, Prentice Hall, 1996. The stability theory of adaptive laws with projection, leakage and unmodelled dynamics.
