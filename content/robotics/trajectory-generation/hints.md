1. Each joint gives $\dot s \le V_i/|\Delta_i|$ and $|\ddot s| \le A_i/|\Delta_i|$, here $(0.6, 0.8, 0.5)$ and $(2, 3, 3)$. So $\dot s \le 0.5$, set by joint 3, and $|\ddot s| \le 2$, set by joint 1. In $s$ the move has length $1 \ge 0.5^2/2$, so the trapezoid has a cruise and $T = 1/0.5 + 0.5/2 = 2.25$ s. Alone, the joints need $1.967$, $1.517$ and $2.167$ s. The shared time scaling is slower than the slowest joint, because two different joints set its two bounds.

3. With $\dot q(0) = 3$ the quintic is $q = 3t - 8t^3 + 9t^4 - 3t^5$. Its derivative is $-3(t - 1)^2(5t^2 - 2t - 1)$, which is zero at $t = (1 + \sqrt 6)/5 \approx 0.690$. There $q \approx 1.0128$, so it overshoots the target and comes back. With $\dot q(0) = 2$ it is $q = 2t - 2t^3 + t^4$, with $\dot q = 2(1 - t)^2(1 + 2t) \ge 0$, so it rises monotonically to $1$.

4. For $D = 0.4 < V^2/A = 0.5$ the profile is a triangle: $T = 2\sqrt{0.4/2} \approx 0.894$ s, with peak speed $\sqrt{AD} = \sqrt{0.8} \approx 0.894$, below $V$. For $D = 3$ it is a trapezoid: $0.5$ s up, $2.5$ s of cruise at speed $1$ and $0.5$ s down, $T = 3.5$ s.

6. Here $D = 1$. The peak speed is $1.875 D/T = 0.9375$ m/s at $t = 1$. The peak acceleration is $(10/\sqrt 3)D/T^2 \approx 1.443$ m/s². The jerk at the start is $60D/T^3 = 7.5$ m/s³. The cost is $720D^2/T^5 = 22.5$.

7. The points are $(0, 2)$ and $(4, 2)$, then $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 4)$, then $(0, 3.5)$ and $(4, 3.5)$. The first segment crosses the rectangle at height $2$. The second passes $(1, 2.5)$, inside the rectangle. Both are rejected, so the length stays $12$. The third passes above the rectangle and is accepted: the path becomes $(0,0) \to (0,3.5) \to (4,3.5) \to (4,0)$, of length $11$. The obstacle stands on the edge of the half-plane, so every free path goes over it. The shortest one goes over the two top corners, $(0,0) \to (1,3) \to (3,3) \to (4,0)$, of length $2\sqrt{10} + 2 \approx 8.325$.

8. Passing in front of the obstacle needs $s = 7$ by $t = 1$, a speed of $7$ m/s, which is too fast. So $s(3) \le 5$, and the last $7$ m take at least $7/3$ s: $T \ge 16/3 \approx 5.33$ s. Driving at $3$ m/s to $s = 5$, waiting there until $t = 3$ and driving on at $3$ m/s reaches it. In the second case the robot at full speed is in $[5, 7]$ only during $t \in [5/3, 7/3]$, before the obstacle arrives, so $T = 4$ s, the time with no obstacle.

10. The least value of $\int_0^T \ddot p^{\,2}$ for a rest-to-rest move of length $D$ is $12D^2/T^3$, so the prior cost is $6D^2/T^3$: $24$ for $T = 1$, the value in the entry, and $3$ for $T = 2$. The curve is the rest-to-rest cubic $p = A + (B - A)(3\tau^2 - 2\tau^3)$ with $\tau = t/T$.

11. Write the end acceleration of the first cubic and the start acceleration of the second in terms of $v$. They are $4v - 12$ and $-(3 + 4v)/2$. Setting them equal gives $v = 7/4$, and the acceleration at the via point is $-5$. With $v = 0$ the acceleration jumps from $-12$ to $-1.5$. With $v = 7/4$ the second segment overshoots to about $2.345$ at $t \approx 1.42$.

13. With neither limit reached, the profile is jerk $+J$, $-J$, $-J$, $+J$ for $T_j$ each, and it covers $2JT_j^3$. So $T_j = (D/(2J))^{1/3} \approx 0.1842$ s. The peak acceleration is $JT_j \approx 1.474 < 2$, the peak speed $JT_j^2 \approx 0.271 < 1$, and $T = 4T_j \approx 0.737$ s. The triangle without a jerk limit takes $2\sqrt{D/A} \approx 0.447$ s.

15. Reflect the problem through the point $(1, 1)$, so that $q(t) \mapsto 2 - q(2 - t)$. The problem maps to itself, and the strictly convex optimum is unique, so it is symmetric. The rest-to-rest degree-7 shape over $[0, 2]$ with $D = 2$ is symmetric in the same way and passes through $1$ at $t = 1$. Its pieces satisfy the optimality conditions, so it is the optimum. The via velocity is $(D/T)\,p'(1/2) = 35/16$, and the cost is $100800 D^2/T^7 = 3150$.

17. Without the velocity limit, accelerating gives $x = 2s$ and braking gives $x = 8(1 - s)$. They cross at $s = 0.8$, $x = 1.6$. The time is $\sqrt{1.6}\,(1 + 1/4) \approx 1.581$. With $\dot s \le 1$ the maximum velocity curve is flat at $x = 1$. The profile accelerates to $s = 0.5$, follows the curve with $\ddot s = 0$ to $s = 7/8$, and then brakes: $T = 1 + 0.375 + 0.25 = 1.625$.

20. The weights are $e^{-S_k/\lambda}/\sum_j e^{-S_j/\lambda}$. For $\lambda = 1$ they are $0.662, 0.244, 0.090, 0.004$. For $\lambda = 0.5$ they are $0.867, 0.117, 0.016, 0.00004$. For $\lambda = 10$ they are $0.300, 0.272, 0.246, 0.182$. The update for $\lambda = 1$ is $\sum_k P_k \varepsilon_k \approx 0.0233$. Adding $5$ multiplies every exponential by $e^{-5/\lambda}$, which cancels in the ratio.

22. Write $\ddot q = 2\pi R\,\ddot s\, t + 4\pi^2 R\,\dot s^2\, n$ with the unit tangent $t$ and the inward normal $n$. A point $x\,n$ plus any multiple of $t$ meets the square $[-A, A]^2$ exactly when the distance $4\pi^2 R x$ of that line from the origin is at most the support value $A(|\cos\varphi| + |\sin\varphi|)$ of the square in the direction $n$. So $\mathrm{MVC}(\varphi) = A(|\cos\varphi| + |\sin\varphi|)/(4\pi^2 R) = (|\cos\varphi| + |\sin\varphi|)/\pi^2$. That is $0.1013$ at $\varphi = 0$ and $0.1433$ at $\pi/4$.

23. The robot cannot pass first: by $t = 2$ it has covered at most $2$ m. So $s(4) \le 4$ and $\dot s(4) \le 2$. Then the last $6$ m from a speed of at most $2$ to rest need at least $4$ s: $2$ s and $2$ m of braking, and $4$ m at the speed limit. Wait until $t = 1$, accelerate to $2$ m/s by $t = 3$, and cruise to reach $s = 4$ at $t = 4$. Go on at $2$ m/s and brake from $t = 6$ to stop at $s = 10$ at $t = 8$.

25. The function $\sqrt{b_k} + \sqrt{b_{k+1}}$ is concave and positive. The map $y \mapsto 1/y$ is convex and decreasing for $y > 0$, and a convex decreasing function of a concave function is convex. In $v$, the time $\sum 2\Delta_k/(v_k + v_{k+1})$ is still convex. But the acceleration $(v_{k+1}^2 - v_k^2)/(2\Delta_k)$ is quadratic in $v$, so a two-sided limit on it gives a feasible set that is not convex.
