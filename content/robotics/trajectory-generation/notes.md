## History

Polynomials in time were the first trajectories. The robot controllers of the 1970s and 1980s moved each joint by a cubic or a quintic between points, or by a trapezoidal velocity profile, the bang-bang solution of the minimum-time problem for a double integrator with a cruise phase added; textbooks call it linear segments with parabolic blends. Lin, Chang and Luh (1983) fitted cubic splines through knots and chose the time intervals to minimize travel time under limits on velocity, acceleration and jerk. Hogan (1984) proposed that single-joint arm movements minimize the integrated squared jerk, and Flash and Hogan (1985) showed that for a rest-to-rest reach the optimum is the quintic $10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5$ and compared it with measured human movements.

Time-optimal motion along a given path was solved twice in 1985, by Bobrow, Dubowsky and Gibson and by Shin and McKay: the optimal profile is bang-bang in the path acceleration, with switch points on the maximum velocity curve. Kant and Zucker (1986) introduced the split of planning into a path and a velocity along it. Pfeiffer and Johanni (1987) and later Pham (2014) showed that the integration method needs special care at singular points, where implementations often failed. Verscheure, Demeulenaere, Swevers, De Schutter and Diehl (2009) showed that in the variable $\dot s^2$ the discretized problem is convex, and Pham and Pham (2018) turned it into a sequence of small linear programs by reachability analysis, which is both fast and reliable.

Optimization of whole trajectories has its roots in optimal control. Mayne introduced differential dynamic programming in 1966, and Jacobson and Mayne developed its theory in their book of 1970; Tassa, Mansard and Todorov later added box limits on the controls. Quinlan and Khatib (1993) introduced elastic bands to deform a planned path in real time. A wave of methods followed for arms with many joints: CHOMP by Ratliff, Zucker, Bagnell and Srinivasa (2009), STOMP by Kalakrishnan, Chitta, Theodorou, Pastor and Schaal (2011), TrajOpt by Schulman and colleagues (2013), and Gaussian process motion planning by Mukadam, Yan and Boots, with GPMP2 in 2018. Posa, Cantu and Tedrake (2014) made contact forces decision variables, so that an optimizer can choose when contacts open and close.

Other lines came from flying robots, learning and motion controllers. Mellinger and Kumar (2011) planned quadrotor flights as splines of minimum snap, using the differential flatness of the vehicle. Ijspeert, Nakanishi and Schaal (2002) encoded demonstrated movements as dynamical systems with learned forcing terms, and the 2013 paper with Hoffmann and Pastor is the standard reference for dynamical movement primitives. Kröger and Wahl (2010) computed synchronized time-optimal motions of several axes from any current state within one control cycle, and Berscheid and Kröger (2021) extended this with the Ruckig library. For smoothing the paths of sampling planners, Geraerts and Overmars (2007) compared shortcut heuristics, and Hauser and Ng-Thow-Hing (2010) made the shortcuts dynamically feasible.

## Further reading

- Kevin M. Lynch and Frank C. Park, Modern Robotics: Mechanics, Planning, and Control, Cambridge University Press, 2017. Chapter 9 covers time scaling, polynomial and trapezoidal profiles, and time-optimal motion along a path.
- Luigi Biagiotti and Claudio Melchiorri, Trajectory Planning for Automatic Machines and Robots, Springer, 2008. The detailed reference for polynomial, trapezoidal and double S profiles, with many formulas and examples.
- Bruno Siciliano, Lorenzo Sciavicco, Luigi Villani and Giuseppe Oriolo, Robotics: Modelling, Planning and Control, Springer, 2009. Joint-space and operational-space trajectories, with the treatment of orientation.
- John T. Betts, Survey of numerical methods for trajectory optimization, Journal of Guidance, Control, and Dynamics 21(2), 1998. A survey of direct and indirect methods for trajectory optimization.
- Hung Pham and Quang-Cuong Pham, A new approach to time-optimal path parameterization based on reachability analysis, IEEE Transactions on Robotics 34(3), 2018. The reachability form of time-optimal path parameterization, with proofs and comparisons.
