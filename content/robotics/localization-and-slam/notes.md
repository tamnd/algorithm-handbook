## History

The probabilistic view of estimation came first. Ho and Lee (1964) wrote the Bayesian recursion for the state of a dynamic system, and Bucy and Senne (1971) computed it on a grid of points, which is the histogram filter. For robots, the first maps were grids: Moravec and Elfes (1985) and Elfes (1989) stored the probability of occupancy per cell and filled it with an inverse sensor model. Kuipers and Byun (1991) made the graph of places, the topological map, the central level of a robot map, and Leonard and Durrant-Whyte (1991) tracked a robot with an extended Kalman filter against known beacons.

SLAM began with the stochastic map. Smith and Cheeseman (1986), and Smith, Self and Cheeseman (1990), kept the robot and all landmarks in one state with one covariance, which is EKF SLAM. Dissanayake, Newman, Clark, Durrant-Whyte and Csorba (2001) proved its convergence properties in the linear Gaussian case, and Julier and Uhlmann (2001) showed that the linearized filter becomes inconsistent. Neira and Tardós (2001) gave joint compatibility branch and bound for data association. Particles entered localization with Monte Carlo localization by Dellaert, Fox, Burgard and Thrun (1999), and Fox (2003) made the number of particles adaptive with KLD-sampling. Doucet, de Freitas, Murphy and Russell (2000) described Rao-Blackwellized particle filtering for this setting, and Montemerlo, Thrun, Koller and Wegbreit (2002) built FastSLAM on it. Grisetti, Stachniss and Burgard (2007) made the grid version practical with an improved proposal and adaptive resampling.

The graph view started with Lu and Milios (1997), who solved for all scan poses at once. Thrun, Liu, Koller, Ng, Ghahramani and Durrant-Whyte (2004) gave the sparse extended information filter, and Eustice, Walter and Leonard (2005) showed that its sparsification makes the map overconfident. Thrun and Montemerlo (2006) described GraphSLAM. Dellaert and Kaess (2006) read full SLAM as a sparse least squares problem solved by QR in square root SAM, and Kaess, Ranganathan and Dellaert (2008) made it incremental in iSAM. The Bayes tree of Kaess, Ila, Roberts and Dellaert (2010) led to iSAM2 (Kaess, Johannsson, Roberts, Ila, Leonard and Dellaert, 2012). Sünderhauf and Protzel proposed switchable constraints against wrong loop closures, and Rosen, Carlone, Bandeira and Leonard (2019) gave SE-Sync, which certifies a global optimum.

Attitude has its own line, from spacecraft. Black (1964) gave the TRIAD method, Wahba (1965) posed the least squares problem, Davenport (1968) solved it as an eigenproblem, and Shuster and Oh (1981) made it fast with QUEST. Lefferts, Markley and Shuster (1982) wrote the multiplicative error-state filter. Mahony, Hamel and Pflimlin (2008) gave the nonlinear complementary filter on $SO(3)$, and Barrau and Bonnabel (2017) the invariant extended Kalman filter.

Complete systems followed the algorithms. Davison (2003), and Davison, Reid, Molton and Stasse (2007), ran EKF SLAM from a single camera in MonoSLAM. Hornung, Wurm, Bennewitz, Stachniss and Burgard (2013) published OctoMap, Zhang and Singh (2014) LOAM, and Mur-Artal, Montiel and Tardós (2015) ORB-SLAM.

## Further reading

- Sebastian Thrun, Wolfram Burgard and Dieter Fox, *Probabilistic Robotics* (2005). The standard text for the Bayes filter, the motion and sensor models, grid maps, Monte Carlo localization, EKF SLAM, FastSLAM and GraphSLAM.
- Timothy D. Barfoot, *State Estimation for Robotics* (2017). Estimation on matrix Lie groups, from the Kalman filter to batch estimation of poses.
- Frank Dellaert and Michael Kaess, *Factor Graphs for Robot Perception*, Foundations and Trends in Robotics 6(1-2), 2017. Factor graphs, elimination, the Bayes tree and iSAM in one short book.
- Giorgio Grisetti, Rainer Kümmerle, Cyrill Stachniss and Wolfram Burgard, *A Tutorial on Graph-Based SLAM*, IEEE Intelligent Transportation Systems Magazine 2(4), 2010. A worked derivation of pose graph optimization on the manifold.
- Cesar Cadena, Luca Carlone, Henry Carrillo, Yasir Latif, Davide Scaramuzza, José Neira, Ian Reid and John J. Leonard, *Past, Present, and Future of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping: Toward the Robust-Perception Age*, IEEE Transactions on Robotics 32(6), 2016. A survey of the field and its open questions.
- F. Landis Markley and John L. Crassidis, *Fundamentals of Spacecraft Attitude Determination and Control* (2014). Wahba's problem, its solvers and attitude filters, in detail.
