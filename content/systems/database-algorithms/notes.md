## History

Edgar F. Codd proposed the relational model in 1970, in *A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks*, and with it an algebra of operations on relations. Design theory followed at once. Codd defined the second and third normal forms, and with Raymond Boyce the stronger form now called BCNF. William Armstrong gave his sound and complete axioms for functional dependencies in 1974. Philip Bernstein gave the synthesis of third normal form in 1976, and Catriel Beeri and Bernstein the linear-time closure algorithm in 1979. In 1977 Ashok Chandra and Philip Merlin showed that containment of conjunctive queries is the existence of a homomorphism.

The first relational systems, IBM's System R and Berkeley's Ingres, turned the algebra into plans. Patricia Selinger and her colleagues described the System R optimizer in 1979, with its cost model, its dynamic program over left-deep join orders and its interesting orders; many optimizers still follow that design. Hash joins came from work on database machines: the GRACE join of Masaru Kitsuregawa, Hidehiko Tanaka and Tohru Moto-oka in 1983, and the hybrid hash join of David DeWitt and his colleagues in 1984. Mihalis Yannakakis evaluated acyclic joins in 1981, in linear time in input plus output for full joins. Worst-case optimal joins are recent. Martin Grohe and Dániel Marx bounded the size of a join by a fractional edge cover in 2006, Albert Atserias, Grohe and Marx showed in 2008 that the bound is tight, and Hung Ngo, Ely Porat, Christopher Ré and Atri Rudra gave the first algorithm that runs within it in 2012. Todd Veldhuizen's leapfrog triejoin followed in 2014.

Transactions got their theory at the same time. Kapali Eswaran, Jim Gray, Raymond Lorie and Irving Traiger defined serializability, two-phase locking and predicate locks in 1976. Christos Papadimitriou showed in 1979 that testing view serializability is NP-complete. Daniel Rosenkrantz, Richard Stearns and Philip Lewis introduced wait-die and wound-wait in 1978, and H. T. Kung and John Robinson optimistic concurrency control in 1981. In 1995 Hal Berenson and five coauthors showed that the ANSI isolation levels were ambiguous and that snapshot isolation is not serializable. Alan Fekete and his coauthors characterized its anomalies in 2005, and Michael Cahill, Uwe Röhm and Fekete made it serializable in 2008; PostgreSQL adopted their method in 2011.

Recovery moved from shadow pages to logs. Raymond Lorie described shadow paging in 1977, and Gray's notes of 1978 set out write-ahead logging and its rules. Theo Haerder and Andreas Reuter named the steal and force policies in 1983 and coined the word ACID. C. Mohan, Don Haderle, Bruce Lindsay, Hamid Pirahesh and Peter Schwarz published ARIES in 1992, and its three passes are the recovery method of many systems today.

Datalog joined logic programming and databases in the 1980s. Maarten van Emden and Robert Kowalski had given the least fixed-point semantics of Horn clauses in 1976, and François Bancilhon described semi-naive evaluation in 1986, the year magic sets appeared. Ranked retrieval came from multimedia databases: Ronald Fagin gave his algorithm in 1996, and Fagin, Amnon Lotem and Moni Naor the threshold algorithm in 2001, work that won the Gödel Prize in 2014.

## Further reading

- Raghu Ramakrishnan and Johannes Gehrke, *Database Management Systems*, third edition (2003). The source of the page cost model of this chapter, with the optimizer, locking and ARIES.
- Goetz Graefe, *Query Evaluation Techniques for Large Databases*, ACM Computing Surveys 25(2), 1993. A long survey of sorting, hashing, joins and aggregation, with the duality of sort-based and hash-based methods.
- Philip A. Bernstein, Vassos Hadzilacos and Nathan Goodman, *Concurrency Control and Recovery in Database Systems* (1987). The theory of serializability, locking, timestamp ordering, multiversion control and logging, with proofs.
- Theo Haerder and Andreas Reuter, *Principles of Transaction-Oriented Database Recovery*, ACM Computing Surveys 15(4), 1983. The classification of recovery methods by steal, force and checkpoint type.
- Serge Abiteboul, Richard Hull and Victor Vianu, *Foundations of Databases* (1995). Conjunctive queries, acyclic joins, dependency theory, the chase and Datalog, as mathematics.
- Hung Q. Ngo, Christopher Ré and Atri Rudra, *Skew Strikes Back: New Developments in the Theory of Join Algorithms*, SIGMOD Record 42(4), 2013. A short account of worst-case optimal joins.
