## History

Knowlton published the binary buddy system in 1965, and Knuth reports that Markowitz had used the same idea in the Simscript system a little earlier. Knuth's *Fundamental Algorithms* (1968) gave the field its standard form: first fit and best fit on a free list, the boundary tags that make a free take constant time, the buddy system, and the fifty-percent rule for the steady state of a random model. In an exercise he also suggested buddies with Fibonacci sizes, which Hirschberg designed and tested in 1973. Randell's note of 1969 separated the space lost between blocks from the space lost inside rounded-up blocks, the two kinds of fragmentation that the chapter still uses.

The worst case came next. Robson proved in 1971 that every allocator that never moves blocks can be forced to use a heap a logarithmic factor larger than the live memory. In 1974 he found the exact value for sizes that are powers of two, and in 1977 he gave the worst cases of first fit and best fit. The offline problem, in which all lifetimes are known, is problem SR2 in Garey and Johnson's *Computers and Intractability* (1979). A line of approximation algorithms brought the ratio of the optimum to the load down to 3 through Gergov's work, and Buchsbaum, Karloff, Kenyon, Reingold and Thorup (STOC 2003) showed how the optimum approaches the load when every item is small.

Weinstock and Wulf's Quick Fit (1988) put quick lists of exact sizes in front of a general allocator. Doug Lea wrote the first version of dlmalloc in 1987, and Wolfram Gloger's ptmalloc, derived from it, became the malloc of GNU libc. Hanson (1990) packaged arenas freed by lifetime as a library, and Tofte and Talpin (POPL 1994) gave regions a type system that makes freeing them safe. Bonwick presented the slab allocator of the SunOS 5.4 kernel at the USENIX Summer conference in 1994, and with Adams he added per-CPU magazines in 2001. Masmano, Ripoll, Crespo and Real presented TLSF for real-time systems at ECRTS 2004.

Measurement changed the view of the old policies. The survey of Wilson, Johnstone, Neely and Boles (1995) separated policy from mechanism and criticized the random traces behind earlier results. On traces of real C and C++ programs, Johnstone and Wilson (ISMM 1998) found that address-ordered first fit and best fit wasted almost nothing once headers and alignment were counted apart.

Multiprocessors brought the allocators of the last group. Hoard (Berger, McKinley, Blumofe and Wilson, ASPLOS 2000) gave a bound on the memory of per-processor heaps in terms of the memory a serial allocator would need. Maged Michael's allocator (PLDI 2004) kept Hoard's superblocks and removed every lock. Sanjay Ghemawat's tcmalloc came out of Google in the gperftools package, Jason Evans presented jemalloc for FreeBSD at BSDCan 2006, and Leijen, Zorn and de Moura described mimalloc in 2019.

## Further reading

- Donald E. Knuth, *The Art of Computer Programming*, Volume 1, *Fundamental Algorithms*, third edition, Addison-Wesley, 1997. Section 2.5, Dynamic storage allocation, has first fit, best fit, boundary tags, the buddy system and the fifty-percent rule.
- Paul R. Wilson, Mark S. Johnstone, Michael Neely and David Boles, Dynamic storage allocation: a survey and critical review, IWMM 1995, LNCS 986. The best map of the field, with a long history.
- Mark S. Johnstone and Paul R. Wilson, The memory fragmentation problem: solved?, ISMM 1998. A short trace study that shows how to measure fragmentation and why the good placement policies waste so little.
- Jeff Bonwick, The slab allocator: an object-caching kernel memory allocator, USENIX Summer 1994 Technical Conference. Object caches and slab coloring, in a readable design paper.
- Emery D. Berger, Kathryn S. McKinley, Robert D. Blumofe and Paul R. Wilson, Hoard: a scalable memory allocator for multithreaded applications, ASPLOS 2000. The problems of false sharing and blowup, and the superblock design that the later allocators of servers built on.
- Richard Jones, Antony Hosking and Eliot Moss, *The Garbage Collection Handbook: The Art of Automatic Memory Management*, CRC Press, 2011. Its chapter on allocation covers sequential fits, segregated fits and bump pointers from the side of managed runtimes.
