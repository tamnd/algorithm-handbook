## History

Packet switching was proposed by Paul Baran in 1964 and built in the ARPANET from 1969. Error control by retransmission is older: Benice and Frey analyzed ARQ schemes in 1964, and Bartlett, Scantlebury and Wilkinson published the [alternating bit protocol](/alternating-bit-protocol/) in 1969. Cerf and Kahn described the design that became TCP in 1974. For shared channels, Abramson built ALOHA in Hawaii in 1970, Roberts showed in 1972 that slots double its throughput, and Metcalfe and Boggs described Ethernet in 1976.

The ARPANET routed by distance vectors from 1969 and moved to link state in 1980, with the algorithm of McQuillan, Richer and Rosen. RIP was written down as RFC 1058 in 1988. Perlman designed the [spanning tree protocol](/spanning-tree-protocol/) in 1985, and BGP first appeared as RFC 1105 in 1989. The theory of when policy routing converges came later: Gao and Rexford gave their safe rules in 2001, and Griffin, Shepherd and Wilfong defined the stable paths problem and the dispute wheel in 2002.

In October 1986 the Internet suffered its first congestion collapse. Karn and Partridge fixed RTT sampling in 1987, and Jacobson's paper of 1988 added slow start, congestion avoidance and the variance-based timer. Chiu and Jain explained in 1989 why additive increase with multiplicative decrease is the right linear rule, and Reno added fast recovery in 1990. Floyd and Jacobson proposed RED in 1993, and Brakmo, O'Malley and Peterson proposed Vegas in 1994. The analytical view came with Mathis and others in 1997, and with Kelly in 1997 and Kelly, Maulloo and Tan in 1998, who read congestion control as distributed optimization. CUBIC appeared in 2008, CoDel in 2012, PIE in 2013 and BBR in 2016.

Fair queueing began with Nagle in 1987 and with Demers, Keshav and Shenker in 1989. Parekh and Gallager proved the delay bounds of GPS and its packet version in 1993 and 1994, and Shreedhar and Varghese made fair queueing cheap with DRR in 1995. For switches, Karol, Hluchyj and Morgan found the $2 - \sqrt{2}$ limit of FIFO input queues in 1987, Anderson, Owicki, Saxe and Thacker gave PIM in 1993, Tassiulas and Ephremides proved that max-weight scheduling is throughput optimal in 1992, and McKeown published iSLIP in 1999. Fast lookup grew from Morrison's PATRICIA tree of 1968, through the papers of Degermark and others and of Waldvogel and others in 1997, to the tree bitmap of Eatherton, Varghese and Dittia in 2004. Gupta and McKeown gave HiCuts, and Srinivasan, Suri and Varghese gave tuple space search, both in 1999.

Interconnection networks have their own line. Beneš described his rearrangeable network in 1964, Kermani and Kleinrock proposed virtual cut-through in 1979, Valiant and Brebner gave randomized two-phase routing in 1981, and Dally and Seitz made wormhole routing deadlock-free in 1987. The overlay and wireless entries are more recent: AODV by Perkins and Royer in 1999, GPSR by Karp and Kung in 2000, network coding by Ahlswede, Cai, Li and Yeung in 2000, compact routing schemes by Thorup and Zwick in 2001, Chord in 2001 and Kademlia in 2002.

## Further reading

- Dimitri Bertsekas and Robert Gallager, *Data Networks*, 2nd edition, Prentice Hall, 1992. The classic text on ARQ, multiaccess, routing and flow control, with the queueing theory they need.
- George Varghese, *Network Algorithmics*, Morgan Kaufmann, 2005. Lookup, classification, switching and scheduling as seen by a router designer, with the principles that make them fast.
- R. Srikant and Lei Ying, *Communication Networks: An Optimization, Control and Stochastic Networks Perspective*, Cambridge University Press, 2014. Network utility maximization, congestion control, and max-weight and backpressure scheduling, with proofs.
- William Dally and Brian Towles, *Principles and Practices of Interconnection Networks*, Morgan Kaufmann, 2004. Topologies, routing, flow control and deadlock in interconnection networks.
- F. Thomson Leighton, *Introduction to Parallel Algorithms and Architectures*, Morgan Kaufmann, 1992. Arrays, trees and hypercubes, with the theory of permutation routing and Valiant's method.
- Van Jacobson, *Congestion Avoidance and Control*, SIGCOMM, 1988. The paper behind slow start, AIMD in TCP and the RTO estimator, and still a clear read.
