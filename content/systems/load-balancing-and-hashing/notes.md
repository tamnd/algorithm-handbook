## History

The policies that read the load came out of queueing theory. Winston (1977) proved that joining the shortest queue is optimal for identical servers with exponential service times, and Weber (1978) extended the result to service times with non-decreasing hazard rate; Whitt (1986) gave examples in which the rule is not optimal once service times vary more. Eager, Lazowska and Zahorjan (1986) showed by analysis and simulation that a load-sharing rule that looks at a few random servers does almost as well as one that sees all of them. This is the idea behind the random sample of two that [peak EWMA](/peak-ewma/) and many client-side balancers use.

The bounds on the maximum load are older in probability than in computing. Gonnet (1981) gave the expected length of the longest chain in a hash table with $n$ keys in $n$ slots, which is the $\ln n / \ln\ln n$ law of [balls into bins](/balls-into-bins/). Raab and Steger (1998) gave the maximum load for all ratios of balls to bins. Azar, Broder, Karlin and Upfal proved the $\ln\ln n / \ln d$ bound for $d$ choices at STOC 1994, with the journal version in 1999. Mitzenmacher's 1996 thesis, published in IEEE TPDS in 2001, studied the supermarket model, the dynamic form of the same idea. Vöcking (2003) showed that breaking ties to the left with $d$ groups lowers the bound to $\ln\ln n / (d \ln \phi_d)$, and Berenbrink, Czumaj, Steger and Vöcking (2006) extended the two-choice bound to any number of balls.

Consistent hashing was introduced by Karger, Lehman, Leighton, Panigrahy, Levine and Lewin at STOC 1997, for web caches; the work led to Akamai. Their paper defined balance, monotonicity, spread and load. Thaler and Ravishankar described rendezvous hashing in a 1996 report and a 1998 journal paper, and Valloppillil and Ross used a weighted form of it for CARP in a 1998 Internet-Draft. Chord (2001) put a ring under a peer-to-peer lookup service, and Dynamo (2007) made the ring with virtual nodes a standard design for key-value stores; the ketama library from Last.fm did the same for memcached clients the same year. Later work reduced the state or the lookup time: Lamping and Veach's jump consistent hash (2014), Appleton and O'Reilly's multi-probe hashing (2015), and Maglev, the table of Eisenbud and his colleagues at Google (NSDI 2016). Mirrokni, Thorup and Zadimoghaddam added load caps in 2016, with the conference version at SODA 2018, and HAProxy and Google Cloud Pub/Sub adopted the method.

The production policies have their own line. Linux Virtual Server, started by Wensong Zhang in 1998, shipped round robin, weighted round robin, weighted least connections and the shortest expected delay rules in the kernel. Nginx gained its smooth weighted round robin, by Maxim Dounin, and its `least_conn` method in 2012. RFC 2991 and RFC 2992 (2000) described hash-based [ECMP](/ecmp-hashing/) and why it should hash flows rather than packets. Twitter's Finagle library introduced peak EWMA, and the Rust library tower carried it into the Linkerd proxy.

## Further reading

- Michael Mitzenmacher and Eli Upfal, *Probability and Computing*, second edition, Cambridge University Press, 2017. Balls into bins, the two-choice bound and the Chernoff bounds used in the chapter.
- Michael Mitzenmacher, "The power of two choices in randomized load balancing", IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems 12(10), 2001. The supermarket model and its fixed point.
- Mor Harchol-Balter, *Performance Modeling and Design of Computer Systems: Queueing Theory in Action*, Cambridge University Press, 2013. The queueing models behind least connections and the dispatching rules.
- David Karger, Eric Lehman, Tom Leighton, Rina Panigrahy, Matthew Levine and Daniel Lewin, "Consistent hashing and random trees: distributed caching protocols for relieving hot spots on the World Wide Web", STOC 1997. The definitions and the first ring.
- Martin Kleppmann, *Designing Data-Intensive Applications*, O'Reilly, 2017. Partitioning by key and by range, rebalancing and skew in real stores.
- Betsy Beyer, Chris Jones, Jennifer Petoff and Niall Richard Murphy (eds.), *Site Reliability Engineering*, O'Reilly, 2016. The chapters on load balancing at the front end and in the data center, and on handling overload.
