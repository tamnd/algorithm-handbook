Many questions about a set of objects in the plane turn into easier questions on a line: whether two segments cross, how much area a set of rectangles covers, or what the outline of a city looks like from far away. The [sweep line technique](/sweep-line-technique/) moves a vertical line from left to right across the input. The line stops only at [event points](/event-point/), where something changes: an object starts, ends, or two objects swap places. At each stop the sweep updates a status structure that holds the objects that the line crosses, and it answers the question for the strip it has just passed. A problem in two dimensions becomes a sequence of updates in one.

Every sweep in this chapter rests on the same two parts. An [event queue](/event-queue/) gives the stops in order, and a [status structure](/sweep-status-structure/) keeps the crossed objects in their order along the line. The proof of a sweep is a [sweep invariant](/sweep-invariant/): after each stop the status is correct for the current position of the line. The cost is usually $O(n \log n)$: a sort of $2n$ events and $O(\log n)$ for each update.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter has seven groups, read in order.

1. **The method.** The [sweep line technique](/sweep-line-technique/) gives the template, [event points](/event-point/) say where the line stops, and the [sweep invariant](/sweep-invariant/) states what must hold at each stop.
2. **The two structures.** The [event queue](/event-queue/) is a priority queue that merges equal points and accepts new events during the sweep. The [sweep status structure](/sweep-status-structure/) is a balanced search tree whose comparator depends on the position of the line.
3. **Ties and degenerate input.** [Degeneracy in sweeps](/degeneracy-in-sweeps/) fixes the order of events at one coordinate and the handling of many segments through one point.
4. **Rectangles.** [Rectangle intersection detection](/rectangle-intersection-detection/) and its [sweep](/rectangle-intersection-sweep/), then the [area of a union of rectangles](/area-of-union-of-rectangles/) and [Bentley's union sweep](/rectangle-union-sweep/) with a segment tree.
5. **Outlines and envelopes.** The [skyline problem](/skyline-problem/), the [skyline sweep](/skyline-sweep/) with a max-heap and lazy deletion, and the [lower envelope of segments](/lower-envelope-of-segments/), whose size is $\Theta(n \alpha(n))$ in the worst case.
6. **Turning the line.** The [rotational sweep](/rotational-sweep/) turns a ray around a point and processes events in angular order.
7. **Sweeping in time.** [Time-driven simulation](/time-driven-simulation/), [event-driven simulation](/event-driven-simulation/), and [collision prediction and resolution](/collision-prediction-and-resolution/) for moving disks. Here the line is the time axis and the events are collisions.

## A first reading

Read the [sweep line technique](/sweep-line-technique/), [event points](/event-point/) and the [sweep invariant](/sweep-invariant/) first, and run the counter sweep on a few intervals by hand. Then read the [rectangle intersection sweep](/rectangle-intersection-sweep/), the smallest complete example with a search tree as the status. The [skyline problem](/skyline-problem/) and the [skyline sweep](/skyline-sweep/) show a heap as the status and the use of lazy deletion. The [area of a union of rectangles](/area-of-union-of-rectangles/) and [Bentley's union sweep](/rectangle-union-sweep/) show a segment tree as the status. Finish with [degeneracy in sweeps](/degeneracy-in-sweeps/) and [event-driven simulation](/event-driven-simulation/). That is ten entries, and they cover the three kinds of status and the main tie rules.

The [event queue](/event-queue/) and the [sweep status structure](/sweep-status-structure/) are the entries to read before an implementation of the [Bentley-Ottmann algorithm](/bentley-ottmann-algorithm/) of Volume 13. The [lower envelope](/lower-envelope-of-segments/) and the [rotational sweep](/rotational-sweep/) are advanced and can wait.

## Before you start

From Volume 1 you need [loop invariants](/loop-invariant/), since each sweep is proved by one, and [element distinctness](/element-distinctness/), the source of the $\Omega(n \log n)$ lower bounds. From Volume 3 you need the [priority queue](/priority-queue/) and the [binary heap](/binary-heap/), the [balanced binary search tree](/balanced-binary-search-tree/) and the [segment tree](/segment-tree/). From Volume 4 you need the [sorting problem](/sorting-problem/), since every sweep starts with a sort. The geometric entries use the [orientation test](/orientation-test/) and [general position](/general-position/) from Volume 13. In this volume, [coordinate compression](/coordinate-compression/) and the [divide-and-conquer paradigm](/divide-and-conquer-paradigm/) appear in the rectangle and skyline entries. The simulation entries use the [hard disk model](/hard-disk-model/), the [dot product](/dot-product/) and the [quadratic formula](/quadratic-formula/).
