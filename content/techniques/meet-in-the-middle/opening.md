Take a block cipher with $k$-bit keys and encrypt a message twice, first with a key $k_1$ and then with a second key $k_2$: $C = E_{k_2}(E_{k_1}(P))$. The pair of keys has $2k$ bits, so a search over all pairs costs about $2^{2k}$ trials. But the value in the middle, $E_{k_1}(P)$, is also $D_{k_2}(C)$, the decryption of $C$ with the second key. So encrypt $P$ under every first key and store the $2^k$ results in a table. Then decrypt $C$ under every second key and look each result up. A match gives a candidate pair, and a second known message removes the wrong candidates. The work falls from about $2^{2k}$ to about $2^{k+1}$ cipher calls, and the price is a table of $2^k$ entries. For two 56-bit DES keys, this is the difference between $2^{112}$ and $2^{57}$.

This is meet in the middle. Split the unknown into two parts so that the condition on the whole becomes an equation $f(x) = g(y)$, with $x$ the first part and $y$ the second. List the values of $f$, list the values of $g$, and find the common values by sorting or hashing. A search over $N^2$ pairs becomes two lists of $N$ items and a join. The same idea runs [baby-step giant-step](/baby-step-giant-step/) for discrete logarithms and [bidirectional search](/bidirectional-search/) in graphs.

Subset sum has the same shape. Given $n$ integers and a target $t$, a subset of the first half with sum $s$ and a subset of the second half with sum $t - s$ together form a solution. Two sorted lists of $2^{n/2}$ sums and one [two-pointer scan](/two-pointer-pair-search/) replace the $2^n$ subsets. Most of the chapter is about this problem: how to keep the time and lower the memory, and how to go below $2^{n/2}$ when the instances are random.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in three groups of two entries, read in order.

1. **Two keys and one table.** [Double-encryption key recovery](/double-encryption-key-recovery/) states the problem: recover both keys from known plaintext-ciphertext pairs. It counts the wrong key pairs that agree with $t$ pairs by chance, about $2^{2k - tn}$ for $n$-bit blocks, and shows why two pairs suffice for double DES. The [meet-in-the-middle attack](/meet-in-the-middle-attack/) gives the algorithm, with a worked run on a toy cipher, the time $O(K + ct)$ for $K$ keys and $c$ candidates, and the variants that trade memory for time.
2. **Subset sum in half the exponent.** The [Horowitz-Sahni algorithm](/horowitz-sahni-algorithm/) builds each sorted list of half sums by repeated [merging](/merging-sorted-lists/), so the whole run takes $\Theta(2^{n/2})$ time and space. The [Schroeppel-Shamir algorithm](/schroeppel-shamir-algorithm/) splits the items into four quarters and walks the sums of each half in order with a min-heap and a max-heap. The time is $O(n 2^{n/2})$ and the space only $O(2^{n/4})$.
3. **Below the square root.** The [representation technique](/representation-technique/) lets a solution be split into two pieces of half its weight in many ways, about $\binom{w}{w/2}$ for a solution of weight $w$, and keeps only the pieces whose partial sum has a fixed residue. Each list then shrinks by that factor. The [Howgrave-Graham-Joux algorithm](/howgrave-graham-joux-algorithm/) applies this to subset sum and reaches heuristic time about $2^{0.337n}$ and memory about $2^{0.256n}$ on random dense instances.

## A first reading

Read the [meet-in-the-middle attack](/meet-in-the-middle-attack/) and work its toy example by hand, then the [Horowitz-Sahni algorithm](/horowitz-sahni-algorithm/) with its trace of the two lists and the scan. These two entries show the whole method, once for keys and once for sums. Then read [double-encryption key recovery](/double-encryption-key-recovery/) for the count of false candidates, which tells how many known pairs an attack needs. For an example outside this chapter, work [baby-step giant-step](/baby-step-giant-step/), which is the same split applied to an exponent.

The [Schroeppel-Shamir algorithm](/schroeppel-shamir-algorithm/) is advanced, and the last two entries are research level. Their bounds rest on heuristics about random instances and are not worst-case bounds. Read them after the first group and the Horowitz-Sahni algorithm feel easy, and work the small examples in each before the analysis.
