High-level synthesis turns a description of what a circuit computes, such as a loop body written in C, into a register-transfer design: a datapath of functional units, registers and multiplexers, and a controller that tells the datapath what to do in each clock step. Take the small differential equation solver used by Paulin and Knight, with six multiplies and five additions, subtractions and compares, all of one step each. Its longest chain of operations has four steps, so no design finishes in three. In four steps it needs two multipliers and two ALUs. In five or six steps it needs two multipliers and one ALU, and in seven a single unit of each kind is enough. [Latency-constrained scheduling](/latency-constrained-scheduling/) gives these optima for this graph. In general the problem is NP-hard, and so is its mirror, [resource-constrained scheduling](/resource-constrained-scheduling/), which fixes the units and asks for the fewest steps.

The work splits into scheduling, which puts each operation into a control step, allocation, which chooses the units, and binding, which assigns operations to units and values to registers. The chapter holds three kinds of results, and each entry says which kind it gives. Some are exact: with unlimited units the critical path gives the least latency, an integer program finds the optimum of a small graph, a straight-line schedule needs exactly as many registers as the largest number of values live in one step, and the balance equations of a synchronous dataflow graph decide whether a periodic schedule exists. Some are bounds: window bounds on units, the iteration bound of a recursive filter, the minimum initiation interval of a loop. The rest are heuristics, judged on examples and benchmarks: list scheduling, clique partitioning, path merging, and the state encoders KISS, NOVA and MUSTANG.

Some neighbouring topics are treated in Chapter 32.2, Logic synthesis and technology mapping: the overview of [high-level synthesis](/high-level-synthesis/), [ASAP and ALAP schedules](/asap-alap-scheduling/), [force-directed scheduling](/force-directed-scheduling/), [retiming](/retiming/) and [state assignment](/state-assignment/). The [left-edge algorithm](/left-edge-algorithm/), which binds registers here, has its entry in Chapter 32.6, Routing, as a channel router. This chapter links to those entries and does not repeat them.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Models of behaviour.** The [control data flow graph](/control-data-flow-graph/), the [finite state machine with datapath](/finite-state-machine-with-datapath/), [Kahn process networks](/kahn-process-network/), [synchronous dataflow](/synchronous-dataflow/) and its [static scheduling](/sdf-static-scheduling/).
2. **Scheduling a block.** Resource-constrained and latency-constrained scheduling, the [ILP formulation](/ilp-scheduling-formulation/), [operation chaining](/operation-chaining/), [module selection](/module-selection/) and [SDC scheduling](/sdc-scheduling/).
3. **Allocation and binding.** The [resource compatibility graph](/resource-compatibility-graph/), [resource binding](/resource-binding/), [register binding](/register-binding/), [Tseng-Siewiorek clique partitioning](/tseng-siewiorek-clique-partitioning/), [weighted bipartite matching binding](/weighted-bipartite-matching-binding/) and [interconnect binding](/interconnect-binding/).
4. **Branches and loops.** [Path-based scheduling](/path-based-scheduling/), [speculative code motion](/speculative-code-motion/), [SDC modulo scheduling](/sdc-modulo-scheduling/) and [memory partitioning](/memory-partitioning-for-hls/).

6. **Controllers.** [Partition pairs](/partition-pair/), [FSM decomposition](/fsm-decomposition/), [KISS symbolic minimization](/kiss-symbolic-minimization/), [NOVA](/nova-state-encoding/) and [MUSTANG](/mustang-state-assignment/).

## A first reading

Take these thirteen entries in order: the control data flow graph and the finite state machine with datapath; resource-constrained scheduling, latency-constrained scheduling, the ILP formulation and operation chaining; the resource compatibility graph, resource binding and register binding; SDC scheduling; synchronous dataflow; the iteration bound; and partition pairs. They give the models, the hardness of the two scheduling problems with their exact and heuristic answers, the exact register count for straight-line code, and the bounds that limit a loop.

Kahn process networks, module selection, the matching and clique heuristics, interconnect binding, the branch and loop group after SDC scheduling, folding and unfolding, the three state encoders and the system-level group can wait for a second pass.
