Hardware verification checks that a design does what its specification says before the chip is made, while a bug still costs an edit and not a new set of masks. Take a 64-bit multiplier. It has $2^{128}$ input pairs, and a simulator that runs $10^9$ of them per second needs about $10^{22}$ years for all of them. So simulation can only sample, and two questions follow: which samples to run, and how to tell when enough have run. Formal methods answer a different question. A [miter](/miter-circuit/) joins the multiplier with the netlist that synthesis made from it, and a SAT call either proves that the miter never outputs 1 or returns an input on which the two differ. That proof covers all $2^{128}$ pairs at once. It has a price: combinational equivalence is coNP-complete, sequential equivalence of netlists is PSPACE-complete, and the BDD of the middle output bit of a multiplier is exponential in size under every variable order ([the multiplier lower bound](/multiplier-bdd-lower-bound/)).

Most entries of the chapter are one of three kinds. Some are exact: a miter, a product machine, an image step or an algebraic reduction gives a proof or a counterexample, and blows up on some inputs. Some are sound but not complete: latch mapping, signal correspondence, STE and tag coverage never pass a wrong design or count a site wrongly, but they can fail to prove a true fact. The rest are measures and heuristics whose value is measured on designs, such as coverage scores and coverage-directed generation. Each entry says which kind it is.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Coverage of simulation.** [Code coverage](/code-coverage-metrics/), [FSM coverage](/fsm-coverage/), [functional coverage](/functional-coverage/) with bins and crosses, and [observability-based coverage](/observability-based-coverage/).
2. **Generating stimulus.** [Constrained random stimulus generation](/constrained-random-stimulus-generation/), its exact [BDD form](/bdd-constrained-random-generation/), and [coverage-directed test generation](/coverage-directed-test-generation/).
3. **Assertions.** [Assertion-based verification](/assertion-based-verification/) with PSL and SVA, and [assertion checker synthesis](/assertion-checker-synthesis/) into monitor circuits.
4. **Combinational equivalence and debugging.** The [miter](/miter-circuit/), [cutpoint-based equivalence checking](/cutpoint-equivalence-checking/), and [SAT-based design debugging](/sat-based-design-debugging/) to locate the error once a check fails.
5. **Sequential equivalence.** The [product machine](/product-machine/), the [problem](/sequential-equivalence-checking/) and its complexity, [latch mapping](/latch-mapping/), [signal correspondence](/signal-correspondence/) and [safe replaceability](/safe-replaceability/).
6. **Symbolic state exploration.** [Symbolic simulation](/symbolic-simulation/), the [partitioned transition relation](/partitioned-transition-relation/), [Coudert-Madre image computation](/transition-function-image-computation/), [symbolic trajectory evaluation](/symbolic-trajectory-evaluation/) and [its generalized form](/generalized-symbolic-trajectory-evaluation/).
7. **Arithmetic and processors.** [Taylor expansion diagrams](/taylor-expansion-diagram/), [algebraic verification of arithmetic circuits](/algebraic-arithmetic-circuit-verification/), [Burch-Dill flushing](/burch-dill-flushing/) and [positive equality](/positive-equality/).
8. **Clock domain crossings.** The [synchronizer MTBF](/synchronizer-mtbf/) formula and [CDC verification](/clock-domain-crossing-verification/).

Some neighbors are in other chapters of this volume. Test generation for manufacturing faults, [fault simulation](/fault-simulation/), [static timing analysis](/static-timing-analysis/) and [layout versus schematic](/layout-versus-schematic/) are in the chapter on timing, verification and test, and diagnosis of failing chips is in the chapter on test and diagnosis. [SAT sweeping](/sat-sweeping/) on the [and-inverter graph](/and-inverter-graph/), the engine of most combinational checkers, and [retiming](/retiming/), which breaks latch mapping, are in the chapter on logic synthesis. The simulators themselves, such as [logic simulation](/logic-simulation/) and [ternary simulation](/ternary-simulation/), are in the chapter on logic and circuit simulation.

## A first reading

Take these fourteen entries in order: code coverage, FSM coverage, functional coverage, constrained random stimulus generation, assertion-based verification, the miter, the product machine, sequential equivalence checking, latch mapping, signal correspondence, symbolic simulation, the partitioned transition relation, synchronizer MTBF and CDC verification. They give the simulation flow with its measures, the main equivalence checks with their limits, the basic symbolic image step and the rules for clock crossings.

Observability-based coverage, coverage-directed generation, checker synthesis, SAT-based debugging, safe replaceability, STE and GSTE, and the arithmetic and processor group can wait for a second pass. They are advanced or research entries and build on the first reading.
