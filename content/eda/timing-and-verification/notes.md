## History

The delay model came from outside chip design. Elmore defined the delay of a network as the first moment of its impulse response in 1948, in a paper on wideband amplifiers. Rubinstein, Penfield and Horowitz applied it to RC trees in 1983 and gave bounds on the true waveform, and Gupta, Tutuianu and Pileggi proved in 1997 that the Elmore delay is an upper bound on the 50 percent delay of an RC tree. Pillage and Rohrer matched more moments with asymptotic waveform evaluation in 1990. Its models could be unstable. Feldmann and Freund's PVL of 1995 and PRIMA, by Odabasioglu, Celik and Pileggi in 1997, used Krylov projections instead, and PRIMA keeps the model passive.

Timing without simulation vectors began at IBM. Hitchcock, Smith and Cheng described its Timing Analysis program in 1982. Du, Yen and Ghanta and McGeer and Brayton studied false paths at the 1989 Design Automation Conference. Clark had derived the moments of the max of two Gaussians in 1961 for project networks, and Visweswariah and his colleagues used his formulas for block-based statistical timing in 2004. On the clock side, Fishburn wrote skew optimization as a linear program in 1990, the same year Jackson, Srinivasan and Kuh gave the method of means and medians. Tsay gave exact zero skew at ICCAD 1991, and Chao, Hsu, Ho, Boese and Kahng gave deferred-merge embedding in 1992. Fishburn and Dunlop's TILOS sized transistors in 1985, van Ginneken placed buffers by dynamic programming in 1990, and Chen, Chu and Wong sized gates by Lagrangian relaxation in 1998. Sutherland, Sproull and Harris wrote up logical effort as a book in 1999.

Layout verification grew with the design tools of the early 1980s. Bentley and Wood gave an optimal algorithm for reporting rectangle intersections in 1980. Ousterhout described corner stitching in 1984 as the database of the Magic editor, and Scott and Ousterhout built Magic's circuit extractor on it. Ebeling and Zajicek presented Gemini at ICCAD 1983, and Ebeling's GeminiII followed in 1988. Nabors and White's FastCap of 1991 brought multipole methods to capacitance, and Le Coz and Iverson published the floating random walk in 1992, on top of Muller's walk on spheres of 1956. Lithography rests on Hopkins's 1953 theory of partially coherent imaging; layout decomposition for double and triple patterning became a graph coloring problem in the 2000s.

On the test side, Eldred described tests for stuck lines in 1959, and Roth gave the D-calculus and the D-algorithm at IBM in 1966, the first complete method for single stuck-at faults in combinational circuits. Armstrong's deductive fault simulation appeared in 1972, and Ulrich and Baker's concurrent simulation in 1973. Ibarra and Sahni proved fault detection NP-complete in 1975. Eichelberger and Williams described the scan design of LSSD in 1977, Goldstein gave the SCOAP measures in 1979, and Könemann, Mucha and Zwiehoff proposed the BILBO register for self-test the same year. Goel's PODEM of 1981 and the FAN rules of Fujiwara and Shimono in 1983 made search on the inputs practical, and Larrabee turned test generation into satisfiability, in 1989 and in a journal paper of 1992.

## Further reading

- J. Bhasker and Rakesh Chadha, *Static Timing Analysis for Nanometer Designs: A Practical Approach* (Springer, 2009). Timing graphs, cell libraries with table models, setup and hold checks and false paths, as the tools use them.
- Andrew B. Kahng, Jens Lienig, Igor L. Markov and Jin Hu, *VLSI Physical Design: From Graph Partitioning to Timing Closure* (Springer, 2011). Clock tree synthesis, buffering, sizing and timing closure in the context of the whole physical design flow.
- Ivan Sutherland, Bob Sproull and David Harris, *Logical Effort: Designing Fast CMOS Circuits* (Morgan Kaufmann, 1999). The delay model, the best number of stages and many worked circuits.
- Charles J. Alpert, Dinesh P. Mehta and Sachin S. Sapatnekar (editors), *Handbook of Algorithms for Physical Design Automation* (CRC Press, 2008). Survey chapters on clock networks, buffer insertion, extraction and lithography.
- Michael L. Bushnell and Vishwani D. Agrawal, *Essentials of Electronic Testing for Digital, Memory and Mixed-Signal VLSI Circuits* (Kluwer, 2000). Fault models, test generation, fault simulation, scan and built-in self-test.
