## History

The FPGA side of the chapter grew around one research tool. Francis, Rose and Vranesic gave Chortle-crf in 1991, one year after Chortle, and Cong and Ding's FlowMap of 1992 made depth-optimal mapping into lookup tables a network flow problem; CutMap (1995), DAOmap (2004) and priority cuts (2007) followed. Chang, Wong and Wong defined universal switch modules in 1996. Betz and Rose released VPR in 1997, with an annealing placer and a router built on PathFinder, the negotiated congestion router of McMurchie and Ebeling from 1995. Marquardt, Betz and Rose added T-VPack in 1999 and timing-driven placement in 2000, and the minimum channel width found by VPR's binary search became the usual measure of an architecture. Nam, Sakallah and Rutenbar posed detailed routing as satisfiability in 1999. AAPack (Luu, Anderson and Rose, 2011) and HeAP (Gort and Anderson, 2012) carried packing and analytic placement to heterogeneous devices.

Analog design automation took longer, because it has no layer like the gate netlist that hides the devices. Pelgrom, Duinmaijer and Welbers published their mismatch model in 1989, the same year as ISAAC, the symbolic analyzer of Gielen, Walscharts and Sansen. Ochotta, Rutenbar and Carley's ASTRX/OBLX (1994, journal version 1996) sized circuits by annealing over a cost compiled from the specification. Hershenson, Boyd and Lee showed in 2001 that an op-amp written in posynomial form can be sized by geometric programming, with a global optimum for that model. Shi and Tan gave determinant decision diagrams in 1997. For layout, Balasa and Lampaert characterized symmetric-feasible sequence pairs in 2000, and Lin and Lin gave the ASF-B*-tree in 2007.

Three-dimensional integration became a design problem around 2001, when Banerjee, Souri, Kapur and Saraswat estimated the gain in wire length from stacked tiers. Cong, Wei and Zhang added temperature to 3D floorplanning in 2004. Studies of TSV stress and keep-out zones came around 2010, and Shrunk-2D (Panth, Samadi, Du and Lim, 2014) reused planar tools to place monolithic 3D designs.

Reversible logic goes back to Toffoli's gate of 1980. Shende, Prasad, Markov and Hayes put synthesis on a firm basis in 2003, and Miller, Maslov and Dueck gave the transformation-based method the same year. Mapping onto quantum hardware became pressing with the first public devices. Siraichi and others, and Zulehner, Paler and Wille, gave mappers in 2018. Li, Ding and Xie gave SABRE in 2019, and Tan and Cong's OLSQ (2020) showed how far exact formulations scale.

## Further reading

- Vaughn Betz, Jonathan Rose and Alexander Marquardt, *Architecture and CAD for Deep-Submicron FPGAs*, Kluwer Academic Publishers, 1999. The book behind VPR: clusters, routing architecture, T-VPack and the timing-driven placer, with the measurements that chose their parameters.
- Georges G. E. Gielen and Rob A. Rutenbar, "Computer-aided design of analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits", *Proceedings of the IEEE* 88(12), 2000. A survey of analog sizing, symbolic analysis and layout as they stood when most of the analog part of this chapter was new.
- Marcel J. M. Pelgrom, Aad C. J. Duinmaijer and Anton P. G. Welbers, "Matching properties of MOS transistors", *IEEE Journal of Solid-State Circuits* 24(5), 1989. The source of the area law of mismatch, with the measurements that fit it.
- Sung Kyu Lim, *Design for High Performance, Low Power, and Reliable 3D Integrated Circuits*, Springer, 2013. Tier partitioning, TSV placement, stress and thermal issues in one book.
- Mehdi Saeedi and Igor L. Markov, "Synthesis and optimization of reversible circuits: a survey", *ACM Computing Surveys* 45(2), 2013. A map of reversible synthesis, from exact search to transformation-based and decomposition methods.
- Gushu Li, Yufei Ding and Yuan Xie, "Tackling the qubit mapping problem for NISQ-era quantum devices", *Proceedings of ASPLOS*, 2019. The SABRE paper: the heuristic score, the decay and the reverse traversal for the initial mapping.
