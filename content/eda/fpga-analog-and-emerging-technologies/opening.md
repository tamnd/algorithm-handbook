Most of this volume assumes a chip of standard cells. This chapter is about targets that break that assumption. A field-programmable gate array has its lookup tables, clusters, wires and switches fixed in silicon, so a design must be mapped, packed, placed and routed onto a fabric whose channel width cannot grow. An analog circuit has few devices, but each one is sized against continuous specifications, and matched devices must be laid out in mirror or common-centroid patterns. A three-dimensional chip stacks tiers of devices joined by vertical vias. A reversible circuit, and the classical part of a quantum program, is built from Toffoli gates, and a quantum device lets only some pairs of qubits interact.

Two small cases show the kind of result to expect. A switch block with $W$ tracks per side needs at least $6W$ switches to route every set of connections its sides allow, and a block with exactly $6W$ switches does it: that is a theorem. A VPR placement, on the other hand, is good because it is measured to be good on benchmarks. The chapter holds both kinds. The proved results are narrow and exact: the switch-block bound, the depth of CutMap and DAOmap, the global optimum of a geometric program for its posynomial model, the cancellation of linear gradients by a common centroid, the symmetry of every packing of an ASF-B*-tree, the gate bound of transformation-based synthesis, the NP-hardness of packing, tier partitioning and qubit mapping. The rest are heuristics with measured results: the packers, the placers, annealing for sizing, Shrunk-2D, thermal floorplanning and SABRE. Each entry says which kind it gives.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in nine groups. The first four are about FPGAs and are best read in order; the others can be read in any order.

1. **FPGA architecture.** The [island-style array](/island-style-fpga/), the [logic cluster](/fpga-logic-cluster/), the [switch block](/fpga-switch-block/) and the [universal switch block](/universal-switch-block/), the [routing resource graph](/routing-resource-graph/) and the [minimum channel width](/minimum-channel-width/).

4. **FPGA placement and routing.** [Timing-driven annealing placement](/fpga-timing-driven-annealing-placement/), [HeAP](/heap-fpga-placement/) and [SAT-based routing](/sat-based-fpga-routing/). ## A first reading

Take these fourteen entries in order: the island-style FPGA, the logic cluster, the switch block, the routing resource graph and the minimum channel width; FPGA packing and T-VPack; timing-driven annealing placement; the Pelgrom model and analog circuit sizing; the symmetry constraint and common-centroid layout; the 3D IC; and qubit mapping. They give the fabric and its measures, one complete FPGA flow after mapping, the two facts that drive analog design (matching costs area, and gradients cancel by symmetry), the reason for stacking, and the hardest step of a quantum compiler.

The universal switch block, SAT-based routing, the symbolic analysis group, the two symmetric placers, Shrunk-2D, thermal floorplanning and the reversible group can wait for a second pass.
