13. Two cubes merge when no position has a 0 in one and a 1 in the other, and the least number of merged vectors is the chromatic number of the graph of incompatible pairs. 15. The registers are 110, 010, 000 and 111, so the signature is 111. The changed words are 111 and 001, and the signature is again 111. The two errors $e_0 = 1$ and $e_1 = x$ add $x^{m-2}(x \cdot e_0 + e_1) = x^{m-2}(x + x) = 0$. An error $e \ne 0$ in word $t$ alone adds $x^{m-1-t} e(x) \bmod q$. Since $q_0 = 1$, $x$ is invertible modulo $q$, so this is 0 only if $q$ divides $e$, which is impossible because $\deg e < 3$.

21. The good outputs $yz$ are 11, 01 and 00. The full dictionary has 10 classes and the pass-fail dictionary 6; $a$/1 is detected by no test. The observed error is $y$ on test 0 only. The full dictionary gives $\{a/0, b_1/0, y/0\}$, and the pass-fail dictionary gives $\{a/0, b/0, b_1/0, b_2/0, y/0\}$, because $b$/0 and $b_2$/0 also fail only test 0, with $z$ wrong. 22. At 111 both $g$ and $h$ are 1, so flipping one input of the OR changes nothing: $g$, $h$, $a_1$ and $a_2$ are not critical. Flipping the stem makes $g = h = 0$ and $z = 0$, so $a$ is critical. 23. The two words add $x^{m-2-t}(x\,e_t + e_{t+1}) \bmod q$ to the signature. With $q_0 = 1$, $x$ is invertible modulo $q$, so the pair aliases exactly when $e_{t+1} = x\,e_t \bmod q$. Each of the $2^k - 1$ nonzero $e_t$ has exactly one such $e_{t+1}$, and $e_t = 0$ forces $e_{t+1} = 0$. The share is $(2^k - 1)/(2^{2k} - 1) = 1/(2^k + 1)$, a little below $2^{-k}$, and it equals the equally-likely formula $(2^{(m-1)k} - 1)/(2^{mk} - 1)$ at $m = 2$.

24. The stream aliases when its error polynomial, of degree below 4, is a nonzero multiple of $q$: $q$ and $xq$ of weight 3, and $(x + 1)q = x^3 + 1$ of weight 2. So $P_4(p) = 2p^3(1 - p) + p^2(1 - p)^2$. This gives $0.0099$ at $p = 1/10$ and $3/16$ at $p = 1/2$, both below $1/4$. The limit is not a bound for short streams: running the error-state Markov chain exactly at $p = 1/10$ gives about $0.0489$ for $m = 8$ and $0.2489$ for $m = 64$.
