## History

Partitioning came first. Brian Kernighan and Shen Lin published their [swap heuristic](/kernighan-lin/) in 1970, and Charles Fiduccia and Robert Mattheyses made a pass linear in the number of pins in 1982 by moving single cells and keeping the gains in buckets. The spectral line began in the same years: Kenneth Hall placed graphs by quadratic minimization in 1970, William Donath and Alan Hoffman bounded the bisection width by Laplacian eigenvalues in 1973, and Miroslav Fiedler studied the second eigenvalue and its vector in 1973 and 1975. Alex Pothen, Horst Simon and Kang-Pu Liou made [spectral bisection](/spectral-partitioning/) practical in 1990. Yen-Chuen Wei and Chung-Kuan Cheng introduced the [ratio cut](/ratio-cut/) in 1989, and Lars Hagen and Andrew Kahng tied it to $\lambda_2$ in 1992. The [multilevel](/multilevel-graph-partitioning/) idea came from Thang Bui and Curt Jones in 1993 and from Bruce Hendrickson and Robert Leland in 1995. George Karypis and Vipin Kumar built METIS on it, and [hMETIS](/multilevel-hypergraph-partitioning/), with Rajat Aggarwal and Shashi Shekhar, brought it to hypergraphs in 1997. Bernard Landman and Roy Russo published [Rent's rule](/rents-rule/) in 1971 from E. F. Rent's IBM memos of 1960.

Placement began with boards and gate arrays. Neil Quinn and Melvin Breuer described [force-directed placement](/force-directed-placement/) in 1979, and Breuer's [min-cut placement](/min-cut-placement/) of 1977 cut the netlist and the chip together. Alfred Dunlop and Brian Kernighan added [terminal propagation](/terminal-propagation/) for standard cells in 1985. In the same year Carl Sechen and Alberto Sangiovanni-Vincentelli released TimberWolf, which used the [simulated annealing](/simulated-annealing/) of Kirkpatrick, Gelatt and Vecchi (1983) and set the standard of quality for about ten years. GORDIAN, by Jürgen Kleinhans, Georg Sigl, Frank Johannes and Kurt Antreich in 1991, made [quadratic placement](/quadratic-placement/) with partitioning the core of a cell placer, and Kraftwerk in 1998 replaced partitioning by spreading forces.

Floorplanning got its combinatorics in the 1980s. Ralph Otten described [slicing structures](/slicing-floorplan/) in 1982, and Larry Stockmeyer [sized them optimally](/stockmeyer-floorplan-sizing/) in 1983, proving at the same time that the orientation problem for general floorplans is NP-complete. Martin Wong and C. L. Liu searched [normalized Polish expressions](/normalized-polish-expression/) by annealing in 1986. General packings followed a decade later: the [sequence pair](/sequence-pair/) of Hiroshi Murata, Kunihiro Fujiyoshi, Shigetoshi Nakatake and Yoji Kajitani in 1995, the [O-tree](/o-tree-floorplan/) of Pei-Ning Guo, Chung-Kuan Cheng and Takeshi Yoshimura in 1999, and the [B*-tree](/b-star-tree-floorplan/) of Yun-Chih Chang, Yao-Wen Chang, Guang-Ming Wu and Shu-Wei Wu in 2000. Xiaoping Tang, Ruiqi Tian and Martin Wong packed a sequence pair in $O(n \log n)$ time in 2000. Bo Yao, Hongyu Chen, Chung-Kuan Cheng and Ronald Graham showed in 2001 that mosaic floorplans are counted by the [Baxter numbers](/mosaic-floorplan-baxter-theorem/).

The ISPD contests of 2005 and 2006 gave the field common benchmarks, and the analytical placers took over. William Naylor, Ross Donelly and Lu Sha patented the [log-sum-exp model](/log-sum-exp-wirelength/) in 2001. NTUplace3 by Tung-Chieh Chen and coauthors and Kraftwerk2 appeared in 2008, and in the same year Peter Spindler, Ulf Schlichtmann and Frank Johannes published [Abacus](/abacus-legalization/). Myung-Chul Kim, Dong-Jin Lee and Igor Markov published [SimPL](/simpl-placement/) in 2010. Jingwei Lu and coauthors published [ePlace](/electrostatics-based-placement/) in 2014 and 2015, and its successor RePlAce is now part of the open OpenROAD flow.

## Further reading

- Andrew B. Kahng, Jens Lienig, Igor L. Markov and Jin Hu, *VLSI Physical Design: From Graph Partitioning to Timing Closure*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2022). The textbook for the whole chapter, with partitioning, floorplanning, global and detailed placement and their algorithms.
- Naveed A. Sherwani, *Algorithms for VLSI Physical Design Automation*, 3rd ed. (Kluwer, 1999). The older standard text, with full treatments of min-cut, annealing and slicing floorplans.
- Charles J. Alpert and Andrew B. Kahng, "Recent directions in netlist partitioning: a survey", *Integration, the VLSI Journal* 19(1–2), 1–81 (1995). The models, objectives and methods of partitioning before the multilevel tools.
- Charles J. Alpert, Dinesh P. Mehta and Sachin S. Sapatnekar (eds.), *Handbook of Algorithms for Physical Design Automation* (CRC Press, 2008). Survey chapters on partitioning, floorplan representations, global placement, legalization and detailed placement.
- Peter Spindler, Ulf Schlichtmann and Frank M. Johannes, "Abacus: fast legalization of standard cell circuits with minimal movement", *Proceedings of the International Symposium on Physical Design* (2008). The exact row algorithm and its measured comparison with Tetris.
