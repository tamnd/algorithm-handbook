Take one node of a chip: a gate output with 10 fF of load, a 1 V supply, a 1 GHz clock, and a rising transition in one cycle out of ten. It draws $\alpha_{0 \to 1} C V^2 f = 0.1 \cdot 10^{-14} \cdot 1 \cdot 10^9 = 1\ \mu$W. A hundred million such nodes draw 100 W, and that current has to come in through a resistive grid of wires, so the supply sags where many gates switch at once. The same current slowly moves the metal of the wires, a neighboring wire couples noise into each net, and the factory makes a fraction of the dies with defects that short two nets or cut one. A design that computes the right function and meets its clock can still fail for any of these reasons.

This chapter collects the models and algorithms for these problems. Many of the models are empirical fits: the power formula itself is exact for its capacitive model, but leakage laws, Black's equation, the NBTI power law and the yield models rest on stated assumptions and are checked against measurements. The algorithms on top of them are often exact for their model. Power grid analysis is a sparse linear solve, vectorless verification is one linear program per node, multiple threshold assignment on a chain is a knapsack problem, and the sum of coherent systems is a truncated eigendecomposition with an error bound. Each entry says which of its statements are proved, which are measured, and under which model.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Power and switching activity.** [Dynamic power](/dynamic-power-dissipation/), [leakage power](/leakage-power/), [switching activity](/switching-activity/) as transitions per cycle, and [glitch power](/glitch-power/), the extra transitions that come from unequal path delays.
2. **Estimating activity.** [Signal probability propagation](/signal-probability-propagation/), [transition density](/transition-density/), the exact [BDD method](/bdd-switching-activity-estimation/), [Monte Carlo power estimation](/monte-carlo-power-estimation/) with a stopping rule, and [RTL power macromodels](/rtl-power-macromodeling/).
3. **Reducing power.** [Clock gating](/clock-gating/), [operand isolation](/operand-isolation/), [bus-invert coding](/bus-invert-coding/), [power gating](/power-gating/), [multiple threshold voltage assignment](/multi-threshold-voltage-assignment/) and [clustered voltage scaling](/clustered-voltage-scaling/).
4. **Power grids.** [Power grid analysis](/power-grid-analysis/) as a linear system, solved by a [multigrid-like method](/multigrid-power-grid-analysis/) or by [random walks](/random-walk-power-grid-analysis/), bounded by [vectorless verification](/vectorless-power-grid-verification/), and protected by [decoupling capacitance](/decoupling-capacitance-allocation/).
5. **Coupling noise.** [Crosstalk noise](/crosstalk-noise/), the [Miller coupling factor](/miller-coupling-factor/) and the [Devgan noise metric](/devgan-noise-metric/).
6. **Reliability and temperature.** [Black's equation](/black-equation/), the [Blech length](/blech-length/), the [reaction-diffusion model of NBTI](/nbti-reaction-diffusion-model/), [soft error masking](/soft-error-masking/) and the [compact thermal model](/compact-thermal-model/).
7. **Yield.** [Critical area](/critical-area/) and its [Voronoi extraction](/voronoi-critical-area-extraction/), the [Poisson](/poisson-yield-model/), [Murphy](/murphy-yield-model/) and [negative binomial](/negative-binomial-yield-model/) yield models, [redundant via insertion](/redundant-via-insertion/), [design centering](/design-centering/) and [simplicial approximation](/simplicial-approximation/).
8. **Fill and lithography.** The [dummy fill problem](/dummy-fill-problem/), [LP fill synthesis](/lp-density-fill/), the [Hopkins imaging model](/hopkins-imaging-model/), the [sum of coherent systems](/sum-of-coherent-systems/) and [inverse lithography](/inverse-lithography-technology/).

## A first reading

Read dynamic power, switching activity and signal probability propagation first, since nearly every power entry uses them. Then clock gating as the standard way to save power, power grid analysis for the linear system that the grid entries share, and crosstalk noise for the coupling model. For reliability and yield, read Black's equation, critical area, the Poisson yield model and the negative binomial yield model, which show how a fitted model and a geometric quantity combine into one number. Finish with the dummy fill problem and the Hopkins imaging model.

That is eleven entries. Skip on a first pass the advanced algorithms that refine these: the multigrid and random-walk grid solvers, the Devgan metric, Voronoi extraction, simplicial approximation, the sum of coherent systems, and the BDD method. Leave the three research entries, NBTI, inverse lithography and vectorless verification, for last.

## Before you start

The chapter leans on the earlier EDA chapters. Wires are [RC trees](/rc-tree/) with the [Elmore delay](/elmore-delay/) as their first moment, and [static timing analysis](/static-timing-analysis/) supplies the slack that voltage and threshold assignment spend; these are in the chapter on timing, verification and test. The grid equations come from [Kirchhoff's laws](/kirchhoff-circuit-laws/) and [modified nodal analysis](/modified-nodal-analysis/) in the chapter on circuit simulation, and circuits are [Boolean networks](/boolean-network/) as in logic synthesis.

From Volume 1 (Foundations) you need the [Poisson distribution](/poisson-distribution/) and the [central limit theorem](/central-limit-theorem/). From Volume 7 (Graphs) come the [Laplacian matrix](/laplacian-matrix/), [maximum independent set](/maximum-independent-set/) and [topological sort](/topological-sort/). Volume 12 (Numerical) has [positive definite matrices](/positive-definite-matrix/), the [multigrid method](/multigrid-method/) and the [heat equation](/heat-equation/). Volume 13 (Geometry) has the [Minkowski sum](/minkowski-sum/) and the [Voronoi diagram](/voronoi-diagram/). Volume 15 (Optimization) has [linear programming](/linear-programming-problem/), the [knapsack problem](/knapsack-problem/) and [gradient descent](/gradient-descent/). The lithography entries use the [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/) and [convolution](/convolution/) of Volume 11 (Algebra), and the BDD method uses [reduced ordered BDDs](/reduced-ordered-bdd/) from Volume 16 (Logic and constraints).
