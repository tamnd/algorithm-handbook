## History

Reliability and yield models came first. Murphy's 1964 paper on the cost and size of monolithic integrated circuits averaged the Poisson yield over a distribution of defect densities, and Stapper and his colleagues at IBM made the negative binomial model with a clustering parameter the standard fit; their 1983 survey of integrated circuit yield statistics and Stapper's 1983 paper on defect sensitivities tie the models to critical area. James Black published his electromigration lifetime formula in 1969 for aluminum lines, and Ilan Blech showed in 1976 that short lines do not fail because a back stress balances the electron wind. Jeppson and Svensson gave the reaction-diffusion explanation of negative bias instability in 1977, which Alam, Mahapatra and others developed for scaled PMOS devices; it is now disputed by trap-based models.

Power became a design objective around 1990. Parker and McCluskey had treated signal probabilities of combinational networks in 1975, for testing. Najm's transition density (DAC 1991, IEEE Transactions on CAD 1993), the exact BDD method of Ghosh, Devadas, Keutzer and White (DAC 1992), and the Monte Carlo stopping rule of Burch, Najm, Yang and Trick (1992 and 1993) made activity estimation a field of its own. Stan and Burleson proposed bus-invert coding in 1995, the same year Usami and Horowitz presented clustered voltage scaling. Clock gating and operand isolation spread through the 1990s, and power gating and dual threshold libraries followed as leakage grew.

Coupling noise and power grids became analysis problems of their own in the late 1990s. Devgan's noise metric appeared at ICCAD 1997. Kozhaya, Nassif and Najm published the multigrid-like solver at ICCAD 2001, Qian, Nassif and Sapatnekar the random walk solver at DAC 2003, and Kouroussis and Najm vectorless verification at the same conference.

On the manufacturing side, Hopkins gave his theory of partially coherent imaging in 1953, and Cobb's 1998 Berkeley thesis turned it into the sum of coherent systems used in optical proximity correction. Saleh and Sayegh posed mask design as an inverse problem in 1981, and pixel-based inverse lithography was formulated as an optimization by Poonawala and Milanfar in 2007. Director and Hachtel gave simplicial approximation for design centering in 1977. Kahng, Robins, Singh and Zelikovsky gave the linear programming approach to dummy fill in 1998 and 1999, and Papadopoulou and Lee the Voronoi method for critical area in the same year.

## Further reading

- Jan M. Rabaey, Anantha Chandrakasan and Borivoje Nikolić. *Digital Integrated Circuits: A Design Perspective*, 2nd edition. Prentice Hall, 2003. Dynamic and leakage power, interconnect, noise and power distribution at the circuit level.
- Farid N. Najm. "A survey of power estimation techniques in VLSI circuits." *IEEE Transactions on Very Large Scale Integration Systems* 2(4), 1994. Probabilistic, BDD and simulation methods for switching activity, with their assumptions.
- Israel Koren and Zahava Koren. "Defect tolerance in VLSI circuits: techniques and yield analysis." *Proceedings of the IEEE* 86(9), 1998. Yield models, critical area and redundancy.
- Alfred Kwok-Kit Wong. *Resolution Enhancement Techniques in Optical Lithography*. SPIE Press, 2001. Partially coherent imaging, the Hopkins model and the correction methods built on it.
- Andrew B. Kahng, Jens Lienig, Igor L. Markov and Jin Hu. *VLSI Physical Design: From Graph Partitioning to Timing Closure*. Springer, 2011. Where power grids, fill and manufacturability enter the physical design flow.
