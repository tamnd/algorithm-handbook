## History

Rule systems go back to Post's canonical systems of 1943, but as a model of reasoning they come from Newell and Simon, whose *Human Problem Solving* (1972) described thought as a production system. The expert systems of the 1970s put the idea to work. MYCIN (Shortliffe 1976) diagnosed infections by backward chaining over several hundred rules, weighed by the certainty factors of Shortliffe and Buchanan (1975), which Heckerman (1986) later reinterpreted as likelihood ratios that are exact only under conditional independence. OPS5 (Forgy 1981) ran forward, with the conflict resolution strategies of McDermott and Forgy (1978) and the Rete matcher of Forgy's 1979 thesis and 1982 paper. Miranker's TREAT (1987) showed that Rete's stored state is not always worth its memory.

The logic of rules with exceptions took shape in 1980, in one issue of *Artificial Intelligence* that carried McCarthy's circumscription, Reiter's default logic and the nonmonotonic logic of McDermott and Doyle. Moore's autoepistemic logic followed in 1985. Doyle's truth maintenance system (1979) and de Kleer's assumption-based version (1986) gave reasoners a record of why they believed things. Reiter (1987) and de Kleer and Williams (1987) turned this into diagnosis from first principles, and Greiner, Smith and Wilkerson (1989) corrected Reiter's hitting set tree. Hanks and McDermott (1987) showed with the Yale shooting problem that plain minimization of change gives unintended models.

Logic programming went its own way. Clark (1978) read negation as failure through the completion. Apt, Blair and Walker (1988) gave stratified programs their standard model and Przymusinski (1988) the perfect model of locally stratified ones, Gelfond and Lifschitz (1988) defined stable models, and Van Gelder, Ross and Schlipf, at PODS 1988 and in a journal paper of 1991, defined the well-founded model; Van Gelder's alternating fixpoint (1989) and the SLG resolution of Chen and Warren (1996) compute it. The complexity was settled early in the 1990s: Marek and Truszczyński (1991) showed that stable model existence is NP-complete, Gottlob (1992) placed default and autoepistemic logic at the second level, and Eiter and Gottlob (1995) did the same for disjunctive programs. Fages (1994) and Lifschitz and Turner (1994) proved the theorems on tightness and splitting.

In parallel, Kraus, Lehmann and Magidor (1990) asked which rules a reasonable consequence relation must obey and found System P; Lehmann and Magidor (1992) defined the rational closure, and Pearl (1990) reached the same relation from probabilities with System Z.

Answer set programming began around 1999, when Niemelä and, independently, Marek and Truszczyński argued that programs under stable models are a language for search problems. The smodels system (Simons, Niemelä and Soininen 2002) and DLV were the first solvers. Lin and Zhao (2002, 2004) added loop formulas to the completion, Lifschitz, Pearce and Valverde (2001) characterized strong equivalence, and the clasp solver (Gebser, Kaufmann, Neumann and Schaub 2007) brought conflict-driven learning, which is the basis of the solvers in use today.

## Further reading

- Victor W. Marek and Mirosław Truszczyński, *Nonmonotonic Logic: Context-Dependent Reasoning*, Springer, 1993. Default logic, autoepistemic logic and logic programs in one framework, with the complexity results.
- Kenneth D. Forbus and Johan de Kleer, *Building Problem Solvers*, MIT Press, 1993. Pattern-directed rule engines and truth maintenance systems, built step by step with code.
- Ronald J. Brachman and Hector J. Levesque, *Knowledge Representation and Reasoning*, Morgan Kaufmann, 2004. A textbook that covers production systems, defaults, circumscription, abduction and diagnosis at an introductory level.
- Martin Gebser, Roland Kaminski, Benjamin Kaufmann and Torsten Schaub, *Answer Set Solving in Practice*, Morgan & Claypool, 2012. How grounders and conflict-driven solvers work, and how to model problems for them.
- Vladimir Lifschitz, *Answer Set Programming*, Springer, 2019. The language, its semantics and the theorems on completion, tightness, splitting and strong equivalence, with exercises.
- Gerhard Brewka, Thomas Eiter and Mirosław Truszczyński, "Answer set programming at a glance", *Communications of the ACM* 54(12), 2011. A short survey of the method and its applications.
