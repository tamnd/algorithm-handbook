1. Admissible: $\emptyset$, $\{a\}$, $\{d\}$, $\{a, c\}$, $\{a, d\}$, $\{a, c, e\}$. Complete: $\{a\}$, $\{a, d\}$, $\{a, c, e\}$; the empty set is not complete because it defends the unattacked $a$. Grounded: $\{a\}$. Preferred and stable: $\{a, d\}$ and $\{a, c, e\}$.

3. The iteration gives $\emptyset$, $\{a\}$, $\{a, c, g\}$, and then stops. The grounded labelling has $a, c, g$ in, $b, d$ out, and $e, f$ undec. In the counting algorithm $a$ starts at count 0, and $c$ and $g$ reach 0 when $b$ is out. The count of $e$ drops from 2 to 1 when $d$ is out, and the count of $f$ stays 1, since its only attacker is itself. So $e$ and $f$ end with no label: they are the undec arguments.

6. Six variables, and six clauses that define $\mathit{out}_a \leftrightarrow \mathit{in}_b$, $\mathit{out}_b \leftrightarrow \mathit{in}_a$, $\mathit{out}_c \leftrightarrow \mathit{in}_b$, three conflict clauses (two of them the same) and three defence clauses $\neg \mathit{in}_a \vee \mathit{out}_b$, $\neg \mathit{in}_b \vee \mathit{out}_a$, $\neg \mathit{in}_c \vee \mathit{out}_b$: twelve in all. The models have the in parts $\emptyset$, $\{a\}$, $\{b\}$, $\{a, c\}$. With $\mathit{in}_c$ added the only model is $\{a, c\}$, so $c$ is credulously accepted. The loop grows $\{a\}$ to $\{a, c\}$, finds no larger set, adds the clause $\mathit{in}_b$, finds $\{b\}$, adds $\mathit{in}_a \vee \mathit{in}_c$, and stops: the preferred extensions are $\{a, c\}$ and $\{b\}$.

8. Show first that a preferred extension $P$ contains, for each variable $x$, one of $x$ and $\bar{x}$. If it contains neither, no clause containing $x$ is in $P$, since $P$ would have to defend that clause against $x$ and so contain $\bar{x}$; then $P \cup \{x\}$ is still admissible. So $P$ fixes an assignment, and the clauses in $P$ are exactly those whose literals are all false. For the converse, check that the set described in the exercise attacks every argument outside it.

9. In a symmetric framework every member of a conflict-free set attacks back each of its attackers. A maximal independent set attacks each argument outside it because it is adjacent to it. For sceptical acceptance, an attacker $b$ of $a$ forms the admissible set $\{b\}$, which lies in some preferred extension that cannot contain $a$.

10. A stable extension is independent, being conflict-free, and dominating, since it attacks everything outside. Independent and dominating means maximal independent. For the lower bound take $k$ disjoint triangles in which every two arguments attack each other: $3^k$ stable extensions, and $4^k$ complete extensions, since each triangle contributes either nothing or one of its three arguments.

12. The arguments are $A_1 = a$, $A_2 = A_1 \Rightarrow b$ and $A_3 = A_1 \Rightarrow \neg b$. $A_2$ and $A_3$ rebut each other, and $A_1$ cannot be attacked. Without preferences the grounded extension is $\{A_1\}$ and the preferred extensions are $\{A_1, A_2\}$ and $\{A_1, A_3\}$. With $d_1 < d_2$, $A_2 \prec A_3$, so only $A_3$ defeats $A_2$, and the grounded extension, also the only preferred one, is $\{A_1, A_3\}$, with the conclusions $a$ and $\neg b$.

15. $K * \neg a = \mathrm{Cn}(\neg a \wedge b)$, from the world $01$ in the second sphere. $K * \neg b = \mathrm{Cn}(\neg b)$, since only the outer sphere meets $\neg b$. So $K \div a$ has the models $11, 01$ and is $\mathrm{Cn}(b)$, and $K \div b$ has the models $11, 10, 00$ and is $\mathrm{Cn}(a \vee \neg b)$.

17. $E(a) = E(b) = E(a \wedge b) = 1$ and $E(a \vee b) = 2$. Contracting by $a$ keeps $b$, because $E(a) < E(a \vee b)$, and gives $\mathrm{Cn}(b)$. Contracting by $a \wedge b$ keeps $a \vee b$ and gives $\mathrm{Cn}(a \vee b)$. The Levi identity gives $K * \neg a = \mathrm{Cn}(b) + \neg a = \mathrm{Cn}(\neg a \wedge b)$, which is Dalal's answer too: these ranks are Dalal's preorder.

18. Put all models of $\psi$ at the bottom, tied, and order the other worlds by $L$. This preorder is total, its least worlds are the models of $\psi$, and it depends only on the models of $\psi$. Maxichoice contraction keeps one extra world, and its revisions decide every sentence.

21. Conditionalization: $01 \mapsto 0$, $00 \mapsto 2$, $11 \mapsto 2$, $10 \mapsto 4$. Darwiche and Pearl: $01 \mapsto 0$, $11 \mapsto 1$, $00 \mapsto 2$, $10 \mapsto 3$. Both believe $\neg a \wedge b$. The firmness of $\neg a$ is the least rank of an $a$-world: 2 after conditionalization, 1 after the revision of Darwiche and Pearl.

22. After $\neg a$, the operator of Darwiche and Pearl and natural revision both order the worlds $01, 11, 10, 00$, and the next revision by $\neg b$ gives $a \wedge \neg b$, which is also the answer of $\neg b$ alone. Lexicographic revision puts $00$ below every $a$-world and gives $\neg a \wedge \neg b$. Here $\neg b$ entails neither $\neg a$ nor $a$, and the revision by $\neg b$ alone entails $a$, so none of the four postulates applies.

23. Within the $\mu$-worlds and within the $\neg \mu$-worlds the ranks shift by a constant, so the order is kept. For a $\mu$-world $w_1$ and a $\neg \mu$-world $w_2$, compare $\kappa(w_1) - \kappa(\mu)$ with $\kappa(w_2) - \kappa(\neg \mu) + n$, and use $n \ge \kappa(\neg \mu) - \kappa(\mu)$ to bound the second from below by $\kappa(w_2) - \kappa(\mu)$.

25. Write the total distance as $\Sigma_1(w) + n \Sigma_2(w)$, with $0 \le \Sigma_1(w) \le |\Psi_1| \cdot N$. If $w$ does not minimize $\Sigma_2$ over the models of $\mu$, a minimizer $v$ has a smaller total. The maximum of a multiset does not change when an element is repeated.
