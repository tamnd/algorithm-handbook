## History

The statistics of text came first. George Kingsley Zipf described the rank-frequency law in 1949, and H. S. Heaps stated the law of vocabulary growth in 1978; both are fits to data. Julie Beth Lovins published the first stemmer in 1968, and Martin Porter wrote his simpler algorithm in 1979 at the Computer Laboratory in Cambridge; it appeared in the journal Program in 1980. For wildcard queries, Manning, Raghavan and Schuetze credit Garfield (1976) with one of the first full descriptions of the permuterm index, and Zobel and Dart (1995) studied k-gram indexes for finding the candidates of a misspelled word.

Inverted files and signature files competed for a long time. Faloutsos and Christodoulakis (1984) analyzed signature files with superimposed coding and their false-drop probability, and Faloutsos (1985) surveyed the access methods for text. Zobel, Moffat and Ramamohanarao (1998) compared signature files with compressed inverted files and found the inverted files better in time, space and the range of queries they support. Moffat and Zobel (1996) answered AND queries from the shortest list and checked each candidate in the longer compressed lists with the help of skips. The theory of intersection grew from the merge: Demaine, López-Ortiz and Munro (SODA 2000) gave adaptive intersection with a bound in terms of the gaps of the shortest proof of the result, Barbay and Kenyon (SODA 2002) gave the Sequential algorithm and its analysis, and Baeza-Yates (CPM 2004) gave the recursive median method. Barbay, López-Ortiz, Lu and Salinger (2009) compared these methods on a log of web queries, and Culpepper and Moffat (2010) studied them on compressed lists.

Compression and construction matured together. Elias (1974) published the representation now called Elias-Fano, after a memo by Fano; Vigna (2013) made it practical for inverted indexes, and Ottaviano and Venturini (2014) partitioned it with a dynamic program. Anh and Moffat (2005) introduced Simple-9. Blandford and Blelloch (2002), Shieh and others (2003) and Silvestri (2007) renumbered documents to shrink the gaps, and Dhulipala and others (KDD 2016) introduced recursive graph bisection, which Mackenzie and others reproduced in 2019. Heinz and Zobel (2003) described single-pass construction with in-memory partial indexes, and Manning, Raghavan and Schuetze (2008) gave the names blocked sort-based indexing and SPIMI. For updates, logarithmic merging is the static-to-dynamic transformation of Bentley and Saxe (1980), and Lester, Moffat and Zobel (CIKM 2005) introduced geometric partitioning.

Ranked retrieval brought dynamic pruning. Turtle and Flood (1995) described MaxScore, and Persin, Zobel and Sacks-Davis (1996) sorted lists by term frequency to stop early. Carmel and others (2001) introduced static index pruning. Broder, Carmel, Herscovici, Soffer and Zien (CIKM 2003) introduced WAND, and Anh and Moffat (2006) gave pruned score-at-a-time evaluation on impact-ordered indexes. Risvik, Aasheim and Lidal (2003) described tiers of documents for web search. Ding and Suel (SIGIR 2011) added block maxima to WAND, Mallia and others (2017) made the blocks of variable size, and Lin and Trotman (2015) used a fixed budget of postings for anytime ranking.

## Further reading

- Christopher D. Manning, Prabhakar Raghavan and Hinrich Schuetze, *Introduction to Information Retrieval*, Cambridge University Press, 2008. Its first chapters cover the index, the dictionary, construction, compression and fast top $k$ retrieval, with small examples.
- Stefan Buettcher, Charles L. A. Clarke and Gordon V. Cormack, *Information Retrieval: Implementing and Evaluating Search Engines*, MIT Press, 2010. Index construction, compression and query processing as they are built in real systems, with measurements.
- Ian H. Witten, Alistair Moffat and Timothy C. Bell, *Managing Gigabytes: Compressing and Indexing Documents and Images*, second edition, Morgan Kaufmann, 1999. The classic book on compressed inverted files, signature files and index construction.
- Justin Zobel and Alistair Moffat, "Inverted files for text search engines", *ACM Computing Surveys* 38(2), 2006. A survey of inverted indexes, from construction to query evaluation and pruning.
- Nicola Tonellotto, Craig Macdonald and Iadh Ounis, "Efficient query processing for scalable web search", *Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval* 12(4-5), 2018. A survey of dynamic pruning, MaxScore, WAND, block-max methods and their measured trade-offs.
- Giulio Ermanno Pibiri and Rossano Venturini, "Techniques for inverted index compression", *ACM Computing Surveys*, 2021. A survey of the integer codes and list representations used for posting lists, with experiments.
