## History

James Allen's paper in *Communications of the ACM* (1983) gave the 13 interval relations, their transitivity table and a propagation procedure with a queue, built on the path consistency algorithms of Mackworth (1977). Vilain and Kautz (1986) showed that consistency of interval networks is NP-hard, and they introduced the point algebra, on which propagation is polynomial; the revised report with van Beek (1990) corrected the treatment of the point algebra. Van Beek (1992) gave the algorithms for consistent scenarios and minimal labels of point networks, and Ladkin and Maddux (1994) proved that path consistency decides the point algebra, within the theory of relation algebras. Nebel and Bürckert (1995) found the ORD-Horn class and proved that it is a maximal tractable class that contains all base relations. Drakengren and Jonsson (1997) found further classes, and Krokhin, Jeavons and Jonsson (2003) completed the classification: every subalgebra of the interval algebra is tractable or NP-complete, with 18 maximal tractable ones.

Spatial calculi followed the temporal ones. Egenhofer and Franzosa (1991) classified the relations between regions by the intersections of interiors and boundaries, and Egenhofer (1991) added exteriors and derived the composition table. Randell, Cui and Cohn (1992) built the region connection calculus from a single connection relation. Nebel (1995) showed that RCC8 networks of base relations are polynomial. Renz and Nebel (1999) proved RCC8 NP-complete and found the maximal class $\hat{H}_8$, and Renz (1999) completed the list of maximal tractable classes that contain all base relations with $C_8$ and $Q_8$. For directions, Frank (1991) defined the cardinal directions of points, Ligozat (1998) classified their complexity, and Freksa (1992) introduced the double cross, which Scivos and Nebel (2001) showed NP-hard for base relations. Balbiani, Condotta and Fariñas del Cerro (1999) extended the tractable interval classes to rectangles. Ligozat and Renz (2004) stated a general definition of a qualitative calculus, and Renz and Ligozat (2005) separated weak from strong composition.

Metric temporal networks start with Dechter, Meiri and Pearl (1991), who defined the simple temporal problem and the TCSP and proved the TCSP NP-hard. Schwalb and Dechter (1993) studied why path consistency fragments the intervals of a TCSP. Stergiou and Koubarakis (2000) set out the disjunctive temporal problem, and Tsamardinos and Pollack (2003) built the solver Epilitis. Vidal and Fargier (1999) defined networks with contingent durations and the three kinds of controllability. Morris, Muscettola and Vidal (2001) gave the first check of dynamic controllability, which was pseudo-polynomial. Morris and Muscettola (2005) made it polynomial, Morris (2006) brought it to $O(N^4)$ and Morris (2014) to $O(N^3)$. Hunsberger (2009) corrected the definition of a dynamic strategy, and Cairo, Hunsberger and Rizzi (2018) gave a faster algorithm for networks with few contingent links.

Qualitative physics grew in parallel. In 1984 the journal *Artificial Intelligence* published, in volume 24, de Kleer and Brown's qualitative physics based on confluences and Forbus's qualitative process theory. Kuipers (1986) gave QSIM, proved it sound, and showed by the frictionless spring that it is not complete. Falkenhainer and Forbus (1991) assembled models from libraries of fragments, and Kuipers's book (1994) collected QSIM and its later filters.

## Further reading

- Gérard Ligozat, *Qualitative Spatial and Temporal Reasoning*, ISTE and Wiley, 2011. A book on the point, interval, region and direction calculi in one algebraic framework, with their tractable classes.
- Frank Dylla and others, "A survey of qualitative spatial and temporal calculi: algebraic and computational properties", *ACM Computing Surveys* 50(1), 2017. A catalogue of many calculi, with the algebraic laws that each satisfies and what is known about its consistency problem.
- Rina Dechter, *Constraint Processing*, Morgan Kaufmann, 2003. Chapter 12 covers temporal constraint networks: the STP, the TCSP and qualitative networks, with path consistency for each.
- Andrei Krokhin, Peter Jeavons and Peter Jonsson, "Reasoning about temporal relations: the tractable subalgebras of Allen's interval algebra", *Journal of the ACM* 50(5), 2003. The complete classification of the interval algebra, and a model of how such a dichotomy is proved.
- Benjamin Kuipers, *Qualitative Reasoning: Modeling and Simulation with Incomplete Knowledge*, MIT Press, 1994. The book account of qualitative differential equations, QSIM, and the filters and numeric ranges that make it practical.
