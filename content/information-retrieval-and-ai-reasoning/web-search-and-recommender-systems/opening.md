A web search engine answers a query in a fraction of a second, but most of its work happens before the query arrives. A crawler has fetched billions of pages by following links, thrown away the copies, and come back to the pages that change. Link analysis has scored the pages, and the anchor text of the links pointing to each page has been indexed as part of it. When the user types, the engine suggests whole queries from the first letters, corrects a misspelled word, and after ranking shows a snippet under each link. Then it watches the clicks, which say something about relevance, though less than they seem to, because users click what is at the top.

A recommender system has a similar task with no query. A film service knows which films each customer rated or watched and must guess which unseen ones each customer will like. The data is a matrix with a row per user and a column per item, and almost all of it is blank. The methods fill in the blanks from the users who agree with this one, from the items that are rated like this one, or from a few hidden factors fitted to the known entries.

The two halves of the chapter share more than they seem to. Both learn from what people do: links, queries, clicks, plays and ratings. Both meet the same troubles with that data. It is biased by what the system showed, it is mostly positive, it can be forged, and the right answer is future behavior that is not in the input. So most of the methods here are heuristics and fitted models whose gains are measured on named collections and logs. The exceptions are worth noticing: the URL frontier provably keeps every host's politeness gap, the greedy diversifier IA-Select comes within a factor $1 - 1/e$ of the best answer, alternating least squares never increases its objective, and nuclear-norm minimization recovers a low-rank matrix exactly, with high probability, when its hypotheses hold.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Crawling the web.** The [crawling problem](/web-crawling/), the [URL frontier](/url-frontier/) with its priority and politeness queues, the [bow-tie](/bow-tie-web-structure/) shape of the web graph, the [revisit policy](/crawl-revisit-policy/) that keeps copies fresh, [focused crawling](/focused-crawling/) for one topic, and [near-duplicate detection](/near-duplicate-document-detection/).
2. **Links, anchors and spam.** [Anchor text](/anchor-text-indexing/) as a description of a page, [link spam](/link-spam/) as the attack on link analysis, and two link scores built with spam and stability in mind: [TrustRank](/trustrank/) and [SALSA](/salsa-algorithm/).
3. **Around the query.** [Query auto-completion](/query-auto-completion/) and its baseline, [most popular completion](/most-popular-completion/); [noisy channel spelling correction](/noisy-channel-spelling-correction/); and [query-biased snippets](/query-biased-snippet/).
4. **Searching many engines.** [Federated search](/federated-search/) and the [CORI](/cori-resource-selection/) method for choosing the engines to ask.
5. **Diversifying the results.** The [diversification problem](/search-result-diversification/) and three greedy methods: [maximal marginal relevance](/maximal-marginal-relevance/), [IA-Select](/ia-select/) and [xQuAD](/xquad/).
6. **Learning from clicks.** [Position bias](/position-bias/) and the [click models](/click-model/) that correct for it: [cascade](/cascade-click-model/), [position-based](/position-based-click-model/) and [DBN](/dbn-click-model/).
7. **The recommendation problem.** The [problem](/recommendation-problem/), the [rating matrix](/rating-matrix/), [collaborative](/collaborative-filtering/) and [content-based](/content-based-filtering/) filtering, the [cold-start problem](/cold-start-problem/) and [hybrids](/hybrid-recommender/).
8. **Neighborhood methods.** [User-based](/user-based-collaborative-filtering/) and [item-based](/item-based-collaborative-filtering/) filtering, [Slope One](/slope-one/) and [SLIM](/sparse-linear-method/).
9. **Latent factor models.** [Matrix factorization](/matrix-factorization-recommendation/) by SGD and by [alternating least squares](/alternating-least-squares/), its probabilistic and feature-based extensions, and the theory of [matrix completion](/matrix-completion/) with the [exact recovery theorem](/exact-matrix-completion-theorem/).
10. **Implicit feedback, ranking and sequences.** [Implicit feedback](/implicit-feedback/), [weighted ALS](/implicit-feedback-als/), [Bayesian personalized ranking](/bayesian-personalized-ranking/), [neural collaborative filtering](/neural-collaborative-filtering/), and next-item prediction with [GRU4Rec](/gru4rec/) and [SASRec](/sasrec/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [web crawling](/web-crawling/), [the URL frontier](/url-frontier/), [near-duplicate detection](/near-duplicate-document-detection/), [link spam](/link-spam/), [TrustRank](/trustrank/), [most popular completion](/most-popular-completion/), [maximal marginal relevance](/maximal-marginal-relevance/), [position bias](/position-bias/), [the cascade click model](/cascade-click-model/), [the recommendation problem](/recommendation-problem/), [collaborative filtering](/collaborative-filtering/), [item-based filtering](/item-based-collaborative-filtering/), [matrix factorization](/matrix-factorization-recommendation/), [alternating least squares](/alternating-least-squares/) and [implicit feedback](/implicit-feedback/). The groups are nearly independent, so you can start with the recommender half if that is what you need. Skip at first the federated search group, the DBN click model, factorization machines, the matrix completion theorem and the neural and sequential models of the last group; they build on the core entries and on machine learning from Volume 25.

## Before you start

The crawl is a [breadth-first search](/breadth-first-search/) of a [directed graph](/directed-graph/), and the bow-tie is described by [strongly connected components](/strongly-connected-components/), all in Volume 7, which also has [PageRank](/pagerank/), [personalized PageRank](/personalized-pagerank/) and [HITS](/hits-algorithm/); this chapter assumes them. Near-duplicate detection uses [shingling](/shingling/), [Jaccard similarity](/jaccard-similarity/), [MinHash](/minhash/) and [SimHash](/simhash/) from Volume 5. Spelling correction uses [edit distance](/edit-distance/) from Volume 8, and completion uses the [trie](/trie/) of Volume 3. The latent factor models use the [singular value decomposition](/singular-value-decomposition/) of Volume 12 and [stochastic gradient descent](/stochastic-gradient-descent/) of Volume 15; the click models use [Bayesian networks](/bayesian-network/) and [expectation maximization](/expectation-maximization/) from Volume 25. The ranking side needs [ranked retrieval](/ranked-retrieval/) and [tf-idf](/tf-idf/) from Chapters 35.1 and 35.2, and the measures used to judge every method, such as [NDCG](/normalized-discounted-cumulative-gain/), are in Chapter 35.3.
