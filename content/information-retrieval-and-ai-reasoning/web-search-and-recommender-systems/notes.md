## History

The first web crawlers and search engines appeared in 1993 and 1994. McBryan's World Wide Web Worm (1994) already indexed the anchor text of links. Brin and Page (1998) described the Google prototype, with [PageRank](/pagerank/) and anchor text, and Cho, Garcia-Molina and Page (1998) studied the order in which a crawler should fetch pages. Heydon and Najork built the Mercator crawler (1999), whose two-stage [URL frontier](/url-frontier/) is the standard design. Chakrabarti, van den Berg and Dom (1999) proposed [focused crawling](/focused-crawling/), Cho and Garcia-Molina (2000, 2003) the Poisson model of [revisiting](/crawl-revisit-policy/), and Broder and colleagues (2000) measured the [bow-tie](/bow-tie-web-structure/) on an AltaVista crawl of about 200 million pages. Duplicates were handled with shingles and min-wise hashing by Broder, Glassman, Manasse and Zweig (1997), and later with the fingerprints of Charikar (2002), used at web scale by Manku, Jain and Das Sarma (2007).

Link spam followed link ranking. Gyöngyi, Garcia-Molina and Pedersen proposed [TrustRank](/trustrank/) in 2004, Gyöngyi and Garcia-Molina gave a taxonomy and the optimal farms in 2005, and spam mass came in 2006. Lempel and Moran (2000) proposed [SALSA](/salsa-algorithm/) as a stochastic version of [HITS](/hits-algorithm/). Around the query, Kernighan, Church and Gale (1990) gave the [noisy channel](/noisy-channel-spelling-correction/) speller, Tombros and Sanderson (1998) query-biased summaries, Callan, Lu and Croft (1995) the CORI method of [federated search](/federated-search/), and Bar-Yossef and Kraus (2011) named [most popular completion](/most-popular-completion/). For diversity, Carbonell and Goldstein (1998) proposed [MMR](/maximal-marginal-relevance/), Agrawal, Gollapudi, Halverson and Ieong (2009) [IA-Select](/ia-select/) with its greedy bound, and Santos, Macdonald and Ounis (2010) [xQuAD](/xquad/).

Clicks became data when Joachims and colleagues (2005) showed with eye tracking that users trust the top ranks. Craswell, Zoeter, Taylor and Ramsey (2008) compared models of [position bias](/position-bias/) and found that the [cascade model](/cascade-click-model/) predicted the clicks in their logs best, most clearly at the top ranks, and Chapelle and Zhang (2009) added satisfaction in the [DBN model](/dbn-click-model/). The book of Chuklin, Markov and de Rijke (2015) collected the models with a common way to fit and compare them.

Recommenders began with Tapestry (Goldberg and colleagues, 1992), where users named whose opinions to follow, and with GroupLens (Resnick and colleagues, 1994), which computed [user-based](/user-based-collaborative-filtering/) predictions automatically. Sarwar, Karypis, Konstan and Riedl (2001) studied [item-based](/item-based-collaborative-filtering/) filtering, and Linden, Smith and York (2003) described its use at Amazon. The Netflix Prize (2006 to 2009) offered a million dollars for a 10% lower RMSE than Netflix's own system on a fixed test set. It made [matrix factorization](/matrix-factorization-recommendation/) the standard model: Funk's SGD (2006), [PMF](/probabilistic-matrix-factorization/) by Salakhutdinov and Mnih (2007), [ALS-WR](/alternating-least-squares/) by Zhou and colleagues (2008) and [SVD++](/svd-plus-plus/) by Koren (2008), followed by Rendle's [factorization machines](/factorization-machine/) (2010). In parallel, Candès and Recht (2009), Candès and Tao (2010), Gross (2011) and Recht (2011) proved that nuclear-norm minimization [completes](/exact-matrix-completion-theorem/) incoherent low-rank matrices from few random entries.

For [implicit feedback](/implicit-feedback/), Hu, Koren and Volinsky (2008) proposed [weighted ALS](/implicit-feedback-als/) and Rendle and colleagues (2009) [BPR](/bayesian-personalized-ranking/). Neural models followed: [GRU4Rec](/gru4rec/) by Hidasi and colleagues (2016), [NCF](/neural-collaborative-filtering/) by He and colleagues (2017) and [SASRec](/sasrec/) by Kang and McAuley (2018). Careful reruns by Ludewig and Jannach (2018), Ferrari Dacrema, Cremonesi and Jannach (2019) and Rendle, Krichene, Zhang and Anderson (2020) found that tuned simple baselines matched many of the reported neural gains.

## Further reading

- Christopher D. Manning, Prabhakar Raghavan and Hinrich Schütze, *Introduction to Information Retrieval*, Cambridge University Press, 2008. Chapters 19 to 21 cover web search, crawling, near duplicates and link analysis.
- Christopher Olston and Marc Najork, "Web crawling", *Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval*, 2010. A survey of crawler architecture, ordering, revisiting and politeness by two of the builders.
- Aleksandr Chuklin, Ilya Markov and Maarten de Rijke, *Click Models for Web Search*, Morgan and Claypool, 2015. The reference for the click models of this chapter, their estimation and their evaluation.
- Yehuda Koren, Robert Bell and Chris Volinsky, "Matrix factorization techniques for recommender systems", *IEEE Computer*, 2009. A short account of the latent factor models that came out of the Netflix Prize.
- Francesco Ricci, Lior Rokach and Bracha Shapira, editors, *Recommender Systems Handbook*, Springer. A broad collection of surveys on neighborhood, factor, content-based and evaluation methods.
