A doctor types *warfarin aspirin bleeding* into the search box of a medical library. The engine intersects three posting lists, the sorted lists of the documents that hold each word, by jumping through the long lists, so the work depends mostly on the short one. Each document left gets a BM25 score, and the ten best are shown. Whether they are the right ten is measured on a test collection judged by people, with a metric such as nDCG. The hospital also keeps an ontology, which says that aspirin is an anti-inflammatory drug and warfarin an anticoagulant, and a rule that flags a patient given both, unless a doctor has approved the pair. A reasoner classifies the drugs, raises the flag by default, withdraws it when the approval arrives, and checks that the dosing times fit the ward's schedule.

This volume is about both kinds of algorithm. Its eight chapters hold 278 entries. The first four are about retrieval, from the index to web search and recommendation; the last four are about reasoning, from description logics and rules to argumentation, belief change and qualitative time and space.

## Why retrieval and reasoning share a volume

Both halves answer a query against a large body of stored knowledge. Retrieval ranks documents by how likely each is to be relevant; reasoning derives what follows from facts and rules. A few ideas come back in both halves:

- **Pay once, query many times.** An [inverted index](/inverted-index/) is built offline so that a query reads only a few short lists. [Knowledge compilation](/knowledge-compilation/) translates a knowledge base once into a form, such as [d-DNNF](/d-dnnf/), in which entailment and model counting take time polynomial in its size, though that size can be exponential. [Ontology classification](/ontology-classification/) and the [Rete algorithm](/rete-algorithm/) also store work for later queries.
- **Exact computation, measured quality.** In retrieval, what a method computes is proved, and how good the answer is gets measured. [WAND](/wand/) returns the exact top $k$ when its score bounds are true bounds, but whether [BM25](/bm25/) puts the relevant documents in that top $k$ is a measurement on a test collection. The [probability ranking principle](/probability-ranking-principle/) is a theorem only under its assumptions.
- **Expressive power has a price.** Deduction from [Horn clauses](/horn-clause/) takes linear time. Concept satisfiability in [ALC](/alc-description-logic/) is [PSPACE-complete](/alc-pspace-completeness/) without a TBox and [EXPTIME-complete](/alc-general-tbox-exptime/) with general TBoxes; stable model existence is [NP-complete](/stable-model-np-completeness/), and extension existence in [default logic](/default-logic/) is [$\Sigma_2^P$-complete](/default-logic-complexity/). Much of the second half studies tractable fragments: [EL](/el-description-logic/), [DL-Lite](/dl-lite/), [stratified programs](/stratified-logic-program/), the [grounded extension](/grounded-extension/), [ORD-Horn](/ord-horn-subclass/).
- **Conclusions can be withdrawn.** [Negation as failure](/negation-as-failure/), defaults, [belief revision](/belief-revision/) and argumentation each say how a new fact can remove an old conclusion, and most define their answers as fixed points.

## The chapters

**Chapter 35.1, Indexing and query processing**, starts from the [term-document incidence matrix](/term-document-incidence-matrix/) and the [posting list](/posting-list/). [Zipf's law](/zipfs-law/) and [Heaps' law](/heaps-law/) are empirical fits whose constants depend on the collection. Indexes are built by [blocked sort-based](/blocked-sort-based-indexing/) and [single-pass](/single-pass-in-memory-indexing/) indexing, kept current by [logarithmic merging](/logarithmic-merging/), and compressed with [gap encoding](/postings-gap-encoding/), [Simple-9](/simple-9/) and [partitioned Elias-Fano](/partitioned-elias-fano/), with ratios measured on named collections. [Merge intersection](/merge-based-list-intersection/) is linear in the sum of the lengths, while [Baeza-Yates](/baeza-yates-intersection/) and the [adaptive method](/adaptive-set-intersection/) of Demaine, López-Ortiz and Munro have proved adaptive bounds. [MaxScore](/maxscore/), WAND and [block-max WAND](/block-max-wand/) are safe with measured speedups; [tiered indexes](/tiered-index/) and [static pruning](/static-index-pruning/) trade measured quality for speed.

**Chapter 35.2, Ranking models**, runs from the [Boolean model](/boolean-retrieval-model/) and the [vector space model](/vector-space-model/) to the [binary independence model](/binary-independence-model/), BM25 and [divergence from randomness](/divergence-from-randomness/), and to [query likelihood](/query-likelihood-model/) with [Dirichlet smoothing](/dirichlet-prior-smoothing/); their parameters are tuned and their gains measured. [Rocchio](/rocchio-algorithm/) and [RM3](/relevance-model-rm3/) rewrite the query. [Kemeny aggregation](/kemeny-rank-aggregation/) is NP-hard already for four rankings; on full rankings the footrule optimum is within a factor 2 of it and the [Borda count](/borda-count/) within 5, while [reciprocal rank fusion](/reciprocal-rank-fusion/) has no guarantee. [LambdaMART](/lambdamart/), [ColBERT](/colbert/), [SPLADE](/splade/) and [retrieval-augmented generation](/retrieval-augmented-generation/) are judged on named benchmarks.

**Chapter 35.3, Retrieval evaluation**, defines the metrics on a [test collection](/test-collection/): [precision at k](/precision-at-k/), [mean average precision](/mean-average-precision/), [nDCG](/normalized-discounted-cumulative-gain/). The metrics are definitions; what they say about users is a model, made explicit by [rank-biased precision](/rank-biased-precision/), [expected reciprocal rank](/expected-reciprocal-rank/) and the [C/W/L framework](/cwl-framework/). [Pooling](/pooling/) leaves judgments incomplete and can count against systems outside the pool, which [bpref](/bpref/) and [inferred AP](/inferred-average-precision/) are built to handle. [Team-draft interleaving](/team-draft-interleaving/) compares rankers by live clicks, and every comparison of two systems needs a paired test.

**Chapter 35.4, Web search and recommender systems**, covers [crawling](/web-crawling/) with a polite [URL frontier](/url-frontier/), [near-duplicate detection](/near-duplicate-document-detection/), and link analysis against [link spam](/link-spam/) with [TrustRank](/trustrank/) and [SALSA](/salsa-algorithm/). [Click models](/click-model/) are fitted to logs, not facts about users. Choosing a diverse top $k$ is NP-hard, and [IA-Select](/ia-select/) gets a factor $1 - 1/e$. [Matrix factorization](/matrix-factorization-recommendation/) is nonconvex; [alternating least squares](/alternating-least-squares/) never raises its error but reaches only a local solution. [Nuclear-norm minimization](/exact-matrix-completion-theorem/) recovers a low-rank matrix exactly with high probability, under incoherence and enough samples. The gains of [collaborative filtering](/collaborative-filtering/) and [SASRec](/sasrec/) are offline measurements on one data set and split.

**Chapter 35.5, Knowledge representation and description logics**, moves from [semantic networks](/semantic-network/) and [frames](/frame-knowledge-representation/) to ALC with [TBoxes and ABoxes](/tbox-and-abox/) under the [open-world assumption](/open-world-assumption/). The [tableau algorithm](/alc-tableau-algorithm/) is sound, complete and terminating, with [blocking](/tableau-blocking/) once a general TBox makes cycles possible. [EL classification](/el-classification/) is polynomial, [PerfectRef](/perfectref-rewriting/) turns DL-Lite queries into first-order queries over the data, and [SROIQ](/sroiq/) (behind OWL 2 DL) is N2EXPTIME-complete. It adds the [situation calculus](/situation-calculus/) with [regression](/situation-calculus-regression/), the [event calculus](/event-calculus/) and the [knowledge compilation map](/knowledge-compilation-map/).

**Chapter 35.6, Rule-based and nonmonotonic reasoning**, starts with [production systems](/production-system/), [forward](/forward-chaining/) and [backward chaining](/backward-chaining/), and the Rete and [TREAT](/treat-algorithm/) matchers, whose speedups are measured; the partial matches that Rete stores can take much memory. [Certainty factors](/certainty-factors/) are not probabilities. A stratified program has one standard model, and the [well-founded model](/well-founded-semantics/) is computed in polynomial time and may be partial. [Stable models](/stable-model-semantics/) drive [answer set programming](/answer-set-programming/); [Fages' theorem](/fages-theorem/) for tight programs and the [loop formulas](/loop-formulas/) reduce them to SAT, and answer sets of [disjunctive programs](/disjunctive-asp-sigma2-completeness/) are $\Sigma_2^P$-complete. [Circumscription](/circumscription/), [System Z](/system-z/), [diagnosis](/reiter-hs-tree/) and [truth maintenance](/justification-based-truth-maintenance/) follow.

**Chapter 35.7, Argumentation and belief revision**, starts from Dung's [argumentation frameworks](/abstract-argumentation-framework/). The grounded extension is unique and is [computed in linear time](/grounded-extension-algorithm/); under [preferred semantics](/preferred-extension/), credulous acceptance is NP-complete and sceptical acceptance $\Pi_2^P$-complete, so solvers use [SAT encodings](/argumentation-sat-encoding/). Belief revision rests on the [AGM postulates](/agm-postulates/), characterized by the [representation theorem](/agm-representation-theorem/) and by the [Katsuno-Mendelzon theorem](/katsuno-mendelzon-revision/). [Gärdenfors' theorem](/gardenfors-impossibility-theorem/) shows that the Ramsey test and AGM revision cannot both hold in a nontrivial setting, and iterated revision needs the [Darwiche-Pearl postulates](/darwiche-pearl-postulates/).

**Chapter 35.8, Qualitative temporal and spatial reasoning**, builds [qualitative calculi](/qualitative-constraint-calculus/) with [composition tables](/composition-table/) and [algebraic closure](/algebraic-closure/), which runs in cubic time but decides consistency only on tractable classes. Consistency is [NP-complete](/interval-algebra-np-completeness/) for Allen's interval algebra and for [RCC8](/region-connection-calculus-rcc8/); the [Nebel-Bürckert theorem](/nebel-burckert-theorem/) and the [Renz-Nebel theorem](/rcc8-tractable-subclasses/) name the classes where closure decides it. The [point algebra](/point-algebra/) is polynomial, and [disjunctive temporal problems](/disjunctive-temporal-problem/) are NP-complete. [Morris's algorithm](/morris-dynamic-controllability/) decides [dynamic controllability](/dynamic-controllability/) in polynomial time, and [QSIM](/qsim/) simulates qualitative physics, sound but not complete.

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course, read these entries in this order: posting list, [posting list intersection](/posting-list-intersection/), merge-based list intersection, [document-at-a-time evaluation](/document-at-a-time-evaluation/), WAND, [tf-idf](/tf-idf/), the vector space model, the probability ranking principle, BM25, test collection, mean average precision, nDCG, matrix factorization for recommendation, ALC, the ALC tableau algorithm, forward chaining, stable model semantics, the abstract argumentation framework, the grounded extension, the AGM postulates and algebraic closure.

If you already know the basics, start with the opening page of each chapter. In the retrieval half, read Chapters 35.1 and 35.2 together, since query processing scores documents with the models of Chapter 35.2; Chapter 35.3 measures what they produce, and Chapter 35.4 builds on all three. The four chapters of the reasoning half can be read in any order: only a few advanced entries cite another chapter, as the event calculus uses circumscription and System Z uses ordinal conditional functions.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained.
