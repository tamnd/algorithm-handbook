Adding a million numbers on one core takes a million steps, one after another. Add them in pairs instead, then add the pair sums in pairs, and so on: the sums form a balanced tree with twenty levels, and with enough processors the whole job takes twenty rounds. It still does 999,999 additions, the same as the loop. The trick works only because addition is associative, so the brackets can be moved freely. Run the same tree down again and it gives every prefix sum, not only the total. Prefix sums in turn give pack, the split step of radix sort, the running totals of a sparse matrix row and the carries of an adder. A short list of primitives like these carries most parallel algorithms, and this chapter is that list.

All costs are in the [work-span model](/work-span-model/) of Chapter 20.1: the work $T_1$ is the number of unit steps, the span $T_\infty$ is the longest chain of steps that depend on each other, and a greedy scheduler runs the program on $p$ processors in time $T_p \le T_1/p + T_\infty$. The question that comes back in every group is [work efficiency](/work-efficiency/): whether the span can fall to a few logarithms while the work stays that of the best sequential algorithm. Reduce gets both at once, with $n - 1$ combines and span $\lceil \lg n \rceil$. A scan can be work-efficient, but not for free: the loop does $n - 1$ combines, and Snir's bound $s + d \ge 2n - 2$ says that a prefix circuit of small depth $d$ needs nearly twice as many. Linked lists are harder still, because their order is hidden in pointers; the work-efficient methods there need random coins or deterministic coin tossing.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Ways to write parallel code.** [Data parallelism](/data-parallelism/) and [task parallelism](/task-parallelism/), and [fork-join parallelism](/fork-join-parallelism/), which structures both with spawn and sync. The [parallel for loop](/parallel-for/) and [parallel map](/parallel-map/) are the first patterns, and their spawn trees cost span $\Theta(\log n)$.
2. **Reduce.** The [associative operator](/associative-operator/), [parallel reduction](/parallel-reduce/) over a balanced tree, and the [reduction](/parallel-reduction/) problem with its monoids and lower bounds. The [histogram](/histogram-computation/) and the [parallel histogram](/parallel-histogram/) are reductions with many counters.
3. **Scan.** [All-prefix-sums](/all-prefix-sums/), the [Hillis-Steele scan](/hillis-steele-scan/) with few rounds and the [Blelloch scan](/blelloch-scan/) with linear work. The [parallel prefix](/parallel-prefix/) entry covers the circuits of Sklansky, Kogge and Stone, Ladner and Fischer, and Brent and Kung. [Segmented prefix sums](/segmented-prefix-sums/) and the [segmented scan](/segmented-scan/) run many scans at once.
4. **Moving data.** [Scatter and gather](/scatter-and-gather/) on the PRAM variants, [compaction](/compaction/) and [parallel pack](/parallel-pack/).
5. **Merging.** [Merging sorted sequences](/merging-sorted-sequences/) by ranks and co-ranks, and the divide and conquer [parallel merge](/parallel-merge/).
6. **Matrices.** The [parallel transpose](/parallel-matrix-transpose/), [parallel matrix multiplication](/parallel-matrix-multiplication/) with and without a temporary matrix, and [parallel Strassen](/parallel-strassen/).
7. **Lists and trees.** [List ranking](/list-ranking/), [pointer jumping](/pointer-jumping/), [list contraction](/list-contraction/) and the work-efficient [parallel list ranking](/parallel-list-ranking/). [Tree contraction](/tree-contraction/) and [tree expression evaluation](/tree-expression-evaluation/) carry the same idea from lists to trees.

## A first reading

Read [fork-join parallelism](/fork-join-parallelism/) and the [parallel for loop](/parallel-for/) first. Then read the [associative operator](/associative-operator/) and [parallel reduction](/parallel-reduce/), and run the tree by hand on eight numbers. Next read [all-prefix-sums](/all-prefix-sums/), the [Hillis-Steele scan](/hillis-steele-scan/) and the [Blelloch scan](/blelloch-scan/), and count the combines of both scans on the same input. Then read [scatter and gather](/scatter-and-gather/), [parallel pack](/parallel-pack/), [parallel merge](/parallel-merge/), [pointer jumping](/pointer-jumping/) and [list ranking](/list-ranking/). These twelve entries are enough for most of Chapters 20.3 and 20.4.

The circuits of the parallel prefix entry can wait until a problem needs a scan of a given depth. The matrix group does not depend on the scan groups and can be read at any time after group 1. List contraction, tree contraction and expression evaluation are the hardest entries of the chapter. Read them before Chapter 20.3, whose graph algorithms contract graphs in the same way.
