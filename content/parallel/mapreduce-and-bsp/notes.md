## History

The bulk synchronous model is the older of the two. Valiant proposed the [BSP model](/bsp-model/) in 1990, in "A bridging model for parallel computation" in Communications of the ACM, as a model that software and hardware could both aim at. He showed that a [PRAM](/pram-model/) program with enough parallel slack runs efficiently on a BSP machine with $g = O(1)$ and $L = O(\log p)$. Culler and coauthors proposed the LogP model in 1993, with more parameters and no barrier. Shi and Schaeffer gave [sorting by regular sampling](/parallel-sorting-by-regular-sampling/) in 1992, a deterministic form of the samplesort of Frazer and McKellar (1970). On the side of synchronization, Hensgen, Finkel and Manber described the dissemination [barrier](/barrier-synchronization/) in 1988, and Mellor-Crummey and Scott compared barriers on shared-memory machines in 1991. Bisseling's book of 2004 made BSP a method for scientific computing.

Dean and Ghemawat described [MapReduce](/mapreduce-model/) at OSDI in 2004, with a journal version in Communications of the ACM in 2008. The paper already had the [combiner](/combiner/), the [partitioner](/partitioner/), [re-execution](/fault-tolerance-by-recomputation/) of the tasks of failed workers and backup tasks for [speculative execution](/speculative-execution/), and it measured [distributed grep](/distributed-grep/) and a large sort. The combiner applies an old idea of databases: Gray and coauthors had classified aggregates in 1997 as distributive, algebraic and holistic. Hadoop made the model widely available, and O'Malley's TeraSort of 2008 sorted with a sampled range partitioner as in [distributed sorting](/distributed-sorting/). Zaharia, Konwinski, Joseph, Katz and Stoica gave the LATE scheduler for backup tasks on uneven clusters at OSDI in 2008. Lin and Dyer collected the design patterns of jobs, from in-mapper combining to [secondary sort](/secondary-sort/) and the [joins](/reduce-side-join/), in their book of 2010, the same year as the comparison of join methods by Blanas and coauthors.

Graph and iterative computation came next. Malewicz and coauthors described [Pregel](/pregel-model/) at SIGMOD in 2010, a BSP system with one process per vertex. In the same year HaLoop, by Bu, Howe, Balazinska and Ernst, and Twister, by Ekanayake and coauthors, kept loop-invariant data near the tasks of [iterative MapReduce](/iterative-mapreduce/). Zaharia and coauthors presented Spark at HotCloud in 2010 and [resilient distributed datasets](/resilient-distributed-datasets/) at NSDI in 2012. Gonzalez, Low, Gu, Bickson and Guestrin introduced PowerGraph, the [gather-apply-scatter model](/gas-model/) and vertex cuts, at OSDI in 2012.

The theory began in 2010. Karloff, Suri and Vassilvitskii defined the [MRC model](/mrc-model/) at SODA and related it to NC and the PRAM. Afrati and Ullman gave the [Shares join](/hypercube-shares-join/) at EDBT in 2010, with a longer paper in IEEE TKDE in 2011. Suri and Vassilvitskii named the effect of [data skew](/data-skew/) "the curse of the last reducer" in 2011. Beame, Koutris and Suciu defined the massively parallel communication model in 2013, called the Shares method HyperCube, and in 2014 proved it optimal for one round on inputs without skew and gave versions for skewed inputs. In 2013 Afrati, Das Sarma, Salihoglu and Ullman gave [lower bounds](/mapreduce-communication-lower-bounds/) on the replication rate for Hamming distance 1, triangles and matrix multiplication.

## Further reading

- Jeffrey Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat, "MapReduce: Simplified Data Processing on Large Clusters" (2004; Communications of the ACM, 2008). The original description, with the execution, the refinements and the measurements.
- Leslie G. Valiant, "A bridging model for parallel computation" (1990). The paper that defines BSP and argues for it as a standard between programs and machines.
- Rob H. Bisseling, *Parallel Scientific Computation: A Structured Approach using BSP and MPI* (2004). BSP algorithms for sorting, linear algebra and the fast Fourier transform, with measured costs.
- Jimmy Lin and Chris Dyer, *Data-Intensive Text Processing with MapReduce* (2010). The patterns of MapReduce jobs, with word count, secondary sort, inverted indexing, joins and graph algorithms.
- Jure Leskovec, Anand Rajaraman and Jeffrey D. Ullman, *Mining of Massive Datasets*, third edition (2020). MapReduce with communication cost, multiway joins, and reducer size against replication rate.
- Maurice Herlihy and Nir Shavit, *The Art of Multiprocessor Programming* (2008). Barriers and the other synchronization methods of shared memory, with their correctness arguments.
