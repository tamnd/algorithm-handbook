A search engine keeps a log of a billion queries and wants to know how often each query was asked. On one machine this is a hash table of counts, the [frequency counter](/frequency-counter/) of Volume 3. On a thousand machines it takes three steps. Each machine reads its share of the log and emits a pair (query, 1) for every line. The pairs are routed by a hash of the query, so that all the pairs of one query reach the same machine, sorted on the way. Each machine then adds up the ones of every query it owns. That is [word count](/word-count/) in the [MapReduce model](/mapreduce-model/); the programmer writes only the first and the last step. The middle step, the [shuffle](/shuffle-phase/), is the expensive one. Without a [combiner](/combiner/) it moves one pair per line, a billion pairs across the network; with one, each machine sends one pair for each distinct query it saw. And if a single query is a tenth of the log, the machine that owns it does a tenth of the work however many machines there are, so the job runs at most 10 times faster than on one machine. That is [data skew](/data-skew/).

The same pattern of local work, an exchange of messages and a wait for everyone makes up a [superstep](/superstep/) of the [bulk synchronous parallel model](/bsp-model/). Valiant proposed it in 1990 as a bridge between programs and machines. A superstep in which each of $p$ processors does at most $w$ operations and sends and receives at most $h$ words costs $w + gh + L$, where $g$ is the cost of a word of communication and $L$ the cost of the [barrier](/barrier-synchronization/) at the end. The two constants decide the algorithm. To send one word from one processor to all $p = 16$ with $g = 4$ and $L = 50$, the root can send 15 copies in one superstep, at cost $15 \cdot 4 + 50 = 110$, or use a binary tree of 4 supersteps, at cost $4(4 + 50) = 216$. When the barrier is expensive, fewer supersteps win. Every entry asks three questions of an algorithm: how many rounds, how many words, and how large the share of the busiest machine.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **The MapReduce model.** The [model](/mapreduce-model/) and its costs: rounds, communication, the reducer size $q$ and the replication rate $r$. The [partitioner](/partitioner/) assigns keys to reduce tasks, the [shuffle phase](/shuffle-phase/) moves and sorts the pairs, and a [combiner](/combiner/) reduces them early when the reduce operation is associative and commutative.
2. **First jobs.** [Word count](/word-count/), [distributed grep](/distributed-grep/), [secondary sort](/secondary-sort/), [inverted index construction](/inverted-index/), and [distributed sorting](/distributed-sorting/) with split points taken from a sample.
3. **Running on a real cluster.** [Data skew](/data-skew/) and its fixes, [fault tolerance by re-execution](/fault-tolerance-by-recomputation/), and [speculative execution](/speculative-execution/) of slow tasks.
4. **Joins.** The [reduce-side join](/reduce-side-join/), the [map-side join](/map-side-join/) for a small relation, and the [Shares join](/hypercube-shares-join/) for several relations in one round.
5. **Bulk synchronous parallel.** [Barrier synchronization](/barrier-synchronization/), the [BSP model](/bsp-model/), the [superstep](/superstep/), and the [h-relation](/h-relation/) that prices the communication of a superstep.
6. **Iteration and graphs.** [Iterative MapReduce](/iterative-mapreduce/), [resilient distributed datasets](/resilient-distributed-datasets/), the [Pregel model](/pregel-model/) and the [gather-apply-scatter model](/gas-model/).
7. **Rounds and communication.** The [MRC model](/mrc-model/), whose cost is the number of rounds, and [lower bounds on communication](/mapreduce-communication-lower-bounds/) for jobs of one round.

## A first reading

Read the [MapReduce model](/mapreduce-model/), then the [partitioner](/partitioner/), the [shuffle phase](/shuffle-phase/) and the [combiner](/combiner/), and run [word count](/word-count/) by hand on two short documents, with and without a combiner. Then read [data skew](/data-skew/) and [fault tolerance by re-execution](/fault-tolerance-by-recomputation/), which show why the slowest task and the failed machine set the time of a job. Read [distributed sorting](/distributed-sorting/) for the range partitioner and the [reduce-side join](/reduce-side-join/) for the most common job after word count. For the other model, read [barrier synchronization](/barrier-synchronization/), the [BSP model](/bsp-model/) and the [superstep](/superstep/), and finish with the [Pregel model](/pregel-model/), where shortest paths become a synchronous Bellman-Ford. That is a first unit of thirteen entries.

Distributed grep and secondary sort are short and can wait until a job needs them. Speculative execution, the map-side join and the h-relation refine ideas of the first unit. Iterative MapReduce and resilient distributed datasets are best read together, since the second answers the cost of the first. The Shares join, the gather-apply-scatter model, the MRC model and the communication lower bounds are research-level. They need little beyond the cost measures of the first group.
