Run a breadth-first search on a path of $n = 2^{20}$ vertices, starting at one end. Each round can reach only one new vertex, so a [parallel BFS](/parallel-bfs/) needs about a million rounds, however many processors it has. Its work is linear, but its span is $O(D \log n)$, and here the number of levels $D$ is $n$. The connected components of the same path need no such chain. In [random-mate contraction](/random-mate-contraction/) every vertex flips a coin, and a tails vertex with a heads neighbour merges into it, so each vertex with a neighbour leaves with probability at least $1/4$ in every round. The expected number of rounds is at most $\log_{4/3} n + 5$, about 53 for this path. The price is work: each round may cost $O(m)$, so the total is $O(m \log n)$ in expectation, a factor $\log n$ above the sequential search.

That trade runs through the chapter. A graph problem is solved in parallel either by a search that follows the structure of the graph, with a span that grows with a length such as $D$, or by a method that shrinks or splits the graph in a polylogarithmic number of rounds, often with more work. The cost model is the [work-span model](/work-span-model/) of Chapter 20.1. The entries write the work as $W$ and the span as $S$, where Chapter 20.1 writes $T_1$ and $T_\infty$, and by [Brent's theorem](/brent-theorem/) a method with work $W$ and span $S$ runs in time at most $W/p + S$ on $p$ processors.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Frontiers and contraction.** [Frontier-based processing](/frontier-based-processing/) does work only at the vertices that changed, and switches between a sparse and a dense form. [Graph contraction](/graph-contraction/) shrinks the graph by edge, star or tree contraction.
2. **Breadth-first search and shortest paths.** Parallel BFS, [direction-optimizing BFS](/direction-optimizing-bfs/), which lets unvisited vertices look for a parent, and [delta-stepping](/delta-stepping/) for nonnegative weights.
3. **Connectivity and spanning trees.** Random-mate contraction, the [Shiloach-Vishkin](/shiloach-vishkin/) algorithm with $O(\log n)$ span, parallel Borůvka for the [minimum spanning tree](/parallel-minimum-spanning-tree/), and [Tarjan-Vishkin](/tarjan-vishkin/) for biconnected components.
4. **Symmetry breaking.** The [maximal independent set](/maximal-independent-set/) problem, [Luby's algorithm](/luby-mis/), [Jones-Plassmann](/jones-plassmann/) colouring, [Israeli-Itai](/israeli-itai/) matching and the [locally dominant matching](/locally-dominant-matching/) for weights.
5. **Strongly connected components.** [Forward-backward](/forward-backward-scc/) search from a pivot, and [colouring](/coloring-scc/) by label propagation.
6. **Flow, reachability and depth-first search.** [Parallel push-relabel](/parallel-push-relabel/), the [Ullman-Yannakakis](/ullman-yannakakis/) sampling method for transitive closure, and [parallel depth-first search](/parallel-dfs/).

## A first reading

Read [frontier-based processing](/frontier-based-processing/) and then [parallel BFS](/parallel-bfs/), and run the BFS example by hand: count the edge slots of each frontier and check that they add up to $2m$. Then read [graph contraction](/graph-contraction/) and [random-mate contraction](/random-mate-contraction/), which give the $3/4$ argument in its simplest form. [Parallel Borůvka](/parallel-minimum-spanning-tree/) uses the same hooking and pointer jumping with a deterministic choice of edge. Next read the [maximal independent set](/maximal-independent-set/) problem and [Luby's algorithm](/luby-mis/), where half of the edges go away in each round in expectation, and [Jones-Plassmann](/jones-plassmann/), which turns one random order into a colouring. Then read [delta-stepping](/delta-stepping/) and [forward-backward SCC](/forward-backward-scc/). That is a first unit of ten entries.

[Shiloach-Vishkin](/shiloach-vishkin/) and [Tarjan-Vishkin](/tarjan-vishkin/) can wait: they reach $O(\log n)$ span, but the star tests and the Euler tour bookkeeping are long. [Direction-optimizing BFS](/direction-optimizing-bfs/), the two matchings and [colouring SCC](/coloring-scc/) are best read when an implementation needs them. The last group is research-level, and each of its entries can be read alone; [parallel DFS](/parallel-dfs/) explains why the sequential search order is hard to parallelize at all.

From Chapter 20.1 you need the [work-span model](/work-span-model/), the [CRCW PRAM](/crcw-pram/) and [work efficiency](/work-efficiency/); from Chapter 20.2, [prefix sums](/all-prefix-sums/), [packs](/parallel-pack/), [pointer jumping](/pointer-jumping/) and [list ranking](/parallel-list-ranking/).
