## History

The oldest method of the chapter is sequential in origin. Borůvka (1926) joined every fragment to its nearest neighbour to design an electric network in Moravia, and since the fragments act independently his rounds became the natural [parallel minimum spanning tree](/parallel-minimum-spanning-tree/) method. The PRAM theory of graphs began with connectivity. Shiloach and Vishkin (1982) found the components in $O(\log n)$ time on the CRCW PRAM by hooking and shortcutting, and Awerbuch and Shiloach (1987) simplified [their algorithm](/shiloach-vishkin/) to the star form and extended it to minimum spanning forests. Reif (1985) used coin flips for connectivity, and Miller and Reif (1985) used them for tree contraction, the idea behind [random-mate contraction](/random-mate-contraction/). Tarjan and Vishkin (FOCS 1984, SIAM Journal on Computing 1985) found the [biconnected components](/tarjan-vishkin/) without a depth-first search tree, and introduced the Euler tour technique on the way.

The same years separated the problems that parallelize from those that may not. Cook (1985) showed that the lexicographically first [maximal independent set](/maximal-independent-set/) is P-complete, and Reif (1985) proved the same for the lexicographically first DFS order. Karp and Wigderson (1985) gave the first NC algorithm for MIS. Then Luby and, independently, Alon, Babai and Itai, both in journals in 1986, gave the simple randomized algorithms with $O(\log n)$ expected rounds, and Israeli and Itai (1986) applied the same ideas to [maximal matching](/israeli-itai/). For depth-first search, Smith (1986) gave an NC algorithm for planar graphs, and Aggarwal and Anderson (1988) and Aggarwal, Anderson and Kao (1990) showed that some DFS tree can be found in RNC. Greenlaw, Hoover and Ruzzo (1995) collected these hardness results.

Flow, closure and strongly connected components came next. Goldberg (1987) and Goldberg and Tarjan (1988) introduced push-relabel together with a [synchronous parallel form](/parallel-push-relabel/). Ullman and Yannakakis (SPAA 1990, SIAM Journal on Computing 1991) gave [transitive closure by sampling](/ullman-yannakakis/). Jones and Plassmann (1993) turned random priorities into a [colouring](/jones-plassmann/) for sparse matrix computations. Fleischer, Hendrickson and Pinar (2000) proposed [forward-backward](/forward-backward-scc/) search for SCCs, McLendon, Hendrickson, Plimpton and Rauchwerger (2005) added trimming, and Orzan (2004) introduced the [colouring method](/coloring-scc/) for distributed model checking. Preis (1999) introduced locally dominant edges, and Manne and Bisseling (2007) made them a [parallel matching](/locally-dominant-matching/).

Recent work is about practice on multicore machines. Meyer and Sanders presented [delta-stepping](/delta-stepping/) at ESA 1998 and published its analysis in 2003. Leiserson and Schardl (2010) gave a work-efficient fork-join BFS, Beamer, Asanović and Patterson (2012) added the [bottom-up step](/direction-optimizing-bfs/), and Shun and Blelloch (2013) built frontiers and the direction switch into Ligra. Blelloch, Fineman and Shun (2012) showed that greedy MIS in a random order is parallel, with an $O(\log^2 n)$ dependence length that Fischer and Noever (2018) cut to $\Theta(\log n)$. For reachability, Fineman (2018) gave the first algorithm with nearly linear work and a span of $\tilde O(n^{2/3})$.

## Further reading

- Joseph JaJa, *An Introduction to Parallel Algorithms*, Addison-Wesley (1992). The PRAM textbook, with connectivity, spanning trees, Euler tours and list ranking worked out in full.
- Umut A. Acar and Guy E. Blelloch, *Algorithms, Parallel and Sequential*, draft book for Carnegie Mellon course 15-210. Work and span, graph contraction and parallel BFS in the style of this chapter.
- Raymond Greenlaw, H. James Hoover and Walter L. Ruzzo, *Limits to Parallel Computation: P-Completeness Theory*, Oxford University Press (1995). The reference for why lexicographic DFS and MIS are hard to parallelize.
- Julian Shun and Guy E. Blelloch, *Ligra, a lightweight graph processing framework for shared memory*, PPoPP (2013). The frontier operators and the dense and sparse switch, with BFS, connectivity and other algorithms built on them.
- Jiří Barnat, Jakub Chaloupka and Jaco van de Pol, *Distributed algorithms for SCC decomposition*, Journal of Logic and Computation (2011). A comparison of forward-backward, colouring and other SCC methods.
